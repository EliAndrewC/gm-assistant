# How big is the credible pre-modern Japanese name stock?

Load this file when: expanding the `/name` pool, judging whether a name is "too modern", or
re-opening the question of how large the pool may grow. The rule this research produced lives in
`.claude/skills/name/SKILL.md` under "Period Sensibility"; this file is the why and the sources.

**The question (GM 2026-08-25).** The pool held ~100 names per gender. Could it go to 300 without
being mostly invented names? Rokugan tolerates made-up names in the historical idiom (the Hunger
Games effect - Katniss reads as a plausible descendant of real names), but a pool that is five
sixths invented breaks verisimilitude, and the project deliberately excludes MODERN Japanese
names (Naomi, Misaki, Haruto) because they do not belong to the periods Rokugan draws on.

## Male: effectively unbounded

- The samurai formal name (nanori, 諱) is two kanji, one usually the family's hereditary character
  (Minamoto 義/頼, Oda 信, Tokugawa 家, Hojo 氏, Mori 元) and one often granted by a lord.
  Three independent enumerations put the element vocabulary at ~30 core characters and ~60-80
  including the recognizable tail; a 53 x 57 grid yields ~3,000 non-identical pairs. Japanese
  Wikipedia carries disambiguation pages for well over a thousand such names. Estimate:
  1,500-3,000 attested nanori, ~3,500 combinatorially plausible.
  Sources: <https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/諱>, <https://www.sljfaq.org/afaq/historical-names.html>,
  <https://sengokudaimyo.com/japanese-names>, <https://www.chokanji.com/magazine/exploration/ex12/>.
- Common names (tsusho): birth-order Taro..Juro with prefixes, office suffixes -emon / -zaemon /
  -bei / -suke / -nosuke / -noshin / -dayu, court-office names (Ukyo, Sakon). ~40 prefixes x ~10
  suffixes gives several hundred. The -emon / -nosuke forms are Edo more than Sengoku.
- Childhood names (-maru, -chiyo, -waka), Buddhist two-kanji on-reading names: open-ended.
- Edo registers show a FLAT distribution: Noshio village 1774, 125 men, 116 distinct names, max
  repetition 2; 88% of household heads in a 1708 Sagami village carried -zaemon / -emon / -bei.
  <https://www.city.kiyose.lg.jp/siseijouhou/kouhou/shishi/1012758.html>,
  <https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/entry/index.php?id=1000281025&page=ref_view>.
- Bottom line: recognizable male stock ~5,000-8,000. A pool of 300-500 is nowhere near it.

## Female: small and flat, ~300-500 in circulation

- Outside the court, women had short kana names, 93% two morae in an 1837-1865 Kyoto register
  series, 117 of 127 in a 1671 village, drawn from auspicious words (matsu / take / ume / kiku /
  tsuru / kame / tora / fuku). Three-mora names (Chiyo, Sayo, Miyo, Kesa) are a small minority.
  <https://www.kishimotoyoshinobu.com/l/江戸～明治時代の女性名について/>,
  <https://nihonjin-name.jimdofree.com/>.
- Concentration: 22 late-Edo lists compiled to a top-100 (Tsunoda Bun'ei, 『日本の女性名』): #1 Kiku
  1.57%, #100 at 0.33%; top 10 ~13%, top 100 ~65%; the remaining third is a long tail of names
  with fewer than ~10 bearers in a ~3,000-woman sample, implying ~300-500 distinct names in real
  use. Noshio 1774: 125 women, 76 distinct, top name 4%.
  <https://maybeitisdaijirou.wordpress.com/2015/12/15/most-common-female-names-in-the-late-edo-period-a-follow-up-to-similar-post/>.
- Pre-Edo: court ladies known by service names (Murasaki Shikibu), nuns by Buddhist names, warrior
  women often unrecorded; named ones number in the low hundreds (Tomoe, Shizuka, Masako, Tomiko,
  Nene, Ichi, Chacha, Go, Hatsu). Azuchi-Momoyama and early-Edo lists:
  <https://issendai.com/wp/japanese-names/japanese-womens-names-from-the-azuchi-momoyama-period-1568-1600/>,
  <https://www.issendai.com/names/japanese/edo-era/early-edo-womens-names.html>.
- The -ko suffix: standardized for princesses by Emperor Saga, near-universal at court by late
  Heian, narrowed to court women after the 11th century, absent among commoners until the 1872
  registry law, then #1 from 1913 to 1964 and over 80% of girls in the 1930s-40s. Edo rule of
  thumb: kuge daughter 孝子, daimyo daughter 孝姫, everyone else 孝 (O-Taka).
  <https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/子_(人名)>,
  <https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/entry/index.php?id=1000083744&page=ref_view>,
  <https://www.meijiyasuda.co.jp/enjoy/ranking/year_men/girl.html>.
- No combinatorial system exists for women. Levers: kanji variants of one short name, the suffix
  ladder (X / O-X / X-hime / X-ko / X-me / X-jo), Heian auspicious-kanji + -ko.
- Bottom line: recognizable female stock ~250-400. A female pool of 300 reaches the floor of the
  attested stock; 500 is past it.

## Modern styles (the anachronism list)

Male, per Meiji Yasuda rankings (<https://www.meijiyasuda.co.jp/enjoy/ranking/topics/others1.html>):
single-kanji virtue names from ~1900 (Noboru, Isao, Tadashi, Osamu, Makoto, Hiroshi, Wataru);
-o 男/夫/雄 and -ichi / -ji / -zo numerals (Meiji-Showa); -hiko (Meiji nativist revival of a
Heian-mythic form); -ya (也 admitted as a name kanji only in 1951), -ki, Yuki, Takumi, Daisuke,
Kenta, Shota (Showa-Heisei); Haruto, Yuto, Sota, Kaito, Riku, Hinata, Ren, Aoi (2000s).
Attested despite sounding modern: Hayato (office title), Hiroki (Bakumatsu), Ukyo.

Female: Meiji-Taisho three-mora -e / -yo / -mi (Michie, Fumie, Kazuyo, Michiyo, Hidemi); postwar
-mi wave (Akemi 1965, Natsumi, Mayumi, Naomi, Emi 1970s); Ai 1980s; Misaki, Sakura, Hina, Aoi, Yui,
Rin-as-kanji 1990s-2000s (凛 not a name kanji until 2004). Attested despite sounding modern: Tomoe,
Shizuka, Rin (#23 late Edo), Saki (#31), Sayo, Chiyo, Hatsu, Natsu.

## Decision record

- The -ko suffix: SOURCE MATERIAL OVER HISTORY (GM 2026-08-25). History says court-only until
  1872; L5R canon uses -ko for samurai and peasants alike. The GM's standing rule for such
  conflicts: decide case by case, defaulting to the game's source material when it does not overly
  detract from verisimilitude. Here it does not, so -ko is allowed for every caste. A first draft
  had made it a samurai-only knob; the GM overruled that the same day. Only the BASE is period-checked.
- Pool growth (GM 2026-08-25): the existing pool is NOT purged; the ratio is repaired by adding
  names. A majority of each pool must be attested; at most 20% may be invented; the invented budget
  is spent only on initials with too few attested names (the set-distinctness rule needs a flatter
  alphabet than Japanese has), never on letters already rich in real names.
- Next step agreed: pull the attested stock from public sources into raw local lists
  (`.claude/skills/name/raw/`) with source metadata only - no per-name research until a name is
  actually promoted into the pool.
- Outcome (2026-08-25): male +401 (target 400), female +89 against a target of 200 (96 selected, 7 lost to cross-gender
  prefix clashes such as Kiyo/Kiyomori and Asa/Asahito). The female shortfall is a data fact, not a choice:
  the clean attested stock under the pool-wide prefix rule is ~66 names plus O- forms and court
  names; the rest of the target would have had to be invented, past the 20% budget. Raising it
  needs either more attested sources (temple registers, more shumon-aratame-cho transcriptions)
  or a GM decision to relax the prefix clause for women (Kiyo + Kiyoko coexisting), declined
  earlier the same day.
- 2026-08-26 follow-up (GM): the old female pool audited and 77 entries retired; court-style
  auspicious-kanji + 子 names ruled attested-idiom (11 added); a `samurai` flag added for register
  names attested on warrior-house women; the pool-wide prefix clause kept (relaxing it was measured
  at +5 court names and declined). The male pool's own junk/modern entries remain a separate decision.
- 2026-08-26 (GM): THREE PROVENANCE CLASSES. `historical` (a real bearer in a source, >= 60% of
  each pool), `idiom` (built on an attested mechanism - nanori elements, prefix + suffix commoner
  names, auspicious kanji + 子 - with no record of the specific name; <= 20%; the notes must carry
  the caveat), `invented` (no attested mechanism; <= 20%). Motivating case: filling the female
  initials W/Z/B/E/G/J. The literal "male name + -ko" idea was measured and rejected (the unused
  male names on those letters are birth-order/office names - Gorobeiko is not a name); the
  element + 子 form of the same idea yielded 10 (Zenko, Zuiko, Jiko, Joko, Jueiko, Giko, Benko,
  Wakako, Wakiko, Eijuko), restricted to qualities Rokugan treats as gender-neutral (compassion,
  purity, righteousness, eloquence, longevity, youth) rather than martial virtues.
- 2026-08-26 (GM): ratios changed to 60% historical FLOOR / 30% invented cap / 10% idiom cap
  (from 60/20/20). The GM's premise was that idiom was under-used; it was in fact at 18%, so the
  stem + 乃/江/代 forms (22 entries) were reclassified from idiom to invented - their mechanism is
  only weakly attested pre-modern, which is what the GM's own definitions turn on - bringing idiom
  to 7% and invented to 26%. The cap split is not the binding limit: with 128 historical female
  names the 60% floor allows at most 213 entries, i.e. 19 more constructed names, and every
  remaining attested candidate on the rare initials is one edit from a pool or roster name.
  Relaxing the loose rule across genders and relaxing the -ko prefix clause were both offered and
  DECLINED by the GM (players confuse similar names regardless of the portrait). Female D stays
  at zero by design; the >= 10-per-initial target is otherwise met for men and approached for women.
- 2026-08-26 research pass for new attested female sources (GM request): two collectors, 858
  lines, 155 names new to the corpus (details in `raw/CLAUDE.md`, `raw/female-sources-2.md`,
  `raw/female-sources-3.md`). Finding: the rare initials are NOT short of attested names - Rin,
  Ruri, Etsu, Ben, Bon, Waka, Ume, Eiko, Ginchiyo, Wakasa, Gyokuran all exist in the record - they
  are short of names that pass the loose similarity rule, and in four cases the blocker was one of
  our own invented names (Wakaba, Gyoku, Eika, Rurie). Two swaps made (Wakasa for Wakaba, Gyokuran
  for Gyoku); Eiko and Ruri stay out on other blockers (Eizo, Furi). Z does not occur as a
  pre-modern female initial in any source found.
