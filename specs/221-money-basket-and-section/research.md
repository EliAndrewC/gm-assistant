# Feature 221 research: the standard basket and its 2026 prices

## The method the basket follows

A purchasing-power reading is a ratio: the basket's cost in today's dollars over its cost in historical silver, scaled to
a koku of rice. Two papers fix the method:

- **Bassino & Ma (2005)**, "Japanese unskilled wages in international perspective, 1741-1913", *Research in Economic
  History* 23 ([published copy](https://www.econhistdbm.com/uploads/8/1/9/7/81977286/reh_japanese_wages_bassino_and_ma.pdf)).
  Table 1 gives Japan basket A (per adult per year): rice 114 kg, soybeans 52 kg (a proxy for miso, shoyu, tofu and
  natto), barley and wheat 10 kg, buckwheat and others 16 kg (buckwheat, foxtail, proso and barnyard millet, sorghum),
  beans other than soybeans 4 L, fish 3.5 kg, edible oil 1 L, linen 5 m, lamp oil 2.6 L. Table 3 prices it in grams of
  pure silver for Kyoto 1745-54 (326 g) and Tokyo 1882-86 (277 g), with the unskilled day wage (2.82 g and 4.5 g). The
  authors say the basket *"did not take into account such important items as housing rents, burning fuel, alcohol"*
  and note that Allen's basket *"also includes fuel and soap"*.
- **Allen, Bassino, Ma, Moll-Murata and van Zanden (2011)**, "Wages, prices, and living standards in China, 1738-1925",
  *Economic History Review* 64 S1 ([open copy](https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/32403/1/Ma_Wages-prices-living-standards-China-1738-1925_2011.pdf)).
  The "bare bones" baskets (tables 3-4) add soap 1.3 kg, cotton 3 m, candles 1.3 kg, lamp oil 1.3 kg and **fuel 3
  million BTU** a year, and *"increase them now by 5 per cent, which is a minimal allowance for rent"*. Table 5 prices
  fuel at 11.2 g of silver per million BTU in Beijing and 5.59 in London around 1750.

Neither has **salt**, and none of the welfare-ratio baskets do.

## The standard basket: what is in, what is out, and why

| good | per adult-year | in because | source of the quantity |
|---|---|---|---|
| rice, soybeans, barley and wheat, buckwheat and others, beans, fish, edible oil, cloth, lamp oil | as basket A | basket A is the published Japanese basket at the "normal" income level, priced for Kyoto 1745-54 - the very silver prices that anchor Rokugan's coins | Bassino & Ma, Table 1 |
| **salt** | 7.3 kg (20 g a day) | needed to live, and to make the miso, soy sauce and pickles of a Japanese diet; the GM named it | **guess**: modern Japanese intake is ~10 g a day ([NHNS 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9899568/)), northern Japan ate ~30 g in 1960 ([NIPPON DATA80](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3920383/)), and the Jiaqing emperor said in 1800 a person spent 1-2 cash a day on salt at ~16 cash a jin (diagram 0273), i.e. 37-75 g with household preserving; 20 g sits low in that range because the soybeans already stand in for miso and shoyu |
| **fuel** | 3 million BTU | cooking and heating; Allen's baskets carry it and Bassino & Ma name its absence | Allen et al. 2011, Table 3 |
| **housing** | 3.31 m² of floor for a year (a third of a 9-shaku-by-2-ken back-tenement room) | the GM approved it; a home is a need | the room size is attested (diagram 0265: a unit 9 shaku by 2 ken, 9.93 m²); the third follows Allen's three adult-equivalents per household |

Out, with the reason:

- **Soap**: not a Tokugawa household good (washing used rice bran and lye); Bassino & Ma leave it out.
- **Candles**: lamp oil was the light; basket A's 2.6 L already carries it.
- **Alcohol, pepper and other seasonings, tea, tobacco**: comforts, not needs; Allen's "bare bones" leaves alcohol to the
  "respectable" basket. The site's custom good lets anyone add one.
- **Allen's 5% rent markup**: replaced by housing as a good. A uniform markup on both sides of a ratio cancels, so it
  would have changed nothing.

**Quantities are per adult**: the reading is a ratio, so the scale cancels; the per-adult figure only matters for the
share each good takes.

## 2026-10 prices (US retail, the cheapest plain version in a household bulk pack)

the only or the cheapest pack found. Prices are from retailer listings found by web search on 2026-10-24; each link is
the listing (or listing page) the price was read from.

the running meter.

## What it comes to

| basket | 2026 dollars | silver g | dollars per koku (151.5 kg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| standard (with housing) | ~$1,784 | ~408 | **~$986** |
| standard without housing | ~$893 | ~363 | **~$556** |
| Bassino & Ma A at 2017 prices | $797 | 326 | $552 (reproduces the sheet) |

Housing is about half the 2026 dollars and a ninth of the silver: in Edo a room was cheap beside rice, and in America it
is dear. That is the largest single lever in the basket, and the reason the no-housing variant ships.

## BLS cross-check

The BLS publishes monthly US city average retail prices for long-grain rice (series APU0000701312, per lb) and dried
beans (APU0000714233, per lb) through its public API (`api.bls.gov/publicAPI/v2/timeseries/data/`). They are averages
of supermarket packs, so they run above the bulk packs here; the cross-check flags only a gap of more than half. A
secondhand relay puts the rice series at $1.11/lb ($2.45/kg) in August 2026.
