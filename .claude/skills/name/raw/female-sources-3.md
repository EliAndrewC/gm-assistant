# Female attested-name sources, pass 3 (2026-08-26)

Companion to `female-attested-3.jsonl`. Search was run in English and Japanese; every source below is new relative to passes 1-2 (issendai, maybeitisdaijirou, Kiyose shishi, nihonjin-name, ja-wiki consort/empress lists, sengokudaimyo, behindthename). ## Rare-initial tally (distinct names)

W 4 (Waka, Wakasa, Wakusa, Wiro) - Z 0 - B 3 (Baka, Ben, Bon) - E 6 (Ei, Eiko, En, Eri, Etsu, Eyo) - G 4 (Gin, Ginchiyo, Go, Gyokuran) - J 2 (Jako, Justa) - R 13 (Reijo, Reo, Rie, Rin, Rishi, Riso, Risu, Riyo, Roku, Rui, Ruri, Ryo, Ryu) - U 5 (Uji, Ume, Uno, Uta, Utako) - D 3 (Dai, Dashi, Den). Z remains empty: no pre-modern female name beginning in za/ji/zu/ze/zo turned up anywhere, which matches the two-mora hiragana pattern (voiced initials are rare; Gin is the standing exception).

## Rejected or empty

- heraldry.sca.org/names/edo-era.html ("The Early Edo Period (1600 to 1700)") - fetched via the Wayback Machine (the live site is behind a Cloudflare challenge); it is Issendai's own "Women's Names in Edo-Era Japan" article republished on the SCA site, so it duplicates an already-used source and was NOT extracted.
- Solveig Throndardottir, *Name Construction in Mediaeval Japan* - only for sale; no female name list of hers is online.
- Cornell & Hayami 1986 (Journal of Family History), Kurosu, Drixler, the Keio IES discussion paper DP2019-012 (Xavier database), the Eurasia Project pages - abstracts and PDF fetched; none prints individual women's names.
- Hayami, *Population, Family and Society in Pre-Modern Japan* - no preview with register transcriptions found.
- Diana Wright on Mantokuji (Monumenta Nipponica 1997) - paywalled; the Kishimoto page above already gives the full 112-name list from the Japanese monograph.
- Tokeiji (Kamakura divorce temple) - no name list online.
- Suganoura monjo Muromachi commoner names surfaced only through the excluded nihonjin-name site; not re-collected.
- corontomomousagi.com "Edo women's name list" - 404.
- Wikipedia "List of female castellans in Japan" - all names already in the categories.
- Kishimoto's separate "characters common in koseki removals" page and the ka-ju/NDL bugencho guides - about surnames or male names.
- Chiebukuro answer on named Sengoku women - all names already captured elsewhere.
- Wikipedia "Category:18th-century Japanese women" - 6 pages, all captured (mostly court).
- Kabuki/joruri cast lists and EdoPop courtesan-name list - not pursued (professional names, low priority per brief).
