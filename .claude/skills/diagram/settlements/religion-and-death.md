# Religion and the dead: shrines, torii, monasteries, cemeteries

*Part of the Mode B settlement docs. The index, the `meta()` knobs, the workflow and the validator contract live in [`../settlements.md`](../settlements.md).*

**Load this file when:** the map carries a shrine, torii, monastery, temple precinct, graveyard, mausoleum, cremation ground, or ossuary.

**Research:** the historical basis for these rules - what was found, the decision it drove, and every disclosed departure - is in [`../research/religion-and-death.md`](../research/religion-and-death.md). Load it when you are CHANGING a rule or questioning one, not to follow it.

---

- **A village/hamlet shrine has a VILLAGE-scale footprint** (`village_shrine_footprint_within_norms`, GM 2026-07-21, caught on Hikari no Sato): the ordinary earth-god/water-mouth hall is ~275 m^2 (30x24 px at 2 ft/px - Ueda, Hoshigaoka), a showcase primary (Kikuta's 7-torii Benten, Hikari's gateway Benten) runs to ~490 m^2 (44x30 px), and the check's ceiling is 600 m^2 - the monastery/temple tier (1,000+ m^2) belongs to towns and cities per the temple-density canon. Hikari's two halls had survived from before these norms at 192x128 / 236x164 ft (small-monastery footprints), which is also why its correctly-sized graveyard read tiny beside the Bishamon hall. `religious_matches_scale` gates the TYPE per tier; this gates the SIZE. Note `s.shrine_well(cx, cy)` takes the HALL CENTER (the ring search finds the nearest clear spot beside it) - Hikari's legacy offset-coordinate calls only passed `remote_shrine_has_own_well` while the halls were oversized.
- **The religious building scales with the settlement** (gated by `religious_matches_scale`): a **hamlet** has NONE; a **village** has a Shinto **shrine** ("Shrine to <Fortune>" / "village shrine"); a **town** has **monasteries** (each "Monastery of <Fortune>", with a torii in front); a **city** would have full **temples**. Never label a village site a "temple" - temples belong at town scale and up (see the `/temple` skill). If the GM says "temple" for a village, silently correct it to "shrine."
- **A town has TWO monasteries by default**, one for each of the **2 patron fortunes of the clan** whose holdings include it. Declare the clan with `meta(clan="Lion")`; the gate then expects exactly those two dedications (`town_monastery_count`, `town_monasteries_dedicated`). Patron pairs: **Crab** Bishamon+Ebisu, **Crane** Benten+Daikoku, **Dragon** Hotei+Ebisu, **Lion** Bishamon+Daikoku, **Phoenix** Fukurokujin+Hotei, **Scorpion** Benten+Jurojin, **Unicorn** Fukurokujin+Jurojin. **When generating a town, if the GM has not named the clan, ASK** - the dedications can't be chosen without it. Override the default with `meta(monastery_fortunes=[...])` (an explicit list, any length) for special cases: a town that **changed hands** (Hirameki is Lion-held but keeps a small older Benten monastery from Crane rule, so `monastery_fortunes=["Bishamon","Benten"]`), or a quiet town with just **one** monastery (Hoshizora, `monastery_fortunes=["Bishamon"]`). `town_declares_monasteries` requires every town to set `clan` or `monastery_fortunes` - this is also how the data persists across regenerations (it lives in the `.gen.py` source, re-run each pass). The same rule extends to **city temples** at provincial-city / capital scale (see the city-temples bullet below). Mark the principal monastery `primary=True`.
- **City temples: a deliberate L7R liberty on size, monks INSIDE the precinct, and a LAY temple neighborhood (GM, 2026-07-20; the *why* is mandatory, so it is recorded here in full).**
  - **Count - two major complexes, plus a handful of small shrines.** A city defaults to **two major temple complexes**, one per patron fortune of the holding clan (the town-monastery rule ported up; declare via `meta(clan=...)` or `meta(monastery_fortunes=[...])`), dotted with a smattering of small wayside shrines (`s.small_shrine`, gated by `city_temple_neighborhood_has_shrines` >= 3 near the clustered temples). **More than two major temples is the marked exception, with three recognized justifications:** an **especially large** city; an **especially pious** city that is a pilgrimage destination; or a city that **changed hands** between clans and kept the old ruler's temple (the town-tier Hirameki case ported up). Nagahara is the plain two-temple case (Bishamon + Ebisu, the Crab pair). **Tango is the changed-hands case:** it passed between Lion and Crane, so it keeps 3 - the union of the Lion pair Bishamon+Daikoku and the Crane pair Benten+Daikoku (Daikoku shared). Its Lion-legacy **Temple of Bishamon is a converted samurai estate**, which is why it stands among the SE samurai rather than in the SW temple neighborhood - and being a **lone** temple among houses (not a >=2 cluster) it needs no wayside shrines of its own. (The union rule is tidy: a changed-hands city gets 3 temples if the two clans' patron pairs overlap by one fortune, 4 if they share none.) **A FOURTH justification, and the one that reframes the other three: a clan whose TEMPLES ARE ITS MERCHANT HOUSES** (`temple_exception="fox_structure"`; Minami, the Fox seat). In Fox lands the question is "which *temples* do X", not which merchant families - the Seven Temples hold usufruct over sections of the Kitsune Mori, moneylending sits with Fukurokujin and Ebisu, wedding loans with Benten (`l7r.md` "Fox Temples", "The Fox Clan's economy") - so its city carries **eight modest precincts** (the seven Fortunes of Good Luck plus a slightly larger Inari), each SMALLER than the single great complex an ordinary clan builds, and each **sited where its TRADE is** rather than gathered into one temple quarter. **Read the reframing before you "correct" such a map back toward the default: the many-modest-temples form is INSIDE the attested historical band, and our two-complex default is the liberty.** (why: [`../research/religion-and-death.md#many-modest-temples-per-walled-city-is-the-historical-norm`](../research/religion-and-death.md#many-modest-temples-per-walled-city-is-the-historical-norm) - Japanese castle towns concentrated their temples in a *teramachi* running to ~70 at Kanazawa and ~80 in Kyoto, with **Takayama, a SMALL castle town, at "over 10"** - the size-matched anchor; on the China side a Ming county seat carried the Confucian school-temple and the City God temple as separate mandatory foundations before any Buddhist or Daoist house was counted.) Two consequences that are easy to get wrong:
    - **The clergy HOUSING inverts.** Only the Three Bonds (High Monk, Temple Master, Chief of Discipline) take a vow of celibacy in a Fox temple; every other position is hereditary, so most of each precinct's clergy are householders living OUT among the laity. A Fox precinct is therefore a small walled WORKPLACE holding three residents, **ringed by the ordinary houses of the families who run its trades** - ~5-9 `monk_house` per precinct against the standard 2-3, and 48 citywide at Minami against Nagahara's 5. This is the Zhengyi householder-priest pattern, corroborated by Shinto shake and Jodo Shinshu (research finding 3), not a game contrivance. The budget follows it: `CityProgram.monk_houses_per_precinct` scales the adept-monk line off the precinct count instead of the retired constant 5.
    - **The GRAVEYARD ceiling does NOT scale with precinct count.** `city_graveyard_count` stays 2-4 at city scale even at eight precincts, and that is deliberate: these are economic institutions holding forest usufruct, **not eight parishes**, so they have no separate congregations of dead, and burial ground is constrained by suitable LAND (off the fields, downstream, set back from water) rather than by how many foundations a city has. Several precincts sharing a few common grounds is the ordinary arrangement. *(Recorded because the research REJECTED the obvious change - see research finding 4.)*
  - **Size - the deliberate liberty.** (why: [`../research/religion-and-death.md#city-temple-size-the-deliberate-l7r-liberty`](../research/religion-and-death.md#city-temple-size-the-deliberate-l7r-liberty))
  - **Initiates - mostly living out.** Each complex carries **~2x its monk count in initiates** ("many are called, few are chosen"), but only a small fraction reside inside (~5-10 per complex at provincial-city scale, ~10-20 at capital scale). The rest are laborers, merchants, and servants engaged in religious study while working ordinary jobs (the "altar boy" tier); on the map they are **ordinary caste-pack houses**, never separate buildings.
  - **Where the clergy live - a BLANK-COURT compound; celibate monks inside, married adepts OUT in the neighborhood (GM 2026-07-24, superseding the earlier draw-no-monk-houses rule).** A city temple draws exactly like the governor's yamen: a walled compound with a deliberately blank court - the interior is the subject of its own Mode A diagram when the PCs visit, so on the city map the big rectangle IS the temple. Celibate monks, resident initiates, and a few lay servants (~25-45 per provincial-city complex) live in dormitories and cloisters **implied within that compound**, **implied population on top of the dwellings-x5 count** - do not house them in the packs. But adepts may marry and raise children, so a share of each complex's 15-30 monks are married adepts whose families keep **2-3 ordinary homes in the temple's neighborhood**: kind `"monk_house"`, drawn DELIBERATELY identical to a laborer house (no label, no glyph of its own - the manifest kind exists so the checks, the budget, and the population math can see them, not the eye). They count as real dwellings (population = dwellings x 5) but ride OUTSIDE the lay caste table's 600 families - clergy are not a lay caste, so the caste bands ignore the kind, and a small budget line (5 x C_PACKED) covers their ground. Gated by `city_temples_have_monk_housing` (>= 2 within ~170px of each major temple) and `city_monk_houses_by_their_temple` (none stranded away from every temple). Beyond those, at most **0-2 exception features** stand apart: a retired abbot's hermitage, or a small daughter-chapel with 2-4 monks of its own - drawn precisely because they are exceptions.
  - **The temple NEIGHBORHOOD is LAY households, and the temple sells its own sacred goods directly.** The crucial correction (GM, 2026-07-20): amulets, charms, talismans, incense, and lamp-oil were **temple-direct sales - a major revenue stream** (Chinese temple accounts book "annual oil-lamp and incense income"; Japanese omamori/ofuda are *bestowed* by the temple for an *offering*, never wholesaled). So there is **NO third-party "religious-goods shop"** competing at the temple's own gate, and none should be drawn. The independent commerce that actually clustered at the gate is:
    - **Food and drink** - teahouses, eateries, sweet-sellers. This is the main draw (worshippers and pilgrims need feeding and resting; the temple does not do that itself) and the bulk of the neighborhood's commerce.
    - **Souvenirs and regional-specialty goods** (*meibutsu*) - local crafts, sweets, keepsakes: **secular** goods branded by the place, distinct from the sacred objects the temple keeps to itself.
    - **Funeral trades**, clustered by the **graveyard** (not the hall) - coffin-makers, grave-marker stonecutters, and (the China-first detail) joss-paper / spirit-money and paper-effigy makers, serving the parish death economy the temple presides over but does not manufacture.
    - **Service trades** - fortune-tellers and diviners at the gate, and the **retained temple-artisan families** (carpenters, gilders, painters) who work *for* the temple on its endless repair cycle and live in the district as workshops-cum-dwellings, not storefronts.
    **At this map scale none of these has a distinct glyph** - a teahouse and an eatery are both the generic `shop` glyph, so **the story is in placement (a shop beside the temple reads as a teahouse), never in the icon**; do not try to draw an "amulet shop" or "religious-goods store". Everything sacred is inside the compound.
  - **Neighborhood SIZE - anchor on ~40-80 lay houses (~1-2 per resident monk).** For a provincial city's two temples (~30-60 monks total), size the lay service band at **~40-80 houses around the temples**, which works out to roughly **1-2 lay service households per resident monk** for a city whose worship traffic is *regional* (not a pilgrimage flood). This is ordinary zoning within the existing caste bands, **not extra population**. A **pilgrimage destination inverts the ratio** to 10:1+ lay-to-monk - the *monzen-machi* / gate-town pattern - which is what the "especially pious city" exception looks like on the ground. (Tango already reads correctly for the opposite reason: its temples sit *in* the merchant district, so the food/drink/souvenir commerce is already all around them. Nagahara does not - its commerce all went to the river/wharf, leaving the temple quarter as bare laborer/servant housing.)
  - **Small shrines can sit at SPECIFIC meaningful locations, not only clustered by the temples.** The `city_temple_neighborhood_has_shrines` floor (>= 3 near the >=2-temple cluster) is a minimum; **additional wayside shrines placed at meaningful spots elsewhere are good flavor** - a **wharf shrine** (fittingly to **Ebisu**, fortune of fishermen and honest commerce) at the docks, a bridge shrine, a gate shrine, a well shrine. Historically wayside shrines stood exactly at such thresholds (a landing, a crossing, a gate), so a shrine placed where the city's life turns reads truer than one more shrine crowded by the halls.
  - **Festival rhythm (China-first, not a drawn feature).** On fair days the great temple's forecourt hosts the periodic market (the *miaohui* / Daxiangguo pattern), the temple renting stall space - a good gameable beat (the temple district *becomes* the market on festival days), though nothing on the static map shows it.
- **(RETIRED 2026-07-24) Do not re-add a rule sizing a torii avenue to its approach space** (`monastery_torii_scale_with_space`, removed). Torii COUNT is patronage, set by the per-temple seeded roll on the tier's `TORII_WEIGHTS` column (`shrine_hall`, pinnable `torii_count=`, gated by `torii_match_roll` + `torii_count_canonical`); the old space-driven rule predated that table and wrongly banned a count of 3, which the town column rolls at 60%. The gen authors the avenue GEOMETRY to fit its ground - see the torii numerology bullet below.
- The main shrine's placement is village-specific: it may stand **on flat ground** reached by a torii approach (Hikari's Benten sits at the southern entry path with a 2-torii gateway; Kikuta's Benten sits in a back-slope sacred precinct reached by a **7-torii approach avenue** - the flat-ground equivalent of a hill ascent) OR sit on a **hill** reached by a torii ascent (if a map uses one). A flat-ground avenue is a straight/gently-curving *sando*: the torii march in a line up to the hall. If on a hill the hall sits ON the summit, never overhanging the edge, with the torii ascent winding across the open face. Torii **counts follow the numerology canon** (`torii_full_avenue_is_seven`, GM 2026-07-21): an approach is either a **modest entrance of 1-2 arches** or a **full avenue of EXACTLY 7** - 7 is the numerologically significant count, and 3-6 reads as an unfinished avenue (Hirameki's Bishamon shipped at 4 and was completed to 7, the motivating example; its pre-fix manifest is the negative fixture). **Monasteries and temples take the full seven whenever there is room** (room is a design-time judgment: Hoshizora's monastery keeps a 1-arch entrance because the theater ground and the Imperial road own its approach; Tango's Daikoku keeps 2 in the dense city fabric). **Village shrines usually keep 1-2** (Hikari 2, Ueda 1); Kikuta's formal 7-torii Benten avenue is the sanctioned showcase, and Hoshigaoka's shrine carries a full 7 because the country monk posted there insists on the potent number (a per-map flavor choice, not the default). Whatever the count, the arches stay clear of the shrine hall and spread out. **The sando packs the arches DENSELY** (~15px / ~30ft pitch at village scale, roughly one arch-width apart): the true-scale torii glyph is small (~8px/16ft, GM 2026-07-21), so a widely-spaced avenue reads as isolated gates rather than a corridor - `torii_spread_out` floors the pitch at one arch-span (16ft) so a *senbon*-style avenue is legal but arches never overlap into a blob. **The pitch is now CAPPED at the other end too** (`torii_avenue_pitch_capped`, GM 2026-07-25 after the spacing research below): arches stand **~20 ft apart center-to-center and never more than two rail-spans (32 ft)**, past which a sando reads as isolated gates strung across the map rather than one approach. The gen authors the avenue's **LINE**; the engine owns its **STRIDE** - `shrine_hall` re-lays anything over the cap at the ~20 ft standard, resampled by arc length along the authored line, so a curved sando keeps its curve and its innermost arch keeps the seat the gen chose (`settlement._avenue_pitch`); the same 20 ft is the stride it extends a single-point avenue by. Inside the band the gen's own spacing stands untouched, which is why the **village avenues stay at ~30 ft** - GM preference, and the cap rather than the target is the rule. The town/city avenues that motivated the cap ran 45-114 ft (Hirameki's Bishamon 61 ft and Benten 45 ft, Nagahara's Ebisu 51 ft, Tango's Bishamon **114 ft = 7.1 rail-spans**, the widest in the pool); all four are now at 20 ft, and the last two are frozen as negative fixtures. **The INNERMOST arch of a sando sits at the hall's THRESHOLD** (`shrine_avenue_fronts_the_hall`, GM 2026-07-22, village shrines only): the nearest arch stands directly in front of the modest hall, not set out with a gap (Kikuta d=34 from the hall center; Hoshigaoka ~10px below its S edge). It exempts the modest 1-2 arch entrance (not a processional avenue) and the **gateway-BESIDE-the-hall** pattern - the hall aside the entrance track while the arches straddle it (Hikari's Benten, well OFF the avenue axis, so "in front" does not apply) - and it is village-scoped, since a large temple/monastery sando may legitimately keep a courtyard between its outer arch and the main hall. **Torii and religious footprints also keep clear of the wall's works and the patrol ring** (`torii_clear_of_halls_towers_ring`, `religious_clear_of_ring_and_towers`; GM placement rules 2026-07-21, caught on Tango where a wayside shrine seated against the SW wall tower): an arch or a hall overlapping a guard tower, a gate structure, or the ring-road corridor reads as impossible construction. An ordinary street running THROUGH a torii stays legitimate - a monzen sando arch spans its road - so only the ring road is treated as a barred corridor. **And an arch NEVER stands in a wall of any kind** (`torii_clear_of_walls`, GM 2026-07-25, caught on Nagahara where the seventh arch of the Ebisu sando stood in the samurai ward fence): a torii is a FREESTANDING gateway - two posts in open ground, carrying no load and closing nothing - while a wall is a continuous barrier whose whole purpose is that you cannot walk through it, so an arch drawn on a wall run is impossible construction twice over (the posts stand inside the palisade, and the "gateway" opens onto a barrier). Where a way genuinely pierces a wall the opening is a GATE STRUCTURE - the city gate, a ward kido - never an arch. Every wall counts: the city rampart, a neighborhood (ward) fence and its wall-caps, and the perimeter of any walled compound (manor, governor's mansion, merchant estate, mausoleum); the geometry is `settlement.wall_runs`, which the placement side reads too. Because either feature may be drawn first (CLAUDE.md "DRAW ORDER"), the rule is enforced from BOTH ends: `shrine_hall` shortens its avenue - scaling the whole run back at a uniform stride, first arch fixed - so a rolled 7 stops SHORT of a wall rather than standing in it or straddling it, while `_torii` and each wall-drawing method (`ward`, `manor`, `merchant_estate`, `mausoleum`) refuse the conflict outright when they cannot fix it. Known limit: a hand-authored avenue whose arches happen to BRACKET a wall without any one touching it is caught at gen time only when the wall is drawn first, since the manifest-level check tests arches, not the walk between them. A recent addition belongs at the village edge/gateway where there is room, not displacing an established structure. Each village district has one tax-free religious figure whose dwelling may double as the shrine.
- The shrine HALL stands BESIDE a lane/road, never ON it (`shrine_halls_clear_of_lanes`, GM 2026-07): a building sits off the way. The **TORII is the deliberate exception** - a gateway arch straddles the approach and the road runs UNDER it (a real, common feature), so torii are exempt from the check. Hikari's Benten shows the pattern: the hall sits just WEST of the entrance track while its two torii straddle the track as the village's southern gateway (the road runs up through the arches). The check uses a **segment-to-rectangle** distance, because a thin lane can thread THROUGH a wide hall between its corners, so corner-sampling (as `houses_clear_of_lanes` uses for small houses) would miss it.
- **Torii - research + the numerology rule** (`torii_count_canonical`, GM 2026-07-21). (why: [`../research/religion-and-death.md#torii-are-votive-donations-the-count-records-patronage`](../research/religion-and-death.md#torii-are-votive-donations-the-count-records-patronage)) *The Rokugan rule (a deliberate liberty):* the number 7 is even MORE numerologically potent in Rokugan than in the real world, so every proper religious site - shrine, monastery, temple - carries **exactly 1, 3, or 7 torii**, never another number, unless the hall is explicitly marked `shrine_hall(torii_outlier=True)` (always with a recorded story). Rolled per tier by `settlement.roll_torii_count`: **village 60/30/10**, **town 30/60/10**, **provincial city 30/40/30**, **capital 10/60/30** for 1/3/7 (capital recorded ahead of need - no capital maps exist yet). *The FULL RE-ROLL (GM 2026-07-23):* the GM noticed the city maps carried suspiciously few arches - the table had landed but the town/city gens' hand-placed `torii=[...]` counts predated it and were never re-rolled (Tango + Nagahara sat at four 1s and one 3, a ~1%-likely draw under the city column). Now the roll is wired into `shrine_hall` itself: `torii=[...]` is the avenue GEOMETRY (points marching AWAY from the hall - drawn first-n if the roll is smaller, extended along the avenue's own stride, or at 44px from a single point, if larger), the COUNT is a per-temple seeded roll on the tier column (deterministic in map seed + hall position), pinnable per hall with `torii_count=N` - the per-hall analog of the village path's map-level `torii_count` knob (that path, `roll_village`'s civic shrine, still rolls for itself and records `meta.torii_count`). The resolved target is recorded on the religious rec and gated by `torii_match_roll` (drawn, ATTRIBUTED count == target), so a stale hand count or a misattributed arch can never ship silently. Pinned exemplars, with their stories: Kikuta's dense 7-arch senbon sando and Hikari's ichi/ni/san 3-avenue are GM-approved canon, so they pin rather than re-roll. Post-re-roll counts: Tango 1/1/3 (the dice agreed with the old map), Nagahara Bishamon 1 / Ebisu 7, Hirameki Bishamon 3 / Benten 7, Hoshizora 3, Ueda 3. The re-roll retired the two space-driven completeness checks (`city_temple_torii_fill_approach`, `monastery_torii_scale_with_space`) - avenue completeness is defined by the roll, and the count is PATRONAGE, not street room. `kind="small_shrine"` is the hokora tier: exempt (its glyph carries a miniature token torii) and excluded from torii ATTRIBUTION so a wayside shed can never absorb a temple's gates. This rule superseded and retired `torii_full_avenue_is_seven` (which sanctioned {1,2,7}) the same day it was written.

  - **DONATION ROWS ARE A DESIGNATED-SITE SPECIAL CASE** (GM 2026-07-25). The Fushimi-style *senbon* row - dozens to thousands of arches, given one at a time and standing almost shoulder to shoulder - is NOT how an ordinary Rokugani sando works. It belongs to a handful of named great sites: **Shinden Togashi**, the **Temple of Amaterasu**, the **Ki Rin Shrine**, and others of that rank. Everywhere else the 1/3/7 set above is the whole vocabulary. A map of one of those sites is therefore an explicit `shrine_hall(torii_outlier=True)` (which also exempts it from `torii_avenue_pitch_capped`), and drawing its row honestly would want the one-arch-span FLOOR relaxed as well, since real donation rows are packed tighter than a 16 ft glyph allows at our scales.
- **A WELL stands BESIDE a shrine, never ON its hall or UNDER its torii arch** (`wells_clear_of_shrine_and_torii`). A well close to a shrine is REQUIRED (above), but its disc must not overlap the hall footprint or the torii arch's box (`x +/-19, y -10..+18`) - a wellhead painted on the hall or in the gateway reads wrong. The fix is ORDER: place `s.shrine_hall(..., torii=[...])` **BEFORE** `s.place_wells(...)` / `s.shrine_well(...)`, so the hall + torii block-outs already exist and the wells (both the scattered village wells and the shrine's own) are laid clear of them. (This caught Ueda, where `place_wells` ran before the shrine and scattered a wellhead under the torii.) Watch the knock-on: `place_wells`'s fallback pass seats an underserved house's well at `~0.6*near` px, so keep `near` modest (~150, not 210) or that fallback can land in open ground and trip `wells_among_dwellings`.
- **A shrine AND its TORII arch NESTLE in a CLEARING within the sacred grove - never UNDER the trees** (`shrine_clear_of_grove_trees`, `torii_clear_of_grove_trees`). The village fengshui/water-mouth grove often surrounds the shrine (the grove IS the shrine's sacred wood), but the hall and its arch must sit in a cleared opening, not painted over tree canopy (which reads as a building lost in the wood). Mechanism: `village_grove` skips any tree clump within an occupied feature's keep-out (houses, yards, gardens, byres, kura, wells at the ordinary `0.35*clump` reach; **religious halls, shrines, and torii at a LARGER `0.90*clump`** so a full clearing opens around the sacred features) - **provided the shrine + torii are placed BEFORE the grove**. So call `s.shrine_hall(..., torii=[...])` FIRST, then the `village_grove(...)` calls. **Canopy, not nominal clump:** the recorded clump `r` is the nominal footprint, but the DRAWN crowns overhang it, so the visible canopy reaches ~1.7x `r`; both checks use that CANOPY radius (a circle-rect test against the hall footprint and against the torii arch's `x +/-19, y -10..+18` box) so they match what the eye sees. (This caught Ueda's shrine + torii buried under the water-mouth grove: the old occ-skip used `0.35*clump` and the checks used the nominal `r`, so canopy that overhung by ~8px slipped through both. A well record carries a radius, not w/h, so it keeps its own occ branch.)
- **Every settlement above a hamlet buries its DEAD - a full funerary geography.** Rokugan always **cremates**, then inters the bones, so a graveyard is a compact field of stone markers, not a body-burial sprawl. Four kinds of site, each with its own setting logic:
  - **Temple graveyards** (`s.cemetery(cx, cy, w, h, label=...)` - rows of *sotoba* markers + a couple of taller memorial stupas). The state merged Shinsei (the Buddhist strand) and Fortune/Shugendo worship, so **ANY temple may host a graveyard** - a temple opts out with `s.shrine_hall(..., graveyard=False)` for a new or special-purpose hall (Tango's Temple of Bishamon, founded in a former samurai estate, keeps none). A graveyard is a parish ground, so it **sits in a temple precinct** (`cemetery_in_temple_precinct`, within ~230px of a `monastery`/`temple`; at city scale only an *intramural* graveyard must - an extramural common ground is exempt). **A village SHRINE hosts its graveyard too - the churchyard rule** (`village_graveyard_by_shrine`). Real-world Shinto shuns death-*kegare*, but **L7R's country shrines are officially Shinseist** (the Buddhist-analog order) even though they house Fortune deities, and their resident **Brother of Shinsei performs the funerary rites** - so the shrine *is* the death-handling institution (the *danka*/*bodaiji* logic), and its graveyard is its churchyard, sitting IN the precinct (within ~250px), NOT held away. So the village graveyard goes BY the shrine - EXCEPT a **hilltop** shrine (`M["hill"]`), which is exempt (graves do not climb the sacred hill; a prominent hill-shrine is not the humble earth-god monk's funerary base). Only the sacred **hall + torii themselves** stay clear of burials (`cemetery_clear_of_shrine` now means "graves fill the yard AROUND them, never ON them" - it fires on a grave/mausoleum overlapping the hall or arch, not on mere proximity). This unifies burial across scales: graves by the monk-run building everywhere (shrine at village, monastery/temple at town/city). **Size the burial grounds to the population they serve** (the core "roughly to scale" principle): a centuries-old settlement's MAIN common ground should read as a substantial, monastery-scale field, not a token patch - and a **town has no ossuary** (city-only) to soak up the poor, so its common ground carries proportionally MORE and should be drawn larger (Hirameki's ~1,200-soul town has a 120x88 extramural common ground that dwarfs its 88x62 intramural parish plot). The intramural parish plot beside a temple is naturally smaller (one parish's families); the extramural common ground is the big one.
  - **Shape of the common ground: ORGANIC, Japan-style - a ruled rectangle is the deliberate exception** (`s.cemetery` `organic` derives from `parish`; GM 2026-07-23, researched + source-verified). The GM caught the city maps drawing the common burial ground as a perfect rectangle and asked whether that was historically accurate. (why: [`../research/religion-and-death.md#burial-ground-shape-japan-organic-china-surveyed`](../research/religion-and-death.md#burial-ground-shape-japan-organic-china-surveyed)) *The decision (GM):* Rokugan follows the **Japanese mode** for its common grounds - like the cremate-then-inter rite, a deliberate exception to China-first - so every NON-parish common burial ground draws as an irregular terrain-hugging earthen plot (`parish=False` -> `organic` defaults True; the recorded bbox and no-build block stay the w x h rectangle, so placement/clearance checks are untouched). A specific city MAY override with `organic=False` to draw a *louzeyuan*-style plotted ground - historically defensible and a real variety axis, but it should be a documented per-map choice, not the default. The cramped *danka* parish plots inside temple precincts stay ruled rectangles (`parish=True` default) - a managed plot inside a walled precinct legitimately squares off.
  - **Clan mausoleum** (`s.mausoleum(cx, cy, w, h, label=..., gate_dir=..., label_below=...)` - a walled crypt precinct with a stone hall + memorial stupas). The ruling clan's ancestral crypts, **inside the walls by the samurai/government quarter** (`city_has_mausoleum` - a prestige monument near the seat of power; ancestor veneration is central to samurai identity). City only. When a wall of such a walled compound **abuts a neighborhood (ward) fence**, the compound *yields* that wall to the fence: `s.mausoleum`/`s.manor` re-stamp the ward fence over the coincident wall so the **fence renders ON TOP and IS that side of the precinct** (the compound's own wall runs underneath) - no doubled, clashing parallel walls. The yielded sides are recorded in `ward_walls`, and `walled_structure_yields_to_ward_wall` gates it: any axis-aligned mausoleum/manor wall inside the city that runs along a ward fence must be recorded as yielded (in Tango the samurai-ward fence IS the mausoleum's north wall).
  - **Cremation ground** (`s.cremation_ground(cx, cy)` - a scorched clearing with a stone pyre platform, a wisp of smoke, and the officiants' shelter). Smoke, fire, and pollution put it **OUTSIDE the walls** (`city_has_cremation_ground`; `town_has_cremation_ground` wants one at a town's edge, clear of the dwellings). It **adjoins an EXTERNAL (outside-the-walls) burial ground** (`cremation_ground_by_external_cemetery`, edge gap <= ~70px): the body is burned and its cremated bones interred next door, so the crematory and the extramural common ground form one funerary complex (an in-wall graveyard does NOT satisfy this - the crematory's neighbor must itself be extramural). And it **stays set back from the main road** (`cremation_ground_set_back_from_main_road`, >= ~130px from `M["road"]`): the pyre is marginal, polluting land reached by a minor funeral path, not high-street frontage. Beyond that floor, it must not sit **between its temple and the road** (`cremation_ground_not_between_temple_and_road`): if a monastery/temple is nearby (within ~400px), the crematory must be at least as far from the road as that temple is (less a ~40px tolerance for "beside, a little away"), so worshippers and funeral-goers don't walk past the pyre to reach the hall - put it BEHIND or beside the temple, never on its road-side approach. (Note the danka split: a temple's own *graveyard* sits beside the temple wherever it is - like a Christian churchyard, and may sit road-side by the temple - but the *cremation ground* is the marginal exception kept apart from the road and off the temple's ceremonial approach; minor town streets don't trigger either rule, only the trunk road.) Monk-run with burakumin assistants - a religious order stands outside the caste system, so handling the dead does not pollute its caste.
  - **Pauper ossuary** (`s.ossuary(cx, cy)` - a low earthen mound with one weathered stupa). The communal bones of the poor and the *muenbotoke* (unconnected dead), **outside the walls by the cremation ground** (`city_has_ossuary`).
- **The scale schedule** (centuries-old settlements): a **CITY** shows **2-4 graveyards** (`city_graveyard_count`), **every non-exempt temple has one in its precinct** (`city_temples_have_graveyards`), plus the mausoleum (inside) and the cremation ground + ossuary (outside) - an extramural funerary complex beyond a gate. A **TOWN** has 1-2 temple graveyards + a cremation ground at the edge. A **VILLAGE** has one burial ground clear of its shrine. A **HAMLET is exempt** (its dead go to the village district's ground, like it has no headman or shrine).
- **Any WALLED settlement (town or city) keeps a graveyard inside AND outside the walls** (`walled_graveyards_inside_and_outside`), **and the exterior common ground is noticeably larger than the cramped intramural one** (`walled_exterior_cemetery_larger`, outside area >= 1.3x the largest inside - there is room beyond the walls, while the intramural temple plots are hemmed in). So a walled town has at least two graveyards (one of each); an unwalled town's graveyards are all parish grounds (no exterior-common-ground exemption). Intramural temple plots are deliberately **small** (Tango's are 46x34 / 56x40, cramped beside their halls), the extramural common ground **large** (104x74).
- **Every solid map feature is a first-class structure for overlap purposes, and a NEW feature must not overlap anything unless the overlap is explicitly declared OK.** This is the governing principle (each new feature historically shipped overlapping streams, channels, fields, walls...), and it is *enforced*, not just documented: `check_village.py` carries a classification registry (`_OVERLAP_STRUCTS` / `_OVERLAP_TARGETS` / `_OVERLAP_LINEAR` / `_OVERLAP_EXEMPT`) and the **`every_feature_classified_for_overlap`** guard, which FAILS when a generator emits a footprint feature whose manifest key is in none of those sets - so a new feature can never silently skip the overlap rules. The DEFAULT is must-not-overlap: a solid feature (houses, civic/urban buildings, the **funerary structures**, wayside shrines, ministries) joins the `structs` set and is automatically caught by `no_structure_overlaps` and every `no_structure_on_*` clearance (building, **wall**, **moat**, **road/street/alley**, **stream**, **channel**, the **pond**, religious hall, gate furniture, torii, and the **ring road** via `ring_road_kept_clear`); every solid feature is normalized to a w/h rect for the test. Overlaps are **opt-in**: a feature that is *meant* to overlap is named in `_OVERLAP_EXEMPT` with its reason - a **label** (text overlay), a **bridge** over water/road, a **guard tower** ON the wall, an **inspection post** at the gate, a **storehouse** annex abutting its shop, a **threshing yard** annex abutting its farmhouse, a **well-head** dropped into a courtyard gap. (`shrines` duplicates the primary religious halls, so it rides with `religious` as a target.) Place solid features AFTER the dwellings (they block placement) in clear pockets; a placement scan must mirror **all** the clearances (wall stroke, moat, ring road, streets, water, fields, torii, gate furniture, and existing structures' *rotated* footprints), and keep extramural funerary sites **outside the wall but inside the cropped view** (Tango clusters its common burial ground + cremation ground + ossuary just west of the wall).
- **Burial grounds sit a SET-BACK from open water that SCALES with the waterway's size** (`funerary_set_back_from_water`, helper `water_setback(width)`) - a burial ground by big water floods out, so the bigger the watercourse the further back the dead must lie - but even narrow open water floods graves, so there is a solid **floor (~75px)**: a stream gets the full ~75px, a **moat** (the heaviest watercourse, ~26px wide) ~130px, a river or canal most (capped ~140px). It applies to the **moat, streams, ponds, and rice-paddy field edges** - a flooded paddy is real standing water (rice grows submerged), a genuine flood hazard, so a burial ground keeps a solid **~50px** margin from any field edge (NOT a token creek distance - graves right against a paddy flood out); thin irrigation channels are not open water and don't trigger it. Two refinements: the **CREMATION ground may sit nearer the water** (a fixed modest ~30px, and exempt from the paddy set-back entirely - fire and ritual want water access, and a fire site is not flood-sensitive like graves), so the graveyard naturally lands *beyond* the cremation ground (Tango's common burial ground sits well back, on the far side of the cremation ground from the moat); and an **INSIDE-wall ground is exempt from the moat term** (the moat is outside the wall, the rampart shields it - streams/ponds still apply on either side). The **walled mausoleum is exempt** entirely (a crypt compound, not a flood-sensitive open plot).
- **A graveyard can be marked NON-parish** (`s.cemetery(..., parish=False)`) - a village-style burial ground not attached to a temple, exempt from the temple-precinct rule. Used for a one-off like **Tango's in-wall agricultural-district burial ground** (a graveyard serving the farm quarter, away from the temples - a Tango-specific extra, not a general rule). bunkotsu (divided remains - a portion at a great head-temple, the rest in the home village) is real but **not drawn** - it isn't visible in this view.

The walled compounds (manors/estates, the governor's yamen, merchant estates, the mausoleum) draw walls + gate + a **deliberately blank court** - the interior is the subject of its own Mode A diagram when the PCs visit - so the wall and gate ARE the feature and must be honest. *Anchors (researched 2026-07-19, full memo below):* a samurai residence gate (nagayamon/yakuimon) opens ~9-12 real ft, a grand yamen gatehouse ~18-24 ft (the old fixed +-34px gap drew a 204 ft opening at city scale - most of a wall missing); walls (dobei/tsuijibei) ~1.5-2 ft thick, drawn true-width-or-floored at 2px; gate posts ~2 ft squares floored ~3px.   - **District catchment - the village ground buries the WHOLE ~800-person district, and hamlets bury nowhere visible (GM asked 2026-07-23; researched + source-verified).** The presence rules always said it (`settlement_has_cemetery`: "a hamlet's dead go to the village district's burial ground, just as it has no shrine or headman") but the sizing was anchored to the central village's own ~350 (the 2026-07-19 resize comments read "a ~350-person village ground") - an internal contradiction the GM caught. *What the research found:* Edo Japan actually buried PER-SETTLEMENT - hamlet burial patches (*shuraku bochi*, today's grandfathered *minashi bochi*) at the fringe of even 12-15-household hamlets were ubiquitous - while the RITUAL center was central (*danka* temple registration, funerals, and stone markers at the main village's temple; the *ryobosei* two-grave regions formalized the split: the corpse in the local *ume-baka*, the venerated stone at the center). Ming/Qing China is the deliberate contrast: scattered fengshui-sited family graves through the farmland (Buck's 1929-33 farm survey: ~2% of ALL farm area under graves), communal grounds only as pauper charity (*yizhong*) - so the discrete communal ground we draw is the Japanese surface convention, correctly per the China-geography/Japan-surface split. TWO things make the one-central-ground convention MORE defensible in Rokugan than in historical Japan: (1) the cremate-then-inter canon - an urn is carried to the district ground in a way a corpse-procession is not; (2) a hamlet's own burial patch is TINY (~750-2,450 sq ft full-body, less for urns - a ~15-20px blob at 2 ft/px), legitimately below map resolution, so hamlets drawing nothing stays honest even where local patches exist in-fiction. *The decision:* the drawn village ground is the marked, ritually active DISTRICT ground, sized to the district: ~800 inhabitants x ~25-30 deaths/1,000/yr x ~30-yr plot reuse = ~600-720 active urn plots x 10-20 sq ft = **~0.15-0.30 acre** (cross-checked against the excavated Umeda-haka commoner ground, ~15-17 sq ft per body including paths). The four village grounds were resized 2026-07-23 from ~0.14 ac (the ~350 anchor) to ~0.24-0.26 ac, and `BURIAL_AC_BAND["village"]` re-anchored from [0.04, 0.30] to **[0.12, 0.38]** (the district band + glyph-rounding slack both ways), so a village-only-sized ground now FAILS `burial_grounds_sized_to_population`. Monotone with population served survives: the district's ~800 still sits below a town's ~1,200, and 0.15-0.30 nests under the town's 0.25-0.75.
- **Swept ground around sacred + funerary features** (drives the `self.clearings` keep-out that `commons` scrub + `marsh` reed scatter skip, added by `_clear_ground` from `shrine_hall` (hall + each torii) and `cemetery`). **Gated by `scatter_respects_swept_clearings`** (since 2026-07-20): every clearing and every cover draw records an ordinal (`seq`) in the manifest, and the check fires when a commons/marsh poly overlapping a clearing drew BEFORE the clearing existed - unless a pre-cover `reserve_clearing` guard already protected that ground (the documented duplicate-registration pattern is recognized).
  - **The verge outline is ORGANIC, never the padded rectangle (GM 2026-07-23: "such clearage rarely conforms to precise geometric shapes").** The old collar was the feature footprint grown by `extra` as an axis-aligned rectangle, and the scatter skip made that rectangle visible: a crisp bare box in the scrub around every shrine and graveyard. *The why:* swept ground is PRODUCED by tending - brooms, feet, the sando's traffic - radiating outward from the feature, and its edge sits wherever the tending peters out into the scrub that season; nobody surveys it. Straight ruled edges belong to things that ARE surveyed or built - walls, paddy bunds, house plots (the same reasoning as the perimeter-dike and organic-burial-ground rules: geometric precision is earned by construction, not by maintenance). *The mechanics:* `_clear_ground` carves irregular INWARD-ONLY bays into the padded rect (16 edge samples, bay depth up to ~55% of the collar, damped at corners, tangential jitter) under a footprint-seeded, state-restored RNG - identical args always yield the identical blob (render-sync determinism) and the map's own RNG stream never shifts, so ONLY collar shapes changed pool-wide. *Why bays-only, no outward lobes:* a collar is a maintenance CLAIM - making it irregular means the sweeping falls short of the surveyed ideal, it never annexes ground - and an outward lobe can newly overlap a cover that legitimately predates the clearing, flipping `scatter_respects_swept_clearings` on a previously-clean map (the first lobed draft did exactly that on Ueda: the graveyard collar's lobe reached the toe marsh, cover seq 3, which draws before the stage-3b reserve). Bays-only means every blob is a subset of the old rectangle, so the change is pool-safe by construction. *Same-center dedupe:* a duplicate registration within 4px of an existing clearing's center (the documented reserve_clearing-then-feature pattern - the torii's internal collar even sits 2px off its reserve) REUSES the first blob verbatim, so guard and late collar can never disagree about the swept outline. The `scatter_respects_swept_clearings` sampling was tightened to match: exposure now tests the exact clearing/guard POLYS, not their bboxes (a bbox guard would credit a blob with ground its bays never swept; a bbox clearing would claim exposure on ground outside the blob). Tests: `test_settlement.py::test_clearings_keep_scrub_off_sacred_and_funerary_ground` (blob shape + containment-in-rect + feature containment), `::test_clear_ground_is_deterministic_and_preserves_the_rng_stream`, `::test_clear_ground_dedupes_same_center_registrations`.
  - *What the research found (China-first, Japan corroborating; GM asked, 2026-07-19).* The ground immediately around these was kept CLEAR of wild scrub, while the surrounding waste stayed scrubby - a tended verge, not a big bald radius. **Shrine precinct**: the keidai / forecourt was a swept, raked-gravel or flagged surface (purity), and in the Chinese case a village earth-god shrine or ancestral hall fronted a hard swept plaza (grain-drying, market, opera). **Torii + sando**: the approach path and the ground under the arch were kept clear and maintained the length of the sando, never overgrown. **Graves**: PARTIAL - graves sat on marginal scrubby back-slope/waste ground, but grave-tending (Chinese Qingming tomb-sweeping, Japanese haka-mairi) clears weeds off the mound plus a modest collar; the waste even a short way out stays scrubby. So: clear AT the graves, wild BETWEEN them.

- **Funerary geography: pollution, fire, and the two faiths** (drives `cemetery_clear_of_shrine`, `funerary_set_back_from_water`, `cemetery_in_temple_precinct` / `city_temples_have_graveyards`, `cremation_ground_set_back_from_main_road` / `cremation_ground_not_between_temple_and_road` / `city_has_cremation_ground`, `city_has_ossuary`, `walled_graveyards_inside_and_outside` / `walled_exterior_cemetery_larger`). Death-pollution (*kegare*) is a Shinto concern: no grave sits hard against a **shrine**, and burial grounds keep well back from **water** (a flooding/leaching grave fouls the supply). Buddhism manages the dead, so a graveyard is a temple **parish (*danka*) ground** sitting in a temple/monastery precinct. The **crematory (*kasoba*)** sits OUTSIDE the walls (smoke, fire risk, pollution), set back from the main road, and never between a temple and the road (no one should walk past the pyre to reach worship); monks officiate with burakumin assistants (a religious order stands outside the caste system, so handling the dead does not pollute it). A pauper **ossuary** holds the *muenbotoke* (unconnected dead). A centuries-old city splits graveyards inside + outside the walls, the main extramural ground the largest.
