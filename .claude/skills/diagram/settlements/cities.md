# The provincial-city tier (`meta(scale="city")`)

*Part of the Mode B settlement docs. The index, the `meta()` knobs, the workflow and the validator contract live in [`../settlements.md`](../settlements.md).*

**Load this file when:** the subject is a provincial city. The largest tier file - wall/moat/tower program, government quarter, quarter density, budget-first wall sizing, the river-city variant. Read alongside `urban-features.md`.

---

- **A city's CASTE MIX must match budgets.md, not just the total** (`city_caste_counts_in_band`). The total-population check alone lets the mix silently DRIFT: the classic failure is one quarter's pack (the laborers) absorbing everyone else's slots while **servants get starved to near-zero because they were appended to the END of a pack list** (`["laborer"] * 445 + ["servant"] * 50` places 445 laborers and then has no room left for the servants). A provincial city is roughly **~40% laborer / 20% servant / 25% merchant / 10% samurai / 5% burakumin** of its ~600 households, and each caste is held within **+/-30%** of that target. Two rules for hitting it: (1) **INTERLEAVE servants** into the laborer and merchant packs (`(["laborer"] * 3 + ["servant"]) * N`), since they live among the households they serve - never tack them on the end. (2) The samurai and merchant **walled ESTATES are households too** - the check counts `M["manors"]` (extramural samurai estates, who commute in) toward samurai and `M["merchant_estates"]` toward merchants, so the in-wall *houses* need only make up the remainder (which is why `city_samurai_housing_sufficient` wants ~39 in-wall samurai houses, not the full ~60). Space-bound quarters (the SE samurai quarter is crowded by the government compounds) lean on their estates to reach band.
- **Walled merchant compounds: 1-3 per provincial city, ROLLED 30/40/30, because the gate is a GRANTED privilege (GM 2026-07-23).** A gated compound is not something wealth can buy - it is a right the daimyo explicitly grants to a merchant family, mirroring the Edo-period system of individually granted merchant privileges (an audience with the daimyo at the New Year's celebration, permission to carry a persistent surname across generations despite not being samurai, and the like). So of a provincial city's ~12 very-rich merchant families (budgets.md, 2% of ~3,000), most can AFFORD the wall but lack the legal standing to build it - only 1-3 hold the grant. `s.merchant_estates(seats)` rolls the count per city (`MERCHANT_ESTATE_WEIGHTS`, seeded on the map seed like the torii re-roll; `count=` pins it), places the first n of a hand-vetted seat list (provide >= 3 so any roll lands), records the target in `meta['merchant_estate_roll']`, and `merchant_estates_match_roll` gates drawn == rolled - the pre-roll state (both cities hand-coding exactly 1, a copied pattern with no recorded reasoning) can never silently return. Seat-vetting lessons from wiring Tango (its gen comments carry the details): scan for court-sized pockets against the PRE-estate fixed features only (streets/wall/moat/water, early fixed wells with the |dx|>=39-or-|dy|>=31 wellhead clearance, frontage shops, civic + label ground - pack housing places after the estates and flows around; late well passes yield to the court blocks), keep seats off samurai/burakumin pack ground (caste), and pin-vet every seat with `count=3` against the full gate even when the current seed rolls fewer - a reseed may seat any of them. The engine registers each court in `self.placed` (SAT-distance) as well as `block_polys` (center-test), so pack houses keep real clearance from the compound wall. Towns hand-place (no roll column yet - Hoshizora/Hirameki carry none today); the capital column gets added when capital maps exist.
- **Provincial city (`meta(scale="city")`):** **~2,000-4,000 inside the walls, average ~3,000 / 600 households** (per `budgets.md`): ~120 servant, ~240 laborer, ~150 merchant, ~30 burakumin, ~60 samurai households - and, structurally, **ZERO in-city farmers** (the farmers who work the city's land live in the surrounding villages/hamlets, counted in other totals). The samurai run ~10% (the governor R8, six provincial ministers R7 + their R6 deputies, R3-5 clerks, bushi) plus a small **foreign** Imperial yoriki sub-station auditing gate tariffs. A city map **places these households densely - near the full ~600** (Tango runs ~600 buildings) - on a crossing street grid, with a smaller building grain (`s.bscale` ~0.5) so the big merchant/samurai footprints fit. The gate checks the required **structures + neighborhood presence + the street network**, not an exact per-caste headcount, but the neighborhoods are sized to the real numbers (~240-310 laborer, ~120-150 merchant, ~50-60 samurai, ~30-60 burakumin). Domain capitals (~12,000) have their own numbers and are a **separate future mode**.

### Provincial cities (`meta(scale="city")`)

A provincial city is the next tier up from a county town - a provincial governor's seat, ~3,000 inside the walls. Like towns, some are walled and some unwalled; the worked example **Tango** (`pool/provincial-cities/tango.gen.py`) is walled. The drawing vocabulary adds a few city-only methods; the gate adds a city block of checks. (Since the budget-first wall sizing of feature 009, dwelling counts are a near-census: the budget targets the full ~600 households and the gate holds the map to its floor - see Scale and density above.)

- **The wall is a CLOSED ring with multiple gates.** Draw it with `s.city_wall(pts, gates=[...])` - a full loop (not the town's open hill-anchored arc), with a gap at each gate where the wall runs ~horizontal. Records `M["wall"]` (the ring), `M["gates"]` (all), and `M["gate"]` (the first, for checks shared with towns). The town-walled checks (`wall_sections_irregular`, `streets_have_buildings`, `wall_hugs_the_town`, the gate market, the monastery torii avenue) are **town-only** and do NOT apply to a city - a city's wall is a regular defensive ring with deliberate open ground inside (drill grounds, refuge space), not a cost-minimized skirt. `walled_city_has_wall_and_gates` requires a closed wall + **>= 2 gates**.
- **PLANNED / NOT YET BUILT - a tunable SALLY / POSTERN gate knob** (GM idea 2026-07-22; captured here per the "deferred note" convention). A secondary defensive gate distinct from the main road/river gates, present or absent as a **defensive-posture** choice (a city expecting war "shores up its defenses" by constructing/reconstituting one; a long-peaceful city may have none or a bricked-up one). **Historical framing to resolve first (needs its own research pass):** a dedicated European-style "sally port" riddling the curtain wall is NOT the China-first form - the Chinese walled seat concentrated defense in a *few grand gates*, each with a **barbican (瓮城 *wengcheng*)** that WAS the sortie-point, plus the wall and moat; it did not pepper the curtain with small posterns. The apt China-first / Japan-second equivalents are a **secondary / rear gate** (Japanese 搦手門 *karamete-mon*, the lesser gate opposite the main 大手 approach), a small **postern** (埋門 *umon*), or a **water gate** (水門 - which Nagahara's river gate already is). So the grounded knob is likely a *rear/postern gate*, not multiple sally ports - confirm the form + count with research before building. **Design sketch:** `meta(postern_gates=N)` or explicit positions, or a `meta(war_footing=...)`-style posture flag that adds one; it draws a SMALL, plain gate in a wall stretch clear of the main gates and mural towers, opening onto **empty field** (no road through it, so no *guan-xiang*). **It is traffic-free, so it is EXEMPT from the gate-market, flophouse, and caravan-cluster rules** - store its gates SEPARATELY from `M["gates"]` (which holds only main road/river gates) so `city_has_gate_market` / `city_flophouse_outside_each_gate` / `city_gate_caravan_facilities` skip them automatically (this is exactly why those checks were scoped to "every MAIN-ROAD gate," 2026-07-22). Furniture is minimal (a plain gate, maybe a small guard post; NO inspection station - nothing to inspect). Its own checks would gate: on the wall, clear of main gates/towers, opening onto field not a road, and the posture-knob's presence logic. When built, add a `specs/NNN-*/` feature via spec-kit (feature work, per CLAUDE.md).
- **A MOAT rings the wall, fed from off-map.** `s.moat(wall_ring, gap=...)` draws the wall ring pushed outward - water all the way around. `city_moat_surrounds_wall` requires every wall point inside the moat ring; `city_moat_fed_offmap` requires a **stream from a map edge** reaching the moat (the off-map source). **That feeder must be as WIDE as the moat** (`city_moat_feeder_matches_width`): a trickle cannot keep a full moat supplied, so a stream reaching the moat must match its width within ~25% - pass `s.stream(..., width=<moat width>)` (the moat's `width`, default 26, is recorded in `M["moat_width"]`; `s.stream` records each stream's `w`). Cities with moats **often** irrigate their outside farmland by tapping the moat (optional, not gated). A stream may legitimately terminate at the moat (the `stream_runs_off_edge` check now allows a moat terminus, as it already allowed a pond). (Waterways - streams, the moat, the pond feeder - render as solid water with a lighter mid-current sheen, **no dashed centerline**: a dash denotes a road's lane, which makes no sense on water.)
- **The Imperial road runs straight N-S through the city, gate to gate** (`city_imperial_road_through` - the road must run off both the top and bottom edges). It quarters the interior together with the implied E-W axis. The **`"Imperial Road"` label must sit OUTSIDE a gate** (`city_road_label_outside_walls`): inside the walls the same roadway is a *city street* the city maintains, not an Imperial responsibility, so pass `s.road(..., label_xy=(x, y))` a point beyond a gate (the default label anchor is the polyline midpoint, which for a city is the center - inside the walls).
- **A city ON the Imperial road LINES that road with commerce** (`city_imperial_road_has_commerce`). The through-road is the city's prime frontage: caravans and travelers pass along it, so it carries a **commercial ribbon** of shops + traveler services (inns), not bare housing-backs or empty ground. This is a **general rule for ANY city with an Imperial road, walled OR not** (a city *without* a road skips it, its commerce staying in the market district). The check measures the road's portion running *through* the city - bounded by the **wall** if there is one, else by the **urban footprint** (the bounding box of the city's buildings) - and wants commercial buildings (`shop`/`merchant`/`inn`) fronting it at roughly **>= 1 per 130px** of that length - a floor that catches a bare spine. Build it as a true **two-sided market street**: `s.frontage(<road sub-segment>, ["shop", ...], skip=<the road poly>, both=False, rows=2)` for the west side and a second call (a north-running sub-segment) for the east, plus a `s.pack(...)` to deepen a road-market block and a `s.inn(...)` or two for travelers. Capture the road polyline in a variable so you can pass it as `skip=` (a frontage row sits *against* the corridor it fronts). The dead frontage to watch for is the stretch between two quarters that both turn their backs to the road (Tango's was the band between the burakumin quarter and the merchant avenue) - fill it. Place the ribbon LAST in the gen (after every quarter's pack) so it flows into the gaps and perturbs no RNG-seeded placement. **General guidance on commercial intensity:** a provincial city's merchant/artisan caste is ~25% of households (~150 of 600, the highest commercial share of any tier), so a healthy commercial face is roughly **1 storefront per ~25-30 residents** (Tango ~3,000 -> ~105 `shop`+`merchant`+`inn` frontages); a road-city sits at the denser end of that band, with the increment concentrated as the road ribbon. (Domain capitals, all of which have an Imperial road, always carry this ribbon.)
- **Each gate has a GUARD HOUSE + attached INSPECTION STATION + GUARD TOWER**, drawn automatically by `s.city_wall(...)` (top layer, so the road passes under). Caravans entering submit cargo to inspection and pay the Ministry of Revenue's import tariff (the "Yasuki Taka system"), audited by the Imperial yoriki sub-station. Gated by `city_inspection_station_at_each_gate` (an inspection station within ~160px of each gate) and `city_gate_has_guardhouse` (>= 2 gate structures - the guard house + tower - within ~180px). Like the towers, the guard house + inspection station sit **SQUARE TO THE WALL / ring road** they line - rotated to follow it, not axis-aligned (`city_gate_furniture_aligned`). Each piece is WALKED along the curving wall from the gate (`_wall_walk`) so it picks up the wall's **LOCAL** tangent *where it actually sits* - NOT the gate vertex's tangent, which has already curved away by then (Tango's gates are on ~8 deg wall stretches, not the flat top/bottom, so getting this wrong leaves the road slicing diagonally across a flat box) - then pulled in radially to the **ring road centerline** (inset matching `s.ring_road`), so the patrol road runs lengthwise *through* each building. `s.city_wall(...)` records each piece's `rot` and `kind` (`"guardhouse"` / `"inspection"` / `"tower"`) in `M["gate_structs"]`; the check compares the furniture's `rot` to the nearest wall edge's angle **mod 180** (these are rectangles whose long axis runs along the wall, so a 180 deg flip is the same but a 90 deg turn stands them across the road the wrong way), within **6 deg** - tighter than the towers' 15 deg, because the furniture is set from the exact local edge angle (not the towers' chord-through-neighbors approximation), so it matches near-exactly and a 15 deg window would wave through the shallow ~8 deg gate slope. The angle is folded to (-90, 90] so the two gates render alike whichever way the wall runs through them.
- **The wall is TOWERED for INTERLOCKING flanking fire - density set by the city's DEFENSE TIER** (`city_wall_towers_spaced` for the coarse "not just at the gates" floor, `city_wall_tower_coverage` for the real curtain-coverage invariant). A fortified city spaced its mural (horse-face / 马面) towers so their fields of fire OVERLAP - an attacker at the wall base is under arrow fire from **2 towers at once** (the 侧射 "flanking shot" principle; Shen Kuo's 11th-c. 矢石相及, "arrows and stones reach each other," describes adjacent bastions covering the curtain between them). Towers are a function of the **wall's defensive geometry, not the interior street grid**, and they also housed the stairs up to the parapet. The **required density is TUNABLE per city** via `meta(wall_defense=...)`, because a city's history sets how hard it was fortified (see "Historical grounding: wall-tower coverage" below):
  - **`"siege"`** - a border city that has had to **repel sieges**: `>= 2` towers cover every curtain point within **aimed-lethal bowshot (60 m / 197 ft)**, so towers sit a bowshot apart (~66px at 3 ft/px). The Pingyao standard. **Tango and Nagahara both use this** (border location + siege history).
  - **`"garrison"`** (the DEFAULT) - a garrisoned interior city: `>= 2` towers within **full war-bow reach (100 m / 328 ft)**, a looser ring.
  - **`"peaceful"`** - a city long at peace: only `>= 1` flanking tower within aimed-lethal range everywhere (curtain midpoints still get 2). The sparser Xi'an spacing (~120 m / 394 ft between towers).
  - `settlement.wall_tower_spacing_px(px_per_ft, tier)` returns the max spacing; `WALL_DEFENSE` holds the `(range_ft, min_coverage)` table. `s.city_wall(...)` reads the tier and **densifies automatically**: it anchors on the gate towers, fills each arc so no sub-gap exceeds the tier spacing, then runs a coverage-remediation sweep that drops an extra mural into any run still short of the tier's min-coverage (a slide off a kido can leave a neighbor gap just over range). All recorded in `M["wall_towers"]`.
  - `city_wall_tower_coverage` samples every curtain point (~every 18px) and requires the tier's min-count of towers within the tier's arrow range **+12px** (a mamian's half-footprint - an archer shoots from the tower's parapet span, not its center point). The gate **barbican** is exempt (gate + guard house + inspection form a defended complex, not open curtain): points within 130px of a gate or 55px of its guard furniture are skipped, as are points within 45px of a **water gate** (a fortified shuimen opening flanked by its own two towers). These check keep-outs deliberately MIRROR the placement keep-outs - a point the placement code structurally will not tower (inside a barbican, on a water-gate opening) must not be demanded by the check, or the map can never pass. The older `city_wall_towers_spaced` (>= 6 towers, no bare arc wider than ~75 deg) remains as a coarse floor beneath the coverage invariant. Two additions from the doubled-tower incident (GM 2026-07-23, Tango's east curtain ran ...54, 76, 32, 54...): **(1)** points within `KIDO_TOWER_KEEPCLEAR` (62px) of a recorded ward-fence junction (`M['wall_tower_keepclears']`, the gens' `tower_skip` spots) are exempt too - the kido ward-gate is a manned chokepoint placement refuses to tower, and demanding 2-coverage of its band is exactly what forced the remediation pass to seat a SECOND tower 32px from a 55px-rhythm neighbor; **(2)** `wall_towers_evenly_spaced` gates the rhythm itself - mamian stand at regular flanking intervals, so no open-curtain mural gap may fall under **0.7x the map's median gap** (gate/water-gate flanking pairs exempt; calibration: the three defective pairs sat at 0.58-0.60x, every legitimate pool gap at >= 0.87x). Placement enforces the same rhythm from its side: mural min-separation floors at 0.75x the tier's spacing cap (was a flat 28px), strictly tighter than the check since the median never exceeds the cap - a rejected remediation seat slides to the local span midpoint instead of doubling up.
  - The towers also sit **SQUARE TO THE WALL** (`city_wall_towers_aligned`): each is rotated to the wall's tangent there - a tower on a 30 deg stretch slants 30 deg, not an axis-aligned box - recorded as `rot` in `M["wall_towers"]`. The check compares each tower's `rot` to the nearest wall edge's angle, mod 90 (a square reads the same every quarter-turn), within ~15 deg.
- **A RING ROAD hugs the inside of the wall** (`city_has_ring_road`) - the Chinese "follow-the-wall street" (順城街), the wall-clear patrol zone a fortified city keeps for moving troops along the rampart. Call **`RING = s.ring_road(WALL, inset=34)`** right after `s.city_wall(...)`, then **`s.bound = RING`** so the quarters pack INSIDE the ring (off the wall), leaving the clear zone. It is deliberately NOT a `town_street`: a fortification road is exempt from the must-be-built-up rule (its wall side is bare, and stretches run behind fields/compounds). Pulling the bound in off the wall costs ~one perimeter row of buildings (~8% area), so re-check the population/caste bands after adding it - usually a small density bump on one or two packs. **Streets AND ALLEYS MEET the through-lanes cleanly at a T-junction** (`city_streets_meet_through_lanes`): a street OR a gravel alley running toward the **Imperial road OR the ring road** must reach the lane's bed and END there - neither a sliver SHORT of it (an undershoot: the lane appears to dead-end in open ground; note `city_streets_connected` is satisfied by merely touching the lane's outer *edge*, so a lane can be "connected" yet visibly stop short of the bed) nor a sliver PAST it (an overshoot: a stub poking through to the far side instead of stopping at the junction). It covers alleys as well as paved streets (the laborer warren's east E-W alley once stopped ~15px shy of the east ring road - an alley heading straight at a through-lane must reach it too). A genuine 4-way crossroads, where the street truly continues well past, is fine. The fix is to extend an undershoot to the lane's centerline (mind clipping a nearby civic compound - extend a street ALONG ITS OWN axis to the lane, not diagonally to the nearest point) and trim an overshoot back to just inside it. This applies to ALL streets, the government quarter's included - the ring road runs inside the ward too (its SE stretch along the wall), so the gov avenue gives onto its own stretch of the patrol road. That works because **where the ring road crosses a ward fence it is GATED** (a kido at each crossing), sealing the ward's patrol stretch from the commoners' - so the ring road is part of `city_samurai_ward_sealed`'s netlines (find the ring x fence crossings and add a kido at each via `s.ward(..., gates=[...])`). **And the ring road stays CLEAR** (`ring_road_kept_clear`): the whole point of a patrol road is an unobstructed lap of the rampart, so NO building, civic compound (ministry, the governor's yamen, temple), or field footprint may sit on its bed. The wall furniture that SHOULD line it - the gate guard houses, inspection stations, and mural towers - is exempt (those records aren't in the building/civic/field lists), and a ward fence may cross it (gated, per above). The engine keeps the packs off automatically: `s.ring_road` lays a no-build corridor `width/2 + 17` wide along the loop, so even a large or rotated footprint's nearest corner stays well off the bed (Tango's closest dwelling clears it by ~14px). The check fires if any footprint's corner falls within the bed half-width (~4.5px) of the ring centerline or the ring crosses it - the failure mode it caught on Tango was 2 ministry offices and an in-wall farm field straddling the loop; move them inward off the ring (mind that nudging a ministry can land it on the gov avenue - clear both). If you ever shrink the corridor or hand-place a compound near the wall, this is the backstop.
- **The provincial government is in the interior** (every provincial city, walled or not): the **governor's mansion** (`s.governor_mansion(...)`, a grand walled compound; `city_has_governor_mansion`), the **six provincial ministry offices** (`s.ministry(x, y, name)`; `city_has_six_ministries` - Rites, Revenue, Retainers, War, Works, Justice), and a **samurai neighborhood** (`city_has_samurai_neighborhood`). **The Ministry of Rites sits in the temple neighborhood** (`city_has_ministry_of_rites`, exactly one) because it oversees temples and shrines; religious buildings at city scale are **temples** (`religious_matches_scale`, all of `kind="temple"`), and a city can have **several**. Where a **city street runs straight UP TO a temple** (a street that terminates at its front, within ~28px), a **torii arch must stand on that street just in front of the temple** (`city_temple_approach_has_torii`) - pass `s.shrine_hall(..., torii=[(tx, ty)])` a point on the approach (the `torii=` param both draws the arch and reserves a no-build block around it). Torii render in the TOP layer, so the street passes *beneath* the arch (`city_torii_over_streets`); each torii records its draw-z in `M["torii"]` as `[x, y, z]`. Temples set back from the grid (no street running up) need no torii - Tango has a Temple of Benten and a Temple of Daikoku in the SW temple neighborhood and a Temple of Bishamon among the SE samurai (the warrior fortune). **A temple's torii COUNT is its per-temple ROLL, not the remaining street room** (`city_temple_torii_fill_approach` RETIRED 2026-07-24, superseded by the seeded roll - a rolled 1 beside an open street is a hall with one patron gate, not an unfinished avenue; `torii_match_roll` gates drawn == rolled/pinned instead). The gen authors the avenue GEOMETRY to fit its ground: Nagahara's Ebisu rolled the full 7 and its authored line marches due south from the monzen lane; the engine lays it at the ~20 ft house pitch, so the run ends at y1236 - well short of the samurai ward fence at y1311. It shipped at a 19px (57 ft) stride until 2026-07-25, which put its seventh arch AT y1310, standing IN that fence (the gen comment had the fence 20px further south than it is): `torii_clear_of_walls` now gates that, and `torii_avenue_pitch_capped` gates the 51 ft stride the first fix replaced it with. Re-pitching also freed the ground the long sando had blocked, so the SE rowpack seated 5 more houses and the pocket's single idobata went over the 26-household ceiling - its `place_wells` spacing was tightened 56 -> 42 to give it a second well. (Knock-ons like that are normal when an avenue shortens: the arches' no-build blocks are what was holding the ground open.) The torii block reserves only the arch's own footprint (`bm=20`, kept *smaller* than a street corridor) so adding arches along a street doesn't shove the frontage houses off it. **A temple NEIGHBORHOOD (>= 2 temples clustered within ~400px) should be dotted with a smattering of small wayside SHRINES** (`s.small_shrine(x, y)` - a vermilion-roofed shed with a little torii, non-residential, recorded in `M["religious"]` as `kind="small_shrine"`: it is not a dwelling, needs no torii avenue, and is exempt from `religious_matches_scale`). `city_temple_neighborhood_has_shrines` wants >= 3 of them near the clustered temples; a LONE temple among houses (the Bishamon temple in the samurai quarter) is not a neighborhood and needs none. Place the shrines BEFORE the packs (they reserve a no-build block) and clear of the temples/wall/streets. The merchant district and laborer neighborhoods are checked for presence too (`city_has_merchant_district`, `city_has_laborer_neighborhoods`). **The samurai neighborhood must be SIZED to the cohort**: a provincial city is ~10% samurai (~300 of ~3,000, budgets.md) = ~60 households, most housed here as individual houses (the governor's compound + extramural estates hold the rest), so `city_samurai_housing_sufficient` wants >= ~65% of the expected household count (~`0.10 x pop / 5`) drawn as samurai houses - a token cluster of a couple dozen is far too few. Pack them with `face_streets="fill"` (NOT `"core"`: the samurai quarter is laced with government streets, and "core" skips the near-street band, leaving almost everything unbuilt). And unlike the poor, **samurai LINE their streets** - `city_samurai_partly_front_streets` wants at least a third of samurai houses within ~90px of a lane (deeper lots sit behind, but the quarter fronts its streets). **In-city samurai houses VARY IN SIZE by rank, and NONE are walled** (`city_samurai_housing_varied`): budgets.md's provincial-city rank table splits the resident cohort roughly ~25% senior (R5-7) / ~75% junior (R1-4), so the neighborhood mixes a **minority of large senior houses** (`kind="samurai_large"`, the bigger urban dwelling) among **many small junior houses** (`kind="samurai"`). Interleave them in the pack (e.g. `["samurai"] * 4 + ["samurai_large"]` repeated) so the large ones front streets prominently. The check wants >= 3 large houses, the small houses a strict majority, **and NO `s.manor(...)` inside the wall ring** - samurai walled ESTATES sit OUTSIDE the walls (see below); the only walled samurai compound within the city is the governor's mansion. **The inverse also holds: NO free-standing samurai HOUSE stands outside the wall** (`city_samurai_houses_inside_walls`, hard-zero) - the only extramural samurai residences are those walled country estates; an unwalled lone samurai house beyond the rampart is defenseless and has no anchor. The commoner inside-walls check exempts samurai *because of the estates*, which is exactly the gap this closes - and placement code must apply the stay-inside-the-wall test to samurai house glyphs like any other dwelling, exempting only the hand-placed `s.manor` estates (validated instance: Tango's SE gap-fill sweep - a rectangular region poking past the round wall arc, run after the ring bound was cleared - leaked 14 samurai houses into the moat berm, 2026-07-20; Nagahara's SW arc leaked 1 the same way). The **governor's mansion is legitimately LARGE** - the historical provincial *yamen* / governor's compound was a whole city block (main hall, residence, retinue quarters, jail, granary, gardens - dozens of buildings), drawn here walls-only with the interior implied; so its size is correct (`city_governor_mansion_large` enforces that it is the grandest compound - a city block at least as large as any estate and >= 3x any ministry), but like the ministries and temples it must sit INSIDE the rampart, clear of the wall stroke and moat (`city_government_clear_of_wall_moat`). **The five other ministries cluster AROUND the governor's yamen** (the government district, threading into the samurai quarter - as in a Chinese provincial seat / Japanese castle town), not strewn about (`city_ministries_cluster_at_government`, within ~480px of the governor); only Rites sits apart, with the temples it oversees. In a *planned* city the offices are not scattered: the yamen sat where the main streets crossed and the bureaus LINED the avenues around it (the Chinese 'official street'; the jokamachi grid where compounds align tightly along the streets), so **every ministry must FRONT a city street** (`city_ministries_front_a_street`, within ~85px of one) - lay a short government avenue (an L wrapping the yamen works well) and seat the ministries along it. A street fronted by ministries counts as served (the `streets_have_buildings` helper now credits civic buildings - ministries, the governor's yamen, temples, flophouses - as frontage, not just houses and shops). **A government office stands in its OWN ground** (`city_government_offices_dont_abut`): a ministry or the governor's yamen is a large, important compound and must keep a clear gap (~14px) from every other building/compound - ordinary city houses may touch each other, but these may not abut a neighbor (this caught the Ministry of Works touching the Ministry of Justice, and the Ministry of Rites touching the Temple of Daikoku). When two offices are too close, separate them - and mind the knock-on (the Rites/Daikoku fix needed the SE caravan inn nudged east to make room, since Rites is wedged against the ring road). The **internal streets** must likewise stay inside the wall (`city_streets_clear_of_wall_moat`) - the elliptical wall is a *polygon* whose chords cut *inside* the smooth ellipse, so size streets/compounds against the actual wall points (or just inset them), not the ideal ellipse.
- **A provincial city is the first tier that TRAINS INDOORS: one state martial hall plus a rolled count of private dojos** (`city_has_martial_hall`, `city_martial_hall_has_archery_range`, `city_dojo_count_follows_samurai`, `city_dojos_among_samurai`; GM 2026-07-25, reasoning in "Historical grounding: martial training in a provincial city" below). A county town draws a **practice ground and no dojo at all** (buildings.md) - a dojo is a city institution. Every provincial city therefore keeps **exactly one `s.martial_hall(x, y)`**, the state training institution: its own walled compound (130 x 100 ft) holding a 120-tatami hall, the sensei's house, and a 100 ft archery lane with an azuchi butt, drawn in **state violet** like the ministries. It is NOT a wing of the governor's yamen - it is a separate compound, though standing near the government it answers to is right. Private **`s.dojos([seat, seat])`** are then rolled from the samurai cohort: **1 per full 200 samurai plus a remainder-fraction chance of one extra, floored at 1**, so a ~3,000 city (~10% samurai = ~300) keeps 1 + a 50% roll and a ~4,000 city keeps exactly 2. Each is a 76 x 44 ft walled lot with a ~59-tatami hall and a training yard, drawn in ordinary building **tan** (private, not state) - and with **no archery lane**, which is the state hall's to keep. Provide **two vetted seats** so either roll can land; the roll is recorded at `meta['dojo_roll']` and gated, so a stale hand count cannot ship. Both kinds must stand **in or against the samurai neighborhood** - a dojo serves samurai and nobody else. Place them BEFORE the quarter's packs (like the fire tower) so the rows flow around them, and find the seat with `s.open_seat(...)` at that point in the gen rather than guessing; mind that the caption is wide relative to the compound (~70px of text against a 43x33px footprint), so in a ward packed to its budget the CAPTION is usually what binds, not the compound - `s.martial_hall(..., label_xy=(x, y))` hands it to a nearby pocket, or centers it low on the compound itself when the ward has no pocket at all (Nagahara).
- **A walled provincial city must keep >= 1 burakumin neighborhood INSIDE the walls** (`walled_city_has_burakumin_inside`): a city under siege cannot be without its burakumin (corpse-handling, leather, sandals, executions), so however many live outside, a neighborhood always sits within. Not all burakumin need be inside.
- **City streets form a crossing GRID of blocks** (`city_streets_have_buildings`, the same "no street to nowhere" rule as towns). Per quarter, draw a few crossing `s.street(...)` lines (2 N-S x 2 E-W = nine blocks is a good unit), then fill the whole quarter with **one dense `s.pack(bbox, [...], face_streets="fill")`**: the pack faces near-street buildings toward the streets (so every street is lined) and packs the block interiors with tight rows, while respecting the street corridors (so nothing sits on a street). **Density is what lines the grid** - the failure mode is a *sparse* pack leaving a street stretch empty, so pack to the real per-caste counts. Two rules of thumb learned building Tango: (1) don't draw two streets on the same line (a doubled corridor pushes buildings out of fronting range); (2) the big footprints (merchant 54x36, samurai 56x40) need `step >= ~0.8 x footprint` or they collide and place *fewer* - a larger step paradoxically packs more. (`s.frontage(street, [...], rows=N)` - front row plus tight rows behind - also works and is good for a single avenue, but multi-row frontages collide at grid intersections, so prefer dense `pack` for a true crossing grid.) **A larger city street must be LINED with buildings close to it** (`city_larger_streets_lined`): roadside land on a commercial/residential through-street is prime real estate, so no long stretch (>~140px) may run with the nearest building more than ~58px off it - stricter than `city_streets_have_buildings` (which tolerates a building up to ~105px away and so misses a street with a wide bare margin). Narrow gravel ALLEYS are exempt (the "small streets" threading block interiors need no frontage), and so is the GOVERNMENT avenue (its frontage is the spaced ministry compounds, governed by `city_ministries_front_a_street`, not shops/houses - the check exempts a street fronted by >= 2 ministries / the yamen). **Frontage `skip`-object GOTCHA** (this caused the very bug the check now guards): `s.frontage(street, ...)` sits tight against the street by *skipping that street's own corridor*, but it matches the corridor by **object identity** (`poly is skip`). If you pass `s.street(...)` one list literal and `front(...)` a *fresh* literal of the same coordinates, the skip will NOT match the registered corridor, the frontage gets blocked off the street by its own corridor, and the storefronts silently fail to place - leaving a bare avenue. **Capture the street in ONE variable and pass that same object to both** `s.street(AVENUE, ...)` and the frontage call. The grid must also be **one connected network wired to the Imperial road** (`city_streets_connected`) - run at least one street per quarter so its paved bed actually OVERLAPS the road's or another street's. Connectivity is **width-aware**: two lanes count as joined only when their centerline gap is less than the sum of their half-widths (the beds physically touch, so you can walk between them) - a street that stops even a roadbed short of the next reads as a *separate* network and fires (this caught the laborer grid ending 40px shy of the road). Extend the road-facing street of each quarter until it MEETS the road (to its centerline). Kido ward gates do not break connectivity - the street centerline runs on under the gate. Streets must also never come ALMOST together without meeting (`city_streets_no_near_miss`): a pair that closes to within ~2-30px but does not cross reads as a mistake (the classic case is a grid corner where a cross-street stops a sliver short of the through-street) - make the streets actually CROSS (extend one past the other) or pull them clearly apart, never leave a sliver gap. The mirror-image fault is a **stub** (`city_streets_no_intersection_stub`): a street that crosses another and then STOPS a sliver (~3-50px) past it. Fine to cross and run on (to the next block or the edge), or to terminate AT the junction (a clean L/T corner), but not to overshoot it by a little - so at a grid's outer corner, end the two outermost streets at their shared corner point rather than extending each past the other. **A related, more general rule covers ALLEYS too** (`city_lanes_meet_when_aligned`): if two lanes - streets OR alleys - are **heading straight at each other and stop just short**, with a CLEAR path between (no building, ward fence, or wall in the gap), they should simply **connect**. Unlike `city_streets_no_near_miss` (which only compares street-vs-street segment *proximity*), this fires on an *aligned endpoint* - a lane end whose outward direction points within ~37 deg at another lane within ~80px - so it catches collinear lanes that resume a sliver further on (the classic case: a quarter's vertical lane and a continuation lane in the adjacent strip, ending 10-56px apart on the same line). The fix is to extend one lane to meet the other (mind any check that scales to lane length, like `alleys_serve_buildings` - if extending a lane tips its served/length ratio, close the gap from the *other* lane's side instead). A gap that a building/fence/wall genuinely blocks is exempt (the lane is correctly routing around it). The rule is generic to any city with lanes, walled or not. **Lane RENDER LAYERING is enforced** (`city_lanes_layered_by_width`): where two linear features cross, the WIDER renders on top - the Imperial road over the city streets it crosses, streets over the alleys. The engine handles this automatically (`s.street`/`s.alley`/`s.road` defer to one ground layer flushed in width order, so a narrow lane never paints over a wider one); the check guards it via each feature's recorded draw-z. **And lanes meet as clean CROSSROADS** (`intersections_are_crossroads`): the engine flushes the ground block in three sub-layers - all EDGE strokes (the dark curb-borders), then all BED strokes (paved surfaces), then all center-marks - so every edge sits below every bed and the beds merge into a continuous junction. Without this, one lane's edge-border paints a line across the other's mouth where they meet (a street looks like it dead-ends at the road instead of flowing into it). The check guards the invariant: `M["ground_edge_zmax"] < M["ground_bed_zmin"]`. **WALLS render OVER the ground lanes** (`city_lane_under_wall`, `city_lanes_under_ward_fences`): a road/street/alley that runs INTO a wall - the exterior city/town rampart OR an internal neighborhood (ward) fence - must pass **under** it, not paint over it. The engine draws ramparts in a dedicated **wall layer** (`s.add_wall`, recorded `wall_z`) above the ground block and below the TOP layer, so this holds by construction; the city wall (and town arc) are drawn with a **genuine gap** at each gate (`_gapped_ring`) rather than a land rect painted over the stroke - so the wall can sit on top yet the road still shows THROUGH the opening (a painted-over rect would erase the road once the wall is on top). A lane only breaches a wall at a gate, where there is no stroke to render over, so touches/crossings within ~50px of a gate are exempt. The checks fire if any lane that meets a wall has a draw-z >= the wall's (i.e. it would render over it) - so if you ever draw a rampart with plain `s.add` instead of `s.add_wall`, or a lane ends up in the wall layer, it is caught.
- **Shops front the streets; poor housing fills the block INTERIOR, reached by alleys** (`businesses_front_streets` - almost every `shop`/`merchant` within ~85px of a street; `poor_housing_mostly_interior` - most `laborer`/`burakumin` are NOT). The historical pattern: a street grid divides a quarter into blocks, businesses line the paved streets (`s.frontage(street, ["shop", ...])`), and the residential majority jams the deep interior (`s.pack(bbox, ["laborer", ...], face_streets="core")` - "core" leaves the street-facing band for the shop frontage and packs dwellings only DEEP, off the street). **The more mercantile a quarter, the more streets**, but **merchant HOUSING is varied and roomy - the deliberate contrast to the laborers** (uniform, small, jammed tight). Behind the storefronts the homes mix by wealth band (`city_merchant_housing_varied`; budgets.md provincial-city merchants, of ~600 households: very rich 8% -> **walled estates** via `s.merchant_estate(x, y)`, a fenced court with a large house inside, recorded in `M["merchant_estates"]` NOT `M["manors"]`; rich 12% -> **large houses** `kind="merchant_large"`; poor+other 80% -> **small houses** `kind="merchant_house"`). And they are **SPREAD OUT** - `city_merchant_housing_spread` wants the merchant homes' median neighbor gap >= 1.3x the laborers' (pack with a bigger `step`; a few denser blocks are fine, the median is robust). Walled estates need DEEP block cores, so do NOT over-street the merchant quarter into shallow strips: a couple of N-S streets off the avenue leave top-to-bottom blocks the estates and roomy homes fit into. **The estate COURT (the fenced rect, not just the house inside it) is checked for clearance** like any compound: it must sit clear of the wall and moat (`city_merchant_estates_clear_of_wall_moat`) and not overlap any other building - temple, compound, house, or another estate (`city_merchant_estates_clear_of_buildings`; the estate's own inner house, centered in the court, is exempt). So keep estates in the block CORES, well inside the rampart and clear of the temple neighborhood - not jammed against the west wall or sitting on a temple. It IS fine (and historically common) for an estate WALL to abut a neighboring building, but then its **GATE must open onto open ground, never into that building** (`city_merchant_estate_gate_clear`): pass `s.merchant_estate(x, y, gate_dir=...)` the side facing clear space (the estate abutting the Temple of Benten opens NORTH, not south into the temple). The gate point + direction are recorded in `M["merchant_estates"][i]`, and the check rejects a gate whose threshold fronts a compound (temple, ministry, the yamen, or another estate court). A laborer quarter, by contrast, has only a couple of through-streets and very deep blocks. Those deep blocks are subdivided by **unpaved gravel/plank ALLEYS** (`s.alley(pts)` - thinner than a street, a pale speckled lane, a narrow no-build corridor so the dense core leaves a gap), the lanes the interior housing is actually reached by. **An alley is checked like a street** - it must not run over a building (`no_structure_on_street` covers alleys too), nor cross the wall or moat (`city_streets_clear_of_wall`, `city_streets_clear_of_moat`); place alleys BEFORE the frontage so no building sits on a lane, and clip them inside the wall (the elliptical rampart curves in). **No GIANT cluster may be cut off from circulation** (`no_isolated_dwelling_cluster`): housing packs deep, but a big contiguous block of dwellings with no street OR alley within ~95px has no way in or out - lace every deep block (including any space-filling band, like Tango's strip between the laborer and government quarters) with alleys so every dwelling is reachable. Small deep cores are fine; the check flags only a large (>30) isolated cluster. And an alley must EARN its length by UNIQUELY serving dwellings (`alleys_serve_buildings`): each building is credited to its NEAREST lane only (the one it actually fronts), exactly as `streets_have_buildings` scores streets, so a lane counts only what no other lane already reaches. This catches BOTH a lane running off into a half-empty corner (a lane to nowhere) AND a **redundant** lane - one laid beside or across another that already serves the same block. The rule of thumb is **one lane per block**: a block's interior needs a single spine to reach it, so don't add a perpendicular cross-arm the spine already reaches (Tango's west laborer blocks share one continuous vertical spine at x1810 and carry no horizontal cross-alley; only the east block keeps its own L), and don't shadow a street with a parallel alley a few tens of px away (the street already fronts those dwellings). The threshold scales to the buildings (~1 uniquely-served dwelling per 30px of the alley's own length), so it bites at the city's dense small-footprint grain rather than relying on a fixed town pixel-gap. **Exception: a gate-access spur.** An alley that runs out to a **ward kido gate** past its last building is providing gate access, not block-service, so that terminal stretch (from the gate to the nearest building the alley fronts) is subtracted from its length before the ratio - extend a commoner alley to its ward gate freely without tripping this check. Why nearest-lane and not raw proximity: a redundant alley still sits *near* plenty of buildings (raw proximity is fooled into passing it), but those buildings are nearer the spine or street that truly serves them, so the redundant lane's UNIQUE tally collapses toward zero and it fails. Samurai (not poor) may front their streets; merchants front as businesses AND count as dwellings (dual-use). **Laborer HOUSING varies by wealth too** (`city_laborer_housing_varied`): like the samurai (senior/junior) and merchant (estate/large/small) tiers, the hinin are not uniform - budgets.md's provincial-city laborer cohort is ~12.5% **"master" (rich) laborers**, the rest standard. So a clear minority get **larger homes** (`kind="laborer_large"`, ~50x34) among the overwhelming majority of small standard `laborer` dwellings (~34x24). Place the large ones on the **prime back-street FRONTAGE** (the wealthier laborers take the roadside lots, with room around them) via `s.frontage(...)`, and keep the standard poor laborers jamming the deep interior cores (`face_streets="core"` packs). The check wants `laborer_large` at roughly **6-20%** of laborers (a generous band around the 12.5% target, since the big homes are room-limited to street frontage) and a strict standard-laborer majority; `laborer_large` is exempt from `poor_housing_mostly_interior` (it is the wealthy tier, so it *should* line streets).
- **`s.bound` makes placement fill the wall's SHAPE.** Set `s.bound = [list(p) for p in WALL]` after drawing the wall; every `pack`/`ring`/`frontage` then rejects positions outside it, so a dense pack over a generous bbox fills the elliptical ring cleanly (partial blocks at the rampart) instead of a rectangle with empty corners. Reset `s.bound = None` before placing anything OUTSIDE the wall (the estates, off-map fields).
- **A small city GATES its samurai/government ward; it does not WALL every neighborhood** (`city_samurai_quarter_gated`, `city_samurai_ward_sealed`). The walled-ward (Chinese *fang*) system - a continuous internal wall around every quarter - was a great-capital, Tang-era feature (Chang'an's wards were 30-80 hectares each, one outsizing a whole provincial city) and was torn down by the Song. At provincial-city scale the samurai/government quarter is **sealed by a light earthwork/palisade fence** (`s.ward(name, boundary, gates)`) whose ends abut the city wall, pierced ONLY at **kido** wooden ward gates (`s.kido`) where a street crosses it - so the gates can't simply be walked around and the road network has no ungated samurai<->commoner path. The gen must run the quarter's access streets THROUGH the fence line (extend them to the road) so each crossing is a gate. The check wants a ward declared, >= 2 kido near the yamen, every street/road/**ring road**/alley crossing the fence gated within ~32px, and both fence ends within ~45px of the wall. (The ring road is a closed loop near the wall, so it crosses the fence at the two points where the fence ends approach the rampart - gate both.) Keep commoner packs/alleys OUTSIDE the fence. **A commoner lane that APPROACHES the ward fence must reach it and END AT A KIDO GATE** (`city_lanes_reach_ward_gates`) - laborers come in to work in the samurai quarter, so the lanes they use pull right up to a gate rather than stopping a sliver short. (`city_samurai_ward_sealed` only catches lanes that *cross* the fence ungated; this catches lanes that stop *short* of it.) A lane endpoint within ~60px of a ward fence, heading at it (within ~37 deg) with a clear path (no building or the main wall between), must sit on the fence (within ~6px) AND have a kido within ~34px, else it fires. Two important distinctions: (1) **the MAIN city wall is exempt** - a lane may stop short of the outer rampart (that is the city's own edge, not a neighborhood boundary); only internal ward fences pull a lane in. (2) **Interior ward lanes are exempt** - a government/samurai lane near the fence from the INSIDE is not an entry; the check uses an interior anchor (the governor's yamen, else the fence centroid) and skips any endpoint on the same side as it. When you extend an alley to a gate, the run-out *past its last building* is a legitimate **gate-access spur** and does not count against `alleys_serve_buildings` (that check now subtracts the spur for an alley ending at a kido). Add the gates via `s.ward(..., gates=[(x, y, horizontal), ...])` - `horizontal=False` for an N-S lane crossing an E-W fence leg. **The fence ends must MEET SOLID wall** (`city_ward_fence_meets_wall`): not merely near the wall *polygon* (a point can be 40px inside the rampart yet read as on it, since `poly_dist` is 0 for interior points - use `edge_dist`), and NOT landing in a **gate opening** (where the wall is cut, so the fence meets nothing and the ward can be walked around) - extend the fence to solid rampart, jogging clear of the gate if need be. **And the wall renders ON TOP of the fence** at the junction (`city_ward_fence_under_wall`): the fence runs UNDER the rampart, not painting over it - `s.ward` lays a short wall cap (recorded with the fence's z AND its polyline `pts` in `M["wards"][i]["wall_caps"]`) over each end so the rampart wins. **That cap must lie FLUSH along the wall** (`city_ward_cap_flush_to_wall`): it FOLLOWS the rampart - walking the wall +/-16px through any vertex in the span - rather than being a single straight tangent. A straight cap laid where the fence abuts AT a wall CORNER cannot follow the bend, so it juts past the corner and reads as **two wall sections overlapping instead of one bent wall** (the Nagahara SW corner, GM 2026-07). The check samples each cap vertex against the wall polyline and fires if any sits > 4px off it. (Full walled wards become plausible only at the future capital-city tier, ~12,000.)
- **A city map crops TIGHT to the walls** (`s.set_view(ox, oy, w, h)` directly, rather than `crop_to_content` - a city is framed to its WALL, not its scattered outside estates). A town map is as much about its surroundings as the town; a **city** map is about the city - the countryside around it matters little, so the rendered window should *just barely encompass the walled city* (wall + moat + a ~50px margin for the title), zooming the city itself as large as the canvas allows. Compute it from the wall: `s.set_view(CX-RX-52-M, CY-RY-52-M, 2*(RX+52+M), 2*(RY+52+M))` (52 ≈ moat gap + wall stroke, `M` ≈ 50). Placement still uses the full coordinate space, so the estates, outside farmland, and the far rows of the gate market simply **run off the cropped edge** - they need not be fully rendered. Crucially, the **checks read `meta["view"]` and treat the crop, not the full canvas, as the map edge**: "runs off the edge" (road, moat-feeder stream, off-map fields) means off the *view*. So size the view to clear the moat but let the estates fall outside it. The title sits top-left *inside* the view (it follows automatically). The off-wall estates remain recorded (`s.manor`), so the estate checks (1-3 shown, dispersed, SE, no-overlap) still pass even though most estates are off-map entirely.

A wall encloses what it must defend; an oversized ring with a big empty perimeter would not have been built. - **Wall towers give INTERLOCKING flanking fire - density is a TUNABLE defense tier** (GM asked whether "every attacker is within arrow range of >= 2 towers" is accurate and whether our maps reflected it, 2026-07-22; drives `meta(wall_defense=...)`, `WALL_DEFENSE`, `settlement.wall_tower_spacing_px`, the tier-driven densification + coverage-remediation in `s.city_wall`, and the `city_wall_tower_coverage` check; fixtures in `test_checks.py`).
  - *What the research found (China first: the mamian 马面 wall-tower system; Warring-States through Ming).* The rule of thumb IS accurate, and it is the ORIGINAL design purpose of the projecting wall tower. Adjacent mamian bastions are spaced so their fields of **flanking fire (侧射) overlap** - an attacker at the curtain base between two towers is shot at from BOTH (Shen Kuo's 11th-c. Mengxi Bitan describes it as 矢石相及, "arrows and stones reach each other"). The spacing that achieves ">= 2 towers everywhere" therefore equals the effective **arrow range**: put towers a bowshot apart and every curtain point has two within a bowshot. Real spacings bracket the effective ranges: **Pingyao** ~50-60 m (>= 2 everywhere, an aimed-lethal bowshot), **Xi'an** ~120 m (= 2 x 60 m; the crossfire covers the curtain but the base near a tower has only that one at full reach - the sparser, peacetime-affordable ring). Effective bow ranges: **aimed-lethal ~60 m** (a war arrow kills reliably), **full war-bow reach ~100-150 m** (harassing fire).
  - *The decision - three tiers, because fortification tracked THREAT, not a universal constant.* A city that has repelled sieges was towered as densely as it could afford; one at peace for centuries was not. So the density is a per-city knob (`meta(wall_defense=...)`), asked at city-generation time alongside "Imperial road?" and "river?":
    - **`siege`** = `>= 2` towers within **aimed-lethal 60 m / 197 ft** (spacing == range, the Pingyao standard). Border / besieged cities. **Tango and Nagahara both use this** (border location + siege history - GM's call).
    - **`garrison`** (default) = `>= 2` within **full war-bow 100 m / 328 ft** (the looser interior-garrison ring).
    - **`peaceful`** = `>= 1` flanking tower within aimed-lethal range (curtain midpoints still get 2) - the Xi'an ~120 m spacing, a long-peaceful city.
  - *The check's shape and its keep-outs.* `city_wall_tower_coverage` samples the curtain every ~18px and requires the tier's min-count within (range + 12px). The **+12px is a mamian half-footprint** - an archer shoots from the bastion's parapet SPAN, not its center point, so a tower reaches ~12px past its recorded center; the placement code adds the same 12px, so the two agree. **Barbican and water-gate exemptions** matter as much as the coverage rule: the gate is the most-fortified point (gate tower + guard house + inspection + gateposts), and a water gate is a grated opening flanked by its own two towers - so points within 130px of a gate, 55px of its guard furniture, or 45px of a water gate are skipped. These check keep-outs deliberately **mirror the placement keep-outs** in `s.city_wall`: `_seat_mural` will not put a tower inside a barbican or on a water-gate opening, so a point the placement structurally cannot tower must not be one the check demands - otherwise the map can never pass. (This alignment is what finally cleared Nagahara's last near-gate and water-gate coverage points, 2026-07-22.) The older `city_wall_towers_spaced` (>= 6 towers, no bare arc > ~75 deg) stays as a coarse floor - cheap, and it still catches a wall with almost no towers.
  - *Deliberate departure / legibility.* Towers draw at the legible upper end of the mamian footprint (see the gate-structures entry above); the SPACING is honest to the tier, but a real 60 m bowshot at 3 ft/px is ~66px, so a siege ring is a dense necklace of bastions - which is the correct read for a border fortress and matches Pingyao's actual tower count per length.
  - *Tower SHAPE - rectangular, never round (GM asked, 2026-07-22).* Every wall/gate tower draws as a RECTANGLE - the mamian spur wider along the wall than deep (62x40 px ~ Xi'an's ~20 x 12 m, a ~1.7:1 spur); the enemy-tower block on it and the corner/gate towers run nearer square, which is also authentic (don't force a ratio there). CIRCULAR towers are deliberately absent: round towers are a EUROPEAN medieval signature (a round front resists sapping and later deflects cannon), and Chinese/Japanese fortification is rectilinear top to bottom. The "why" is an inversion of the European logic worth keeping straight - Europe shaped its towers against the ATTACKER's siege engineering; China/Japan shaped them for the DEFENDER's flanking-fire VOLUME: a rectangular tower's flat faces let more archers line up and shoot straight down the wall base, with LESS dead zone than a round one. Same problem, opposite answer. The only genuine round bastions are archaic pre-rebuild survivals (Xi'an's SW-corner roundel) - reserve a round tower ONLY to signal "an older fragment of the wall", never as the default. Japanese yagura/tenshu are likewise square/rectangular timber on stone bases; the one place Japan curves is the ishigaki WALL batter, not the towers.

- **Fire towers: the fire-defense of a dense, ENCLOSED wooden core** (drives `walled_town_has_fire_tower` / `city_has_fire_towers`, `fire_tower_in_commoner_quarter`). Premodern Japanese cities burned constantly - Edo was the "city of fires," and the Meireki fire of 1657 killed ~100,000. The institution we depict is the **fire-watch tower** (*hinomi-yagura*, 火の見櫓) - a tall braced-timber tower with an alarm bell (*hanshō*, 半鐘) the watchman struck in a cadence that told the city how *near* a fire was. **Why walled towns and cities but NOT open towns:** the tower belongs to the dense, contiguous, *enclosed* wooden core. Two reinforcing reasons. (1) **Density** - fire is catastrophic only where roofs touch (the packed intramural *machiya*/tenement warren); an open road-town strung along the highway, fields running off every edge, has natural breaks (the road, the field-gaps, the surrounding open ground), so a standing tower does not earn its cost. (2) **The wall traps the fire** - the rampart that keeps raiders out also keeps a blaze *in*, so the watch over the rooftops is most needed exactly where there is a wall. **The unwalled county seat is NOT an exception - it is the confirming case (GM question 2026-07-24, researched; this reverted the 2026-07 audit's brief widening of the town check to unwalled seats).** The real-world analog of an unwalled tax-administration seat is the *jin'ya*/*daikansho* town, and its fire posture was defensive, not observational: a *hanshō* bell on a ladder, rooftop platform, or simple frame at the office or watch post (the local temple bell doubling as alarm elsewhere), night patrols with clappers, stored water (Takayama Jin'ya, the surviving Hida daikansho, preserves its fire-prevention water system), and above all the plastered fireproof *kura* protecting the tax rice and ledgers - losing the tax rice was the career-ending disaster, so the storehouse was fireproofed rather than the horizon watched. The freestanding countryside fire tower everyone pictures is a *Meiji-and-later* institution (early Meiji rural fire watch was still a wooden ladder and a bell; the standalone towers spread end-of-Meiji through Showa with the volunteer *shōbōdan*), so an Edo-flavored unwalled town with a central tower is roughly a century anachronistic. China agrees at both ends: the Song built staffed *wanghuolou* fire towers in hyper-dense Kaifeng and Hangzhou, while ordinary market towns had nothing of the kind (and Chinese county seats were walled anyway). On our maps the unwalled seat's fire story is carried by the kura on the yamen, headman, and merchant houses; a small *hanshō* bell-frame at the magistrate's compound would be the one defensible extra glyph, and plain omission is equally accurate. **L7R/Sengoku adaptation:** the famous chartered townsman brigades (the *machi-bikeshi* "iroha" companies) are a peace-era Tokugawa institution; in a Sengoku-flavored Rokugan the fire-watch is the **magistrate's** (yoriki/doshin/ashigaru manning the tower), not self-governing townsman companies. The tower is drawn as an OPEN braced frame with a red bell (distinct from the solid guard towers ON the rampart - a fire tower is a *civilian interior* structure watching the rooftops, not the wall); it is an overlap-checked struct, so it must stand clear of the wall, roads, buildings, and open water (the pond - Tango's west tower once landed on the pond rim, which is what closed that gap with `no_structure_on_pond`), and `fire_tower_in_commoner_quarter` keeps it in the commoner warren, never the samurai ward. It also keeps **>= 5px of daylight** around its frame (`fire_tower_standoff`): the blanket overlap check only catches true intersection, so a tower butted flush against a house passed it while reading as a collision - the glyph's roof cap overhangs the recorded frame by ~2px a side, and an open braced tower's footing and ladder sit clear of the neighboring eaves (it stands on a seam, not in a party-wall row). The one neighbor neither pass can see is a **wellhead** (`fire_tower_clear_of_wells`): wells are overlap-EXEMPT (a wellhead's nominal footprint may kiss a dense-city building, because houses ring a tenement court closely), so a tower dropped onto a well trips no generic check - but the tower's footing would stand in the well court blocking the quarter's shared draw-point (the idobata), so the tower keeps the same >= 5px of daylight from every well's clearance disc (this caught Tango's west tower standing squarely on a well; the bad manifest is pinned in `pool/regressions/`). **When a settlement rates MORE than one tower, they DISPERSE ACROSS THE QUARTERS** (`fire_towers_dispersed` + `fire_tower_amid_its_district`): historically the fire-watch was parcelled out per neighborhood - each Edo machi block-group kept its own tower, and the shogunate's official watch stations were likewise distributed one to a district - because a tower's useful watch is its visual neighborhood of rooftops. Two rules enforce it. (1) Pairwise floor: two towers inside one watch radius of each other duplicate coverage, so every pair stands at least one watch radius (the same ~230px the commoner-quarter check uses for "near its rooftops") apart. (2) District centering: a comfortable pairwise gap is NOT enough - Tango's original pair stood 341px apart yet both NW of center, leaving the NE laborer warren (the city's biggest rooftop mass) unwatched. So every commoner dwelling (inside the walls, when walled - the extramural gate-market rows are not what the towers exist for) is assigned to its NEAREST tower; that partition is the de facto watch districting the drawn towers imply, and each tower must stand near its district's center of mass (offset <= max(0.9 x district RMS radius, one watch radius)) - AMID the blocks it watches, not at their far edge with a whole unwatched quadrant in its ledger. In practice: put one tower amid each major commoner quarter, sized to the tower count (Tango: NE laborers, west servant/burakumin, south-central merchants -> 3 towers). Place it LAST, after the packs and `farmsteads()`, on a cleared seam the finished map already has. *(Deliberate divergence from literal scale, per the legibility license: a single tower stands in for a quarter's whole fire-watch; real cities had many.)*
- **Why we DON'T depict firebreaks (a deliberate omission, so it is not re-added).** The other historical urban fire-defense was the **firebreak** (*hiyokechi*, 火除地 / *hirokoji*, 広小路) - a cleared strip of open ground a blaze could not jump, which, too valuable to leave idle, filled with removable stalls and theater stages and so doubled as the market/amusement ground (Edo's *hirokoji* became its entertainment districts). We tried two depictions and dropped both. A **drawn** plaza (a shaded clearing placed before the dwelling packs) forces the packer to flow houses around an *invented* clearing, which reads as a designed plaza rather than the *absence* of building a firebreak actually is. A **label-only** marking (a run-aligned annotation dropped onto a gap the finished map happens to leave) is just naming existing dead space - it adds a word, not a feature, and getting the gaps and label orientations to read well takes real effort for no real payoff. So a firebreak is **not a feature of these maps**: the towers carry the fire-defense story on their own. If a future map genuinely needs a firebreak (a wide ceremonial fire-plaza that is itself the subject), build it as a real, deliberately-placed feature with its own glyph - do not resurrect the marked-gap approach.

- **City commoner housing is CONTIGUOUS - the row-packing doctrine** (drives `s.rowpack(...)`, `city_row_housing_touches`, `city_row_housing_gap`; GM-confirmed 2026-07 in the 1px=3ft scale pass).

Machiya street frontage was continuous - party walls or touching eaves, because street frontage was taxed and precious; back-lot *nagaya* were literally ONE ROOF over a row of family units; Chinese county-seat courtyard housing shared rammed-earth or brick party walls in continuous street walls. Detached-with-yard was a samurai (and rural) form.
  - *What we had.* The measured pre-doctrine Tango: 553 row-class dwellings with a median nearest-neighbor gap of 12px (~31 ft at the old grain) and ZERO touching pairs - a suburb, not a city quarter. Pinned as `pool/regressions/city_row_housing_fires_on_scattered_housing.json`.

Obstacles break a terrace naturally - the occasional gap is historically honest (post-conflagration cities kept a few firebreak gaps), it just isn't the default texture.

- **In-wall VEGETABLE tracts (`kind="vegetable"`) vs paddy** (drives the `kind != "paddy"` exemptions in `fields_show_water_source`, `field_ringed`, `city_interior_fields_farmhouse_density`).
  - *Why they exist.* At the declared 3 ft/px city scale, ~600 contiguous households genuinely occupy a small fraction of a ~0.9 mi walled ring - the real walled seat filled that surplus with intensively-worked garden ground (well- and night-soil-fed vegetable plots), which is also Tango's canon (average population, above-average space per capita BECAUSE of the agricultural district).
  - *Why the exemptions.* A paddy must show its water (channel/pond/stream) and its farmstead ring; a city garden tract is watered from wells and worked by the residents of the surrounding quarters - there is no separate farm household to draw and no surface channel to demand. East of the Imperial road a channel CANNOT reach anyway (it would have to cross the road).
  - *Bed SIZE - intensive, the SMALLEST dry parcels* (`vegetable_beds_are_intensive`, veg-bed area <= 0.15 real acres; fixture `vegetable_beds_intensive_fires_on_tangos_fat_veg_slabs`). A kitchen/vegetable garden is hand-worked ground; its beds are ~55 ft, distinctly smaller than a grain-field hem strip (~90-100 ft) - the veg tract should be the map's smallest dry parcels, not its largest. Tile the tract width into `round(width / 55 ft)` EVEN columns with only small jitter; a single uniform cut leaves one fat ~150 ft column (0.24-acre slabs, the biggest dry parcels on Tango - backwards for a garden; GM 2026-07-22). The check keys on the VEG_CROPS labels (daikon/greens/onions/beans), distinct from grain DRY_CROPS (barley/millet/buckwheat/soy), so it fires only where a veg tract exists and never touches village grain hems.
- **Gate furniture never overlaps; watch-towers keep off the graves** (`city_gate_guard_inspection_separate`, `fire_tower_clear_of_graveyards`; GM, 2026-07). The guard house and inspection station walk fixed arcs (80/144) along the wall from the gate - on a small tight ring (Tango's 480x450 shrink) the arcs converge and the annex was drawn through its guard house, so the generator now walks the inspection OUTWARD (+22px steps) until the two rects clear, and the check pins it. A fire-watch tower's braced footing gets the same 5px daylight from a graveyard as from wells and buildings. Both captured red-first in `pool/regressions/gate_furniture_overlap_and_tower_on_graveyard_tango.json` per the regression-corpus discipline.
- **The kido aligns with its fence; the guard box stands ward-side** (`kido_aligned_with_ward_fence`; GM 2026-07-24). The kido is a gap IN the ward fence: its roofed bar spans that gap, so its long axis follows the LOCAL FENCE TANGENT - a 30-degree fence run gets a 30-degree gate, never an axis-aligned stamp (same doctrine as `city_wall_towers_aligned` for rampart towers). Both cities carried the live defect: Nagahara's SW ring-road kido sat axis-aligned on its ~159-degree fence run (and its top-fence kido was a full 90 degrees off - a wrong legacy orientation flag the tangent rule now makes impossible), Tango's S ring-road kido likewise on the ~44-degree SW jog; both pre-fix manifests are frozen in `pool/regressions/`. `s.ward` computes each gate's rotation from the boundary polyline at its seat and hangs the guard box on the WARD-INTERIOR flank - the gate watch belongs to the ward it seals, so the neighborhood guard house rotates and sides with its fence too (the old fixed-per-orientation flanks put Nagahara's E-fence boxes on the street side). Gates in `s.ward(...)` are plain (x, y) seats now (a legacy horizontal flag is accepted and ignored); `s.kido(rot=, guard_side=)` covers hand placement. The check pins the recorded `rot` (legacy manifests fall back to the horizontal flag) to the nearest fence segment's tangent within 7 degrees mod 180; a kido more than ~16px from every fence is exempt (nothing to align to). Kido reservations (`KIDO_SPOTS` block_polys) should be SYMMETRIC squares - which flank the box lands on now depends on fence direction, so one-sided rects tuned to the legacy flanks no longer bound the glyph.

- **River cities (`s.river`, the `Nagahara` worked example)** - drives `s.river`, `s.moat(river=...)`, `s.canal`/`s.dock`/`s.jetty`/`s.water_gate`, the multi-road `M["roads"]`, and the river-aware check variants (`moat_is_heaviest_watercourse`, `city_moat_surrounds_wall` / `city_moat_joins_river`, `city_roads_run_offmap`, `city_canal_reaches_dock`, `city_wharf_jetties_on_bank`).
  - *The history (China first, Japan agreeing).* MOST provincial cities sit on a river (Tango, on an off-map stream, is the exception). The trunk river NEVER runs *through* the walls - the one great city that let a river in, Kaifeng, silted its bed meters above the plain and was devastated by floods seven times (the 1642 flood alone killed ~300k of 378k). Instead the city stands ON one bank: the RIVER is the water defense on its flank, and the dug MOAT covers the three landward faces, tapping the river upstream and returning downstream so the current flushes it (Xiangyang's Han-river face + dug moat; Pingyao on the Fen; Okayama's Asahi "natural moat"; Shanghai's county-seat moat "connected to the Huangpu"). The ONE way water legitimately enters the walls is a **water gate** (*shuimen*) admitting a navigable CANAL, not the river - Suzhou's Pan Gate, a paired land-and-water gate with a sluiced arch, feeding the city's inner canals + a dock basin. The riverfront **wharf** (jetties, warehouses, market) sits OUTSIDE the water-side gate, the river-city version of the gate market (*guan-xiang*).
  - *One mouth on the river, not two (GM 2026-07-23, spotting Nagahara's water-gate corner).* Pre-fix, the cargo canal opened its OWN river tap 36 real ft beside the moat's downstream junction and rode collinearly inside the moat arm's stroke for the whole bank crossing - rendering as a smeared doubled channel with a sliver fork at the mouth. The historically grounded fix MERGES the mouths rather than separating them: in the Suzhou pattern the city's canals communicate with the MOAT, and the moat's downstream river junction is the single navigation entrance (separating them would also be geometrically worse here - a distinct mouth further downstream would have to cross the moat's southern arc at grade). So Nagahara's canal now ends ON the moat's outfall-arm corner (`CANAL[0] = MOAT[1]`, self-correcting if the wall resizes) and boats run river -> moat arm -> water gate -> dock basin. `city_canal_reaches_dock` accepts the moat handoff, and `city_canal_shares_moat_mouth` pins the defect (a canal end tapping the river directly must not sit inside the moat's stroke corridor - a mouth on open bank away from the moat stays legitimate, real cities had water gates on the river face itself); the pre-fix manifest is frozen in `pool/regressions/city_canal_shares_moat_mouth_fires_on_nagaharas_second_river_mouth.json`.
  - *Sluggish far-side circulation is ACCEPTED as-drawn - do not "fix" it.* The two junctions sit only ~800 real ft apart along the river, so the driving head is tiny and the far side of the ring circulates slowly. That is true of every real example of the pattern: river moats were kept full by the river's stage more than they were flushed, so partial stagnation is period-accurate rather than a defect.
  - *Junction angles follow the current (GM 2026-07-24, overturning the initial accepted-as-drawn call).* The GM noticed both junction arms were EXACTLY parallel square tees and asked whether they shouldn't angle with the flow. They were parallel only because `s.moat(river=)` projected each open end onto the river by perpendicular foot - an algorithm artifact, never a decision. The research verdict split by end, and the ASYMMETRY is itself the historical signature:
    - **Outlet: the GM's intuition is standard practice.** Junction angle is a primary control on confluence flow - small downstream-pointing angles merge smoothly, large angles drive the entering jet across the receiving channel (stagnation, separation, far-bank attack). Natural tributaries curve to join pointing downstream; engineered drainage returns do the same. The outlet now sweeps ~22 degrees off square, downstream.
    - **Inlet: gentler-in-theory, but sediment pushes back.** A flow-aligned intake is the smooth-entry answer, BUT an offtake swallows a disproportionate share of the river's bedload - the more smoothly aligned, the more it drinks, and a silted moat is a maintenance failure. Classical headworks therefore kept the offtake itself near-square (control by sluice, siting on the outer bank, skimming the cleaner upper water); a moat also wants STAGE (staying full), not throughput, so it never needs a momentum-capturing aligned mouth. The inlet now tilts only ~10 degrees upstream-of-square.
    - *Engine + convention.* `s.moat(..., river_inlet_tilt=10.0, river_outlet_tilt=22.0)` - each junction foot shifts along the river by arm-length x tan(tilt), so the arm sits exactly its tilt off square; the upstream end is the inlet. **`river` pts run UPSTREAM-FIRST** (source before mouth) - both the tilt and the check key on this order; Nagahara's Hayakawa (N -> S) complies.
    - *The check.* `city_moat_junction_angles`: the outlet's junction must sit >= 12px downstream of its arm's square foot, the inlet's <= 4px (square or upstream-tilted, never swept downstream). Pre-tilt Nagahara frozen in `pool/regressions/city_moat_junction_angles_fires_on_nagaharas_square_tees.json`.
  - *The engine.* `s.river(pts, width=)` draws the trunk waterway (a wide off-map-to-off-map stream) and records `M["river"]` so the checks know which stream legitimately outweighs the dug moat. `s.moat(ring, gap=, river=<pts>, river_cut=, river_inlet_tilt=, river_outlet_tilt=)` builds an OPEN moat arc: the vertices within `river_cut` of the river are dropped and both open ends extend onto the river, which closes the water ring itself - the junction feet tilted with the current (see the junction-angles bullet). `s.canal(pts, width=)` is a mid-tier watercourse (heavier than a hairline ditch, lighter than the moat); `s.water_gate(x, y, rot=)` is the grated shuimen arch (the wall is drawn with a matching gap via `city_wall(water_gates=[...])`, and mural towers skip its vertex); `s.dock(cx, cy, w, h)` is the in-city basin at the canal head; `s.jetty(x, y, rot=, length=)` is a planked finger from the bank. `s.road` now appends to `M["roads"]` (a city with no Imperial spine has several roads, none the "Imperial road").
  - *The checks.* A river city declares `meta(imperial_road=False)` (its Imperial highway passes off-map) - then `city_imperial_road_through` is waived and `city_roads_run_offmap` instead requires the road net to leave the map in >= 2 directions. `city_moat_surrounds_wall` gains a river-arc variant (every wall vertex behind the moat OR the river) plus `city_moat_joins_river` (both open moat ends meet the river) and `city_moat_junction_angles` (outlet swept downstream, inlet square-to-upstream - the junction-angles bullet). `moat_is_heaviest_watercourse` excludes the river's own record from the comparison (the river IS the heavier defense). `no_structure_on_canal` keeps buildings out of the canal water; `city_canal_reaches_dock` requires the canal to connect the dock to the water - the river directly OR a handoff onto the moat (like a street reaching the road, no visible gap); `city_canal_shares_moat_mouth` forbids a canal opening its own river mouth inside the moat's junction corridor (the shared-mouth doctrine above); `city_wharf_jetties_on_bank` requires each jetty's root at the near bank and its tip in the near-half water (a finger, not a bar spanning the river). The **water set-back** and `city_moat_fed_offmap` work unchanged (the river is a wide stream). **THE DEAD CROSS THE RIVER**: the moat's water set-back leaves no dry landward fringe, so the funerary complex sits on the far bank, downstream, clear of the bridge road.
  - *Gateposts are tangent-aware* (`city_wall`): each gate's two posts stand to either side of the opening ORIENTED to the wall's LOCAL tangent - so an E/W gate's posts stand N and S of it (the old code hard-coded N/S vertical posts, which floated parallel to an E/W wall). Recorded as `gate_structs` kind `"gatepost"`.
  - *Samurai estates face OTOSAN UCHI* (`city_estates_toward_capital`, replacing the hard-coded `city_estates_in_southeast`): a samurai builds his country seat on the capital-facing side, so the direction is per-city - `meta(capital_dir=<cardinal>)` (Tango SE, Nagahara NE). And `city_estates_clear_of_roads` keeps an estate off the highway out of the city.
  - *City temples honor the clan's TWO PATRON FORTUNES* (`city_temples_dedicated`, extending the town `CLAN_FORTUNES` rule to cities - l7r.md: Hantei X codified that every city holds a temple to each of its clan's patron fortunes). Declare `meta(clan="Crab")`; the labeled (major) temples must be the two patrons (+ optionally Bishamon, the warrior fortune of any samurai quarter), the rest small wayside shrines. Nagahara (Crab) = Bishamon + Ebisu; a great Temple of Suitengu (the river fortune, thematic but not a patron) is demoted to a small shrine. Tango declares `meta(clan="Crane")` = Benten + Daikoku.
  - *Funerary grounds stay off the fields* (`funerary_clear_of_fields`, `structures_clear_of_dry_plots` extended to funerary): a burial / cremation ground sits BESIDE the farmland, never on a paddy body or its ditches.
  - *Render / layout order (GM doctrine).* Lay the immovable infrastructure FIRST so everything flows around it and overlaps become impossible: **river -> walls (+ moat) -> roads/streets -> ring road -> guard towers -> gates + gate furniture -> canal / water gate / dock -> civic compounds (temples, ministries, yamen, theater, flophouses - each reserving a no-build block) -> the dense packs -> farmsteads / wells / fire towers LAST**. A feature placed after the packs (a fire tower, a hand-placed merchant house) must reserve its ground up front (`block_polys`) or it lands on a packed dwelling.
  - *Nagahara is DONE (v2, population 3,000).* The river-hemmed ring is sized `RX,RY = 494,460` (no agricultural district) and passes the full validator with zero mechanical failures, 560 dwellings in band. Getting there is what drove the **wall-sizing process** below - the first-cut wall (430x400) was too small for 3,000, and rather than grind placements by trial and error we MEASURED and resized the wall once.

### Historical grounding: martial training in a provincial city

Researched 2026-07-25, prompted by the GM asking how many dojos a provincial city should have. It extends the county-tier finding already recorded in [`buildings.md`](../buildings.md) ("A dojo is a city institution; county training is courtyard keiko") up the settlement ladder, and it is the reasoning behind every number in the martial-training bullet above.

**What the research found.** The standalone dojo with a resident sensei and enrolled students is an urban institution in both anchor cultures. In Edo Japan formal martial instruction lived in the **castle town**: the domain school (*hanko*) and its martial hall (*bugeijo*) were built in castle towns for the domain's own retainers, and by late Edo nearly every one of the ~260 domains had one. Private commercial halls - *machi-dojo*, run by low-income bushi and eventually by commoners teaching for a living - are a **late phenomenon and a metropolitan one**: the boom runs roughly 1830-1860 and its famous examples (the Three Great Dojo of Edo; Chiba Shusaku's Genbukan claimed ~3,600 disciples over its life) sit in a city of a million. The Chinese anchor is starker: military examinations were held at county and prefectural level and candidates prepared at schools, but there was no dense network of private martial academies at a county seat - drill happened at garrison grounds.

**What the L7R numbers force.** Working from budgets.md rather than from the anchors:

- A **county town** holds ~20 resident samurai (~15 working). No student body, no living for a sensei. Zero dojos - it draws a practice ground instead, which is the finding already in buildings.md.
- A **provincial city** holds **~300 resident samurai** (~10% of ~3,000; ~225 working, ~80% of them bushi-track per the caste table). That is a small *han*'s retainer band, which is exactly the size that historically seated a domain school - so the tier that seats a governor seats a martial hall.
- A **domain capital** holds ~1,560 resident samurai; **Otosan Uchi** ~40,000.

**The decision.** Roughly **one martial establishment per ~100 resident samurai**, counting the state hall as one of them. That ladder reads right at every rung: a county town at 0.2 rounds to none; a provincial city lands at ~3; a domain capital at ~15, which is where l7r.md's "the city's many dojos" starts being literally true; Otosan Uchi at ~400, of which 3-4 would be famous - the same long-tail-under-a-few-greats shape late-Edo Edo had. Note the ratio is on the **samurai count, not the population**: a dojo serves samurai and nobody else, and the samurai-share schedule in budgets.md already carries the density variation between tiers.

The generator splits that total into the two kinds it actually draws. The state hall is fixed at exactly one (a state institution is a program item, not a roll). The private tail rolls on the GM's formula - 1 per full 200 samurai plus a remainder-fraction chance of one extra - which puts a provincial city at 1-2 private halls and a 2-3 total, the lower-middle of the ~1-per-100 band. Keeping the private tail thin is deliberate: the *machi-dojo* boom was a million-person-city event, and a seat of ~3,000 has not had it.

**The countryside is deliberately not counted** (GM 2026-07-25). A province holds ~550 samurai in all - ~300 in the city, ~120 across its 6 county towns, ~90 on country estates - but only the city cohort supports institutions; the rural samurai either ride in or make do with the magistracy's practice ground. Asking separately about the countryside would double-count, because a provincial city's size already scales with the countryside that feeds it.

**Sizes, in real feet.** Everything is sized to its program, with no legibility inflation:

- The **hall** is 60 x 36 ft = 2,160 sqft = **120 tatami** (a mat is 3 x 6 ft). At the ~90-135 sqft per drilling samurai that buildings.md established for the county practice ground, that floor holds ~20 pairs at once - the officer cohort plus a school class.
- The **archery lane** is 100 ft long, covering the kyudo standard 28 m (92 ft) shot, with an **azuchi** earthen butt at its head. It is the same ~90 ft clear lane the Mode A azuchi uses, and it sits **inside** the compound wall - an unwalled shooting lane in a city street is a hazard, and the hall's whole point is that the province's youth train in one enclosed place.
- The **compound** is therefore 130 x 100 ft (13,000 sqft, ~0.30 acre): lane plus butt plus wall margins across, hall plus lane plus circulation deep. That is well below a ministry office compound (224 x 148 ft), which is right - a ministry is a bureau of clerks and archives, this is one hall and a yard.
- A **private dojo** is a 76 x 44 ft lot holding a 44 x 24 ft hall = 1,056 sqft = ~59 tatami, the **low end** of the Edo town-dojo band on purpose, plus a training yard. No archery lane: there is no room for a 92 ft shot on a 76 ft lot.
- The **weapon rack** (~8 x 2 ft) and the **striking posts** (*tategi*, ~1 ft) are sub-glyph at 3 ft/px, so they draw as fixed-size location markers under the Mode A stroke convention. Marking the gear rather than scaling it is the honest form of the finding - what makes a training ground read as established is the durable equipment practice leaves behind.

**Two deliberate departures.** (1) The compound is drawn as walls plus its three interior features with the rest implied, like the governor's yamen - a real bugeijo also had a changing room, an armory, and a viewing platform. (2) State violet vs private tan is a **map convention, not a historical one**; nothing about a domain school's construction differed from a private hall's. The color carries the ownership distinction that the map otherwise could not show.

**One knock-on worth knowing.** Adding the compound to `citybudget.CIVIC_PROGRAM` grew the derived interior, which grew Nagahara's budget-sized ring by 1px, which flipped which wall edge `seg_dist` called nearest for one untouched mural tower - and a correct tower failed `city_wall_towers_aligned`. The check now scores the best of the **two** nearest edges, because a tower seated near a vertex of the wall N-gon is legitimately square to either run that meets there. See the comment at the check.

### Sizing the wall to the population (`city_capacity`, `--capacity`, `--capacity-map`)

The hard question every city raises is *"do the walls enclose too much empty space or too little for the target population?"* Guessing a wall size and then grinding placements to fit is backwards. Instead **measure** with the space-budget analysis in `check_village.py`:

- **`city_capacity(M)`** grid-samples the walled interior and classes every cell as **dwelling / civic-overhead / water / trunk-road / residential-street / field / OPEN**. From that it reports: `target_dwellings` (population / 5), `placed` (dwellings actually drawn), **`inherent_capacity`** (dwellings the residential-capable ground CAN hold at the canonical well-packed density `RHO_CANONICAL = 0.00149` dwellings/px^2 - Tango's 561 placed over its ~378k px^2 of non-reserve residential ground; feature 009 recalibrated this from the original 0.00127, which had divided by ground that still contained the agri reserve's ~72k of non-field slack and so under-read what packed urban ground delivers), the area budget, `open_frac`, and a **`suggested_wall_scale`** (>1 enlarge, <1 shrink) that resizes the ring ONCE to the right size. Reserve ground of ANY kind (agri included) is deducted from the residential-capable ground - it is committed to non-housing and must never dilute the density norm.
- **The verdict** is one of four, and each says exactly what to do next:
  - **`too_small`** - even well-packed the wall cannot hold the target (`inherent_cap < 0.9*T`). ENLARGE the wall (multiply all coordinates by `suggested_wall_scale` about the wall center).
  - **`too_big`** - far more room than the target needs (`inherent_cap > 1.4*T`). SHRINK it.
  - **`underpacked`** - the WALL is right but the placement is too sparse (`placed < (1 - population_tol)*T`, i.e. below the *same* line the `population_consistent_with_housing` check fails at, default 7%). Do NOT resize - add dwellings / densify the quarters.
  - **`about_right`** - wall sized and packed to target. Done.
- **`city_wall_sized_to_population`** is the gate check that fails on `too_small` / `too_big`, so a mis-sized wall is a first-class, automated finding rather than something discovered by hand late.
- **`--capacity-map` prints an ASCII map of the interior classification** (each cell 40px), so the report shows WHERE the open ground is, not just how much. Read it to decide *where* to add a quarter (or that the open ground is all civic precinct / setback and the answer is denser packing, not a new quarter).

**The Nagahara worked example (the wall-sizing process in action, 2026-07):** first cut at 430x400 declared 3,000 -> `enlarge` (inherent 446 < 600), suggested scale x1.13. Scaled the whole generator x1.15 about the center -> `densify` (wall now right, placement sparse). The `--capacity-map` showed the open ground was the civic-committed west half (temple neighborhood NW, sealed government ward SW). The first pass densified the east; feature 006 later showed the honest fix was to fill the empty NW itself (the monzen pocket) so the quarter is not merely under-built - see the per-quarter doctrine below. End state: `sized_and_packed`.

**Why `RHO_CANONICAL` is honest but the aggregate model over-read:** the density is calibrated on Tango (a fully-packed city), so `inherent_capacity` is what a *well-packed* quarter delivers. The ORIGINAL `city_capacity` was a global aggregate (total residential-capable area x that density vs total placed), which is blind to DISTRIBUTION - a dense east plus an empty west averages to "fine." It passed a visibly-broken Nagahara (dense east, empty NW temple quarter, ~35 commoner dwellings spilled outside the walls). Feature 006 (below) replaced the aggregate with a per-quarter model.

### Quarters and per-quarter density (feature 006)

Density is a SPATIAL property, so it is judged PER QUARTER, not city-wide. The measured proof: Tango (good) and the broken Nagahara had the SAME in-wall dwelling count (521 vs 525) and the SAME median block density (4.6/10k px^2) - an aggregate or anonymous-grid check literally cannot tell them apart. The only difference was WHERE the empty ground sat. So the generator DECLARES its intent and the check reads it.

- **The generator declares quarters.** `s.quarter(poly, zone, kind=None)` records a region + `zone` in {`residential`, `civic`, `mixed`, `reserve`} into `M["quarters"]`; a `reserve` also carries a `kind` (`drill_ground` / `garden` / `agricultural_district`). A `drill_ground` (bare earth + dashed muster perimeter) and a `garden` (green sward) are DRAWN so their open ground reads as deliberate, not accidental; an **`agricultural_district` draws NOTHING of its own** (GM 2026-07-22) - its combs, in-wall farmhouses, and label already carry the visual, and the faint dashed boundary it used to add read as a stray dotted line cutting through the farmhouses and across the Imperial road above the burakumin neighborhood. The quarter is still recorded in `M["quarters"]` regardless, so per-quarter density judging is unaffected. A walled city tiles its interior with wedges split at the crossroads (road x=CX, axis y=CY); Tango and Nagahara both do this.
- **The checks (all walled-city-scoped):**
  - `city_quarters_declared` / `city_quarters_tile_interior` - a walled city MUST declare quarters that cover the interior (>=85%) without overlap (<=5%) or spill outside the wall (<=3%).
  - `city_residential_quarters_dense_enough` - every `residential`/`mixed` quarter's average density (over NON-civic ground: quarter area minus the civic compound footprints inside it, so a government ward's yamen does not read as under-built) must sit in `[QUARTER_DENSITY_FLOOR, QUARTER_DENSITY_CEIL]` = `[0.30, 2.30]/1000px^2`. A `residential` quarter additionally must hold no DEAD ZONE - `largest_empty_gap` (the biggest empty pocket radius, counting wells and civic compounds as served) must stay under `DEAD_ZONE_MAX` = 150px, so a half-dense/half-empty quarter fails even with an in-band average. The band spans ~7.7x because a commoner warren runs ~4-6x denser than a samurai/official ward (Edo land-vs-population inversion); the dead-zone guard applies only to PURE residential (a mixed quarter legitimately holds a civic forecourt).
  - `city_commoner_dwellings_inside_walls` - HARD ZERO commoner dwellings (laborer/servant/burakumin/merchant) outside the wall. Only samurai country estates, farmhouses, the wharf SHOPS, and gate-market shops are legitimately extramural (the guan-xiang/wharf/villa/farm categories); a lone commoner house outside defeats the wall and has no economic anchor. `population_consistent_with_housing` also counts IN-WALL dwellings only, so a generator cannot hit its target by spilling.
  - `city_civic_quarter_not_mostly_open` - a `civic` quarter may be up to `CIVIC_OPEN_TOL` = 70% open (a yamen/temple precinct is majority courtyard/garden) but must actually HOLD its compounds; open-and-structureless reads as merely empty.
  - `city_reserve_within_cap` - total `reserve` ground <= `RESERVE_CAP_FRAC` = 20% of the interior. Civic buildings alone are only ~3-6% of a Chinese county seat; the big open consumer is the drill ground plus under-built garden/farm, and ~20% (drill ground + gardens + an agricultural district) is historically conservative. Beyond it the wall encloses more open ground than a provincial seat justifies -> shrink.
- **The reworked verdict** (`city_capacity`, surfaced by `city_wall_sized_to_population` + `--capacity`): `enlarge` (can't hold target even packed), `shrink` (far more room than needed, OR only fillable via over-cap reserve - emptiness cannot be laundered as reserve), `densify` (wall right, placed below the population floor), `sized_and_packed` (done). `placed` counts in-wall only; `--capacity` prints the per-quarter density table so you see WHICH quarter is under-built. The `densify` boundary tracks `population_tol` so the capacity verdict and the population check never disagree.
- **Anti-trap discipline:** the pre-feature broken Nagahara is pinned as `pool/regressions/city_density_broken_nagahara.json` and MUST keep firing the new checks (a known-bad map that has to fail - the discipline the earlier aggregate model skipped). The band was calibrated against BOTH Tango (good, must pass) and that fixture (bad, must fail).

**Worked examples:** *Tango* declares 4 wedges (NE laborer residential; SE government mixed; SW merchant+temple mixed; NW split into an agricultural-district reserve above + a burakumin residential quarter below) and, per the GM's call, was enlarged a hair (uniform x1.015, RX/RY 480/450 -> 487/457) so its honest inside figure meets 3,000 rather than trimming the declared population - the earlier "hugged" ring had been faking ~12 dwellings past the wall. *Nagahara* declares 4 mixed/residential wedges; its formerly-empty NW temple quarter is filled by a MONZEN-MACHI commoner pocket (temple-town housing - historically a temple neighborhood was dense), which fixed both the emptiness and the population without enlarging the wall. Both cities: zero extramural commoners, every residential quarter in band, `sized_and_packed`.

  - *Known WIP (none).* Both worked cities (Tango, Nagahara) pass clean, per-quarter. Capitals are the next tier (a later task).

### Budget-first wall sizing (feature 009, `citybudget.py`)

Every settlement mode grows from a first principle - villages from water flow, manors from a declared square footage - and the city's is the **space budget**. The old order (guess a wall, place everything, iterate on the `city_capacity` verdict) converged on maps the checks accepted but the GM did not: the shipped Nagahara read fully green while ~17% of its interior (~116k px^2, five pockets >= 35k px^2) was unaccounted open ground, because every aggregate measure treats open ground as "fillable" rather than "unjustified". So the order is inverted: **compute the budget FIRST, derive the wall from it, and hold the drawn map to the promise.**

**The workflow** (see `specs/009-city-area-budget/` for the full feature):

1. `budget = plan_city(CityProgram(population=..., river=..., agricultural_district=..., aspect=..., nring=...), canvas=(W, H))` - BEFORE anything is drawn. Audit it with `python3 citybudget.py --plan --population 3000 --river`.
2. Take the wall from `budget.wall.rx/.ry` (never hand-pick RX/RY) and record the promise: `s.meta(budget=budget_to_manifest(budget))`.
3. Render order as ever (river -> walls -> roads -> ring -> ... -> packs), with the budget's `dwelling_target` feeding the same population/caste floors as always.
4. `city_wall_matches_budget` (walled-city gate) compares the wall's enclosed area against `budget.required_interior_px2`: over +8% = the empty-space defect (shrink, or declare+draw the ground as an itemized line); under -5% = the program cannot fit (enlarge, or trim the program). `city_capacity` must read `sized_and_packed` on the FIRST derivation - if it wants a resize, the budget model (not the wall) is what needs fixing.

**The model**: `required interior = (packed + spaced + civic + water + extras) / (1 - circulation% - agri%)`, then the wall is the ellipse **N-gon** enclosing exactly that (`0.5*N*sin(2pi/N)*rx*ry` - the drawn 20-22-gon's polygon area, NOT the smooth ellipse's `pi*rx*ry`, which would systematically undersize every wall).

**Historical grounding / the why behind each constant** (research: `specs/009-city-area-budget/research.md`; China first, jokamachi tiebreaker):

- **Inventory** from the budgets.md Provincial city caste table (600 families at pop 3,000: servants 120 / laborers 240 / merchants 150 / burakumin 30 / samurai 60; zero farmers unless an agricultural district overrides). ~2/3 of samurai families live in-wall (measured: Tango 33, Nagahara 41 of 60; the rest hold extramural estates per the estate doctrine).
- **Gross ground costs at DRAWN footprints** (legibility floors included - budgeting real-world square footage would undersize the wall): packed row dwelling ~690 px^2 gross (Tango healthy-quarter range 448-822), samurai house ~2,480 px^2 gross (measured samurai-ward ~2,915; the pair solves Tango's back-prediction to +0.2% while keeping the measured ~3.6x packed:spaced ratio).
- **Civic program is a FLOOR, not per-capita** - a pop-2,000 seat still carries the yamen, 6 ministries, temples, theater, gate furniture. Itemized at Tango's measured compounds (~64k px^2 total ~ 9% of interior; the historical civic share of a county seat is ~10%, range 5-15% - yamen 3-5% + temples/school 3-5% + granary/drill 2-4%, per Chang in Skinner's *The City in Late Imperial China*). The program also carries a small **adept-monk housing** line (5 x C_PACKED; GM 2026-07-24): 2-3 ordinary homes per temple precinct for the married adepts among its monks, real households OUTSIDE the lay caste table's 600 families (see "City temples" above).
- **Circulation 7% of interior at drawn widths** (measured 6.8-7.0% on both shipped cities). The historical envelope is 10-20% of ground (Chinese county seats ran deliberately sparse nets - Pingyao's "4 big streets, 8 small streets, 72 lanes"; Edo machi-wari grids compute to 13-18%) - but no source states the Chinese figure outright, so it is a triangulated envelope, and the MAP-calibrated constant wins because the budget must price what the maps actually draw (deep blocks + alley warrens sit at the sparse end).
- **Deliberate open ground must be DECLARED and DRAWN, never ambient.** The Chinese norm was 25-30% of the enclosure unbuilt (walls sized to administrative rank and growth expectation; intramural fields/ponds as siege insurance and flood refuge - Quanzhou still 1/4 vacant in 1945, Suzhou enclosed farmland into the Republican era). On a diagram that roominess reads as emptiness unless drawn as its kind, so the budget credits open ground only as itemized lines: the **agricultural-district toggle** (+15% of interior, Tango's measured declared reserve; inside the feature-006 20% reserve cap) or explicit `extras` lines (drill ground, gardens). A future city that wants the roomier historical character opts in legibly; it can never launder slack.
- **Tolerances (+8%/-5%)** are pinned by the two anchors: shipped Tango encloses ~0.25% under its budget (must pass); the pre-feature Nagahara encloses ~+21% (must fail - pinned at `pool/regressions/city_budget_fires_on_the_too_empty_nagahara.json`). Under is tighter than over because an undersized wall breaks packing immediately.

**Calibration provenance**: constants measured 2026-07 from `pool/provincial-cities/tango.json` (GM-accepted) and the pre-feature `pool/provincial-cities/nagahara.json` (GM-rejected); the back-prediction test in `test_citybudget.py` re-derives Tango's shipped 487x457 ring to +0.1% and is the regression guard on the whole constant set.
