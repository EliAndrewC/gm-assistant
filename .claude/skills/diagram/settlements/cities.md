# The provincial-city tier (`meta(scale="city")`)

*Part of the Mode B settlement docs. The index, the `meta()` knobs, the workflow and the validator contract live in [`../settlements.md`](../settlements.md). The feature vocabulary a city SHARES with a county town - notice board, justice works, trade works, tanning yards, stable yards, wells, drum tower, caste zoning - is in [`urban-features.md`](urban-features.md).*

**Load this file when:** the subject is a provincial city. **This file is the INDEX for the tier** - what a city IS (population, caste mix, framing) and what the gate demands of one. The program itself is split by subject under [`cities/`](cities/); load the pieces the map actually calls for.

## ASK THESE THREE BEFORE DRAWING ANYTHING

A city cannot be laid out until the GM has answered three questions, and none of them can be
guessed from the brief. Ask all three at once, at intake:

1. **Which way does the water flow?** Everything downstream of it: which end of the river the
   wharf takes, which way drainage ditches discharge, which way a feeder is tapped so the current
   turns INTO it, where the nuisance trades stand relative to the drinking water.
2. **Which clan holds it?** The clan's two patron Fortunes get the sovereign temples, and one of
   the two is drawn larger than every other temple on the map.
3. **Which direction is the Imperial capital?** (GM 2026-08-11, catching Shiro Daika's country
   seats on the wrong flank.) **The samurai country estates go on the side of the city that faces
   the capital** - that is where a clan's senior houses want their land, on the road their lord
   and their peers travel, and the answer is not derivable from the map's own geography. It is the
   same class of question as the other two: a single fact from the GM that fixes a whole family of
   placements.

Record the answer in `meta` so the checks can hold the map to it, rather than leaving it in a
comment: the water flow as `down_deg` / channel anchors, the clan on the temples, and the capital's
bearing as `capital_dir`.

## A CITY IS RINGED BY ITS FARMLAND - ALWAYS

**GM, 2026-08-11:** *"The city should be surrounded by farmland. Cities grow up around fertile
land, so we should do as we do for Tango and Nagahara and Minami and keep adding rice paddies and
farmhouses until there are no more places to put them. Farmland ringing a city is the DEFAULT which
should always happen in the future."*

This was already the pool's practice before it was written down - Tango draws 11 fields, Nagahara
7, Minami 6 - and the capital's first pass drew ONE, which is what prompted the ruling. A city is
not a thing set down on a plain: it is the market and the garrison of the land that feeds it, and a
single token field on one flank draws that relationship backwards.

`city_is_ringed_by_farmland` holds the floor: **4+ fields across 3+ flanks**. Fewer means the ring
was not attempted, not that the ground ran out. Keep adding until the ground genuinely refuses.

**Each field is a whole water chain, not a polygon.** The lesson from Shiro Daika's ring, where the
first cut of every field failed a different water rule: a field needs a tap ON the water (derive it
from the watercourse's own polyline - every hand-picked tap in the first cut stood on dry ground), a
head-race that BENDS rather than ruling straight, sluice gates at both ends, a moat offtake SWEPT
downstream (`moat_swept_tap` - a square tap sheds sediment into its own mouth), the source and the
drain both DECLARED with `topo_channel`, the drain carried clear OFF the sheet rather than stopping
on-canvas, and the farmsteads that work it. Two numbers that cost the most iterations:

- **The dry hem is what QUILTS the fan head.** Too thin (or absent) and `city_fan_heads_quilted`
  fires on bare parchment along the supply canals; `dry_band=(0, 0)` crashes the comb outright. The
  pool's 3 ft/px value is `(47, 88)` - use it.
- **Farmsteads go on the UPSLOPE flanks, above the drainage line.** The ground below the drain is
  the wettest in the valley and nobody builds there (`dwellings_above_field_drain`). They must also
  sit IN VIEW: the density rule counts only houses inside the cropped frame, and `crop_city` clips
  a fringe paddy at the sheet edge by design.

## What a provincial city is

A provincial city is the next tier up from a county town - a provincial governor's seat, ~3,000 inside the walls. Like towns, some are walled and some unwalled; the worked example **Tango** (`pool/provincial-cities/tango.gen.py`) is walled. The drawing vocabulary adds a few city-only methods; the gate adds a city block of checks. (Since the budget-first wall sizing of feature 009, dwelling counts are a near-census: the budget targets the full ~600 households and the gate holds the map to its floor - see Scale and density above.)

- **Provincial city (`meta(scale="city")`):** **~2,000-4,000 inside the walls, average ~3,000 / 600 households** (per `budgets.md`): ~120 servant, ~240 laborer, ~150 merchant, ~30 burakumin, ~60 samurai households - and, structurally, **ZERO in-city farmers** (the farmers who work the city's land live in the surrounding villages/hamlets, counted in other totals). The samurai run ~10% (the governor R8, six provincial ministers R7 + their R6 deputies, R3-5 clerks, bushi) plus a small **foreign** Imperial yoriki sub-station auditing gate tariffs. A city map **places these households densely - near the full ~600** (Tango runs ~600 buildings) - on a crossing street grid, with a smaller building grain (`s.bscale` ~0.5) so the big merchant/samurai footprints fit. The gate checks the required **structures + neighborhood presence + the street network**, not an exact per-caste headcount, but the neighborhoods are sized to the real numbers (~240-310 laborer, ~120-150 merchant, ~50-60 samurai, ~30-60 burakumin). Domain capitals (~12,360) have their own numbers and are a **separate tier, designed but not yet implemented** - see [`capitals.md`](capitals.md), which inherits this whole file and overrides it.
- **A city's CASTE MIX must match budgets.md, not just the total** (`city_caste_counts_in_band`). The total-population check alone lets the mix silently DRIFT: the classic failure is one quarter's pack (the laborers) absorbing everyone else's slots while **servants get starved to near-zero because they were appended to the END of a pack list** (`["laborer"] * 445 + ["servant"] * 50` places 445 laborers and then has no room left for the servants). A provincial city is roughly **~40% laborer / 20% servant / 25% merchant / 10% samurai / 5% burakumin** of its ~600 households, and each caste is held within **+/-30%** of that target. Two rules for hitting it: (1) **INTERLEAVE servants** into the laborer and merchant packs (`(["laborer"] * 3 + ["servant"]) * N`), since they live among the households they serve - never tack them on the end. (2) The samurai and merchant **walled ESTATES are households too** - the check counts `M["manors"]` (extramural samurai estates, who commute in) toward samurai and `M["merchant_estates"]` toward merchants, so the in-wall *houses* need only make up the remainder (which is why `city_samurai_housing_sufficient` wants ~39 in-wall samurai houses, not the full ~60). Space-bound quarters (the SE samurai quarter is crowded by the government compounds) lean on their estates to reach band.

## Framing

- **A city map crops TIGHT to the walls** (`s.set_view(ox, oy, w, h)` directly, rather than `crop_to_content` - a city is framed to its WALL, not its scattered outside estates). A town map is as much about its surroundings as the town; a **city** map is about the city - the countryside around it matters little, so the rendered window should *just barely encompass the walled city* (wall + moat + a ~50px margin for the title), zooming the city itself as large as the canvas allows. Compute it from the wall: `s.set_view(CX-RX-52-M, CY-RY-52-M, 2*(RX+52+M), 2*(RY+52+M))` (52 ≈ moat gap + wall stroke, `M` ≈ 50). Placement still uses the full coordinate space, so the estates, outside farmland, and the far rows of the gate market simply **run off the cropped edge** - they need not be fully rendered. Crucially, the **checks read `meta["view"]` and treat the crop, not the full canvas, as the map edge**: "runs off the edge" (road, moat-feeder stream, off-map fields) means off the *view*. So size the view to clear the moat but let the estates fall outside it. The title sits top-left *inside* the view (it follows automatically). The off-wall estates remain recorded (`s.manor`), so the estate checks (1-3 shown, dispersed, SE, no-overlap) still pass even though most estates are off-map entirely.

## What the gate demands of a city

The city-scale block of the validator, enumerated. Each requirement's reasoning and generator surface is in the subject file above.

- **City-scale (`meta.scale=="city"`):** the interior carries the provincial government - the governor's mansion (`city_has_governor_mansion`), the six ministry offices one of which is Rites (`city_has_six_ministries`, `city_has_ministry_of_rites`), and a samurai neighborhood sized to the ~10% samurai cohort with many houses fronting its streets (`city_has_samurai_neighborhood`, `city_samurai_housing_sufficient`, `city_samurai_partly_front_streets`); a merchant district (its housing varied by wealth band - walled estates, large and small houses - and spread out, unlike the dense uniform laborers; the estate courts clear of the wall/moat and of any building, and each estate gate opening onto open ground not into a compound) and laborer neighborhoods are present (`city_has_merchant_district`, `city_merchant_housing_varied`, `city_merchant_housing_spread`, `city_merchant_estates_clear_of_wall_moat`, `city_merchant_estates_clear_of_buildings`, `city_merchant_estate_gate_clear`, `city_has_laborer_neighborhoods`, `city_laborer_housing_varied`); the population is dwellings x5 not total buildings (and its caste MIX - ~40% laborer / 20% servant / 25% merchant / 10% samurai / 5% burakumin - is within +/-30% of budgets.md, servants interleaved not starved), shops front the streets, and poor housing fills the interior (`population_consistent_with_housing`, `city_caste_counts_in_band`, `businesses_front_streets`, `poor_housing_mostly_interior`); a city with an Imperial road running through it (walled or not) lines that road with a commercial ribbon of shops/inns (`city_imperial_road_has_commerce`); extensive farmland runs off the edge outside the walls (`city_has_outside_farmland`); religious buildings are temples (`religious_matches_scale`); the commoner warren is dotted with public wells, each in a block interior off the lanes, enough that no neighborhood lacks water access AND none is over-burdened (~1 per 10-20 households), while the samurai/government quarter has none (its water is private, inside the compounds) (`city_neighborhoods_have_wells`, `city_well_density_sufficient`, `city_wells_in_block_interiors`, `city_samurai_quarter_has_no_public_wells`); and the civic amenities ported from the town tier are present - merchant-house kura, a flophouse, a theater stage (`city_has_merchant_storehouses`, `city_has_flophouse`, `city_has_theater_stage`); fire defense ports up too - >= 2 fire-watch towers over the commoner quarters (`city_has_fire_towers`, `fire_tower_in_commoner_quarter`). **Walled (`meta.walled`):** a closed wall + >= 2 gates (`walled_city_has_wall_and_gates`); a guard house + attached inspection station + guard tower at each gate (the guard house + inspection station rotated square to the wall/ring road, like the towers), plus guard towers spaced around the whole rampart and rotated square to the wall - every tower NUDGED INWARD so only ~8px of its outer face projects past the wall centerline (the horse-face bastion's stride: a tower centered on the wall line would stand its footing in a close-set moat's bed; the gateposts likewise stop ~5px past the wall line on the moat side, and are recorded as `gate_structs` kind="gatepost" so the gate sees them), all gated by `city_wall_furniture_clear_of_moat` (wall towers + gate furniture footprints clear of the moat bed; bridges exempt - they span it by design). **And the ward gates keep their ground against the towers** (`kido_clear_of_wall_towers`): a kido hangs where a lane or the ring road crosses the ward fence - a FIXED crossing - and the fence ends abut the rampart, so the last kido can land against a mural tower's footprint (its guard box reads as a stray "small square building" inside the tower; both classes are overlap-exempt, so no generic pass catches the pair). The TOWER yields: pass the kido spots as `city_wall(..., tower_skip=[(x, y), ...])` - a mural tower whose vertex falls within ~62px **SLIDES a short way along the wall** (growing arcs, both directions, shortest clear slide wins - a full vertex jump left a ~360px bare stretch of rampart, a defensive hole on the attack side; dropped entirely only when boxed in both ways, which the 75-deg spacing check tolerates once), and a GATE tower walks further along the wall until clear. **The kido's own ground must be RESERVED before any pack runs** (`kido_clear_of_buildings`): the gates hold fixed crossings but `s.ward` draws them near the END of the gen, long after the quarters are built - so the gen block_polys each kido spot up front, via `s.kido_reservation(x, y, fence)`, which asks the engine for the seat `s.ward` will actually take rather than guessing a rect (the glyph is not symmetric and its angle follows the lane it bars). Note the ordering trap both rules solve: `city_wall` and the packs run long before `s.ward`, so neither can see the kido - the gen must declare the spots once, up front, and feed them to both; and the reservation must come after the ring road is drawn, since that is the lane two of the gates square to. **And the gate's watch box keeps out of the roadbed** (`kido_guard_box_clear_of_lanes`): the bar spans the lane, the box beside it does not stand in it. **The same distinction governs the FENCE** (GM 2026-07-27, "ward gates seem to sometimes overlap with neighborhood walls"): the GATEWAY - roof and posts - stands on the ward line, because the gate IS the opening in it, but the GUARD BOX is a small building on the verge and must stand clear (it may sit hard against the fence; it may not be cut by it). Perpendicular crossings were always fine - the box sits along the lane, off the fence line - and it is the OBLIQUE crossings that cut it, which is why 2 of the pool's 14 gates were wrong and neither showed: `kido` records no `w`/`h`, so `matrix_extents` never extracted the gate AT ALL, and the whole glyph rode the gateway's `{WAY, wards}` fixture mount. Both halves are fixed at the root rather than per-map - the extractor now reads the gate's recorded `parts` (each drawn rect's rotated corner quad - the ink, not the `bbox`, which at 45 degrees claims ~2x the ground) and splits the guard box out as `kido_guard_box`, classed SOLID, so the matrix forbids it against the fence, the roadbed and everything built; and `_kido_rects` seats the box clear of its own fence, SAT-tested rather than by corner distances (a line through a 15x16 box's middle leaves every corner ~8 px clear). - plus guard towers spaced for INTERLOCKING flanking fire at a density set by the city's DEFENSE TIER (`meta(wall_defense="siege"|"garrison"|"peaceful")`) - `>= 2` towers cover every curtain point within the tier's arrow range (siege = aimed-lethal 197 ft, garrison = full war-bow 328 ft, peaceful = `>= 1` within 197 ft), the gate barbican and water-gate openings exempt (`city_wall_tower_coverage`; `city_wall_towers_spaced` remains the coarse floor); and a ring road in the wall-clear patrol zone just inside it, kept clear of buildings/civic compounds/fields (only gate guard houses, inspection stations, towers and gated ward fences may sit on it), which the streets meet cleanly (reaching its bed, neither short nor poking past) - as they do the Imperial road (`city_inspection_station_at_each_gate`, `city_gate_has_guardhouse`, `city_gate_furniture_aligned`, `city_wall_towers_spaced`, `city_wall_tower_coverage`, `city_wall_towers_aligned`, `city_has_ring_road`, `ring_road_kept_clear`, `city_streets_meet_through_lanes`); >= 1 burakumin neighborhood inside the walls (`walled_city_has_burakumin_inside`); the samurai/government quarter sealed off the commoner streets by a kido-gated ward fence whose ends meet SOLID wall (no walk-around gap, clear of gate openings), JOIN it rather than crossing it (the fence ENDS at the rampart - no palisade ink sticking out the far side into the berm, the wall member of the same join-not-intersect family as the lanes and the watercourses) and run UNDER the rampart, with commoner lanes that approach the fence reaching it and ending at a kido gate, and with ONLY samurai households, their live-in servants and government ground inside the fence - commoner dwellings and commerce are barred; a temple standing in the ward keeps its clergy row (`city_samurai_quarter_gated`, `city_samurai_ward_sealed`, `city_samurai_ward_residents_only`, `city_ward_fence_meets_wall`, `city_ward_fence_joins_wall_not_crosses`, `city_ward_fence_under_wall`, `city_ward_cap_flush_to_wall`, `city_lanes_reach_ward_gates`); 1-3 dispersed walled samurai estates of varying size toward the capital (SE), spread >= 200px apart, not overlapping each other or the wall/moat (`city_samurai_estates_outside`, `city_samurai_estates_dispersed`, `city_samurai_estates_vary_in_size`, `city_estates_toward_capital`, `city_estates_no_overlap`, `city_estates_clear_of_wall_moat`); the governor's mansion is the grandest compound and the ministries cluster around it and front its avenues, each office standing clear of its neighbors (not abutting) (`city_governor_mansion_large`, `city_ministries_cluster_at_government`, `city_ministries_front_a_street`, `city_government_offices_dont_abut`); all temples inside the walls and clear of the wall/moat, with a torii in front of any a street runs up to, and torii drawn OVER the streets they span (`city_temples_inside_walls`, `city_temples_clear_of_wall_moat`, `city_temple_approach_has_torii`, `city_torii_over_streets`); the governor's mansion + ministries clear of the wall/moat (`city_government_clear_of_wall_moat`); the farm fields and the in-wall pond clear of the wall/moat (`city_fields_clear_of_wall_moat`, `city_pond_clear_of_wall_moat`);
