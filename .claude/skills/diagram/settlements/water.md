# Water: flow, sources, channels, moats, marshes

*Part of the Mode B settlement docs. The index, the `meta()` knobs, the workflow and the validator contract live in [`../settlements.md`](../settlements.md).*

**Load this file when:** the map has ANY water on it - which is nearly always. Settle the drainage bearing BEFORE placing a single feature; everything downstream depends on it.

**Research:** the historical basis for these rules - what was found, the decision it drove, and every disclosed departure - is in [`../research/water.md`](../research/water.md). Load it when you are CHANGING a rule or questioning one, not to follow it.

---

- Villages **must** have a water source, but its KIND is village-specific (regional). Historically not every paddy village had a pond: a reservoir **pond** (*tameike*, never "tank") suits a dry/rain-shadow locale (Kikuta); a **stream diversion** from off-map feeding the main field, which then feeds the smaller fields field-to-field, suits a river valley (Hikari). A pond, if used, must be larger than a house, on flat ground, set back from steep hills (erosion). Channels route AROUND buildings (never through the headman's house), take a near-direct path, **wind gently** (not dead straight), and must be **anchored at both ends** - each end starts/ends inside its source (pond / off-map edge / another field) and well inside its target field, so the field paints over the end (a visible connection, not a line stopping in the gap). Anchors are declared per channel as `frm`/`to` in the manifest. Channels are **no-build corridors**: the generator reserves ~33px around each, which is enough to keep a farmhouse's whole **footprint** off it (its half-diagonal is ~26px), not merely its center - and `no_structure_on_channel` gates the rendered map with the *same rotated-rect footprint test the streams use* (a corner clipping a channel fails it, not just a center on it). The earlier center-only guard (`houses_off_corridors`, still kept for the lane) let corners slip through - that is how Hikari once shipped farmhouses sitting on its field-to-field channels.
- **A source brook is DIVERTED into the head-race, it does not run over the field (Ikegami).** A river-valley settlement whose water comes from an off-map **brook** (rather than a pond) taps it at the **field head**: the brook flows in from the map edge, reaches the sluice, and there **BECOMES the irrigation channel** - it hands off to the comb and stops. It must NOT continue on over the paddies, and it must NOT run parallel to the head-race (two overlapping water lines reads as a mistake - the GM's exact catch on the first Ikegami draft). So the brook is recorded as a stream `frm={"kind":"offmap"}` with **no `to`**, ending AT the sluice; the field's water is then the head-race ditch below it, plus an **undrawn hairline anchor channel** `frm={"kind":"stream"}, to={"kind":"field"}` from the sluice winding into the paddy interior (the same undrawn-topology trick as Hoshigaoka's `frm:pond` feed anchor at line ~300 of its gen). That anchor gives `fields_show_water_source` its channel and `field_ditches_reach_source_and_sink` its external source, while the visible water stays the brook + head-race with no overlap. The `stream_runs_off_edge` check accepts this via the **`at_ditch` allowance**: a brook may END within ~22px of an irrigation ditch/channel (diverted into it), not only at the map edge / pond / moat / drain-outfall / field head. Teeth: `test_stream_diverted_into_a_channel_passes_and_open_ended_brook_fires` (in `tests/check_village/`) (a diverted brook passes; the same brook ending in open ground fires).
- **A DRAINAGE pond sits BELOW the field, the mirror of a source pond (Ikegami, `meta(pond_role="drainage")`).** The usual *tameike* is a SOURCE at the valley head, above the paddies (default `pond_role="source"`). A drainage pond is the opposite: a reservoir at the field's low foot that CATCHES the runoff, so the field's **drain** must actually reach it (`pond_connected_to_field`: a source pond is fed by a channel that touches it; a drainage pond is reached by the drain - this caught the disconnected-drain bug where the drain stopped short of the pond). Either way the pond sits **clear of the field envelope** (`pond_clear_of_field`: no field vertex inside the pond, no pond-rim point inside a field - a pond is a distinct water body beside/below the crop, joined by a channel, never laid over it). The role flips grounding direction in `field_ditches_reach_source_and_sink`: for a drainage pond the pond is a SINK, not a source.
- **Water flows downhill.** Streams and channels follow the land's slope. If a map declares its slope with **`meta(downhill=<dir>)`** (a cardinal name like `"south"`, or a `[dx,dy]` vector in map coords where `+y` is south), every channel must run *with* it - its source (the tap on the stream/pond, `poly[0]`) sits **uphill** of where it feeds the field (`poly[-1]`), gated by `channels_flow_downhill`. So when a north-flowing stream feeds a field to its east, tap the stream **upstream/north** of the field and angle the channel down to it; a channel run flat or angled back uphill would carry the water *away* from the field. Hirameki's hill is in the north, so it sets `downhill="south"`. Maps without the tag are exempt (slope unknown) - tag a map when its elevation gradient is clear (a hill, a falling valley).
- **Where WATERCOURSES meet, they MERGE like a confluence** (`waterways_merge_at_crossings`, universal) - they must not stack opacity into a dark seam where they cross. Water is semi-transparent, so two overlapping strokes drawn independently double up to a darker patch at the junction (the old bug). The fix mirrors the road crossroads: `s.stream` / `s.channel` / `s.moat` / **`s.pond`** / **`s.field_channel`** (the comb-net irrigation channels) all defer through a shared **`_water`** block that renders, bottom-to-top, **all the pond RIMS (edges - the only water feature with a border), then all the beds (the blue water bodies, one color) inside a single opacity group, then all the lighter sheens in another group above** - so overlapping beds composite as one continuous body of water (an X-crossing or a Y feeder-into-moat reads as a real confluence), and the check enforces it by recording each course's bed/sheen draw position (`bedz` / `sheenz`) and firing if any course's opaque bed is drawn *over* another's sheen at a crossing or feeder junction. Just route every watercourse through those methods (don't hand-draw a water `<path>` with `s.add`) and it holds automatically.
- **A pond JOINS its feeders at the rim, the same way** (the "`_| |_` not `_|_|_`" rule the streets already follow). The pond is not a standalone ellipse painted over its inflows; it goes through the SAME `_water` block, with its **rim in the bottom EDGE layer (below every bed)** and its **fill in the shared bed group as the TOPMOST bed** (`pond_fill=True`). So a feeder brook's or irrigation channel's bed, sitting in that bed group above the rim, **covers the rim where they meet** - leaving a clean gap in the shore exactly at the mouth, so the water reads as continuous (channel water flowing into pond water) instead of the rim drawing a hard border across the junction; and because the fill is drawn LAST among the beds, it paints over any feeder's small **inside-the-rim overshoot** - an irrigation channel's round end-cap bulging a few px past the rim, which was showing as a darker nub on the open water when the channel was drawn *after* the pond (a feeder drawn *before* it is hidden by the fill anyway). So the open water stays clean whatever the draw order. The other half of the trick is that a feeder must **stop AT the rim, not draw across the open pond**: `_clip_to_pond` snaps a pond-anchored course's endpoint onto the rim - trimming a run that lies *inside* the pond (a channel whose sluice sits within the water) or extending one that sits *just outside* (the sluice foot), for both the bed and the sheen. This is **deferred to flush time** (a course carries a `clip` spec through `_water`), so it works even when the feeder is drawn BEFORE the pond in the gen (the pond geometry, `M["pond"]`, is not yet known at call time). A course is treated as a feeder when its `frm`/`to` anchor has `kind=="pond"` (`_pond_anchored`) - so declaring the anchors (which the connectivity checks already require) also drives the clean join, no extra call. The visible result: on Ueda / Hoshigaoka / Kikuta / Moritono the inflow brook merges seamlessly into the pond and the supply canals branch out of its downhill foot, none of them laying a colored stripe across the open water.  **The LATE-block wrinkle** (GM 2026-07-23, Tango's in-wall tank; gated by `pond_fill_covers_channel_mouths`): the comb ditch nets render in the LATE water block, which draws after the whole shared block - so when a `late=True` channel joins the pond, the shared block's fill-topmost trick cannot cover its mouth, and the head-race's round end-cap rode ON TOP of the open water (its `#6C9CBE` supply color visibly mismatched against the `#9CB4C8` fill), reading as the channel INTERSECTING the pond.  The engine now RELOCATES the pond's fill + sheen into the late block (re-emitted last among the late beds) whenever a late stroke's clipped endpoint lands in the rim zone; the rim EDGE stays early, below every bed, so the mouth still covers it - the covering order of the shared block, reproduced one block later.  The check reads two new manifest records: `M["drawn_channels"]` (every `field_channel` stroke's post-clip geometry + `bedz` + `late` flag) and `M["pond_layer"]` (the fill's `bedz` + which block it landed in).  `bedz` values are offsets within their OWN splice block, NOT globally comparable (a late bed's raw z can be numerically smaller than an early fill's while drawing after it - the broken-engine shape the fixture pins), so draw order across blocks is the `(late, bedz)` pair compared lexicographically.  A `channels` record with no `bedz` is a gen-side topology entry (its visible stroke lives in `drawn_channels`) and is exempt, as are `drawn: false` underground conduits; a joining stroke that runs THROUGH the open water mid-run (interior vertex deep inside the rim) fires the same check - mouths, not crossings, the pond sibling of `channels_join_water_not_cross`.
- **Water sources for city fans - three honest patterns.** (1) **Moat-fed** (`{"kind": "moat"}` channel anchor): the sluice a stride outside the rim, tap picked UPSTREAM of the sluice when the moat has a current (`moat_channels_flow_with_current`). (2) **Off-map source, moat as storm drain** (`{"kind": "offmap"}` source + `{"kind": "drain"}->{"kind": "moat"}`): a north-band fan falling toward the city whose collector EMPTIES INTO the moat (Tango fn1/fn2, Nagahara fnn2). (3) **Tail-water reuse** (`{"kind": "stream"}` or `{"kind": "river"}` on the OUTFALL): fans below the city tap the moat's outfall stream - a city's drainage watered the fields downstream, the historically standard arrangement (Tango fse1/fs3); Nagahara's far-bank fan taps the Hayakawa directly (the `"river"` channel-anchor kind, added for it). Per-comb `dry_keepout` circles hold a fan's hem quilt off estates and other structures placed before it. **Mouths are CONFLUENCES, never crossings** (GM 2026-07-23: "they just run into the moat... and aren't intersecting at the edge"): a drawn tap/culvert ends at the receiving water's BANK (the engine's `_clip_to_moat`/`_clip_to_river` trim to the bed edge MINUS the stroke's cap radius - a round linecap inks past the endpoint, which is how a 7px tap once plunged to mid-moat), and it wears the receiving water's OWN color (`#9CB4C8` - the tap carries moat/river water; the color change to the comb's canal palette happens at the sluice, where a transition reads naturally). The recorded topology still ends ON the centerline for the anchor checks; `channels_join_water_not_cross` is the ratchet - no channel/ditch segment may strictly cross a moat/river centerline mid-run. **And every water-to-water HANDOFF shows its control gate** (`s.sluice_gate(x, y, rot=heading+90)`, gated by `channel_gates_at_water_junctions`): where the water-colored tap hands off to the comb's own canal (the sluice - the palette seam sits exactly there) and where a drawn drain hands off to its outfall culvert, a small timber board-gate glyph marks the junction - the control structure the "sluice-fed head-race" doctrine always implied but no map drew; it is what makes the color/direction change read as engineered plumbing rather than two strokes crossing (GM 2026-07-23, the junction-seams pass). An UNDROWN drain record (`drawn: false`) is an implied underground conduit (Tango's in-wall nw1 drain beneath the rampart) - no visible seam at the MOAT side, no gate there.  **An IN-WALL drain cuts off at a gate before the ring road** (`s.inwall_drain_outfall(drain_pts, moat_bias)`, gated by `inwall_drains_gated_at_cutoff`; GM 2026-07-23, generalizing the nw1 handoff into a rule for ANY drainage ditch inside a city wall): the visible runoff ditch never reaches road, rampart or moat - it is TRIMMED back to half the ring-road width + 10px clear of the ring-road centerline and drops through a **sluice-gate glyph at the cut end** into the underground stone culvert (the undrawn conduit records the drain->moat topology; the culvert's connection to the moat is real but invisible, and the CONTROL GATE at the drop is the one visible piece of the plumbing - without it the trimmed ditch reads as unfinished linework, which is exactly what the pre-fix Tango showed: the ditch lapped 3px past the patrol road's centerline and simply stopped, bare).  The helper trims the drain polyline BEFORE it is drawn or recorded (assign the return value back onto the net's drain channel - `comb_field(..., inwall_drain_bias=...)` in the Tango gen, `draw_comb_field(..., inwall_drain_moat_bias=...)` on the shared path), so the drawn ditch, the `field_ditches` record, and the conduit all share the same cut geometry - placement and check reading the same source, per the standing engine rule.  `moat_bias` aims the conduit's far end: the moat vertex nearest cut+bias (nw1 uses (-40, 30), W-SW, so the implied culvert runs downhill with the moat's current).  The check: every undrawn drain->moat conduit starting inside the wall needs a drawn stroke ending at its start (within 8px), the cut clear of the ring-road centerline by half-width + 4px with no touching stroke crossing it, and a sluice_gate within 16px (fixture `inwall_drains_gated_at_cutoff_fires_on_tangos_bare_outfall`).
- **Streams stay between fields, never through them** (`streams_avoid_fields`): run a stream parallel to the road between field pairs. **Every fully-on-map field must SHOW a water source** (`fields_show_water_source`) - a channel ending inside it, or the field directly abutting a stream/pond. Being merely *near* water with no visible connection does not count, so tap the stream with a channel (`s.channel(stream_pt, field_pt, {"kind":"stream"}, {"kind":"field","name":...})`). Fields that run off the map edge are exempt (their water may be off-map too).
- **DRAINAGE SLOPE IS PER-FIELD, NOT PER-MAP** (drives the `down_deg` on each field record, the `trimmed` flag on a ditch, and the three checks `drain_flows_downhill`, `drain_runs_cross_slope`, `dwellings_above_field_drain`; GM 2026-07-25).
  - *The fix.* `build_comb` now returns the fall it carved to, each field record carries its own `down_deg`, and every drain is judged against ITS OWN field. `field_ditches` already recorded a `field` name, so the attribution was free. Maps with a map-level `down_deg` and no per-field slopes fall back to it, and their behavior is provably unchanged - all 15 non-city maps produce byte-identical check-by-check output before and after.
  - *A MIRRORED fan must stamp the fall of the DRAWN geometry.* Tango negates `down_deg` before `build_comb` and mirrors the points back afterward, and mirroring about a horizontal axis negates a direction - so the two cancel and the drawn fall is the CALLER's original value. Stamping the negated working value put nw1 90 degrees out. Any future mirrored fan must stamp the pre-negation figure.
  - *A TRIMMED in-wall drain is not a contour collector.* An in-wall fan's drain is cut off short of the patrol ring and sluice-gated into an underground conduit to the moat, so what survives is the last leg to that outfall - Tango's nw1 is 2 points and 134 px where every untrimmed drain on the map runs 159-231 px. Judging its bearing against "must run along the contour" is a category error, so the trim records `trimmed: true` on the ditch and `drain_runs_cross_slope` skips it.
  - *An at-the-edge outfall must be at the DOWNHILL edge.* `drain_flows_downhill` identifies the discharge end partly by "is it at the frame edge", and Nagahara's fnn2 has its HEAD 28 px from the frame's top - inside the 32 px tolerance, on the UPslope side - so the check called the high end an outfall and then reported the water running backwards. Proximity to an edge is only evidence of a discharge when that edge is downhill; proximity to a STREAM remains evidence on its own, since a brook is a real sink wherever it lies.
  - *HOW THE DISCHARGE END IS IDENTIFIED - every discharge channel NAMES its field* (GM 2026-07-25). `drain_flows_downhill` has to know which end of a collector discharges before it can say the water runs backwards, and guessing from geometry kept mis-reading real maps. The fix: every `frm.kind == "drain"` topology channel now carries `name: <field>`, wired at all 19 call sites across four gens plus `inwall_drain_outfall`, so a drain matches ITS OWN discharge channels exactly. Proximity matching was the thing that failed - Hirameki carries seven such channels and several sit on top of a DIFFERENT field's drain - and so did every positional shortcut: keying on the channel's `poly[0]` mis-read six of Tango's ten drains, keying on `poly[-1]` broke Hirameki, because which end touches the drain varies by gen (some pass `drain_tail()`, some pass `[tail, sink]` straight, and on a 2-point drain `drain_tail` overshoots its 52 px span and hands back the whole drain).
  - *Read the channel's SINK end, then pool ALL the evidence and take the LOWEST.* A discharge may leave from a point PARTWAY along the collector (Hirameki's w2 runs off-map west from x=35, mid-drain), so the informative end is the channel's sink - whichever of its ends lies farther from the drain line, falling back to `poly[-1]` when both lie on it. And a collector legitimately has SEVERAL sinks: w2 also tees a relief culvert back to the stream at its east end, and s1 runs off the bottom edge at BOTH ends. So the check gathers every end with evidence of discharging - a named channel, a brook alongside, the frame edge - and takes the lowest. Its teeth are on the case that matters: when EVERY piece of evidence puts the discharge at the drain's HIGH end, the water is running backwards.

- **WATER FLOW - every map declares a DRAINAGE BEARING, every watercourse declares its direction** (drives `meta(water_flow=...)`, the `flow=` argument on `s.stream`/`s.river`/`s.canal`, `s.moat_flow`, and the checks `water_flow_declared`, `water_flow_consistent_with_slope`, `watercourses_declare_flow`, `watercourses_flow_downstream`, `moat_declares_circulation`; GM 2026-07-24).
  - *WHAT THIS IS FOR: verisimilitude, NOT uniformity* (GM 2026-07-25, and the standard to hold every future map to). The goal is that a map reads like a real place of the kind that actually existed - not that its water all points one way. Do NOT flatten a map into a single flow direction; real landscapes do not, for two separate reasons. **Local topography** rises and falls at small scale, so neighboring fields legitimately drain different ways (Tango's fans span 210 degrees, and the farms round its moat differ by their own ground - some drain INTO the moat, others have paddies FED BY it). And **most of these works are ARTIFICIAL**: a ditch dug ALONG a slope, rather than down it, is exactly what people build to INTERCEPT downhill flow and keep it out of somewhere - so a contour-hugging channel is not an error, it is engineering with a purpose.
  - *The ONE hard rule, and the anti-pattern it exists to kill.* Water never gains elevation. The GM's named pet peeve in the original L5R maps: **a river running parallel to a mountain range along its base** - it looks superb and is impossible. Water at the foot of a slope runs ALONG the valley that slope drains into, descending as it goes; a course that hugs a contour without descending is a CANAL, and a canal needs an engineered gradient and a reason to exist. So when a watercourse runs across the fall rather than down it, that is fine - but be able to say WHY (it is a collector gathering runoff along the low margin, an interception ditch protecting something, a navigation cut held at level), and record the why. A course with no descent and no purpose is the mistake.
  - *A MOAT JUNCTION IS SWEPT WITH THE CURRENT* (drives `moat_junctions_swept_with_the_current` and `settlement.moat_swept_tap`; GM caught it by eye 2026-07-25; fixtures: the pre-fix Tango and Nagahara manifests in `pool/regressions/`).
    - *The rule.* Where a channel meets the moat, its LOCAL heading at the junction must carry a downstream component. (why: [`../research/water.md#canal-junction-angles-an-offtake-leaves-pointing-downstream`](../research/water.md#canal-junction-angles-an-offtake-leaves-pointing-downstream)) - and a tributary likewise joins pointing downstream. A square tap sheds sediment into its own mouth, and on the page it says nothing about which way the water runs. The engine already held moat<->RIVER junctions to this (`city_moat_junction_angles`); this extends it to moat<->CHANNEL junctions, which nothing checked.
    - *Measure the LOCAL segment, not the channel's net vector.* The net vector is near-arbitrary for an offtake leaving the ring roughly perpendicular - that is why `moat_channels_flow_with_current` keeps its coarse cardinal test and is a DIFFERENT check from this one.
    - *Use the AVERAGED vertex tangent.* The tangent at a polygon vertex is the chord between its two NEIGHBORS. Taking the forward segment alone tilts every reading by about half the vertex's turn angle - ~8-9 deg on these rings, consistently in one direction - which was enough to make every square tap look upstream-facing and sent a whole diagnosis down the wrong path.
    - *Fixing it moves only the MOAT-SIDE end.* `moat_swept_tap` walks the rim attachment upstream (downstream, for an arriving drain) until the throat is swept enough; the sluice does NOT move, so the comb field it feeds does not shift a pixel. Walk by ARC LENGTH, not by vertex: a vertex step is ~140 px against a ~30 px throat, which overshoots into a channel running nearly parallel to the rim. And cap the ARRIVING walk (~90 px): wander further and a culvert's far end lands nearer the drain's OTHER endpoint, flipping the outfall attribution `drain_flows_downhill` depends on - which is exactly what Nagahara's fnn2 did.
    - *What it caught.* All five Tango offtakes and all four Nagahara offtakes were square (86-93 deg), because the tee was built purely radially; plus Tango's fn2 drain culvert doubling back to enter at 146 deg and Nagahara's fnn1 at 124 deg and fnn2 at 98 deg. After the fix every offtake sits at 47-48 deg and every drain at 14-60 deg. The two junctions that were already correct (nw1 35, fn1 41) moved 5 px.

  - *ONE DIRECTION MODEL, NOT THREE* (GM 2026-07-25). The recorded flow was at first a THIRD parallel model beside two older ones, and two sources of truth for one fact is how drift starts. All three are now converged, and the audit that forced it found two more silent skips of the same shape as the original bug:
    - `channels_flow_downhill` and `downhill_direction_valid` were gated on the LEGACY `meta(downhill)` - a cardinal name or vector - which only **2 of 17** maps ever declared. Fifteen maps, both provincial cities among them, skipped both checks entirely behind a green gate. They now take the fall from `downhill` where a map declares it, else `meta(down_deg)`, and per-channel from the TARGET FIELD's own fall where it has one. All 17 maps now run them, and all pass.
    - `moat_channels_flow_with_current` re-derived the moat's current from the FIRST stream whose end touched the ring, snapped to a cardinal. Fragile twice: on a river-cut city both feeder and outfall touch the ring, so the answer depended on draw order; and it needed a stream END within 35 px of the ring, which Nagahara's river (ends off-map, the MOAT's ends meeting IT) never satisfies - so it **never ran on Nagahara at all**. It now reads `moat_flow`'s inlet -> outlet. Both cities run it; both pass.
    - `city_moat_junction_angles` took increasing arc length along the river to mean "downstream", hard-coding the upstream-first authoring convention. It now reverses the points when a river is tagged `flow="reverse"`, so the convention is asserted rather than assumed.
  - *Why the moat current is deliberately SNAPPED TO A CARDINAL, and not measured precisely.* A ring has no single current: water entering the inlet travels BOTH ways round to the outlet. Modelling that exactly - taking the ring TANGENT at each tap, in the direction of travel along that tap's own arc - was tried and is the physically correct picture of the water, but it makes the check meaningless: an irrigation offtake leaves the ring roughly PERPENDICULAR, so its component along a precisely-measured tangent is near arbitrary, and it flagged Tango's fw2 and Nagahara's fs1/fss1 while sparing their neighbors on nothing more than which side of the tap the field sat. The coarse hemisphere is what makes the test mean "the field is not back upstream". Keep it coarse; the fine version measures the wrong quantity.

  - *These maps carry NO topographic markings, deliberately* (GM 2026-07-25: they already hold a great deal of visual detail, and contour lines would add clutter faster than they add understanding). Elevation therefore exists ONLY in the declared data - `meta(down_deg)`, the per-field falls, `meta(water_flow)`, each course's `flow` - and in the checks that read them. That is precisely why those declarations are MANDATORY rather than optional (`settlement_declares_a_land_fall`): they are the only place the terrain exists at all, so a map that omits them has no terrain to be judged against, and the reader cannot catch by eye what the data does not say.

  - *Establish the bearing BEFORE drawing anything* (GM rule). `water_flow` is a property of the landscape, not of the settlement - a mountain range running northwest to southeast throws settlements off both flanks, draining northeast on one side and southwest on the other. Settle which side of which range this place sits on first; the water then dictates where the fields, the mills, the polluting trades and the quarter can go.
  - *Two fields, not one, because slope and flow genuinely differ.* `down_deg` is the LAND's fall; `water_flow` is where the water leaves. A valley floor runs across the fall of the valley sides it lies between, so the two diverge by design - Hoshizora slopes SSW (115) while its valley stream runs 145, and hikari-no-sato has a drain 88 degrees off its map's fall. Deriving flow from slope would force every stream straight down the fall line, which is wrong for exactly the valley-floor and contour-drain cases the pool already contains.
  - *How far they may diverge: up to but never reaching 90 degrees.* An artificial contour canal is aligned NEARLY PARALLEL to the contours, deviating only enough to keep a working gradient - so a divergence approaching 90 is the defining property of a dug channel, not a defect. What is impossible is 90 or more, which would give the water a net UPHILL component. Hence both angle checks use a strict `< 90` and not a tighter tolerance: an earlier draft of this rule proposed +/-60 and would have condemned legitimate contour works. Widest real divergences in this pool: 60 (map bearing vs fall), 69 (a course vs its map's bearing).
  - *Polylines are authored UPSTREAM-FIRST.* This formalizes a convention `s.moat`'s `river=` argument already relied on (its junction tilts key on it). `flow="reverse"` tags a polyline stored the other way round - reversing the points renders identically, so the tag is only for where the drawing code wants the other order. Hoshizora's valley stream is the case in point: stored west-end-first while the water runs NE to SW, so without the tag its point order reads as a stream climbing 151 degrees uphill.
  - *A navigable canal declares `flow="level"`, not a bearing.* A cargo canal is cut at the level of the water it joins - which is what lets barges pole BOTH ways along it - so its gradient is nil, the water gate's sluice holds it at river level, and a dead-end dock basin has no through-flow at all. Giving it a downstream direction would be a fiction, so it declares "level" and the drainage-bearing check skips it. Nagahara's canal runs 187 degrees, against the map's 90-degree drainage, and is correct: it is a navigation cut, not a drain.
  - *LIMIT of both angle checks - they compare against ONE regional bearing.* `water_flow_consistent_with_slope` and `watercourses_flow_downstream` measure a local fact (a map's fall, one course's heading) against a single map-level bearing, and confluence geometry can legitimately break that: a bank settlement whose land falls PERPENDICULAR into a trunk river that runs along it has a fall and a drainage bearing ~90 apart, or more, with nothing wrong anywhere. Nagahara is exactly that shape. The checks are kept because the defect they actually catch is a REVERSED flow tag - a 180-degree error, which no legitimate geometry produces - and because no map in this pool comes near the bound (widest: 60 and 69). If a future bank map trips one, revisit the bearing model; do not "fix" the map.

  - *A moat records an INLET and an OUTLET rather than a direction.* A ring has no upstream end - its water enters at one point and leaves at another, running both ways round the circuit - so "downstream of a moated city" has to be reasoned from those two points. - **Water-width ladder: ditches, creeks, streams, moats, ponds** (drives `irrigation_channels_hairline`, `watercourses_wider_than_ditches`, `moat_is_heaviest_watercourse`, `moat_dwarfs_ditches`, and the `channel()` / `stream()` / `moat()` / pond-feeder drawn widths). Wet-rice water systems are a strongly **tiered, self-similar hierarchy**: each tier steps up ~2-4x in width because channel width scales with the square-root of the command-area flow it carries. The honest real-world ladder, off the smallest field ditch:

  The real-world tiers, their anchors, and the ~250:1 true linear span are in [`../research/water.md#water-width-ladder-the-real-world-tiers`](../research/water.md#water-width-ladder-the-real-world-tiers).
  - *The stroke convention here (GM ruling 2026-07-21: watercourses are LINEWORK, so this is the sanctioned minimum-visibility floor, never a size license).* The true linear span is ~250:1 (1700:1 with reservoirs) - undrawable on one sheet without the ditch vanishing (a 1 ft ditch at city scale is 0.33 px). So the thin end draws true-width-or-floored: the field ditch parks at the visibility floor (~2.5 px) rather than its true hairline, while honesty anchors on the **larger** features, which draw true (the city moat is `px(66)` = its real ~66 ft, low end of a provincial-seat moat's 66-115 ft). The *ordering* and coarse steps always survive: **channel/ditch ~2.5 px** (hairline floor), **pond-feeder ~5 px** (a lateral), **village-creek stream ~9 px**, **town-river / moat-feeder stream = the moat width** (conservation of flow - a stream supplying a moat carries its full flow), **city moat px(66)** (heaviest watercourse; a provincial city, not the Osaka grand-moat tier). A pond is **areal** - drawn as a polygon at paddy scale, never on the line-width ladder; its *outlet* is a main canal. The checks pin ordering and the key gaps (ditch hairline; creek >=2.5x the ditch; moat the heaviest watercourse; moat >=4x the ditch) rather than exact pixels, so the tiers can never collapse back into one undifferentiated blue line.
  - *A TAPER IS A SQUARE ROOT, never a straight line* (`waterfields.taper_w`; GM 2026-08-17). A channel's width goes as the square root of the discharge it carries, and the discharge falls (or, for a collector, accumulates) linearly along the run - so it is the width SQUARED that interpolates from head to tail, not the width. (why: [`../research/water.md#a-channel-taper-is-a-square-root-not-a-straight-line`](../research/water.md#a-channel-taper-is-a-square-root-not-a-straight-line)) The PROFILE is the point: a delivery ditch holds most of its working width while it still has most of its water to deliver and does its narrowing late, instead of shedding evenly along its whole length. **That is a property of the data, not something the eye can follow at hamlet scale** - read the sub-perceptual ruling below before you go looking for it on a map. Never re-derive the interpolation at a call site - `taper_w` is shared by the drawn stroke, both bank clearances (which the gate reads), the seam buffer, the carve's burial filter and the keep-out corridor, and a second formula would draw a bund inside the water.
  - *The finest DRAWN channel STOPS at the lateral tier - it does not taper to a point* (the `w_tail` floors in `build_comb`). (why: [`../research/water.md#where-the-drawn-net-stops-the-tier-below-the-last-ditch-we-can-draw`](../research/water.md#where-the-drawn-net-stops-the-tier-below-the-last-ditch-we-can-draw)) The real hierarchy is FINITE and named - China's 干/支/斗/农/毛, Japan's 幹線 -> 支線 -> 末端用水路 - and it ends in a designed terminal channel with a working section, not in a vanishing. Below the ~1 m lateral we draw sits the ~0.3 m field ditch (one pixel at hamlet scale, a third of one at city scale) and, in a PRE-MODERN system, frequently no channel at all: paddy was watered 田越し灌漑 (*tagoshi kangai*), plot to plot, water cascading through the *aze* from each paddy to the one below. So **a delivery ditch ending mid-field is the handoff to the cascade, not an unfinished line** - the comb already carries each column several to ~10 rows past where its ditch stops. Do not "fix" a blunt ditch end by tapering it away.
  - *The bund runs UNBROKEN along every channel bank, and the paddy intake is deliberately NOT drawn* (`supply_bank_clearance`, `_drain_bank`, `aze_w`; GM asked 2026-08-17). (why: [`../research/water.md#the-bund-runs-along-the-channel-bank-and-the-mizuguchi-is-too-small-to-draw`](../research/water.md#the-bund-runs-along-the-channel-bank-and-the-mizuguchi-is-too-small-to-draw)) The paddy's *aze* IS the channel's bank - a basin must be watertight on the ditch side like every other - and water crosses it at the ***mizuguchi*** (水口), one per plot, **within 50 cm wide**, which is **0.5 px at 1 ft/px and 0.17 px at city scale**. It is therefore never rendered at any Mode B scale and no check demands it, exactly as a farmhouse's door is never drawn. **Do not notch the bunds**, and do not read a continuous bund as paddies with no water supply. (The real *aze* is a 30 cm x 30 cm 1:1 trapezoid, ~1 ft at the crest and ~3 ft at the base; `aze_w` of ~1.5 ft draws that trapezoid as one line.)
  - *THE COMB NET IS DRAWN AT TRUE SIZE* (GM ruling 2026-08-17, reversing an inflation nobody had ever decided). Pricing the net against a real irrigation duty found it **5-6x oversize** - Inashiro's 8.5 ha fan wants a ~5 ft head-race and was drawn at 14. The ladder now lives in `waterfields/frame.py` as **true widths in FEET** (`HEAD_RACE_FT` 5.0, `CANAL_A_FT` 4.5 -> 1.5, `CANAL_B_FT` 4.0 -> 1.5, `DELIVERY_FT` 2.5 -> 1.2, `DRAIN_FT` 1.2 -> 5.5), converted by `chan_px(ft, grain)`, so the same real channel is the same real size on every sheet. (why, with both derivations: [`../research/water.md#the-comb-net-is-drawn-at-true-size`](../research/water.md#the-comb-net-is-drawn-at-true-size)) **Do not re-inflate these to make a map read better** - raise it with the GM, per the standing no-size-inflation rule for to-scale modes.
  - *THE TAPER IS SUB-PERCEPTUAL AT TRUE SCALE, AND THAT IS A DELIBERATE, PRICED DECISION* (GM ruling 2026-08-17, asked while looking at the map: *"Is that getting narrower as it goes down into the fields, or along the edge at the top, though? It doesn't visually look like it is."* It is not, and the honest answer was no). Measured on Inashiro: a delivery ditch sheds **1.0 px over ~500 px of run** and canal A sheds 3.0 px over 1,504 - both **0.20 px per 100 px**, against 0.93 before the net went to true size. The ink is monotone and the head-to-tail RATIO is honest (3:1 on the canals), so the two ends differ plainly when set side by side; what does not exist is a gradient you can follow along a stroke. Only canal B, being short, reads as tapering (2.5 px over 343). **This is what true scale means here, not a defect**: a real ditch narrowing from ~1.4 m to ~0.45 m over 460 m is not dramatic from above either, and the visible taper on pre-2026-08-17 maps came from the 2-5x inflation rather than from the taper. **The alternatives were priced and declined**: x1.5 (delivery 3.75 -> 1.8, gradient 0.39) and x2 (5.0 -> 2.4, gradient 0.52) were both put to the GM against "keep true size", and true size was chosen. So: do not "fix" the invisible taper, do not re-inflate to make it show, and do not write copy promising a reader they will see it.
  - *SCALE enters the ladder in exactly ONE place: `MIN_CHANNEL_PX`* (1.5 px). True widths are honest at hamlet and village resolution and vanish above it - at a provincial city's 3 ft/px the terminal tier is 0.4 px, which is not a line - so a stroke draws at its true width **or** the floor, whichever is larger, and the coarser the sheet the more of the ladder collapses onto it. That is the honest form of the minimum-visibility floor this convention already sanctions, and it is the knob to reach for if a coarse-scale map's net becomes unreadable. **Raising the FEET to fix a coarse map is the wrong lever** - it lies about every scale to fix one.
  - *A DELIVERY IS NEVER DRAWN WIDER THAN THE CANAL FEEDING IT* (`DELIVERY_PARENT_FRAC`, 0.8; `SUB_PARENT_FRAC`, 0.75 for a mid-block sub-ditch). A delivery's head is capped at `DELIVERY_PARENT_FRAC` of its parent canal's **LOCAL** width where it takes off, so the cap only bites low in the tree where the canal has already dwindled - it binds on roughly half the deliveries on a hamlet fan. A mid-block SUB-ditch is instead sized against its parent delivery's **HEAD**, and that asymmetry is deliberate: at true scale 0.75 of a delivery's local width lands within a fraction of a pixel of the visibility floor, leaving a sub-ditch nothing to taper into, which `delivery_ditches_taper` rightly rejects. This is NOT conservation-at-junctions, which is separately ruled against (drawn width is RANK, not discharge - never chain widths down from the source); it is the weaker guarantee that a child reads as subordinate to its parent, which is the one thing width-as-rank exists to convey.
