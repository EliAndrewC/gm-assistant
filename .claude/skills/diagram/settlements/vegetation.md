# Vegetation and terrain: windbreaks, commons, hinterland, forests, relief

*Part of the Mode B settlement docs. The index, the `meta()` knobs, the workflow and the validator contract live in [`../settlements.md`](../settlements.md).*

**Load this file when:** filling ground OUTSIDE the cultivated area - scrub, grazing commons, woodland patches, a true forest - or showing slope.

**Research:** the historical basis for these rules - what was found, the decision it drove, and every disclosed departure - is in [`../research/vegetation.md`](../research/vegetation.md). Load it when you are CHANGING a rule or questioning one, not to follow it.

---

- **Pastures and large terrain** - hayfields/grazing (`s.pasture(...)`), forests (`s.forest_patch(...)`): a large area feature near an edge must **cross** that edge rather than sitting wholly on the map (`edge_features_run_off_map`). Barns sit within the grazing.
- **Village windbreak - the Chinese FENGSHUI FOREST (风水林), a COMMUNAL grove, is the NUCLEATED village's windbreak (NOT per-house groves)** (drives `village_windbreak_present`, `village_windbreak_on_windward_side`, `village_groves_clear_of_paddies`, `groves_clear_of_lanes`, and the nucleated SKIP of `groves_where_possible`; drawn by `s.village_grove(poly, role=..., dense=...)`, recorded in `M['village_groves']`; researched + source-verified 2026-07, this is the record - do not re-research).
  - *Trees stay OFF the paths (`groves_clear_of_lanes`).* A lane/street/road is bare trodden earth - you do not plant trees on it - so `village_grove` skips any clump within a corridor's keep-out (lanes + town streets + the road, via `_corridor_buffers`), just as it skips houses/yards/gardens/paddy. Each `village_grove` records its **actual drawn clump centers + radius** (`clumps`, `r`), and the check verifies none sits on a corridor (the recorded bounding `poly` covers the whole scatter region, so the check must test the real clumps, not the poly). The per-house *yashikirin* groves (`M['groves']`, a rect each) are checked the same way. This caught the leafy copse scatter landing on the village spine lane.
  - *Trees stay OUT of the SOUTHERN SUN of yards + gardens (`village_trees_unshade_yards_and_gardens`).* The drying yard and the kitchen garden need the sun, which comes from the SOUTH (+y), so no village-grove clump may sit in the strip directly south of either - `village_grove` skips any clump in a yard's/garden's southern sun-corridor (a touch wider than the check, so it stays clear), and the check verifies it against the recorded clumps. (This is the communal-grove companion to `yards_unshaded_by_groves`, which does the same for the per-house groves; the scatter avoids sitting ON a yard/garden, but that is not enough - it must also avoid being just SOUTH of one.)
  - *A clump ABUTS but never OVERLAPS a farmstead (`grove_clumps_clear_of_structures`, GM 2026-07).* Trees stand right up against a house wall - "directly next to" is correct and common - but a clump drawn ON a roof/yard/garden reads wrong. The copse keep-out reserves each house/yard/garden/byre/shed by its circumscribing radius PLUS the clump's FULL drawn radius (clump/2) and a hair - not a fraction of it. The old 0.35xclump was ~0.15xclump short, which let a blob corner clip a small farmhouse (found on Hikari). The check measures the tree blob (a round mass, radius r) against each footprint rect and fires on real penetration; a few px of canopy over the eaves is fine (the GM's "directly next to is OK").
  - *Shape - ORGANIC and EMBRACING, never a ruled wall (a correction the GM caught).* Real groves hug the terrain and WRAP the settlement; a rectangle + a square read as machine-planted. So `village_grove` takes an **irregular polygon footprint** (ragged, terrain-following) and FILLS it with clumps on a jittered grid, SKIPPING any clump that would land on a house / threshing yard / garden / paddy - which lets the belt nestle right up to the village edge without ever drawing trees on a building or in the crops. The 背山面水 ideal ("back to the hill, face the water") drives the whole layout: the DENSE wood sits on the high/windward BACK and wraps the flanks (the 玄武 back-forest + 青龙/白虎 embracing arms), while the FIELD/water side stays OPEN. The back belt is drawn as a CRESCENT embracing the windward edge, deepest in the middle and tapering into horns that wrap the corners.
  - *Why communal, not per-house.* The per-house *yashikirin* (the bullet above) is the DISPERSED-farmstead windbreak; a NUCLEATED cluster (the China-leaning default, per 'Settlement form') shelters itself and cannot fit a ~6x-house grove-bundle on every farm. So the nucleated windbreak is a single **village-scale grove**, exactly the dominant Chinese pattern: the **fengshui forest**, a grove protected by taboo (sacred "dragon" woodland, cutting forbidden - which is why these survived as the most mature forest stands in southern China). This is the CHINA-first answer per the core principle (surveys across Fujian, Jiangxi, Guangdong, Hunan, Anhui - our rice-south analog; Chen & Coggins et al.).
  - *Three parts, sited by role - the DENSE wood is concentrated, but the village is LEAFY, not bare (a second GM correction).* The greenery is STRUCTURED (not a uniform forest), and specifically does NOT line the main road or shade the paddies/drying yards (the field/downslope side stays open - same crop-and-drying logic as the *yashikirin* sunny-lee rule). But a south-China village was NOT bare tan ground with a few tree-blobs: houses nestled in scattered **bamboo and dooryard fruit** (litchi, longan, persimmon, citrus, mulberry - some villages effectively bamboo-encircled). So the three parts are:
    1. **Back-village belt (`role="windbreak"`, 后龙林)** - the big one: a dense grove crescent EMBRACING the high **WINDWARD back edge** (default NW - the winter-monsoon wall), nestled against the cluster and wrapping the NW/SW flanks. Villages backed against the high ground, so where the high side is windward the back grove IS the wind wall (Hoshigaoka: the WEST, away from the E/SE paddy). `village_windbreak_present` requires it; `village_windbreak_on_windward_side` requires its centroid windward of the house-cluster centroid.
    2. **Water-mouth cluster (`role="water_mouth"`, 水口林)** - a smaller irregular cluster of big old trees at the LOW entrance / water-mouth (where the stream/track exits down-valley), "locking in" the water and qi and marking the entrance.
    3. **Leafy scatter (`role="copse"`, `dense=False`)** - bamboo + dooryard fruit clumps filling the OPEN gaps THROUGHOUT the cluster (not a light dusting - the village reads as embedded in green), the conifer-free 'dooryard' species mix. Drawn by passing the whole-cluster region as one sparse-filled polygon; `village_grove` drops a clump into every gap that is clear of a house/yard/garden/paddy, so the houses end up nestled in bamboo and fruit trees while the lanes, yards, and field side stay open.
  - *(why: [`../research/vegetation.md#the-fengshui-forest-real-scale-and-why-ours-is-honest`](../research/vegetation.md#the-fengshui-forest-real-scale-and-why-ours-is-honest))*
  - *The belt SCALES with the cluster, and it is DEEP (`village_windbreak_scales_with_cluster`, GM 2026-07-25).* The embrace test (>= 12 clumps within 150px) is a FIXED floor, so a belt sized for a five-house corner passes unchanged behind a whole hamlet - which is how Moritono came to shelter 16 farmhouses behind 11 crowns (0.07 ha, the pool's smallest belt by half). The check now measures the shelter actually drawn - the windbreak's canopy disks plus any per-house *yashikirin* footprints, since a map may do both - against the ROOF area it shelters, and wants **canopy >= 0.40x roofs**. Both sides are px², so the ratio is scale-free. It is a floor against absurdity, not a target: the research figure above (a modest back grove under 1 ha, ~1,800 sq ft per household) sits near ratio ~1.3 at our house sizes, and the approved pool runs 0.45 (Hoshizora, a town whose farm zone is a thin wedge) to 7.27 (Hikari-no-Sato). Two authoring rules follow, both from the Moritono post-mortem: **set the outline BACK off the homesteads** (its inner edge ran through the northern house row, so most clumps it could have grown were rejected as landing on a building - the copse, not the belt, fills the inner gaps), and **make it ~80-120 ft deep** so the grid packs 3-4 rows of overlapping canopy; a ~30 ft band holds ONE row and reads as scattered blobs, not a wall.
  - *A neighboring FOREST only counts as the windbreak when it actually shelters the cluster (`village_windbreak_embraces_cluster`).* A map whose wood is a real edge-feature forest (`M['forest']`) may let that wood be its wind wall - it is the strongest windbreak there is - but only where the tree line comes within the same 150px nestling distance of a farmhouse AND stands WINDWARD of the cluster. The blanket "has a forest -> exempt" rule this replaced was written FOR Moritono and then excused it: the Shirin Forest sits 1,089 ft east of the hamlet under an NW wind, i.e. downwind and a fifth of a mile off, sheltering nothing.
  - *The FAR side of the back-grove is NOT more forest - it is degraded FUEL-AND-FODDER COMMONS + the back-slope waste (`s.commons(poly)`, `M['commons']`; drives `commons_clear_of_paddies`, `commons_beyond_the_windbreak`).* The lush grove is a THIN protected band; the taboo is exactly *why* it is green when the surrounding hills are not. South China's hills were progressively stripped for firewood/timber over ~1,000 years, so past the grove the ground is cut-over and open - "a monotonous succession of hills either deforested or carrying open stands" of masson pine / China-fir, with severe erosion. So `commons` draws the far/upslope side deliberately OPEN and SPARSE - coarse grass tufts, low brush, and a FEW scraggly individual pines - VISUALLY DISTINCT from the dense dark closed-canopy grove (it is a *commons*, not a field, and never the flooded paddy). It has **NO solid fill and NO hard outline** (an early filled quad read as a literal rhombus): the scrub is a FEATHERED scatter that thins to nothing at the margin, so the ground has no boundary at all, just scrub petering out onto the open slope. It is drawn as a ragged BAND that PARALLELS the grove's back at a jittered width and is drawn UNDER the forest, so the grove's canopy overlaps its inner edge and the two ABUT seamlessly (no bare gap between wood and scrub). The land TOPOSEQUENCE is **village -> back-grove -> fuel commons -> (off-map domain boundary)**, so the commons sits on the windward/high side and FURTHER out than the windbreak (`commons_beyond_the_windbreak` gates the ordering; `commons_clear_of_paddies` keeps it off the wet crop). Sources: South China degraded lands (millennium of fuel/timber cutting); FAO forest-rehabilitation (deforested hills, open pine, erosion).
  - *`commons(poly, role="grazing")` for the OTHER dry margins.* The same scrub glyph fills any dry, marginal, non-paddy flank of the SATOYAMA toposequence (grass/scrub grazing hill) - the NW pond CATCHMENT, the NORTH up-valley head above the dry-field (*hatake*) margin, the NE flank, the SW/south back-slope behind the village. `role="grazing"` is general marginal hill-grazing, so it is **exempt from `commons_beyond_the_windbreak`** (that windward-back-slope ordering only applies to the fuel/fodder commons proper, `role="commons"`, the default); `commons_clear_of_paddies` still applies to all. **Fill the WHOLE margin as a CONTINUOUS RING, not isolated corner patches** (the GM's rule): every "empty" edge of the map is DRY marginal land in a NW-high/SE-low valley - so it is a continuous band of the toposequence (pond-catchment -> dry fields -> grazing scrub -> grove -> graveyard -> marsh), never open plain or forest-everywhere and never a few little rectangles floating in tan. Broad polygons that span a whole edge (e.g. one north band across the entire top above the dry fields, dropping at the east edge to meet the SE marsh; one south band filling the back-slope below the village) read as real hillsides; small disconnected quads read as patches. **`margins_form_continuous_ring` gates this** (village/hamlet): no more than 12% of the FRAMED view may be bare of every ground feature - calibrated so the ring-conforming maps (0-8.4% bare, the tan showing only as feathered seams between scatters) pass with margin while open plain fires. **Draw the bands for the FRAME, not the canvas**: `crop_to_content` hugs the hard content (houses/fields), and commons are NOT crop content - so a band placed against the canvas edge can fall entirely OUTSIDE the cropped view. This bit Ueda (2026-07-20): its W/SW back-slope band sat at canvas x 120-480, the crop tightened the frame to x>=505, and 28% of the rendered map was bare open tan with the ring off-camera. Check `meta["view"]` after a first render and span the bands across the framed window; generous overshoot is safe (the scatter auto-skips paddy, dry plots, lanes, water, structures, and swept clearings, and `commons_clear_of_paddies` rejects only a patch with no open ground at all). Keep each `grazing` polygon OFF the crops: `commons` auto-skips every paddy AND dry plot plus a 6 ft crop margin (see "Scrub stands off the crops" below), and - since the urban-clearance halo (see the scrub-off-buildings bullets under the grove section) - every structure plus its 30 ft halo; but a poly should still not CLAIM built or cropped ground (`scrub_clear_of_urban_fabric` fires on a poly containing any dwelling/well center), so hold the polygon ABOVE the dry-field tops and WEST of the paddy edge (query those extents; don't eyeball). On a fully-filled map the auto-title has no blank margin to land in, so leave it a small tan CLEARING (dip the band's top edge over the title's x-range - a natural col in the ridge) and call `s.title()` AFTER `crop_to_content()` as usual so `title_clear_of_features` still passes.
  - *DRAW ORDER for the margin fill (the GM's rule): place the grazing/scrub AFTER the farmhouses + groves but BEFORE the graveyard + shrine.* After the houses/groves so the scrub fills the gaps THEY leave (not drawn over them); before the funerary + religious features so those sit ON the scrubland, not on bare tan - burial + *kegare* belong on the waste back-slope, and a shrine at the water-mouth sits in its grove/scrub. In the gen this means: farmhouses -> village_grove -> `commons(role="grazing")` margin bands -> `crop_to_content()` -> `shrine_hall` / `cemetery`.
  - *Scrub/reeds are ground-cover UNDER the trodden road, never over it (the GM's rule: "the road should not look overgrown").* A constantly-walked way stays BARE - so `commons` and `marsh` skip any scatter point on ANY corridor tread: lanes, town streets, AND the road (`_corridor_buffers`, clearing each tread's half-width + ~3 px). The skip originally knew only `M['lanes']`, which is how Hoshizora's scrub drew on the Imperial Road bed (GM 2026-07-21) - the corridor set now covers all three. The connector track that leaves the village always crosses the back-slope scrub; without the skip, scrub tufts drawn AFTER the lane sit on top of it and it reads as overgrown. The skip is per-tuft (the corridor must already be recorded, i.e. drawn *before* the ground-cover - which it is: lanes/roads lay their no-build corridor before the houses), so the tread reads clean ON the scrub regardless of z-order, and it generalises to a marsh causeway. No manifest-level check (scatter points are not individually recorded, same as the paddy-skip) - covered by `test_settlement` unit tests (`test_commons_keeps_scrub_off_the_road_bed`).
  - *Scrub/reeds are also kept OFF every building footprint.* `commons` and `marsh` skip any scatter point that falls on a `block_polys` no-build footprint (houses, wellheads, and especially the **shrine hall + its torii arch**) - a commons polygon that OVERLAPS the shrine (Ueda's water-mouth commons contains the shrine) must not dot grass tufts and scraggly pines over the hall and gateway. Same per-tuft mechanism as the lane skip (the structure must be drawn *before* the ground-cover; place `shrine_hall` before `commons`), same no-manifest-check caveat - covered by unit tests (`test_commons_keeps_scrub_off_a_shrine_and_torii`, `test_marsh_keeps_reeds_off_a_building`). Distinct from `shrine_clear_of_grove_trees` / `torii_clear_of_grove_trees`, which gate the dense *grove* clumps (those ARE recorded, so they carry a check); the loose commons scatter is handled purely at draw time.
  - *The URBAN-CLEARANCE HALO: scrub is cleared from the whole BUILT-UP fabric, not just off footprints (GM 2026-07-21, Hoshizora).* The town's gen drew deliberately "generous" scrub polys over the urban core, trusting the per-point skips - and the render showed scrub speckle through the streets, dooryards, and gaps between the shops, merchant houses, laborer housing, and burakumin quarter, crowding right up to the wellheads. *What the reasoning found:* the ground nearest occupied structures is the FIRST stripped bare - dooryards are packed swept earth, the alleys between packed houses are walked bare daily, and a settlement's fuel/fodder-gathering pressure crops brush closest to home first - so scrub survives only past the daily-traffic halo, on the outskirts. *The decision, three mutually-reinforcing layers, one shared constant set (`settlement._urban_keepouts`; real FEET, converted at the map grain):* **(1) engine** - the `commons`/`marsh` scatter skips a **30 ft halo** around every occupied structure (houses, buildings, storehouses, flophouses, byres, farm sheds, religious, manors, ministries, inspection stations; a dooryard's working depth, which also closes the gaps in packed districts whose ~40-48 ft spacing leaves no strip wider than two halos), a **20 ft apron** around every wellhead (the most-trodden, spill-puddled ground in any settlement), and an **8 ft edge** on tended plots (gardens, threshing yards); **(2) check** - `scrub_clear_of_urban_fabric` (town/city ONLY): no occupied structure's or wellhead's CENTER may lie inside a commons cover poly of any role - the recorded poly must itself trace the outskirts, order-blind so a structure drawn AFTER the cover fires too (field `barn`s exempt: a hay barn stands in the grazed ground it serves; at village/hamlet scale the check deliberately does NOT run - the satoyama doctrine interleaves dispersed farmsteads, the water-mouth shrine, and wells WITH the marginal scrub, the farms standing ON the waste, so there only the engine halo applies); **(3) the tm interplay** - `town_margins_clothed` counts the same 30 ft halo around structures as covered "urban floor" (the unwalled-town equivalent of its inside-the-rampart exemption), so the bare trodden ground between districts no longer needs scrub to pass and the two checks cannot fight. Regression fixture: `pool/regressions/scrub_clear_of_urban_fabric_fires_on_hoshizora_town_core.json` (the actual pre-fix manifest, 74 offenders); engine side covered by `test_commons_clears_the_urban_halo_around_buildings` (in `tests/settlement/`) + `_wellhead_apron` + `test_marsh_clears_the_urban_halo_and_wellheads`.
  - *Scrub/reeds are kept OFF open WATER too (GM 2026-07: "scrubland rendered over the pond and stream is clearly wrong").* `commons` and `marsh` skip any scatter point on the POND (its ellipse) or on a stream / irrigation channel BED (`_on_watercourse`: within the watercourse's half-width + a hair of `M['streams']` + `M['channels']`). Vegetation never floats on water - the same reasoning as the lane/building skips. This matters most on a hamlet where the scrub matrix fills the whole frame (Moritono's scrub used to cover the tameike + the forest brook + the comb ditches). `marsh` already skipped the open pond for its `pond_fringe` reeds; now both also skip watercourses. Per-tuft draw-time skip (the water must be drawn *before* the ground-cover - which it is: fields + water go down before the hinterland), no manifest check (scatter points aren't recorded) - covered by `test_on_watercourse_detects_stream_and_channel_beds` (in `tests/settlement/`) + `test_commons_and_marsh_skip_the_pond_and_watercourses`.
  - *Scrub stands OFF the crops - never ON a paddy or dry plot, and clear of a narrow cut margin around every crop edge (GM 2026-08-15: scrubland was overlapping farm fields, visibly so on dry crops, and the fields need "a little bit of clearance").* The `commons` scatter's crop keep-out is every paddy (`field_polys`) AND every dry plot (`dry_polys` - read directly, not via `block_polys`, so it survives a gen that registers only one), each padded by **`_CROP_MARGIN_FT` = 6 real ft** (converted at the map's `ftpx`). *Historical grounding (the why of 6 ft):* a paddy levee (*keihan*/*aze*; Chinese *tian'geng*) is a ~1-2 ft earthen ridge, ~3 ft where it carries a footpath (*azemichi*); the levee grass and the strip beside the crop were CUT several times a season for fodder and green manure, so woody scrub never establishes within about a scythe's swath (~1-2 m) of the field edge - the same ~1 m clean strip that separates crop from boundary vegetation in traditional field-margin practice (the 6 m+ conservation headlands of modern European agri-schemes are NOT the model - East Asian land hunger keeps margins narrow). 6 ft (~1.8 m) = bund + one cut swath; deliberately small, per the GM ("not very much"). *Glyph lean rides on top:* a scatter element's BASE point clearing the margin is not enough for the tall glyphs - a scraggly pine's drawn tip reaches ~14*bs px up and a woodland crown ~11.5*bs px out, so before this rule a pine rooted just outside a field leaned its canopy over the crop (an audit of the 2026-08-15 pool found near-zero scatter bases ON crops but hundreds within 6 ft of a crop edge on almost every map - edge-crowding plus lean WAS the visible overlap). The exact test therefore adds each glyph's drawn reach to the margin (`_sparse(..., lean=...)`), with the keep-out boxes pre-padded for the worst case (`boxed_hit`'s prefilter contract). Grass-tuft blade TIPS are the deliberate exception (settlement-review, 2026-08-16): a blade runs 2.4-4.2*bs px, so at the coarser tiers a tip can lean a couple of real feet past the margin line - accepted rather than leaned-for, because grass overhanging a bund is real and the overhang is sub-pixel-to-invisible at render; bases and tall-glyph reach are what the rule enforces. Same caveats as every per-tuft skip: the crop must be drawn *before* the ground-cover (it is - fields + dry plots go down before the hinterland), and there is NO manifest check (scatter points aren't recorded) - covered by `test_commons_keeps_scrub_off_dry_plots_and_the_crop_margin` (in `tests/settlement/`). (why: [`../research/vegetation.md#the-crop-margin---scrub-stands-6-ft-off-every-field-edge`](../research/vegetation.md#the-crop-margin---scrub-stands-6-ft-off-every-field-edge)) The reed `marsh` is deliberately NOT given the margin: wet ground genuinely starts at the polder dike, so reeds abutting a field's low bund is honest.
  - *A WELLHEAD is a clean draw-point - no tree canopy over it* (`wells_clear_of_trees`, GM 2026-07). A well lost under the wood reads wrong; the check fires on any well whose drawn head (`vr`) overlaps a **fengshui grove clump** (windbreak / water-mouth / copse), a **per-house grove** rect, a **forest**, or a **coppice-woodland** patch. FIX at placement: wells go down BEFORE the grove, and `village_grove` skips a clump within `well.vr + 0.9*clump` of a well (was `well.r + 0.35*clump` - too tight, so a clump's canopy still overhung the wellhead; Hoshigaoka + Ueda each had wells buried in the windbreak belt). Teeth: `test_wells_clear_of_trees_fires_on_grove_forest_woodland_grect_but_passes_when_clear` (in `tests/check_village/`).
- **Non-topographic relief: show slope by its CONSEQUENCES, never by shading** (drives the pictorial `hill()` mound, the `irrigation_ditch` + drainage water direction, and the deliberate ABSENCE of hillshade/contours). These maps are deliberately **not topographic** - no contour lines, no spot heights. Relief is shown three ways, which stack for a rice village:
  - *Pictorial hills.* A hill is a shaded **mound** (`hill()`: concentric tiers + radial hachures + a wooded cap) - the old *ezu* / estate-map convention, a picture of high ground, not survey lines.
  - *The farming shows the slope.* Water enters HIGH and leaves LOW; on genuine slopes paddies become **terraces** (*tanada*: long, narrow, CURVED contour-following strips, stacked with a riser between). Terracing is a **STEEP-slope feature** - a **gentle** valley tilt produces NO visible terraces, only a flat patchwork with imperceptible level steps, so we do NOT draw terraces on a gently-tilted village (it would misread as mountainous). Kikuta is a gentle tilt: flat patchwork, slope carried by water alone.
  - *Water direction is the honest gentle-slope cue.* Source high, drainage low. Kikuta: **NW = uphill** (the cold NW winter-monsoon wind pours off the high ground, so windward and uphill coincide) - pond/source at the NW corner, irrigation (*yosui*) distributary ditches (`irrigation_ditch`, drawn OVER the paddy since the deferred water layer sits UNDER the fields) fanning water in from the high NW, and a **drainage (*haisui*) ditch leaving the LOW SE corner** off-map (a hairline `channel(..., {"kind":"field"...}, {"kind":"offmap"})`). Wet rice needs water IN **and** water OUT - irrigation + drainage are the two halves of every paddy system (often separate networks) - so a drainage outlet is a real, expected feature, and it doubles as the downhill cue.
  - *What we REJECTED and why:* directional **shading / cast shadows** to imply the gradient. On a plan map, shaded relief reads as a **light source** ("the sun is in the NW"), not as a rising gradient - it fights the flat-plan convention and misleads. Slope is shown by water + (steep-only) terraces + pictorial hills, never by shading.
- **Hinterland - the SCRUB-dominant catena, with a FEW woodland patches (China-first)** (`s.hinterland(down_deg=None)`, GM 2026-07). The marsh + commons catena above was, for the villages, hand-placed per map. `hinterland()` makes it a **standard step**: given the cultivated extent + `down_deg` (defaults to `meta`'s), it drops a reed **MARSH** at the **downhill TOE** (a band just beyond the field's downhill edge - the low, undrained valley toe below the drainage line) and fills the surrounding non-arable margins with the **cut-over SCRUB commons** (`role="grazing"`: coarse grass + a FEW scraggly pines). **CHINA-FIRST is the key rule here (do not re-litigate): the south-China rice hills were stripped for fuel/timber over ~1,000 years, so the DOMINANT cover past the settlement is denuded scrub / rough grazing, NOT forest.** The protected fengshui grove is the green exception, and managed **WOODLAND** (coppice / bamboo / tung-oil / tea-oil "economic forest") is a **FEW discrete PATCHES** the gen adds by hand on the higher / farther ground (`s.commons([...], role="woodland")`), SET BACK from the sun-needing crops by the scrub between. So hinterland lays the scrub + marsh; the woodland patches are per-map. Realism rules baked in: (1) the scrub clothes ALL non-arable open ground, INCLUDING the voids an irregular field leaves inside its own bbox - not just the outer margin strips (see "Interior fill" below); the check `commons_clear_of_paddies` tests the DRAWN OUTCOME (does the patch cover any open ground?), NOT the bbox centroid - the scatter cannot draw on a paddy by construction, so a centroid test was a wrong proxy that failed a legitimate interior fill; (2) the **dwellings stay CLEAR of cover** via a **keep-out** (`avoid=`) - but its SHAPE is form-dependent: a NUCLEATED cluster keeps out its house-bbox (one tight blob), while a DISPERSED settlement keeps out **each homestead individually** (house + yard + garden + grove), because the bbox of ringing farmsteads is the whole map and would forbid all cover (`commons()` and `marsh()` both take `avoid=`, a list of keep-out polygons). `down_deg` orients BOTH the marsh (a CONTOUR BAND perpendicular to the fall, spanning the valley - not an axis-aligned box; see the `dwellings_above_field_drain` note) and which frame side the scrub ring omits.
  - **Interior fill (the fan-void fix, GM 2026-07).** The scrub ring lays strips only OUTSIDE the cultivated bbox, but an irregular field (a comb FAN) does not fill its own bbox - it leaves open VOIDS INSIDE it. Those voids used to render as BARE ground (nothing clothed them: the strips are outside them, the marsh is a contour band below them), which read as conspicuous "empty space" (the only uncovered land on the map). `hinterland()` now also scatters scrub over the WHOLE cultivated bbox; since the scatter already skips every field/lane/watercourse/building/keep-out, it lands ONLY in those voids, clothing them as the rough grazing they are (ground the crop does not use is still grazed). This RETIRED the old per-map hack (Akagahara/Ikegami used to hand-add a `role="grazing"` quad to fill the fan corner). This applies across scales: hamlets call `hinterland()`; the villages that have the water-first hinterland (Hoshigaoka, Ueda) hand-place their scrub + marsh and just add the `role="woodland"` patches on their high ground. (Kikuta / Hikari are the older DISPERSED villages with per-farmstead groves and no surrounding hinterland at all - they would need the full scrub+marsh+patches treatment added if wanted.)
  - **Three DISTINCT `commons` looks so land types read APART at a glance (the GM's rule - grass and woods must NOT look the same).** The bug this fixed: a single grass-plus-scattered-pine glyph made "grazing" and "forest" indistinguishable. Now `s.commons(poly, role=...)` draws three clearly different textures: **`role="woodland"`** = individual, spaced tree CROWNS, an OPEN coppice canopy (gaps show) - clearly TREES, the managed-wood PATCHES, but lighter/more-open than the dense DARK closed-canopy fengshui village **grove** (so those two also stay distinct); **`role="commons"` (default) / `"grazing"`** = the cut-over fuel/fodder SCRUB, grass + a FEW scraggly pines - the DOMINANT denuded-hill cover; **`role="pasture"`** = OPEN grass tufts + brush, NO trees (available for a purely-grazed margin, not used by the scrub-dominant default). So a map reads distinct covers: **marsh** (blue-green reeds, wet) / **scrub** (grass + sparse scraggly pines, dominant) / **woodland** (open green crown clumps, the few patches) / **grove** (dark closed canopy, the fengshui exception). `pasture` and `woodland` are general hinterland land, so - like `grazing` - they are **exempt from `commons_beyond_the_windbreak`** (only the fuel/fodder `commons` proper follows the village->grove->commons toposequence); `commons_clear_of_paddies` still applies to all.
  - **Placement mechanics.** The scrub is placed as picture-frame side-strips of the CULTIVATED bbox (paddy + dry plots) on the three non-toe sides (the toe side is the marsh), each strip lying OUTSIDE the bbox so its centroid clears the paddy. The scatter SKIPS fields/pond/lanes/houses + the hamlet keep-out and BLEEDS off the frame (neither marsh nor commons is a `_CROP_HARD` crop anchor), so it fills only the OPEN ground and the frame still hugs the hard content. **Call it AFTER fields + cluster + pond + dry fields, BEFORE the windbreak grove** (so the dense grove draws over the sparse cover) **and before `crop_to_content`**; the `role="woodland"` patches go AFTER (crowns on top of the scrub). The interior fill (above) now clothes the comb-FAN voids automatically, so a strongly one-sided fan (Ikegami's NE, Akagahara's SW) no longer needs a hand-added scrub quad; the `role="woodland"` patches are still per-map on the high ground. The pond-fringe reeds (`marsh role='pond_fringe'`) and the village funerary/back-slope hand-placements still layer on top as before. Tests: `test_hinterland_scrub_ring_and_marsh_downhill_each_cardinal` (in `tests/settlement/`) (four cardinal branches), `test_hinterland_honors_hamlet_keepout_and_dry_plot_extent` (keep-out + dry extent + avoid-skip), `test_hinterland_flags_default_downdeg_and_empty_field`, `test_commons_glyph_variants_draw_and_record_each_role` (glyphs + avoid); `test_commons_beyond_the_windbreak_exempts_general_hinterland_land` (in `tests/check_village/`).
  - **Woodland patches must not OVERLAP crops nor BLOCK THEIR LIGHT** (`woodland_clear_of_crops`, GM 2026-07 - both the placement and the check enforce it). Two rules: (1) a coppice patch never overlaps a field (a canopy over the crop competes for root/light); (2) the SUN is to the south (maps are north-up), so a tree casts its shadow toward the NORTH - a patch may sit just **north/beside** a crop, but on the crop's **SOUTH (sunny) side** it must stand a canopy's-shadow back or it shades the field. Covers BOTH the paddy and the dry *hatake* plots. The check uses a fixed crown-radius no-overhang `CLEAR` (~14px) everywhere plus a scaled shadow-reach `SHADE` (~14 + 55 ft/px) on any crop's south side (a patch south of a crop with an x-overlapping shadow column). WHY this matters: Ikegami/Hoshigaoka first shipped with coppice patches placed "above the paddy" that actually landed ON the dry-hatake ribbon (the hatake is the N hem, *above* the paddy) - the GM caught trees sitting on and shading the dry crops. So place woodland on the genuinely-open high ground (north of / beside the fields, clear of the dry ribbon), not merely "off the paddy." Helper `poly_gap(a, b)` (0 if two polys overlap/cross, else min distance) backs the check. Teeth: `test_woodland_clear_of_crops_fires_on_overlap_and_shade_passes_when_set_back_north` (in `tests/check_village/`) + `test_poly_gap_overlap_containment_edgecross_and_separated`.
  - **A woodland patch is a DISTINCT wood from the fengshui GROVE** (`woodland_clear_of_grove`, GM 2026-07). The coppice-commons patches and the protected village grove (`village_groves` - windbreak / water-mouth / copse) must NOT overlap, or the two woods merge into one indistinct green mass. The check fires if any grove clump center falls inside a patch or within the clump's drawn radius of it; place the coppice on its OWN stretch of high ground. This bit Hoshigaoka: an NW pond-catchment patch landed on the west windbreak belt - moved to the N ridge (east of the title bay), well clear. So the map keeps separate woods-textures: the dense dark fengshui grove (near the cluster) vs the lighter open coppice patches (on the far high ground) vs the sparse scrub (dominant). Teeth: `test_woodland_clear_of_grove_fires_when_on_the_fengshui_grove` (in `tests/check_village/`).
  - **A settlement can have a real FOREST as its wood (Moritono), and the hinterland adapts** (GM 2026-07). Moritono - the forest-hamlet - was redone to-scale water-first: it keeps its distinctive `forest()` (the Shirin Forest filling the EAST, the high wooded ground + the water SOURCE) and the magistrate's `manor()` at the forest's edge, with water flowing **E->W** (`down_deg=180`) - a source *tameike* fed by a brook out of the forest, the comb paddy draining W to a marsh at the low toe. Three engine tweaks let `hinterland()` serve this: (1) the scrub `ring()` **clamps its bands to the canvas + a small bleed** instead of overshooting to `max(W,H)` - a wide E->W hamlet otherwise scattered a huge off-canvas apron of scrub that blew resvg's node limit; (2) `crop_to_content` now **frames to include a `forest`** (a point-list edge feature, so it sets the frame like `_CROP_HARD`) and **clamps the view to the canvas** so an edge feature that fills to the map edge is not cropped off and does not "stop short" of the frame (the brook likewise runs to the E map edge, `frm={"kind":"offmap"}`, under the forest that is drawn over it); (3) a new **`hinterland(skip_sides=(...))`** param can suppress the scrub band on a named frame flank (e.g. a forest side) - Moritono in the end does NOT skip: the E rough ground (around the manor, between the pond and the forest) reads better carrying scrub, and the scatter already skips the forest/manor/pond blocks, so the wood, the walls, and the water stay clear while the grazing fills the gap.
  - **A FOREST is drawn as INDIVIDUAL TREES, never as a terrain type** (GM 2026-07-25; `settlement._tree_stand`, used by both `forest()` and `forest_patch()`). The old rendering was a flat pale wash under a widely-spaced grid of identical dots - it read as patterned GROUND rather than as trees, and it clashed with every other piece of vegetation on these maps (yashikirin, fengshui belt, dooryard copse), all of which were already drawn as real crowns. So a wood is now a STAND: one drawn crown per canopy tree, and the polygon survives only as DATA (what it blocks, what it frames, where the brook comes from) - never as a shape whose outline you can see.
  - *(why: [`../research/vegetation.md#forest-density-and-crown-size`](../research/vegetation.md#forest-density-and-crown-size))*
    - *The edge is made of TREES, not a line.* No boundary stroke, and the crowns are NOT clipped to the polygon (a clipped stand reads as a ruled terrain boundary - the exact defect this replaced), so they break the outline the way a real wood does. The litter FLOOR (`FOREST_FLOOR`, glimpsed between crowns) is therefore drawn INSET from the polygon - a crown's width back from the tree line, or shrunk toward a copse's center - so its hard edge lies under the canopy. Beyond the line, `_stand_fringe` scatters thinning **advance growth on the cut-over margin**, in THICKETS rather than an even sprinkle (a coarse position-seeded mask decides which stretches have seeded and which have been kept clear by grazing and fuel-cutting), each fringe tree registered as a block poly so later placement never drops a building on one, and each tested by `_fringe_blocked` so the margin never grows in the crop, on open water, or on a way.
    - **NO TREE IS DRAWN ON A ROOF OR A WELLHEAD** (GM 2026-07-25; `settlement._crown_covers` at placement, `structures_clear_of_trees` + `wells_clear_of_trees` in the gate). A crown over a building erases the building - the reader loses a structure the map exists to show - so no drawn crown may overlap any ROOFED footprint (`_CANOPY_STRUCT_KEYS`: dwellings, urban buildings, kura/flophouses/byres/sheds, temples and shrines, manors, ministries, the trade works, the notice board) or any wellhead. This supersedes the old "a grove may hug the eaves" allowance, which let the reserved-area keep-outs stop at `clump*0.5` while the DRAWN canopy reached `clump*0.90`.
    - *The filter is PER CROWN, not per feature.* A yashikirin belt is drawn hard against the house it shelters and a village copse threads between the dwellings, so pushing the whole stand back would gut the feature. Instead every crown - stand, fringe, fengshui clump, homestead arm, bamboo top - is tested individually and simply not drawn if it would cover a structure: the wood THINS around the building and keeps its shape everywhere else. Open-air work grounds (threshing yards, kitchen gardens) are deliberately out of scope: they have their own sun-corridor rules, and a canopy overhanging a yard corner is a real thing.
    - *Trees are drawn LAST, so the filter sees the whole map.* Two ordering fixes make the rule hold by construction rather than by luck. (1) A tree stand's CANOPY is queued at `forest()`/`forest_patch()` time and drawn at crop time (`flush_tree_stands`, with a backstop in `finish()` for a map that never crops) - the same deferral the stable yards use, and for the same reason: a wood is drawn EARLY because it is terrain the settlement is sited against, but half the buildings are placed after it. Only the litter floor stays down early, under everything. (2) The per-house yashikirin arms now draw AFTER their farmstead's house/shed instead of before. They used to be a back layer the house painted over, which HID the overlap rather than preventing it and left crowns geometrically under roofs; drawing them last means the keep-out sees the house, so one rule covers every path and no z-order trick is load-bearing.
    - *The check reads the DRAWN crowns, not the reserved area.* Every crown the engine emits is recorded in `M['tree_crowns']` as a flat `[x, y, r, ...]` run (flat because a to-scale map draws thousands - Tango 7.4k, Hirameki 7.2k, Moritono 3.5k). A grove's recorded rect or clump center is where trees MAY stand; `tree_crowns` is where they DO, and since placement drops crowns individually, only the drawn geometry can tell the two apart. Both sides use the DRAWN box of a structure (`vw`/`vh` when a location marker like the kosatsuba draws above its true footprint), exactly as the well clearances use `vr` over `r`. Placement leaves a 0.6px margin (`CANOPY_PAD`) purely so a crown drawn tangent to a wall cannot round, at the manifest's 0.1px precision, into a hair of overlap; the check itself is exact.
    - *A canvas-filling wood is REVEALED only a band deep* (`FOREST_REVEAL_FT = 110`, ~8 ranks of trees past the tree line; `forest_reveal_x`, mirrored in `check_village.py` so `crop_hugs_content` measures by the same rule). `forest()` records its TREE LINE (`M['forest_edge']`) separately from the filled polygon, and `crop_to_content` frames to the tree line + the reveal band instead of to the whole canvas-clamped wood: enough canopy to read as a wood running off the map, while everything deeper is identical crowns and would be wasted image (GM 2026-07-25: "only enough to make it clear that the forest is there"). A BOUNDED copse (`forest_patch`) is exempt - it is framed whole, because unlike a canvas-filling wood its shape is the feature.
    - *... and it is NOT frame-setting at all on the axis it RUNS ALONG* (`forest_frame_span`, shared by `crop_to_content` and `crop_hugs_content`). The reveal band fixes the axis the wood FACES; the other axis needed the same argument and did not have it, so Moritono's north edge sat pinned at the canvas top while the northernmost real content - a well - stood 157px in (GM 2026-07-25). A tree line that runs off BOTH ends of an axis is running along it, not bounding anything: the wood continues past whatever frame we choose, so holding the view open to the canvas edge for it buys nothing but more of the same crowns, and the line still runs off a tighter edge - which is the reading we want. On such an axis the wood takes the span the rest of the content sets (it can neither extend nor shrink the frame); a tree line that ENDS inside the canvas bounds something real, so its own span stays content.
