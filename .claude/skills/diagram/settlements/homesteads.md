# Homesteads: farmhouses, yards, gardens, groves, village form

*Part of the Mode B settlement docs. The index, the `meta()` knobs, the workflow and the validator contract live in [`../settlements.md`](../settlements.md).*

**Load this file when:** placing dwellings and their appurtenances at any rural tier, or rolling a village's variation knobs.

**Research:** the historical basis for these rules - what was found, the decision it drove, and every disclosed departure - is in [`../research/homesteads.md`](../research/homesteads.md). Load it when you are CHANGING a rule or questioning one, not to follow it.

- **The headman is a FARMSTEAD, not an exception** (GM 2026-07-21, caught on Hikari no Sato): `s.headman(x, y)` routes through the standard homestead bundle in BOTH settlement styles (nucleated AND dispersed), so the grandest house in the village gets its threshing yard + dooryard garden like every other farm - capped to an ORDINARY farm's appurtenance sizes in both paths (a big house does not mean a field-sized saien; `garden_area_within_norms` holds). In the dispersed style the bundle brings the per-house grove too when room allows, and the solver drops it gracefully in a dense cluster (neighbor tree cover shelters the house - the grove is the one optional part). `harvest_yards_present` and `gardens_present` no longer exempt `role=="headman"` - that carve-out was written around the old lone-house headman bug. **And the headman ALWAYS has an attached fireproof kura** (`headman_has_kura`, GM 2026-07-21): the shoya/nanushi is by definition among the village's most prosperous farmers, and the office functionally needs one - tax ledgers, land registers, and tax rice awaiting collection are exactly what fireproof storage is for. The ~30% kura roll is for ORDINARY plains (the kura keeps meaning wealth because most farms lack one); the headman sits at the top of that distribution by construction, so `role=="headman"` forces `shed=True`. Watch the knock-on when retrofitting: the headman's larger bundle reservation re-rolls the whole packing sequence, so expect to re-seed the house scatter (Hikari went `SEED + 1` -> `SEED + 4`) or re-order a fixed-coordinate set-apart feature to before the crop (the kura repack shifted Hoshigaoka's west extent and clipped its graveyard).
### Village variation knobs (feature 005) - the roll engine, the Family-A catalog, the twin-detector

Two villages with the SAME water direction (`down_deg`) used to come out near-identical (Kikuta was a copy of Hoshigaoka down to the headman's house position), because the generator fixed one value on every layout axis. Feature 005 replaces those hidden constants with **historically-typed, seed-rolled knobs**, and adds a pool-level check that mechanically guards against re-twinning.

**The roll engine** (`settlement/_knobs.py`: `Knob`, `knob_rng`, `register_knob`, `resolve_knob`, and the `Settlement.pin_knob`/`resolve`/`knob_context` surface). A spec MAY **pin** any knob; every unpinned knob is resolved by an **independent, deterministic roll off the map seed** (`knob_rng` derives a per-knob sub-seed with SHA-256 - Python's `hash()` is per-process salted, so it can't give cross-run determinism), filtered by the knob's **typing rule**. Resolution order is **pinned → rolled → default**; a pinned value that is out of its value space or violates its typing rule is a loud error, never silently drawn. *Why independent per knob, not preset "village-type" bundles (research.md D3):* independent draws give the widest variety and make "pin one knob, roll the rest" natural, while the per-knob typing rules - not a bundle - keep a combination historically coherent.

**The Family-A catalog** (registered at import; China-first value spaces + typing rules, per research.md D2). The typing rules read the village's stated geography from the context (`water_kind`, `field_origin`, `scale`, ...) so a roll respects it:
- `cluster_position` (high_margin / flank / mid_margin / valley_mouth / valley_head / on_rise) - villages sat on whatever non-arable higher/drier ground existed; 背山面水 ("back to the hill, face the water") orients the back to high ground and the face to water, but which corner that is depends on the valley, so the "high windward margin" is one common case, not the only one.
- `cluster_shape` (round / elongated / crescent / split) - cluster shape followed the available dry ground: a knoll gives a round blob, a levee a linear string, a field-edge a crescent, two flanking patches a 2-hamlet split. *Typing:* `split` needs room for two hamlets to read separately (a village/town, not a hamlet).
- `lane_skeleton` (spine / T / Y / cross / waterside) - a nucleated village's lanes followed its site: a spine along a ridge, a T where a spur met the field track, a Y where two approaches merged, a cross at a small market node (草市), a waterside lane in a stream village. The headman compound and the primary-shrine gateway are **derived from the skeleton's focal points** (`skeleton_layout` / `Settlement.lane_skeleton`), never a fixed pixel offset - which is precisely why two same-water villages stop sharing a headman position. *Typing:* `waterside` needs water running ALONG the cluster (a stream/canal beside it), so it is excluded for a pond-fed valley whose only water is a valley-head reservoir uphill.
- `water_source_position` (pond: corner_{NW,NE,SW,SE} / mid_margin / chain; stream: edge_{N,E,S,W}) - a *tameike* sat wherever the catchment delivered water above the command area (not always the NW corner); small villages often had a chain of little ponds rather than one big reservoir; a stream diversion entered from whichever edge the valley fed. *Typing:* the `edge_*` entries need a stream source and the pond positions need a pond; gravity feed (source uphill of the intake) is enforced at placement, not as a value exclusion.
- `plot_size` (small_irregular / medium / large_block / strip) + `plot_regularity` (organic / grid) - plot size and regularity varied with land-division history: tiny hand-shaped plots in old organically-grown fields, rectilinear blocks under a survey/allotment system (the 条里 *jōri* grid in Japan; well-field/allotment remnants in China), long strips under strip tenancy. *Typing:* a `grid` bund pattern implies a planned/surveyed field, so it is excluded unless `field_origin="planned"`.
- `grain_drift` (degrees off the fall-line) - real valleys let the paddy grain drift rather than holding one rigid angle; this resolves the "uniform 45° grain" residual flagged under Fields. Always allowed. Wired through `build_comb(..., grain_drift=)` (offsets the dry-plot contour heading, so the recorded `theta` - and the twin-detector's `grain_orient` - shift without moving any plot polygon).

Generator surface for the shipped knobs: `s.lane_skeleton(kind, cx, cy, ex, ey)` lays the lanes and returns the DERIVED `headman`/`gateway` focal points; `s.cluster_seeds(shape, cx, cy, ex, ey, n, rng)` yields shape-aware house seeds (round / elongated / crescent / split) to feed `try_place`; `s.line_seeds(p0, p1, n, half_band, rng)` yields a LINEAR ribbon of seeds strung along a road/margin/dike (the `settlement_form="linear"` archetype - houses front a line instead of clustering in a blob; per research.md D5 the cheapest big structural difference between two same-region villages, and the reason Kikuta now reads as a ribbon village against Hoshigaoka's nucleated blob); `s.scatter_seeds(cx, cy, rx, ry, n, rng)` yields an EVEN scatter over a broad area (the `settlement_form="dispersed"` archetype - the kainyo strewn-farmstead pattern; pair it with `s._nucleated = False` so each farm draws its OWN yashikirin grove instead of a communal windbreak). **Sizing caveat for dispersed (grounding):** a dispersed hamlet needs roughly TWICE the margin room per farm as a nucleated one - each homestead carries its own grove and keeps its own spacing (no tight packing), and the bundle solver hugs them all to the field edge into a single-file frontage - so a settlement declared at the nucleated-pack household count will not fit that many dispersed farms in the same margin. Demonstrate `dispersed` on a roomier field (a full village) or declare fewer households, not on a hamlet sized for a nucleated cluster. **Focal-feature catalog** (the optional foci the `focal_set` axis reads): dedicated glyph methods `s.crescent_pond(cx, cy, r, facing_deg)` (半月塘, drawn in the shared water block, reserved as a keep-out; **it labels itself "geomantic pond"** - GM 2026-07-21, the banyuetang is culturally specific and does not read by itself (the one deliberate exception to don't-label-the-obvious), and "geomantic" is the GM's chosen term. *What it is (recorded because the GM asked)*: GEOMANTIC, not religious - no deity, no rites; cosmological engineering like orienting a house south. The village wants "mountain behind, water in front" (背山面水): the hill + fengshui grove are the back half, and where no river obliges the village DIGS the front half - still water gathers and holds qi/wealth where flowing water carries it away, and the HALF shape leaves the lineage room to grow (complete things can only wane). Grove-arc behind + water-arc in front cradle the village as one system; practically it is the rain-fed runoff basin (deliberately UNCONNECTED to irrigation), fire water, fish/duck/washing water, with the flat-side bank as the open threshing/ceremony forecourt. The shrine is where religion happens; the pond is how a well-sited village should be shaped. And **no vegetation touches its water**: the grove placement keeps a full-disk canopy keep-out (the flat-side band doubles as the pond's open forecourt, historically the settlement's ceremony/work ground) and the scrub/reed scatter skips the water like any other water body; gated by `trees_clear_of_fengshui_ponds` + `crescent_pond_labeled`) and `s.mill(x, y, wheel_side)` (水磨, a mill house + waterwheel beside a watercourse); the composition-based foci (a secondary earth-god shrine via `shrine_hall(primary=False)`, an ancestral hall, a market clearing at a lane junction) are recorded with `s.note_focal(kind)` so they read on the manifest without a bespoke glyph. Every focal feature is placed by the same overlap / set-back invariants as hand-authored content and is classified in `check_village`'s overlap system (`crescent_ponds`, `mills`).

**Knob resolvers + the roll-from-seed entrypoint (US2/US3/US4).** Every registered knob now has a geometry *driver*, so a knob is a real lever and not just a recorded label:
- `s.cluster_anchor(position, field_bbox, down_deg)` resolves `cluster_position` (6 dry-margin seats: high_margin / valley_head / mid_margin / flank / valley_mouth / on_rise) into a cluster anchor + margin-oriented extents; off the paddy, off the flood toe. *Grounding (research.md D2):* a village took the best DRY ground beside its paddy (背山面水), and which dry ground was available - the high back, a head terrace, a side levee, a valley-mouth shoulder, a knoll - is the variation.
- `s.water_source_anchor(position, field_bbox, down_deg)` + `s.water_sources_for(down_deg, water_kind)` resolve `water_source_position` into the sluice/stream-entry point on the field's UPHILL margin, and REJECT a downhill (gravity-impossible) source; the roll picks from the legal set. *Grounding:* Chinese canal doctrine feeds a comb from its high end; the varied high-side entry is the knob.
- `s.plot_texture(plot_size, plot_regularity)` maps those two knobs onto `build_comb`'s `(plot_across, row_step)` so the paddy grain actually changes (small = many small paddies, large_block = few big, strip = narrow-long; `grid` tightens the row-step spread into a surveyed look). Sizes are picked in REAL FEET via `waterfields.paddy_grain` (see **Paddy cell size** below), not raw px, so the same knob means the same real paddy at every scale.

- `s.roll_village(name, households, down_deg, water_kind, field_fall)` **rolls every unpinned knob and builds a complete gate-passing map with ZERO hand-placed coordinates** (SC-004): water source → sluice, plot_texture → grain, cluster_position/shape → homestead cloud (seated constructively on the field's actual drawn edge, biased clear of the intake so it always abuts + rings the field on any fan shape), lane_skeleton → lanes, plus wells / windbreak / bridges / hinterland. Same seed → byte-identical; different seeds → different combinations (`pool/hamlets/shimizu` seed 7, `pool/hamlets/honda` seed 8). It shares `s.draw_comb_field(net, name, source)`, the ~50-line comb-drawing + water-topology helper. *Current scope:* a nucleated hamlet/village; ~1/3 of arbitrary seeds pass the full gate today (the constructive placement is robustened over time), so a shipped rolled map is a chosen passing seed.
- **Completed focal catalog** (T020): beyond `crescent_pond` + `mill`, dedicated glyph methods `s.ancestral_hall` (祠堂), `s.water_mouth` (水口), `s.market` (墟/市), and `s.secondary_shrine` each draw, record, reserve a keep-out, and `note_focal(kind)`.

**The twin-detector** (`check_village.py`: `twin_axes`, `twin_diff_count`, `twin_report`) is a POOL-level tool run alongside - not inside - the per-map gate. For every pair of villages that SHARE a `down_deg`, it counts how many of 8 coarse structural axes fall in different buckets - `cluster_region`, `cluster_shape`, `headman_side`, `lane_skeleton`, `water_source`, `focal_set`, `grain_orient`, `settlement_form` (nucleated / linear / dispersed / water-town - the biggest structural read, defaulting to `nucleated`) - and flags the pair `TWINNED` when fewer than **4** differ (else `PASS`). *Why these choices (research.md D6):* **same-`down_deg` scoping** - villages that already differ by water direction are trivially distinguishable, so comparing them would dilute the signal. **Coarse buckets** (which side / which type / which octant, via `_dir8`), never pixel positions - the axes must answer "a different KIND of place?", not "moved a few px?", so genuine near-variants are not falsely flagged. A `None` axis is "no evidence", never counted as a difference, so a data gap can't manufacture distinctiveness. The **4-of-8 threshold** is the tuning target: high enough that a pair sharing most axes really does read as a copy, low enough that two honestly-different villages clear it. *Worked calibration:* the re-varied **Kikuta** (a LINEAR ribbon along the field margin, drifted field grain) vs **Hoshigaoka** (a NUCLEATED blob with a fengshui crescent pond) differ on `settlement_form`, `cluster_shape`, `headman_side`, `focal_set`, and `grain_orient` = 5 axes → PASS; before the re-vary they differed on 1 → TWINNED. The GM's read confirmed the calibration: the earlier 4-axis PASS (a cross vs a spine skeleton) still "looked similar" because it varied only INTERNAL detail on a shared archetype - the fix was the structural `settlement_form` knob (ribbon vs blob), which is why that axis was added and weighted as the biggest structural read.

### Houses

- Stylized pitched-roof glyphs: two shaded roof planes (darker north slope, lighter sunlit south slope) with a ridge highlight along the long axis, entry on the south face. NOT flat envelope/box icons.
- South-facing convention: ridge runs E-W, facade faces south (historical minka sun orientation).
- **A fireproof *kura* grain storehouse is a WEALTH MARKER on ~30% of plain farms - an ATTACHED annex, never a free-floating shed** (`farm_sheds_attached`; recorded `M['farm_sheds']`, a `_CROP_HARD` feature). It abuts the house wall and is drawn WITH the house in `farmsteads()` so a move-procedure can never strand it (the regression that prompted the check: the kura got detached and drifted into the courtyard). The decision is position-seeded (`_hjit(x,y,3.0) < 0.30`, no RNG ripple) so it is stable against a re-RUN of the same map - but NOT against a re-PACK, because the seed is the house's POSITION and a placer that re-seats a house re-rolls its kura (corrected 2026-08-17, after a re-pack took Kashikawa 25% -> 15% in one roll and it read as a disappearing-kura bug; the hash itself is honest, measuring 0.2993 over 200k coordinates, and the live pool sits at 343/1208 = 28.4% against the 30% knob). Key the roll on a household index instead if stability across re-packing is ever actually wanted, and the kura's footprint is RESERVED in the homestead bundle bbox so a neighbor never lands on it. **Which wall:** a DISPERSED farm puts it on the WEST; a NUCLEATED-cluster house puts it on the shaded NORTH (back) wall - the cluster hugs the field to the east so the sunny walls are the garden's, and the north is the one side the garden never takes, so a north kura stays clear of it (`gardens_clear_of_sheds` reads the recorded footprint and guards this). Distinct glyph from the shared draft-animal byre below. The headman's house is the largest and carries no kura.
- Houses never overlap and never sit on a road/lane or have water running through them.

- **Draft animals live in the farmhouse, not a barn; rural settlements have no hay barns.** East Asian wet-rice farming is not herd-based, so there are no European-style barns. The peasant farmhouse - specifically its *doma* (earthen-floored work bay) - stalls the draft ox or horse **under the same roof**, including for winter, so the farmhouse glyph (with its optional attached `shed` storeroom) already implies both the storeroom and the animal housing. Do **NOT** add barns to a village or hamlet. Barns + grazing pasture (`s.pasture(...)`, `s.building(..., "barn")`) appear ONLY for a specific reason: a **road / post town's horse-relay infrastructure** (Hoshizora, on the Imperial Road - remounts + hayfields to feed them) or a **horse-breeding domain** (Unicorn / Moto). Town **stables** are likewise not drawn separately - warhorses are inside the magistrate's manor, travelers' mounts at the (unlabelled) inns.
- **DRAFT-ANIMAL BYRES** (`s.draft_byres(fraction, gap)`; recorded `M['byres']`, a first-class overlap struct). Wet-rice plowing/puddling turns on an ox or water buffalo, but a buffalo was a costly asset that poorer households SHARED or hired - so a village keeps only a MINORITY of byres (~one per 4-5 households, `fraction~0.2`), shared open-fronted sheds standing in the COURTYARDS among the homesteads, NOT one per farm. HOUSE-DRIVEN placement: for the wealthier homesteads (buffalo owners) in turn, spiral out from the house to the nearest clear gap just past its reserved footprint (fits-checked off every other footprint / lane / block / crop, kept `gap` px apart), skipping any homestead boxed in - a grid scatter like the wells finds almost nothing because the nucleated homestead bboxes pack the cluster. Call AFTER `farmsteads()` + `place_wells` and BEFORE the grove (which then skips the byres). The glyph is a small plank-roofed shed with a dark stall mouth, distinct from the solid gray *kura* and the dwellings. **Sizing (1px = 2ft at village scale):** the shed footprint is ~15.5x10.5px = ~31x21ft = **~16 m^2**, right for a single-bay lean-to stalling 1-2 draft animals (an ox/buffalo needs ~6-8 m^2 of stall plus fodder/passage room); it must NOT read as a European multi-stall barn (there are none - see the draft-animals-live-in-the-house rule above), so keep it small.
- **Field-adjacency is TUNABLE by settlement form** (`meta.nucleated`). DISPERSED (default): every farmhouse fronts its own fields, so each must be within ~165px of a field (`all_houses_field_adjacent`). NUCLEATED (`meta(nucleated=True)`): the houses cluster and the fields radiate from the cluster edge, so interior houses are legitimately a cluster-span back - per-house adjacency is wrong. Instead the whole CLUSTER must ABUT its fields (`cluster_abuts_fields`): the nearest house is field-adjacent AND no house is farther than the cluster's own diameter past that edge. Set `nucleated` to pick the invariant.
  - **...and on a dispersed map the field OUTLINE must BE the planting** (`field_outline_matches_planting`, GM 2026-07: *"a single line of farmhouses that stretches much further south than the rice paddies themselves do"* / *"there are still 3 farmhouses south of the paddies"*). A field carries two extents: `outline`/`bbox` is the smoothed ENVELOPE the water net claims, `vis_bbox` is the extent of the plots actually DRAWN. They diverge when a gen declares more field than the comb can fill - `build_comb`'s plots stop where the terrain/drain stops them whatever `field_fall` asks for, and the surplus becomes a **PHANTOM TAIL**: invisible on the rendered map, but fully real to every distance test. Because the adjacency above measures to the OUTLINE, a farmstead hugging that tail reads as "field-adjacent" while standing well out past the last rice. **Akagahara** shipped a 181px tail (`field_fall=1440` against plots that ended ~180px higher), which is precisely how it grew a line of farms hanging south of its paddy - and `all_houses_field_adjacent` PASSED the whole time, because both the gen's placement filter and the gate were measuring the same phantom. The fix was to size `field_fall` to what the comb actually fills (1260), not to nudge the farms. **Scoped to DISPERSED maps**, where the envelope is load-bearing for placement; a NUCLEATED cluster is seeded as a unit and never rides the envelope, so a tail there is inert (**Hoshigaoka** and **Kikuta** both carry ~210px tails harmlessly, and reshaping their GM-tuned fields to satisfy a check that protects nothing would be churn). Tolerance 60px allows the honest rounding of a smoothed rim over irregular plots while staying well under the ~165px band it protects. **The general lesson**: when a check measures distance to a derived outline, something must pin that outline to what is actually drawn - otherwise the check is only as honest as its geometry, and a silently-generous envelope makes a green gate meaningless. Teeth: `test_field_outline_matches_planting_fires_on_a_phantom_tail` (in `tests/check_village/`), which asserts BOTH that the new check fires AND that `all_houses_field_adjacent` stays blind on the same manifest.
- **Every farmhouse-bearing map needs a HARVEST-PROCESSING layer** (`harvest_yards_present`, `harvest_yards_smaller_than_farmhouse`, `harvest_yards_on_sunny_side`, `harvest_yards_clear_of_paddies`, `harvest_yards_clear_of_structures`, for `town` / `village` / `hamlet` AND `city`). A rice settlement does not just grow the crop - it threshes and dries it, and that working landscape is as characteristic as the paddies. Historically Japan processed rice at the **farmstead**, not on a central communal floor: drying happened on *hazakake* racks erected in the fields, and threshing/hulling/winnowing on each household's own packed-earth work yard (the *niwa* / *kadoniwa*). So the model is **per-farmstead, not communal**, and the work yard was **UNIVERSAL** - every farming household processed its own rice, so **every farmhouse gets a yard** (a firm 100%). The yard is **independent** of the ~30% that carry a shed (a farmhouse may have neither, either, or both - the shed/storehouse, by contrast, was NOT universal: a fireproof *kura* was a wealth marker and a simple *naya* was common but not on every plot, so it stays a minority). The yard is a small earthen apron with a straw mat + a little drying rack. **Architecture (important): farmstead drawing is DEFERRED.** `try_place` / `headman` reserve + record a farmhouse but do NOT draw it; the gen then calls **`s.farmsteads()` LAST** (after every other feature) which, for each farmhouse, finds a yard spot, NUDGES the house a little if needed (with every obstacle now known), draws the yard then the house over it, and **drops** any farmhouse that truly cannot host a yard anywhere nearby. This keeps base placement identical to a yard-free map (so population/plurality/household counts are unaffected) while guaranteeing the yard. Wells go AFTER `farmsteads()` so they sit among the final houses; `place_wells` has a coverage pass that guarantees no dwelling is left in a well-gap. Five rules: (1) **present (100%)** - EVERY farmhouse has a yard (`harvest_yards_present`: no house lacks one, matched by the yard's `of` = parent center). Because farmsteads drops the rare un-yardable house, ring counts are set a little generously so enough survive (e.g. plurality/household floors still hold). (2) **smaller than its farmhouse** (`harvest_yards_smaller_than_farmhouse`) - the niwa is a small dry apron, not a second dwelling; it scales with `bscale` so it stays smaller even on a compressed city map. (3) **on the sunny SOUTH/front side** (`harvest_yards_on_sunny_side`) - the yard is the *maeniwa*, the south-facing front work yard: rice must dry in the SUN and minka face south (`houses_face_south`), so the yard sits on the house's south/front side, NEVER the shady north back. (+y is south.) This follows the *sun*, not the field - so when the paddy is to the south the yard falls between house and field, but a northern paddy does NOT pull the yard onto the shady side. (4) **clear of the paddies** (`harvest_yards_clear_of_paddies`) - a DRY floor, footprint out of every flooded field. (5) **clear of every OTHER structure** (`harvest_yards_clear_of_structures`) - the yard abuts only its OWN farmhouse, never another building (placed before OR after it - which is why farmsteads runs last, when all obstacles are known). Applies to the **city** too (its in-wall agricultural district + the outside farm rings); only a map with no farmhouses at all is exempt.
- **A nucleated cluster is a COMPACT FABRIC, not a wide hollow arc** (drives `village_cluster_compact`; the crescent seed shape in `cluster_seeds`).
  - *The root cause (non-obvious).* The seed SHAPE barely matters: `_place_bundle_nucleated` pulls every house to hug the paddy edge and then packs ALONG it, drifting each house up to ~400px from its seed. WIDTH is what that pull amplifies, so an over-wide seed cloud (the old crescent spread `t*ex*1.15`, the widest of the four shapes) strings houses into a stranded arc. A NARROW seed cloud (like `elongated`, the tightest-packing shape) stays compact. So the fix narrows the crescent's lateral reach (`t*ex*0.5`) and lets DEPTH carry the frontage - a deep narrow blob packs far better than a wide shallow ribbon.
  - *The check + its calibration.* `village_cluster_compact` measures the BUILT coverage (houses + gardens + yards + farmstead groves) of the house convex hull - the thing `cluster_abuts_fields` misses, because that check allows each house a cluster-span from the field, so a big hollow cluster passes. Measured coverage: the pathological arc ~0.20; the `roll_village` placer's healthy nucleated villages ~0.28-0.31; the tightly hand-placed villages (Hoshigaoka, Hikari, Ueda) ~0.40. Floor **0.25** sits clear below the healthy band and above the pathology. Village scale + >=12 houses only (a hamlet is legitimately loose; a tiny hull is degenerate). The pre-fix Kikuta manifest is frozen as `pool/regressions/kikuta-hollow-crescent.json` so the check keeps its teeth.
  - *Honest limit.* The `roll_village` nucleated placer tops out around 0.31 (the hug-and-pack-along design is looser than hand placement), so this is a floor that catches the pathology, not a demand for hand-placed density. The fix improved Kikuta's coverage 0.20 -> 0.31 and halved its span (948 -> 533px); a few back-row houses still sit ~350px from the crops, which reads as the honest back of a compact village rather than a stranded arc.

- **Threshing / drying yards are PER-FARMSTEAD and UNIVERSAL** (drives `harvest_yards_present` = 100%, `harvest_yards_on_sunny_side`, `_smaller_than_farmhouse`, `_clear_of_paddies`, `_clear_of_structures`; the near-square irregular footprint also feeds `garden_plots_are_quads`).
  - *Per-farmstead, not communal.* Japan processed rice at the individual farmstead, on the household's own packed-earth work yard - the *niwa* / *kadoniwa* / *maeniwa* (front yard). This is a real divergence from **many European farming villages, which shared a common threshing floor / threshing green**, and from the Mediterranean/Chinese communal threshing floor. So we do NOT draw one big village threshing ground; every farm has its own.
  - *Universal.* Every farming household had to thresh, hull, and winnow its own crop (hand tools: *senba-koki* threshing comb, *tōmi* winnower, *dō-usu* hulling mill), so a work yard was an operational necessity, not a luxury - hence 100% of farmhouses, density permitting (the generator drops only a farmhouse too boxed-in to host a yard at all).
  - *Sun, not the field, sets its side.* Rice must dry in the sun and *minka* faced south, so the yard is the south-facing FRONT yard. When the paddy is south it lands between house and field (the classic gradient); a northern paddy does NOT pull the yard onto the shady back.
  - *Relative size.* A household's real work yard was small - on the order of ~100-300 m² against a ~1-*chō* (~1 ha = 10,000 m²) holding, i.e. **~1-3% of the cultivated area** - so a threshing yard is much smaller than the paddies, never larger. On the map we draw it a touch smaller than the farmhouse, and the audit of 2026-07-21 VERIFIED this honest end to end: the drawn yard (~60 m² at village scale) is actually below the real 100-300 m² yard range and inside the true 1-3%-of-holding band, and the farmhouse itself is true-scale (an earlier version of this note claimed a "legibility exaggeration" that the current code does not perform). Drying *racks* (*hazakake* / *inagi*) historically stood in the fields; we fold a small indicative rack into the yard glyph rather than tracking field racks separately.
  - *Size varies (DOWN from a maximum), shape stays near-square.* The base yard dims are the household's MAXIMUM apron (sized to the harvest it must dry), so they jitter DOWN (a smaller holding needs less drying floor) - the inverse of the garden, whose base is its minimum and jitters up. The footprint is a slightly-irregular inscribed quad like the garden but with **LESS** corner jitter: a threshing floor is a deliberately-swept, tamped work surface kept roughly rectangular for flailing and mat-drying, not a hand-dug bed, so it reads near-square (`garden_plots_are_quads` validates both). At 1 px = 2 ft the generator lands ~40-255 m² per yard (the largest is the headman's capped apron), squarely in the ~100-300 m² band.
- **Dooryard kitchen gardens (*saien*) are PER-FARMSTEAD and UNIVERSAL** (drives `gardens_present` = 100%, `gardens_on_sunny_side`, `gardens_smaller_than_farmhouse`, `gardens_clear_of_paddies`, `gardens_clear_of_channels`, `gardens_clear_of_structures`, `gardens_clear_of_sheds`, `garden_plots_are_quads`, `garden_area_within_norms`; placed by `farmsteads()` alongside the yard).
  - *Universal, and a SIDE feature - not the south front.* Every farmstead kept a small intensive vegetable plot (*saien*) for the household's daily greens (*daikon*, onions, beans, herbs), so it is drawn on 100% of farmhouses (the generator drops only a farmhouse too boxed-in to host BOTH a yard and a garden). Crucially it does NOT sit on the south front: that ground is the threshing/drying **work apron** (the *niwa*, which must stay clear hard-packed earth for flailing and sun-drying rice). The garden takes a **sunny SIDE** instead - we prefer the **east**, the *doma*/kitchen end where the cook steps out to it, then the sunny **SE/SW corners** (each tried flush AND a little further out, to slip the garden past the south yard into the corner), and the **windward WALL itself LAST** (the west, for the default NW wind) so that wall stays free for the homestead grove's belt; **never the shady north back**. The windward CORNER (SW) is fine - it tucks below the grove's arm - it is only the windward *wall* the garden yields. This matters most for a farm whose EAST faces the paddy: its garden can't go east, so it takes the SW corner rather than the west wall, leaving the west for the grove (a west garden vetoing the west grove arm was the #1 reason a windward arm went missing). (So the historical homestead reads: grove on the windward N/W, house, garden on a lee side, work yard on the S front, paddy beyond - all four drawn, and the homestead SOLVER shifts the whole farm to a nearby spot where they all fit without overlapping rather than stacking the garden under the grove.)
  - *And clear EASTERN sky where a small nudge buys it (`gardens_unshaded_from_east`, GM 2026-07).* The garden goes on the lee/EAST side, but a NEIGHBOR's windward grove arm sits on that neighbor's W wall - i.e. just EAST of our garden - and would block its MORNING sun (the sun rises in the E). Two levers keep the east open: (1) the communal COPSE keep-out gained an EAST sun-lane beside its south one, so a scattered clump never sits directly east of a garden; (2) once every per-house grove is drawn, `_relax_gardens_south` nudges an east-shaded garden **a little SOUTH** - into open ground, so the tree falls to its NE and the eastern sky opens (the GM's "move it a bit south" remedy). It is BEST-EFFORT and an OPTION, not a hard slot: most farms keep the plain east garden; only the shaded, movable ones shift, and a garden boxed in to the south (paddy/lane/neighbor) stays put - so the check fires only on an AVOIDABLE case (still east-shaded though a clear south-shift existed). Two subtleties the implementation had to get right: the nudge clears EVERY east arm in one move (a small shift can otherwise slip a garden out of one arm's shade and straight INTO a taller arm just south - a 1-D interval fit, not "dodge the nearest tree"); and it tests REAL footprints, so a garden may drift into a neighbor's empty reserved-bbox MARGIN, it just may not touch a drawn house/yard/garden/grove. WHY: morning light matters for a kitchen bed, and the fix is placement (move the garden) - not felling the neighbor's windbreak.
  - *Three appurtenances, three sides - they never collide.* The farmstead's yard (south front), its **storehouse/*kura*** (WEST wall for a dispersed farm, the shaded NORTH wall for a nucleated-cluster house, on the ~30% of plain farms that have one - see the kura bullet above), and its garden must sit on DIFFERENT sides of the house. The garden yields whichever wall the kura holds (`gardens_clear_of_sheds` enforces no overlap). The kura is a **recorded annex** (`M['farm_sheds']`) reserved in the homestead bundle, so the check reads its footprint straight from the manifest.
  - *Off the water, too (`gardens_clear_of_channels`).* A raised-bed *saien* is dry-ground - it must not sit in a running irrigation line (a feeder CHANNEL, an in-field/drain DITCH, or a stream). `gardens_clear_of_paddies` covers the flooded basin, but the **drain outfall and feeder channels thread the DRY village margin** where the gardens are, so they were the gap this check closes. The fix is in the SOLVER, not just the check: `_rect_blocked` now treats every water polyline as a keep-out for the SOLID bundle parts (house/yard/garden/kura), so the homestead self-heals off a ditch (the grove stays exempt - it may hug a bund). Found on Ueda, where two field-edge gardens sat on the SW drain.
  - *Why a side and not the front.* The south apron is occupied by the work yard and must stay open; a kitchen garden tolerates partial sun better than grain-drying needs full sun, so it can take an east/west side. This is the historically dominant *nōka yashiki* layout (idealized - real plots bent to terrain/wealth), which is why the generator encodes *preferences* (sunny sides, east first) and places "where there's room," not fixed slots.
  - *Relative size.* Small - smaller than the threshing yard and well under the farmhouse (a kitchen plot, not a second field); drawn as a tilled bed with tidy planted rows, distinct from the tan work yard and the blue-green paddy quilt.
  - *Size varies, and is held to a HISTORICAL AREA band (`garden_area_within_norms`).* No two households worked an identical plot, so the garden's base dims (its MINIMUM - the least ground that feeds a household's daily greens) jitter UP, by a different fraction in each dimension (which also varies its proportions). Unlike the house/yard - drawn oversized against the fields for legibility - a dooryard garden at **1 px = 2 ft is near its TRUE size**, so its area is a real quantity we can pin to history: the *saien* is the small INTENSIVE daily-greens bed by the kitchen (*daikon*, onions, greens, herbs); the household's bulk vegetable and dry-crop growing happened out in the **hatake dry fields**, not here. Historically that dooryard bed ran **a few *tsubo* up to ~1.4 *se*, i.e. ~10-140 m²** (1 *tsubo* = 3.31 m²; 1 *se* = 30 *tsubo* ≈ 99 m²). The check sums ALL of a household's beds (a fragmented plot is still one household's garden) and requires the TOTAL in that band - bigger reads as a field, tinier as no garden at all. (China-first per the core principle: rice-south Chinese farmhouse dooryard vegetable plots were the same small intensive kitchen beds; the bulk growing was likewise out in the household's fields.) The generator lands ~35-115 m² per household, centered ~55 m² (~1/2 *se*), comfortably inside.
  - *Shape - an irregular hand-worked quad, not a surveyed rectangle (`garden_plots_are_quads`).* Real dooryard beds were bent to paths, walls, and awkward soil, so the garden is drawn as a slightly-irregular **4-sided polygon** - each corner pulled INWARD from the reserved rect by a small position-seeded fraction, losing the perfect 90° corners while staying **entirely inside** its reserved w×h (so the shape can never create an overlap the rect-based placement/checks already cleared). The manifest records the `poly`; the check validates it is a non-degenerate inscribed 4-gon. (A swept threshing yard gets the SAME treatment but LESS jitter - a work surface stays near-square.)
  - *Occasionally FRAGMENTED into two beds, in THREE arrangements (`_garden_beds`, nucleated only).* Sometimes the land just works out that way - you garden the good topsoil where it lies rather than dig up a rocky patch when there is better ground a few feet over - so ~1 in 4 households (position-seeded), where each bed still reads as a real garden, shows TWO beds. WHERE the two beds sit varies: ~half the splits **FLANK the house on OPPOSITE walls** (a bed on the E and one on the W, or one in each south corner - the workable ground fell on both sides); the rest share ONE side, either **SIDE-BY-SIDE** or - for a south garden, where the upper bed stays sunny - **STACKED above/below**. It is the larger (well-off / headman) plots that fragment (each half must stay ~12 ft wide), which is also where a household works enough ground to bother. **Crucially the beds are decided in `_bundle_geom` and returned in `geom["gardens"]`, so they are RESERVED and collision-checked as part of the whole bundle** - an opposite-side bed grows the bundle bbox and the packer spaces the homestead out for it, so a flanking bed can never overlap a neighbor or a paddy (this is why the split is nucleated-only: a dispersed farm's W wall carries its windbreak grove, and its E-only garden has no clean opposite side). Every bed sits on a sunny band (never the north back), and all beds of one house carry the same parent - so `gardens_present` still counts one garden per house and the AREA band sums them. (Gardens can therefore outnumber houses in the manifest; that is expected.)
- **Storehouses / sheds are a MINORITY (~30%), not universal** (drives the `shed` flag rate, deliberately left unchecked - a visual flourish - and kept a minority).
  - A fireproof **kura** (土蔵) was an expensive **wealth marker** - only well-off farmers and landlords had one. A simpler **naya** (納屋) / **koya** (小屋) outbuilding was more common but still not on every plot: many households stored inside the *doma* (the farmhouse's own earth-floored work room), and the landless *mizunomi* had little to store. So a separate storehouse is a *portion* of farms (~30%), independent of the threshing yard (a farm may have neither, either, or both).
- **Settlement form: NUCLEATED cluster (the DEFAULT) vs DISPERSED ring (drives `s._nucleated`; researched + source-verified 2026-07, this is the record).** WHICH is more common: **nucleated clustered villages predominated in China** (Knapp, *Chinese Landscapes: The Village as Place*) - houses grouped together, "planted like islands among the fields," worked outward. The regional split is SIZE, not form: North China = large compact villages (1,000+ households); **the South-China rice regions** (Middle-Lower Yangzi - our analog, since Rokugani density anchors to Song/Ming rice China) = **small nucleated villages of ~30-60 households** (rice's intensive labor keeps farmers clustered by their paddies; 200-500 villages per 100 km2). **Dispersed** scattered farmsteads appear only in **mountainous/hilly terrain** (Sichuan-type, where fragmented arable forces homesteads apart). The Japan tiebreaker agrees (wet-rice Japan = *shuson*, nucleated). So the **default is NUCLEATED**; dispersed is the hill/upland TERRAIN mode (and dovetails with the multi-block-fields note - broken ground gives both scattered farmsteads AND several field patches). The ~30-60 rice-village band validates Hoshigaoka's ~70 households (a healthy single rice village, just above the band).
  - *The consequence for groves.* A nucleated cluster has **NO per-house windbreak grove** - the houses stand close and shelter each other, and a tight cluster of grove-bundles literally cannot nucleate (each ~6x-house grove-bundle forces the farms to RING the field, i.e. the dispersed pattern). Per the grove bullet below (and Knapp), the nucleated windbreak is instead a **village-EDGE belt** on the windward side (a *fengshui*/shrine grove), placed as one feature - NOT one yashikirin per house. So `_nucleated` swaps the homestead bundle: house + south threshing yard + a dooryard garden on an **adaptive sunny side** (SE/SW tucked beside the yard first - sunny and tight - then the E/W walls), the side chosen by the placer for **fit + no shading** (a garden with a farmhouse close to its SOUTH is shaded, so the placer avoids sandwiching it between houses; this also varies the gardens instead of all-east). The compact grove-less bundle lets the cluster pack into a real nucleus; the placer hugs each homestead to the paddy edge then packs it against its neighbors.
  - *DISPERSED (the shipped ring-village bundle) is unchanged* - house + windward L-grove (*yashikirin*) + yard + east garden, spaced so all fit; it is the terrain mode for upland/fragmented country. The existing ring villages (Hikari, Kikuta) stay dispersed; the water-first base case (Hoshigaoka) sets `s._nucleated=True`. The **windward village-edge belt** for nucleated clusters is a deferred TODO (the cluster currently renders grove-less pending it).
  - *Headman = just a LARGER plain farmhouse (tunable size), NOT its own logic.* Farmhouses are individually sizeable: `try_place(x, y, kind, role, size=(w,h))` overrides the footprint (base px). In nucleated mode `s.headman()` is a thin wrapper - it places a **plain** farmhouse of **~46x28 px** (~92x56 ft at 1px=2ft) through the STANDARD collision-checked bundle path, `role="headman"`. So it gets its (capped) yard + garden like any farm, packs without overlapping a neighbor, and carries no special reservation or drawing. It is the grandest house (clearly larger than a plain 23x14 or well-off ~26x16 farmhouse; the old 108x68 ~216x136 ft was a pre-scale fortress; `headman_is_largest` holds) purely by SIZE. The yard/garden dims are **capped** in `_bundle_geom` (yard ~34x22, garden ~22x17) so they read as an ordinary farm's beside his grand house (span ~the adjacent wall but shallower/narrower); plain houses are well under the cap, so they are unaffected. NOTE the `"big"` kind's glyph draws a small storeroom WING to its north that is NOT in the reserved footprint (it overlapped a neighbor's yard when used for the headman) - so the nucleated headman uses `"plain"`, not `"big"`.
  - *The cluster HUGS the paddy (algorithmic, not per-map seed tuning).* Villages sat as "islands among the fields," abutting their paddies - not floating with a bare buffer. So the nucleated placer, after shoving each homestead toward the nearest paddy edge, packs it against its neighbors with a **field-distance-monotone** constraint (`_slide_nuc(keep_field=True)`): a neighbor-pack move is rejected if it would drift the bundle FURTHER from the paddy than where it started. The cluster therefore glues its field side to the paddy and builds outward in rows (a blob anchored on the field), regardless of where the seed disk is centered - the fix lives in the ALGORITHM, so a map need only say roughly where the village sits, not hand-tune it onto the field edge.
  - *Lanes lace the cluster, and a spur bridges it to the fields.* A nucleated village is threaded with lanes. Lay them with `s.lane(pts)` **BEFORE** the houses - a lane lays a no-build corridor, so the homesteads FRONT it (place lanes after the water/fields, before `try_place`). The pattern: a **main spine** through the cluster (the headman fronts its head) plus a **spur running out to the paddy edge** - the spur is the village-to-field transition the bare buffer should carry (its field end is computed from the field geometry via `_nearest_field_point`, not hand-placed). Remaining house-to-field bareness is later filled by near-homestead hatake (a deferred layer). **Run the spine along the cluster's FIELD-FAR side** (not down the middle) when the field is on one flank: the placer hugs houses TOWARD the field, so a mid-cluster spine catches the houses it pulls across it (`houses_clear_of_lanes`); a spine on the far side lets them pack cleanly between it and the paddy.
  - *EVERY house is reached by a way, and the FORM of that reach is a KNOB (researched 2026-08-18; [`../research/homesteads.md#is-every-farmhouse-reached-by-a-lane-and-in-what-form`](../research/homesteads.md#is-every-farmhouse-reached-by-a-lane-and-in-what-form)).* "Threaded with lanes" above was the whole doctrine for a long time and the generator did not honor it - a spine plus a field spur left back-rank houses touched by nothing (nine of Sawada's nineteen more than 120 ft from any way). The research is decisive that this is wrong: compactness exists so that "every house in the nucleated village is accessible via the interconnected system of narrow lanes and alleys". What the research does NOT settle is the form, and both attested shapes are drawn per settlement on a seeded knob rather than picked once: **alleys off the spine** - narrow laterals between plots, colonised as semi-private space by the houses they pass, the accretive Chinese gridiron - or **a back lane** parallel to the main lane behind the plots, which doubles as the village/field edge, the planned form (rear-access ground behind housing lots is attested in traditional Manchu villages, so this is not a European import). They read differently at a glance, which is the point: a back lane says the place was laid out, alleys say it grew. Per constitution Principle XII, do NOT ask the GM which - two supportable forms are a knob.
  - *Name-informed siting: read the village NAME and let it place the settlement (GM, 2026-07, a GENERAL rule).* A Rokugani place name often encodes the settlement's position relative to its fields, water, or terrain - so before seeding the cluster, ask what the name SAYS and honor it. Worked example: **Ueda (上田, "upper paddy")** - the village's paddies are the UPPER (upslope) ones, so the dwellings sit DOWNSLOPE of them, facing up-valley toward the fields; on Ueda's NE-high slope that puts the cluster on the LOWER (SW) flank with the bulk of the paddy rising above it (NOT the default "village on the high flank"). Other cues to watch for and site to: *shimo-* (下, lower) -> the opposite; *kawa-/-gawa* (river) -> hard by the watercourse; *yama-/-take* (mountain), *saka* (slope), *hara* (plain/moor), *tani/-dani* (valley floor), *sawa* (marshy stream), *hama* (shore), *guchi* (a mouth/entrance/pass). When the name is neutral, fall back to the default (nucleated village on the dry high flank hugging its paddy). The point: the map should be able to be READ back into the name.
- **Homestead groves (*yashikirin*) are a UNIVERSAL-where-possible windbreak L-belt on the WINDWARD side - the largest homestead appurtenance** (drives `groves_on_windward_side`, `groves_clear_of_paddies`, `groves_clear_of_structures`, `groves_are_substantial`, `groves_where_possible`, `gardens_clear_of_groves`, `yards_unshaded_by_groves`, and `no_groves_inside_walls`; the homestead SOLVER spaces each farm so all its features fit, then groves are placed by `farmsteads()` in a second pass).
  - *What it is.* The **yashikirin** (屋敷林) is the cluster of shelter trees the farm homestead planted around its house - regionally *igune* (Tohoku), *kainyo* (the Tonami plain). It is a **windbreak** (*bofurin*) against the cold prevailing winter wind, and it doubled as a **larder of wood**: firewood and fallen-branch fuel, construction timber (prized cedar/zelkova, often on the west), bamboo, fruit/nuts (persimmon, chestnut), leaf-litter for compost, summer shade, and a buffer slowing fire jumping between homesteads. Drawn as a **size-adaptive L-BELT** wrapping the windward N + W walls (one `groves` record per belt ARM), each arm a **dense, overlapping MIXED stand** of three species - tall **evergreen** conifer (dark, dense apex; the windbreak backbone, cedar/pine), **deciduous** broadleaf (mid green; timber/fruit), and a **bamboo** clump (*take*; fine culms with leafy tops) - so it reads as a real grove, not a few scattered trees. Each arm's canopy count scales with its area (grain-invariant, so a grove reads the same at town and city scale), with a few large emergent crowns over many small; the whole belt is sized to be the LARGEST homestead feature (bigger than the house), distinct from the big `s.forest` area feature and the striped kitchen-garden bed.
  - *(why: [`../research/homesteads.md#homestead-groves-yashikirin-the-real-scale-and-prevalence`](../research/homesteads.md#homestead-groves-yashikirin-the-real-scale-and-prevalence))*
  - *Prevalence - near-universal in dispersed-settlement country.* In a *sankyoson* (dispersed-farmstead) landscape - Tonami, Izumo, the Shonai/Sendai plains - the grove was **effectively universal, one per farmhouse (~100%)**; it IS the defining feature of that landscape (Tonami: ~7,000 grove-ringed homesteads over ~22,000 ha, about one grove per ~3 ha, farmhouses ~150-180 m apart). In **nucleated** villages (the more common East-Asian/China-leaning pattern) the per-house windbreak is less universal and the landscape instead carries **village-edge / shrine *fengshui* groves**. Our farmhouse-ring villages are semi-dispersed, which justifies a HIGH per-house prevalence. So the grove is **universal where physically possible**: `meta(grove_prevalence=N)` defaults to **1.0** (every farm is a candidate; lower it for a sheltered/atypical microclimate). The homestead SOLVER shifts each farm to make windward room, and a **minimal-clump fallback** means any farm with even a sliver of windward room gets *some* grove (small is fine - the spacious farms carry the big stands). The only farms left grove-less are those whose windward side is genuinely blocked - all paddy/edge, the sun-corridor south of a neighbor's yard, or intramural - which **`groves_where_possible`** verifies (it fires if a grove-less farm actually had clear windward room).
  - *Intramural farms (inside city walls) carry NO windbreak groves (default).* The *yashikirin* is the shelter of an ISOLATED farmstead alone in open fields; an in-wall plot is embedded in the urban fabric (surrounding buildings already break the wind) and sits on land too precious for tree belts - so a walled city's interior agricultural district draws NO groves (the grove pass skips any farm inside `M['wall']`; `no_groves_inside_walls` gates it; `meta(inwall_groves=True)` to override). The driver is "the house is not isolated + land is precious", NOT "the wall blocks the wind" (a wall shelters only a narrow downwind band).
  - *Why the WINDWARD side (N/W by default), never the sunny side.* The grove shelters the wall the wind hits and stays OFF the sunny lee, because the house faces the sun and the work yard needs it. The default windward is **N/W**: the **East Asian winter monsoon** (the Siberian high draining cold air southeastward) blows from the **NW across China AND Japan alike** - China is the source region, not an exception - so N+W is windward and the S/E is the sheltered, sunny side. The grove places a deep belt ARM on each windward face (N **and** W for NW), wrapping the corner into an L; a cardinal windward gets one deep band. **Since Rokugan leans continental (China-like), the wind is the same NW but drier and dustier** (continental dust, not maritime snow) - the orientation is unchanged; only the flavor shifts.
  - *Per-map geography overrides the default.* A map keys the grove off its own exposure with **`meta(windward=...)`** (`"N"`/`"NE"`/`"E"`/.../`"NW"`, default `"NW"`) - e.g. farmland downwind of a specific sea, mountain wall, or the western Burning Sands/steppe. The check reads the same `meta.windward`, so the geometry gate follows the declared wind.
  - *Size-adaptive, merging, never drops a house.* Each windward arm is tried at full depth, then shallower, then a shorter partial belt, then a **minimal clump** to slip into any sliver. Arms are kept OUT of `placed`, so a neighbor's grove may **MERGE** with this one - clustered homesteads read as one continuous windbreak (historically each grove was individual, but adjacent ones ran together; we do not model *shared* groves). Groves are placed in a **second pass after every farmhouse + its yard + garden**, so an optional grove can never block a neighbor's mandatory appurtenances and drop that house. The checks gate GEOMETRY per arm (windward / off-paddy / off-buildings), the typical grove's SCALE (`groves_are_substantial`: median grove >= **0.5x** its house - the spacious farms carry real stands, small clumps on cramped farms are fine), and presence (`groves_where_possible`, above).
  - *The homestead SOLVER - one rule that keeps the whole farmstead coherent.* `_solve_homestead` searches a **widening spiral of house positions** (the placed spot first, then shifts) for one where the threshing yard, the dooryard garden, AND room for a windward grove all fit, non-overlapping - keeping the farm **field-adjacent** and dropping it only if even its yard+garden won't fit anywhere (rare). This **spaces a crowded homestead out** instead of stacking features (so the grove no longer needs to overlap the garden - `gardens_clear_of_groves` gates that the two never stack), and it leaves grove-room so the second pass can place a grove on nearly every farm. To help it, the **dooryard garden prefers the LEE sunny side** (east, then the SE/SW corners) and takes the **windward wall LAST**, leaving that side free for the grove.
  - *NOR DOES A NEIGHBOUR'S FARMHOUSE - and this is the SCRIPTED tier's rule first* (`yards_unshaded_by_neighbors`; GM 2026-08-13, researched in [`../research/homesteads.md#the-threshing-yards-sun-and-how-far-a-farmhouse-shades`](../research/homesteads.md)). Thatch is pitched 45 degrees or steeper, so a 46 x 28 ft minka's ridge stands ~20 ft up; at 38N in the threshing month that is **21 ft of shadow at noon and 39 ft by 9am**, so a yard needs **39 ft of clear ground to its south** to keep the 9-to-3 drying day. The placer enforces it with `s.sun_corridor(39)`, testing both directions (this house must not shade a standing yard; this yard must not sit in a standing house's shadow) - and reading the neighbours' yards off the PLACED BUNDLES' `geom`, not off `M["threshing_yards"]`, which is empty until `farmsteads()` flushes long after every house is seated. The rule that the engine already applied to GROVES was simply never applied to houses, which are taller and shade further.
    - **THE LEGACY POOL IS DELIBERATELY EXEMPT, and the exemption cannot rot** (GM 2026-08-13). Every hand-authored nucleated map breaks this - Ueda 45 of 85 yards shaded at noon, Hoshigaoka 31 of 70, Kikuta 27 of 55, Ubame 21 of 36, Hoshizora 19 of 44, the hamlets 3-10 each, with neighbours' walls commonly **2-8 ft** off a yard's edge; the provincial cities are clear (0-1 each) because their farm belt is loose. Re-packing all of them by hand was judged the wrong trade. So the check is gated on **`meta.generated_by`**, which only a generator script sets: a legacy map keeps its packing and **starts obeying the rule the moment it is CONVERTED**. That is the migration doing the work - there is no exemption list for anyone to prune, only the absence of a tag that conversion supplies.
  - *And a grove never shades a yard.* **A grove never sits in the sun-corridor directly south of a threshing yard** (`yards_unshaded_by_groves`): the yard dries rice in the southern sun, so a *neighbor's* grove immediately to its south would shade it (a grove is N/W of its own house, so this only steers it off a neighbor's yard).
- **Farmer wealth + homestead variation - houses come in a modest range of sizes and PROPORTIONS** (drives `farmhouse_sizes_vary`, `farmhouse_aspect_in_range`). Real holdings ranged from the landless *mizunomi* to *honbyakushO* landholders, and no two farmsteads were copy-pasted, so a plain farmhouse varies TWO ways depending on the settlement mode:
  - *DISPERSED (ring villages): a wealth TIER scaling the whole glyph.* A plain farmhouse takes a modest **wealth tier** (deterministic in its position): ~30% poor (0.9x), ~50% typical (1.0x), ~20% well-off (1.12x), recorded as the house's `wealth`. The tier scales the **rendered house** and, with it, the **homestead grove** (a richer farm reads bigger across the board). **Scope limit:** the wealth factor is applied to the rendered glyph ONLY - the footprint is reserved and the yard/garden placed at BASE size, then the house is drawn (and grove sized) at `base x wealth` - so it never perturbs packing/household counts. Here the yard/garden/shed stay uniform (scaling them coupled into placement and dropped houses), so the wealth signal rides the two largest features.
  - *NUCLEATED (clustered villages): the FOOTPRINT itself jitters, LENGTH-dominant (minka bays).* A clustered house varies its base `w,h` directly (position-seeded, wealth stays 1.0): the **length** (E-W long axis) jitters more (~0.85-1.35x), the **depth** (N-S) far less (~0.90-1.10x) - because a *minka* grew by **adding bays along its length** (a *hisashi* aisle, an extra *ma*), staying "longer but not much wider," never ballooning into a square or a 4:1 shed. `farmhouse_aspect_in_range` guards the result (fires above ~2.7:1; a minka norm is ~1.3-2.5:1). And here the **yard and garden vary too** (see their bullets - garden jitters up from its minimum, yard down from its maximum, both gaining irregular-quad shape and the garden an occasional split), because the nucleated bundle reserves each appurtenance's footprint before packing, so varying them within that reservation does not tip a boxed-in farm over.
  - *One check, both encodings.* `farmhouse_sizes_vary` measures the **effective rendered area = w × h × wealth²**, so it catches a regression that flattens houses under EITHER path (uniform footprint + wealth tiers, OR jittered footprint at wealth 1.0). The **headman** is exempt and stays the largest (`headman_is_largest`). All the jitter is a deterministic sin-hash of position (`_hjit`), so it never draws from the placement RNG and never ripples household counts.
- **Farmhouses face SOUTH** (drives `houses_face_south`, and the yard's south-front placement). *Minka* were oriented south for sunlight (warmth and drying); we render the long axis E-W with the entrance on the south (+y) side.
