# Field archetypes: land-use overlays and the polder build

*Part of the Mode B settlement docs. The index, the `meta()` knobs, the workflow and the validator contract live in [`../settlements.md`](../settlements.md).*

**Load this file when:** the map uses a non-default `field_archetype` (contour_terraces, polder_grid, ribbon_valley, mulberry_dike) or a land-use overlay (mulberry_fishpond, lotus, tea_fringe). A plain valley_paddy village does NOT need this file.

**Research:** the historical basis for these rules - what was found, the decision it drove, and every disclosed departure - is in [`../research/archetypes.md`](../research/archetypes.md). Load it when you are CHANGING a rule or questioning one, not to follow it.

---

- **Archetype knobs (US4).** `field_archetype` (valley_paddy / contour_terraces / polder_grid / ribbon_valley / mulberry_dike_fishpond) and `land_use_overlay` (none / mulberry_fishpond / lotus / tea_fringe) are registered with **terrain typing** (research.md D4: terraces need hill ground, polder/dike-pond need low reclaimed ground, tea needs a hill fringe). `s.apply_land_use(net, overlay, rng)` is implemented in full - it recolors a fraction of paddy plots (or a hill-tea fringe) as the overlay crop, gated by the `land_use_overlay_drawn` check (a declared overlay must actually be drawn). **Every overlay value is a PERMANENT use, never a rotation** (GM, 2026-07): an overlay recolors some plots while the rest stay standing rice, so it may only hold crops that coexist with rice in the same season - a dike-pond, a lotus paddy and a hill tea garden are given over all year, so the plot next door can be rice. (why: [`../research/archetypes.md#why-rape-was-tried-and-removed`](../research/archetypes.md#why-rape-was-tried-and-removed))

  **LAND-USE OVERLAYS ARE SITED BY TOPOGRAPHY, NOT BY CHANCE (feature 010, `specs/010-land-use-overlay-grounding/research.md`).** The rape failure exposed a second defect that survived it: the overlay picked its plots with a uniform random sample. The corrected rule, which governs every value:

  > **Topography sets which plots are ELIGIBLE; economy decides how many of the eligible plots CONVERT.**

  The old code had only the economic term (`fraction`) and no topographic filter at all, so it drew ponds and lotus on ordinary upper-field rice ground. Eligibility is now the plots the field builders flag `low` (comb = the bottom two levels of each sector, terraces = the lowest 3, polder = the lowest 2 rows, ribbon = the lowest 3 bands). Zero eligible plots means the overlay honestly draws NOTHING rather than falling back to random placement.

  **`low` (topography) is NOT `FLOODED` (the picture) - do not re-conflate them.** `FLOODED` tints a random 45% of the comb's bottom level blue for visual texture, so keying eligibility off the fill made it an accident of RENDERING. The two are now separate fields on the plot record.

  **`fraction` is a share of the ELIGIBLE set, not of the whole field.** As a share of the field it was inert - the eligible set is always smaller, so every village converted 100% of its low ground and the economic term decided nothing. As a share of eligible the two terms vary independently, which is exactly what the evidence demands: Shunde was ~5% dike-pond county-wide while containing townships past 50% the same year, on identical terrain.

  (why: [`../research/archetypes.md#overlay-extent-a-calibrated-liberty-disclosed`](../research/archetypes.md#overlay-extent-a-calibrated-liberty-disclosed)) What this does NOT do is invent a range: lotus stays strictly on genuinely low ground and cannot touch the upper field. Gated by `overlays_on_wet_ground_only`, whose teeth come from `wet_plots` being written by the FIELD pass while `land_use[].plots` is written by the OVERLAY pass - two independent records, so the check cannot be satisfied by a self-report.

  Per value:

  *(why: [`../research/archetypes.md#the-three-overlay-values-mulberry-fishpond-lotus-tea-fringe`](../research/archetypes.md#the-three-overlay-values-mulberry-fishpond-lotus-tea-fringe))*

  **A check was written and then REMOVED for having no teeth** (`dikeponds_are_clustered`). Household-by-household conversion really did spread as patches, and `_pick_overlay_plots` models it - but it is not independently observable, because the eligible set is always a thin contiguous strip of low ground, and every subset of a strip is "clustered" by any nearest-neighbor-vs-span metric. An even random scatter of the same count passed the check, so it would have been a check that cannot fail - worse than no check. It becomes testable if a future archetype ever yields a genuinely 2-D eligible region. ALL FIVE field-GEOMETRY archetypes are now DRAWN: the `valley_paddy` comb (`build_comb`), **`contour_terraces`** (`waterfields.build_terraces` - stacked contour terrace bands stepping down a hillside from the high catchment, a flank supply channel cascading terrace-to-terrace to a foot drain; each step is split ALONG the contour into ~0.05-ac leveled cells - see 'Paddy cell size' - so a step reads as a ROW of small paddies, not one band; demo `pool/hamlets/tanada.gen.py` 棚田, validator `contour_terraces_are_stepped_bands` = >=8 cross-slope retaining bunds, which reads `terrace_bunds` not the cells so the split is transparent to it). *Grounding (research.md D4):* the south-China / SE-Asia rice terrace (Yuanyang 元陽, Longsheng 龍勝) is THE field archetype for HILL ground where valley-bottom paddy is impossible - the defining alternative to the comb. and **`polder_grid`** (`build_polder` - a rectilinear wei-tian 圩田 block of paddies inside a perimeter dike on LOW reclaimed ground; demo `pool/hamlets/enokida.gen.py`, validators `polder_fills_its_bbox` + `polder_parcels_vary`). **`mulberry_dike_fishpond`** (polder geometry + a full mulberry_fishpond overlay = a reclaimed block of dike-ponds, demo `kuwabata.gen.py`, validators `dikepond_is_ponds_in_a_block` + `polder_parcels_vary`), and **`ribbon_valley`** (`build_ribbon` - a long narrow paddy MEANDERING down a confined valley floor, the brook down the center; the valley floor steps down in cross-bunds AND splits across its width into ~0.05-ac leveled cells - see 'Paddy cell size' - so it reads as a chain of small paddies, not one long sheet; demo `yatsuda.gen.py`, validator `ribbon_is_long_and_narrow` checks the field OUTLINE so the split is transparent to it). Each archetype has terrain typing (terraces=hill, polder/dike-pond=low, ribbon=narrow_valley), an archetype-specific validator with a negative fixture, and China-first grounding (research.md D4). A roll with no declared terrain resolves to `valley_paddy`; a declared terrain unlocks its archetype.

  **The polder's PARCEL fabric is a patchwork, never identical cells** (`polder_parcels_vary`; researched + source-verified 2026-07-21; full grounding in `build_polder`'s docstring). The original `build_polder` drew every cell as one identical `cell x cell` square (Kuwabata/Enokida: 66 identical 150-ft squares - the GM flagged it as "not the organically developed farms I'm used to seeing"). (why: [`../research/archetypes.md#polder-parcels-were-a-private-tenure-patchwork`](../research/archetypes.md#polder-parcels-were-a-private-tenure-patchwork)) The rework keeps the interior ditch-grid module lines straight (that part was genuinely surveyed - see the `Perimeter dike` note below for the outer dike, which is NOT straight) while most module bays split into 2-3 oblong strips, a few merge into double-bay parcels (along the fall only), and interior bund nodes jitter ~6 px. **Parcels are TRUE SCALE** (GM directive 2026-07-21 - no legibility inflation on to-scale maps): rice polders run a ~110 ft module so parcels average ~1 mu (Buck's mean; whole bays ~1.9 mu, halves ~0.9, thirds ~0.6), and dike-pond blocks run a ~160 ft module with a merge-heavy `parcel_mix` so most ponds are ~160x320 ft (~0.48 ha - traditional ponds were 0.4-0.6 ha oblongs; earliest attested sizes are 20th-century surveys of the traditional landscape, flagged in `build_polder`'s docstring) behind ~22 ft (~6.7 m, attested 6-10 m) mulberry dikes via the `gap` knob.

  **Perimeter dike: an irregular earthwork band following the natural water edge** (`polder_dike_is_earthwork`, `s.perimeter_dike`; GM 2026-07-22, researched + source-verified). The GM noticed the perimeter dike was drawn as a single uniform-width, perfectly straight, right-angled tan line and asked whether a hand-built earthwork would really look like that. It would not. (why: [`../research/archetypes.md#the-perimeter-dike-followed-the-natural-water-edge`](../research/archetypes.md#the-perimeter-dike-followed-the-natural-water-edge)) *The decision:* `s.perimeter_dike(inner_env, ...)` takes the rectilinear grid boundary as the dike's INNER face and draws the OUTER face as an organic curve bulging outward by a varying amount (broad shoreline-following bulges + finer pinch-and-repair variation, ~14-40 ft wide - a perimeter dike ran wider than the ~6.7 m inter-pond mulberry dikes it rings, so it is drawn to TRUE width, not the old under-scale 4.4 px line), corners rounded not square, filled as a mottled earthwork with scattered soil-binding mulberry/willow crowns. The interior grid stays rectilinear (the surveyed part that IS historically correct). *Departure:* specific dike base/crest/height figures are not attested for pre-modern polders, so the ~14-40 ft band width is a plausible low-earthwork assumption, not canon; the width VARIATION and organic outer form are the well-attested parts. The dike is LABELED (the GM asked for it named - a polder dike is a real diegetic feature, not an "obvious" one exempt under the don't-label-the-obvious rule). Both polder demos (`kuwabata.gen.py` dike-pond, `enokida.gen.py` rice polder) call it; the check requires the recorded band's width to vary (`w_max >= 1.4x w_min`), so a reverted uniform-width or missing dike fires (negative fixture: `pool/regressions/polder_dike_is_earthwork_fires_on_a_ruled_uniform_dike.json`).

  **Perimeter dike planting: rows with a purpose, not a scatter** (GM 2026-07-24, researched - accuracy pass on the dike greenery). (why: [`../research/archetypes.md#why-dikes-were-planted-and-what-the-row-spacing-rests-on`](../research/archetypes.md#why-dikes-were-planted-and-what-the-row-spacing-rests-on)) *The decision:* the old uncalibrated random crown scatter is replaced by two planted rows that follow each band run (skipping the sluice notches): a willow row at ~3/4 of the band width toward the water face (larger pollard crowns, r 3.5-5.5 px, in-row 8.5 px - drawn at the loose end of the attested ~5.5 ft for the touching-crown read, the pond-bank legibility precedent) and a mulberry row on the inner face (coppice form and 4.4 px spacing identical to the pond banks). The earth mottle stays SCATTERED deliberately - patch-repairs, unlike planting, are haphazard. Both polders inherit this (a rice polder's dike was equally willow-armored, and Lake Tai attests mulberry on rice-polder banks). (why: [`../research/archetypes.md#why-dike-willows-do-not-replace-the-village-windbreak`](../research/archetypes.md#why-dike-willows-do-not-replace-the-village-windbreak))

  **Polder ring canal: the trunk runs on the INNER TOE of the dike, not buried in it** (`polder_channels_clear_of_dike`; GM 2026-07-22, researched + source-verified). Once the dike became a wide earthwork band, the GM caught that the perimeter feeder - which `build_polder` ran at `s = +-12` px, OUTSIDE the field envelope - now sat buried in the dike, and asked where the channel really belonged and whether it too should be a little "watery". (why: [`../research/archetypes.md#the-ring-canal-runs-on-the-inner-toe`](../research/archetypes.md#the-ring-canal-runs-on-the-inner-toe)) *The decision:* `build_polder` now reserves the ~14 px `RING` corridor inside the dike on ALL FOUR sides (was two) and runs the feeder / drain / two side toe-ditches as a connected ring canal in it, at `RING*0.5` just inside the envelope, gently wavy (`waver`); the header pond sits OUTSIDE the dike as the wild-water source, and only the pond inlet + the brook outfall cross the dike (the two sluices). The dike's inner face wobbles OUTWARD only, so it never intrudes over the ring canal. The check counts field-ditch vertices inside the recorded dike band and fires above 4 (the sluice-crossing allowance); negative fixture `pool/regressions/polder_channels_clear_of_dike_fires_on_a_canal_in_the_dike.json` intrudes the dike over the canal to trip it.

  **Polder ring canal, second pass** (GM 2026-07-22, researched): the GM then made eight more observations about the reworked Kuwabata, each answered here. (1) **Filleted corners** (`build_polder` `cr` fillets): a hard 90-degree ring-canal corner scours the outer bank and silts the inner - FAO sets an earthen-canal minimum bend radius of tens of times the width - so every ring corner is a quadratic-bezier FILLET, never a right angle (the finer laterals stay crookeder). (2) **Clean lateral/ring joins**: the laterals now START on the feeder inner-toe line and END on the drain inner-toe line, and the rounded corners removed the old feeder-meets-toe overlaps. (3) **The pond visibly charges the ring**: the dike is drawn FIRST (a ground layer) so the fields + ring canal + the two sluice crossings draw ON TOP of it; the feeder is recorded NW-end-last and extended with a short INLET STUB up through the dike to the pond rim, so the pond plainly feeds the ring at the north sluice. (4) **The south drainage exits**: the drain (bottom ring side, cross-slope) taps at mid-south into the brook, which runs off-map SOUTH through the dike (the outfall sluice) - water has somewhere to go. (5) **Structures + windbreak keep OFF the dike** (`structures_clear_of_dike`): `perimeter_dike` registers its band as a placement keep-out and `village_grove` skips clumps on it, so no farmhouse or windbreak clump sits on the earthwork bank (it carries only its own soil-binding trees; negative fixture `pool/regressions/structures_clear_of_dike_fires_on_a_house_on_the_dike.json`). (6) **The windbreak wraps the WINDWARD (NW) fringe, not just the north**: the cold winter monsoon blows from the NW, so the belt is an L covering the cluster's north end AND its west side. Because an east-dike village abutting the ponds has open water to its west, the gen reserves a windward no-build gap over the cluster's north third (the north houses shift east; the south houses still hug the field for `field_ringed`) and runs the west arm down that gap - the only way to fit a west windbreak against a pond-facing village. (7) **Footbridges cluster on the SETTLEMENT side** (research: crossings concentrate at the dike->field transition where people live, ~one per field-access lane; the unsettled sides are walked on the dike crest and crossed - if at all - at a sluice/culvert, not a plank; most real crossings were earthen culvert-causeways, a few plank spans): each ring side carries a `seg` tag and `channel_footbridges(seg_caps=...)` caps planks per side (east toe several, laterals sparse, feeder/far-toe/drain none) - Kuwabata dropped from 41 planks to 8. `long_ditches_have_a_footbridge` exempts the unsettled ring segs. (8) **The three green rectangles are correct**: the `mulberry_dike_fishpond` archetype converts ~90% of parcels to fish ponds (`fraction=0.9`), leaving a few (Kuwabata: ~4 of 43) as standing rice - the historically-attested mixed dike-pond district (mulberry sat on the tang banks with rice remaining the polder's crop permanently), NOT wall-to-wall ponds. (Fourth pass: those leftovers now draw as textured standing rice with no bund outline - see 'Polder fourth pass' below.)

  **Polder third pass: edge wander, sluice gaps, and inset rounded fish ponds** (GM 2026-07-22, researched). Looking at the reworked Kuwabata the GM flagged five more things, each an "it's too machine-perfect / the water does the impossible" note, and each is now both a placement fix and (where the manifest can carry teeth) an automated check. (1) **The dikes WANDER** (`polder_edges_wander`; `build_polder` `edge_wander` knob): a hand-dug wei-tian dike followed the old water edge, so it runs at a slight angle and gently changes direction with the ground (the fish-scale-polder read); a dead-straight axis-aligned block is the post-1949 consolidation shape. `edge_wander` drives a SEPARABLE low-frequency warp of the whole `(s, t)` grid - `cwarp(s)` shifts every contour-row in the cross direction (so all N-S dikes AND every interior column line share it and stay parallel while the block bends), `fwarp(t)` shifts every column in the fall direction (E-W dikes + row lines). Because every point - envelope, parcels, ring canal, laterals, and the dike that follows the envelope - is placed through `grid()`, the whole polder warps as ONE coherent piece and each field edge stays parallel to its dike section (exactly the GM's "the edges would be parallel to each section of dike"). A linear term is the overall tilt, the sines are the topology-following bends; phases come from a separate rng so every `edge_wander=0` map stays byte-identical. Both polders now pass `edge_wander=0.4`. The check requires >=30% of the field outline to run off-axis in the down-frame (a pure rectangle scores 0). (2) **Water crosses the dike through a DUG GAP, not over the top** (`polder_dike_gapped_at_sluices`; `perimeter_dike(gaps=...)`): the old inlet + outfall ran the channel OVER the unbroken earthwork band (it read as the pond water running uphill onto the dike and back down). `build_polder` now returns `dike_sluices` (the inlet + outfall crossing points) and `perimeter_dike` NOTCHES the band there - it splits the earthwork into the runs BETWEEN the gaps and draws each as its own capped strip, so the sluice channel passes through a dug notch. The check finds every THROUGH-CROSSER (a water line with a point inside the dike band AND a vertex outside the field outline - it genuinely runs field -> through dike -> off-map) and requires a recorded gap within ~26 px; incidental ring-canal clipping of the inner toe at a concave bend never leaves the field, so it is not a crosser and is not required to have a gap. (3) **The fish-pond water is INSET within its mulberry banks** (`dikepond_water_within_banks`): the old overlay filled the whole parcel with teal to its very edge, so the pond "ran off the green interior". A 桑基魚塘 unit is a raised mulberry dike (基, drawn green) surrounding an inset pond (塘, water), so `apply_land_use` now fills the parcel green and draws the water homothetically inset inside it (`Settlement._rounded_pond`), recorded as `M['dikeponds']` (parcel + water polygon per pond); the check requires every pond's water polygon to lie inside its parcel. (4) **The pond corners are ROUNDED** (`dikepond_corners_rounded`): a premodern dug earthen pond erodes to rounded corners - it had no way to hold a poured-concrete right angle - so `_rounded_pond` fillets every corner with a quadratic bezier of slightly irregular reach (erosion is uneven); the check requires the recorded water polygons to carry the rounding as many sampled vertices (>=10), where a sharp quad has 4. (5) **The laterals FEED the ring, they do not overshoot it** (a rendering fix, no manifest teeth): the old laterals left a little stub poking past the feeder into the dike corridor. This was a draw-order artifact (the ring's gentle waver plus widest-drawn-first left the lateral's top segment uncovered), not a geometry error - the endpoint sits on the ring line in both the before and after manifests, so there is nothing for a manifest check to separate. The fix is to draw the polder RING trunk (feeder / drain / toe collectors) LAST, ON TOP of the laterals, so every lateral-to-trunk junction is a clean T covered by the trunk (comb nets set no `seg`, so their widest-first order is unchanged and byte-identical); the laterals are also snapped exactly onto the feeder/drain centerlines. `field_ditches_terminate` still guards termination; the clean junction itself is verified visually. **Superseded in part (GM 2026-07-24)**: the claim here that "the endpoint sits on the ring line in both the before and after manifests, so there is nothing for a manifest check to separate" was wrong - the st-space snap leaves a 2-5 px residual in DRAWN space because `grid()` is a nonlinear warp, which is exactly what a manifest check CAN separate. See 'Watercourses JOIN, they do not cross' below. *Village hug:* the east-edge wander pulled the field away from the straight house line, so Kuwabata's cluster was moved ~30 px closer to the dike to keep >=5 houses ringing the field (`field_ringed`). Negative fixtures: the pre-fix manifests of both polders are frozen - `pool/regressions/dikepond_and_polder_wander_gaps_fire_on_prefix_kuwabata.json` (fires all four new checks) and `pool/regressions/polder_wander_and_sluice_gaps_fire_on_prefix_enokida.json` (the rice-polder variant, fires the two dike checks). (6) **The green floor IS the ring-canal interior** (`polder_floor_is_ring_interior`; GM 2026-07-22 follow-up): the field greenery (`comb_base_fill`) was drawn to the dike-boundary ENVELOPE, a separate rectangle that - once the ring canal wavers under `edge_wander` - drifts in and out of the outermost channels (sometimes green spills past the ring toward the dike, sometimes it falls short). `build_polder` now returns `floor` = the four ring sides concatenated into the closed inner-toe loop, and `comb_base_fill` fills THAT (the ring canal draws on top), so the greenery is bounded exactly by the outermost irrigated channels. The check requires every recorded field-floor vertex to lie within ~8 px of a ring channel centerline; the pre-fix envelope floor sat ~9-22 px out at the dike boundary and fires (negative fixture `pool/regressions/polder_floor_is_ring_interior_fires_on_an_envelope_floor.json`).

  **Watercourses JOIN, they do not cross - and the snap must happen in DRAWN space** (`water_channels_join_not_cross`, `field_ditch_tips_land_on_the_trunk`; GM 2026-07-24, Enokida: "many of the irrigated channels intersect rather than joining"). Second pass item (2) above snapped each polder lateral's ends onto the ring feeder/drain by solving for the trunk's `s` at the lateral's own `t` - correct in (s, t) space, but `grid()` is a NONLINEAR warp (`edge_wander`'s cwarp/fwarp, plus `mosaic`), so a point lying exactly on the straight st-space chord between two trunk vertices maps a few px OFF the straight xy-space segment the trunk is actually STROKED along. The gross stubs went away and a 2-5 px residual stayed, which is precisely the range where a near-perpendicular lateral pokes a visible tail out the far side of the trunk: the pair reads as a 4-way crossroads instead of a T. (why: [`../research/archetypes.md#wet-rice-hydrology-has-no-crossings-to-draw`](../research/archetypes.md#wet-rice-hydrology-has-no-crossings-to-draw)) *The fix:* `build_polder` keeps `_s_on_side` as the position GUESS and then projects the tip onto the trunk's own drawn xy polyline (`_onto`) - exact whatever the warp does; Enokida's column-lateral tips went from 2.3-5.3 px off-centerline to <= 0.05 px. *The check, and why it is measured the way it is:* at every crossing between two DRAWN strokes, take each stroke's endpoint nearest that point and ask whether that tip sits inside the OTHER stroke's drawn band (perpendicular distance to its centerline <= its stroke half-width, +1 px for coordinate rounding and the round linecap). If either tip is buried, the meeting is a junction and the overrun hides under the ink; if neither is, the stub shows. The measure is PERPENDICULAR TO THE OTHER STROKE rather than run-length along the stub's own line, and that is the whole trick - a delivery ditch taking off at a shallow angle overruns the crossing by 4-8 px along its own line yet never leaves the trunk's band (Honda, Ikegami, Nagahara and Hoshizora all do this, and all four read as clean Y-offtakes), while the same overrun on a perpendicular T pokes straight out. A run-length rule cannot separate those. Swept over every pool map the perpendicular rule flagged Enokida's four laterals and nothing else. Both checks read the POST-CLIP record (`drawn_channels`, which now carries `w0`/`w1`) rather than `field_ditches`/`channels`, per the same-source doctrine under PLANK BRIDGES: the pre-clip geometry differs wherever a mouth was snapped onto a pond, moat, or stream, so judging a junction there would judge a position that is never drawn. Negative fixture: `pool/regressions/water_channels_cross_the_polder_ring_enokida.json` (the real pre-fix Enokida manifest).

  **Polder mosaic vs grid: a tunable knob for two historically-real looks** (`build_polder` `mosaic`; GM 2026-07-22, researched). The GM asked whether real dike-ponds were the neat rectangular grid the maps drew, or something rougher. (why: [`../research/archetypes.md#grid-vs-mosaic-the-arrangement-differed-by-system`](../research/archetypes.md#grid-vs-mosaic-the-arrangement-differed-by-system)) *The decision:* since both are accurate AND two same-type polders that look different multiply map variety, `mosaic` is a tunable knob (0 = the surveyed grid, the default). It displaces the INTERIOR bund-node lattice by a smooth CORRELATED field tapered to 0 at the pinned perimeter (so the envelope + dike stay put and parcels never leave the block): neighboring nodes move together, so the lattice lines stay continuous but BEND - the interior laterals become meandering creeks and the parcels skew to trapezoids, while the individual ponds stay 4-sided (matching the research: the mosaic is in the ARRANGEMENT, not in ponds being triangular). Displacement draws from a separate rng, so every `mosaic=0` map stays byte-identical. Enokida stays the **grid** (its 圩田 identity); Kuwabata now runs **mosaic=0.5** (its 桑基魚塘 identity), and declares `meta(pond_layout="mosaic")`. *Testing (and why no gate check):* the knob is verified by an ENGINE unit test (`test_build_polder_mosaic_knob`: byte-stable at 0, deterministic, and the parcels measurably more trapezoidal at 0.5) plus the existing gate, which already passes on the mosaic map. A per-map manifest CHECK was deliberately NOT added: the mosaic signature lives in the RELATIVE bending of neighboring lattice lines, but every aggregate manifest metric tried (lateral straight-line deviation, quadratic-fit residual, parcel-area cv) overlaps heavily with the smooth `edge_wander` bend, and the full parcel polygons are not recorded - so a manifest check would be theater, not teeth. Instead the variety is made to COUNT structurally: `pond_layout` is a 9th `twin_axes` axis (grid vs mosaic), so the twin-detector reads a grid polder and a mosaic polder of the same water direction as genuinely distinct places.

  **Polder fourth pass: the dike-pond reads as what it is - planted banks, textured rice, no floating outlines** (GM 2026-07-23, researched). Looking at Kuwabata the GM asked three questions, each answered here. (1) *What are the orange outlines?* The base polder draws every parcel flat rice-green with a tan bund stroke; the ~10% of parcels the wholesale conversion left as rice (item 8 of the second pass) kept that rendering, and with no surface texture and a fill near the floor green they read as floating tan outlines around nothing - and the one FLOODED leftover read as "a pond with no mulberry dike". *Decision:* under `eligible="all"` (the archetype's wholesale case ONLY - a partial overlay's unconverted plots are ordinary textured comb paddies already), `apply_land_use` repaints every leftover parcel as textured standing rice (`_paddy_surface` transplant mottle; a flooded leftover keeps its flood tint + sheen, and - GM follow-up, same day - its waterline draws ROUNDED and slightly irregular via `_rounded_pond` at a small inset with no edge stroke, because bund corners silt round and a hand-piled bund toe wanders; the missing stroke and missing mulberry bank are what keep it reading as a wet field, not dug infrastructure) and erases the bund outline with a same-color covering stroke - inside a dike-pond block the neighbors' raised banks are what bound a rice parcel, so its own drawn bund was noise. Enokida (the rice polder) is deliberately untouched: its tiled bund grid is the established look and the GM has not yet interrogated it. The repaint records `leftover_plots` centroids in the `land_use` manifest entry.   **Polder fifth pass: a gap is only as wide as what runs in it, and hand-piled earth is never ruled** (GM 2026-07-24, looking at Enokida). Two parcel-scale questions, both now placement rules with teeth. (1) *What is between the rectangles?* The parcels are drawn as separate insetted polygons, so the space between two of them IS the bund - `gap[0]` = 3 px (~3 ft, the walking bund / azemichi, attested 20-50 cm of ridge plus the footpath) between parcels stacked down the fall, `gap[1]` = 8 px (~8 ft) on the module column lines, where a 3.2 px lateral plus its spoil banks actually runs. But `inset()` applied the CROSS gap uniformly, so when a bay split into side-by-side strips those intra-bay cut lines got the full ditch-corridor width too: a bare 8 ft strip between two strips of one holding, wider than any attested plain aze and a promise of water that is not there. *The decision:* a gap is only as wide as what runs in it. `inset()` now takes the two cross-direction gaps PER PARCEL, and `build_polder(split_gap=...)` (default `gap[0]`) gives intra-bay split lines the same plain walking bund the row edges get; only the module column lines, where a lateral is actually drawn, keep the corridor width. Kuwabata is unaffected - its dike-pond `gap=(11, 11)` is already symmetric. (2) *Why are the lines perfectly straight with sharp angles?* (GM: "even when a human has created a 4-sided shape out of earth with the intention of making the sides straight and regular, there will be variation and probably rounded corners rather than sharp angles.") A farmer piling a rectangular basin out of puddled mud INTENDS four straight sides and four right angles and gets neither, for two separate physical reasons worth drawing separately. **Corners round**: a right angle in soft earth is the thinnest, least-supported point of the whole ridge - it slumps under its own weight and under every rain - and it is the one spot on the bund that every person, ox, and carrying-pole cuts across instead of walking to the point; the spring re-plastering (azenuri) restores the ridge, not the geometry, so the corner converges on a walked-and-slumped curve of a few feet' radius. This is the same argument `_rounded_pond` already made for the dug fish ponds and the mulberry banks (third and fourth passes above) - it applies to every earthwork the farmers piled, not only the ones holding fish. **Edges wander**: a run is paced and eyeballed between two corners and re-cut a little differently every time a holding is split, sold, or re-plastered, so a "straight" 100 ft bund bows by a foot or two - straight in intent and to the eye at a distance, never to the ruler. *The decision:* new `waterfields.organic_parcel()` fillets every corner with a quadratic bezier and wanders every run between them, returning a SAMPLED polygon (36 vertices at the shipped density) rather than a path, so the manifest record, the point-in-poly checks, and the dike-pond overlay all keep working on an ordinary polygon. Three details earn their keep: the fillet reach is drawn INDEPENDENTLY for the two legs of each corner (a corner slumps toward whichever side is walked or under-plastered; one symmetric reach per corner is exactly what makes a filleted rectangle read as a drawn-on CSS border-radius instead of as earth), each wander sample offsets independently rather than tracing one clean arc, and the wander amplitude is CAPPED at half the inset the caller opened less a hairline, so two neighbors can never bow into contact. The organic pass draws from its own rng, so every parcel CORNER stayed byte-identical and only the outline between the corners softened. `build_polder(organic=(fillet_frac_of_cell, bow_frac_of_run))` is the knob; at the shipped `(0.05, 0.02)` a 110 ft module rounds at ~5.5 ft and wanders ~1 ft. *Teeth:* `polder_parcels_are_organic`. `draw_comb_field` now records two more numbers per parcel - vertex count and sharpest turn in degrees - because a ruled quad is 4 vertices turning ~90 degrees at each while the drawn outline runs 36 vertices with a worst turn of ~52 across both pool polders, so a 12 / 65 threshold sits clear of both readings; recording the full outlines instead would roughly double a polder manifest for no extra teeth. The pre-fix Enokida manifest, whose parcels were ruled 4-gons recorded with no outline shape at all, is frozen as `pool/regressions/polder_parcels_are_organic_fires_on_prefix_enokida.json` (the no-passing-by-omission arm), with the ruled-outline arm covered synthetically by `test_polder_parcels_must_be_organic` (in `tests/check_village/`). *Scope, deliberately:* this pass covers the two POLDER archetypes only. The comb fan, terraces, and ribbon valleys carve plots that SHARE their edges (`paddy_fan_gapless` requires the carve to tessellate), so rounding each independently would open corner slivers the gapless check would rightly fire on - extending the rule there needed a different OPERATION, which is the next paragraph rather than a second call to the same helper. *Extended to the SHARED-BARRIER archetypes* (GM 2026-07-25, answering the scope note above): the GM's read of it was that a polder is genuinely the special case, because there the parcels are separate polygons with a real gap and rounding is SUBTRACTIVE - each parcel gives up its corners and the bund, being the space between, simply widens. A comb / terrace / ribbon carve has no gap: the bund IS the shared line and the carve must tessellate (`paddy_fan_gapless`), so shrinking the cells would tear holes in the field. The GM's framing of the correct operation there is exactly right and is what shipped: keep the carve untouched and ADD material - pile earth into the crossings, so a junction stops being two hairlines meeting at a point and becomes a lumpy node of bund, and the four basin corners read rounded because the earth has taken them. This is the truest part of the whole rule: a bund junction is the most-worked point in a field (four basins push water at it, it carries the crossing foot traffic, it is where someone stands to open and close the water, and it is the first thing to slump and get re-piled), so it genuinely carries more earth than the runs between. `Settlement.bund_junctions(plots, name)` finds a junction from the DRAWN geometry - wherever >=3 plot corners coincide, bunds cross - which makes the pass SELF-SELECTING: a polder's parcels are inset away from each other and share no corner at all, so nothing is drawn on the one archetype that must not get it (asserted in `test_bund_junctions_pile_earth_only_where_bunds_actually_cross` (in `tests/settlement/`)). TRUE SCALE: a plain aze is ~1.5 ft (`AZE_FT`) and a junction node widens to ~4-6 ft, so the drawn node is 4-6 px at hamlet scale and honestly sub-2 px at city scale - floored at the stroke width so it never vanishes, never inflated past the attested node, since a legibility-sized dot here would be a fake 15 ft earthwork at every crossing. The eight gens that draw their plot loop inline call it themselves; `draw_comb_field` calls it for the rest. *No manifest check*, on the same reasoning as the mulberry rows of the fourth pass: comb fields do not record plot outlines at all (only the city gens record `plot_polys`), so a manifest check would be theater rather than teeth - the honest coverage is the unit test above plus the pool sweep. *Sixth pass - the regularity was in the LATTICE, not the corners* (GM 2026-07-25, two notes in sequence). First: the fillets above read as "a circle at each intersection, which actually makes it look less natural" - true on both sides, and for two different reasons. On the polder the corner reach was drawn from a tight band, so all four parcels meeting at a junction rounded by the same amount and cut a clean disc out of it; on the comb the node was a blob CENTERED on the crossing, and at 4-6 px no jitter on a 7-gon's radius survives rasterization - every junction got an identical stamp, which is MORE machine-made than the sharp cross it replaced. Then the deeper diagnosis, which outranks the first: "maybe a big part of the solution is to make the lines less straight generally - even human-made bunds won't be perfectly parallel to one another in the way that these are depicted." That is the root of it. Every wander the engine had was COHERENT: `edge_wander` and `mosaic` warp the polder lattice as one piece on purpose (so each field edge stays parallel to its own section of dike), and the comb's rows were constant-fall contours, so in both archetypes every interior line kept exactly its neighbors' shape and the spacing between two bunds never changed anywhere. Real fragmented tenure does not look like that: a bund is laid by eye between two points, by a different household in a different decade, so neighboring lines drift, converge, and open out again and a strip is wider at one end than the other. *The decisions:* (1) **Per-line wander, polder** (`build_polder(line_wander=)`, default 0.10 of the module): each row line and each column line gets its OWN low-frequency profile, amplitude and phase drawn per line. A node is shared by its row and its column, so the lattice stays consistent - lines still meet, parcels are still quads, the laterals that follow the column nodes just meander with them - and each profile tapers to zero at both ends and is suppressed on the four boundary lines, so the envelope, the perimeter dike, and the ring canal do not move. (2) **Per-row wander, comb** (`_carve`): the existing cross wobble moved a point ALONG its contour, which bent the column bunds while leaving the rows dead straight and exactly parallel - the machine read survived in the one place the comb still had it. A point now also drifts IN FALL by a profile that depends on where it sits along the row and on which row it is, so no two rows carry the same shape. It tapers to zero at the sector's first and last row, because those two are pinned by water rather than free: the head row sits ON the supply canal (it takes water through bund cuts) and the closing rank sits ON the drain collector. That taper also keeps each fan's extent exactly where the gen placed the village, gardens, and label against it - without it, Akagahara's field grew a few px at the toe and tripped `all_houses_field_adjacent`, `gardens_clear_of_paddies`, and `labels_within_image`, which is the check bed catching the knock-on correctly. (3) **Corner reach spread wide**, from a narrow band to a triangular distribution with a long tail (~2.2x) and a floor near 0.12x that leaves a corner all but square - because corners really do differ that much: the one on the walked side wears to a broad sweep while the one behind a neighbor's bund keeps its angle for years - plus roughness on the arc itself, since a mathematically clean curve is the other half of what reads as stamped. (4) **The comb's junction fill is no longer a disc**: it is a separate fillet in each quadrant, one per plot corner meeting there, each with its own two legs and its own bulge, and roughly a quarter of quadrants left bare - so a crossing can be piled heavily on one side and untouched on the other. *Two engine traps this pass surfaced, both worth keeping in mind for any jitter added to drawn geometry:* roughness must scale to the feature it roughens, and sampling density must follow feature SIZE. A corner drawn nearly square spans a fraction of a pixel; six samples across it sit 0.2 px apart, where a wobble sized for the global fillet - or even plain float noise - reverses the direction of travel and folds the outline into a burr (measured: turns of 180 degrees). Both are fixed at the source: roughness scales to that corner's own reach, the run wander to that run's own length, and a corner is sampled `min(arc, reach*0.8)` times, so a big sweep gets the full curve and a tight corner gets a single midpoint - which is also the honest record of a corner that never really rounded. *Teeth revised:* `polder_parcels_are_organic` measured the SHARPEST turn per parcel, which stops meaning anything once some corners legitimately stay square - it is a statistic about the single least-rounded corner and says nothing about the parcel. The manifest now records the COUNT of still-square corners (>60 deg) per parcel, and the check reads two levels: per parcel >=12 vertices (a ruled quad has 4, and no easing gets a hand-drawn outline near that), and across the fabric a mean of <=2.5 square corners per parcel (a field where nothing eased scores the full 4.0; both pool polders sit at ~1.4). Individual all-square parcels stay legal, which is the point.
