# The provincial government quarter and martial training

*Part of the provincial-city docs - the tier definition, population and caste numbers, and the list of city-scale checks live in [`../cities.md`](../cities.md). The shared town/city feature vocabulary is in [`../urban-features.md`](../urban-features.md).*

**Research:** the historical basis for these rules - what was found, the decision it drove, and every disclosed departure - is in [`../../research/cities/government.md`](../../research/cities/government.md). Load it when you are CHANGING a rule or questioning one, not to follow it.

- **The provincial government is in the interior** (every provincial city, walled or not): the **governor's mansion** (`s.governor_mansion(...)`, a grand walled compound; `city_has_governor_mansion`), the **six provincial ministry offices** (`s.ministry(x, y, name)`; `city_has_six_ministries` - Rites, Revenue, Retainers, War, Works, Justice), and a **samurai neighborhood** (`city_has_samurai_neighborhood`). **The Ministry of Rites sits in the temple neighborhood** (`city_has_ministry_of_rites`, exactly one) because it oversees temples and shrines; religious buildings at city scale are **temples** (`religious_matches_scale`, all of `kind="temple"`), and a city can have **several**. Where a **city street runs straight UP TO a temple** (a street that terminates at its front, within ~28px), a **torii arch must stand on that street just in front of the temple** (`city_temple_approach_has_torii`) - pass `s.shrine_hall(..., torii=[(tx, ty)])` a point on the approach (the `torii=` param both draws the arch and reserves a no-build block around it). Torii render in the TOP layer, so the street passes *beneath* the arch (`city_torii_over_streets`); each torii records its draw-z in `M["torii"]` as `[x, y, z]`. Temples set back from the grid (no street running up) need no torii - Tango has a Temple of Benten and a Temple of Daikoku in the SW temple neighborhood and a Temple of Bishamon among the SE samurai (the warrior fortune). **A temple's torii COUNT is its per-temple ROLL, not the remaining street room** (`city_temple_torii_fill_approach` RETIRED 2026-07-24, superseded by the seeded roll - a rolled 1 beside an open street is a hall with one patron gate, not an unfinished avenue; `torii_match_roll` gates drawn == rolled/pinned instead). The gen authors the avenue GEOMETRY to fit its ground: Nagahara's Ebisu rolled the full 7 and its authored line marches due south from the monzen lane; the engine lays it at the ~20 ft house pitch, so the run ends at y1236 - well short of the samurai ward fence at y1311. It shipped at a 19px (57 ft) stride until 2026-07-25, which put its seventh arch AT y1310, standing IN that fence (the gen comment had the fence 20px further south than it is): `torii_clear_of_walls` now gates that, and `torii_avenue_pitch_capped` gates the 51 ft stride the first fix replaced it with. Re-pitching also freed the ground the long sando had blocked, so the SE rowpack seated 5 more houses and the pocket's single idobata went over the 26-household ceiling - its `place_wells` spacing was tightened 56 -> 42 to give it a second well. (Knock-ons like that are normal when an avenue shortens: the arches' no-build blocks are what was holding the ground open.) The torii block reserves only the arch's own footprint (`bm=20`, kept *smaller* than a street corridor) so adding arches along a street doesn't shove the frontage houses off it. **A temple NEIGHBORHOOD (>= 2 temples clustered within ~400px) should be dotted with a smattering of small wayside SHRINES** (`s.small_shrine(x, y)` - a vermilion-roofed shed with a little torii, non-residential, recorded in `M["religious"]` as `kind="small_shrine"`: it is not a dwelling, needs no torii avenue, and is exempt from `religious_matches_scale`). `city_temple_neighborhood_has_shrines` wants >= 3 of them near the clustered temples; a LONE temple among houses (the Bishamon temple in the samurai quarter) is not a neighborhood and needs none. Place the shrines BEFORE the packs (they reserve a no-build block) and clear of the temples/wall/streets. The merchant district and laborer neighborhoods are checked for presence too (`city_has_merchant_district`, `city_has_laborer_neighborhoods`). **The samurai neighborhood must be SIZED to the cohort**: a provincial city is ~10% samurai (~300 of ~3,000, budgets.md) = ~60 households, most housed here as individual houses (the governor's compound + extramural estates hold the rest), so `city_samurai_housing_sufficient` wants >= ~65% of the expected household count (~`0.10 x pop / 5`) drawn as samurai houses - a token cluster of a couple dozen is far too few. Pack them with `face_streets="fill"` (NOT `"core"`: the samurai quarter is laced with government streets, and "core" skips the near-street band, leaving almost everything unbuilt). And unlike the poor, **samurai LINE their streets** - `city_samurai_partly_front_streets` wants at least a third of samurai houses within ~90px of a lane (deeper lots sit behind, but the quarter fronts its streets). **In-city samurai houses VARY IN SIZE by rank, and NONE are walled** (`city_samurai_housing_varied`): budgets.md's provincial-city rank table splits the resident cohort roughly ~25% senior (R5-7) / ~75% junior (R1-4), so the neighborhood mixes a **minority of large senior houses** (`kind="samurai_large"`, the bigger urban dwelling) among **many small junior houses** (`kind="samurai"`). Interleave them in the pack (e.g. `["samurai"] * 4 + ["samurai_large"]` repeated) so the large ones front streets prominently. The check wants >= 3 large houses, the small houses a strict majority, **and NO `s.manor(...)` inside the wall ring** - samurai walled ESTATES sit OUTSIDE the walls (see below); the only walled samurai compound within the city is the governor's mansion. **The inverse also holds: NO free-standing samurai HOUSE stands outside the wall** (`city_samurai_houses_inside_walls`, hard-zero) - the only extramural samurai residences are those walled country estates; an unwalled lone samurai house beyond the rampart is defenseless and has no anchor. The commoner inside-walls check exempts samurai *because of the estates*, which is exactly the gap this closes - and placement code must apply the stay-inside-the-wall test to samurai house glyphs like any other dwelling, exempting only the hand-placed `s.manor` estates (validated instance: Tango's SE gap-fill sweep - a rectangular region poking past the round wall arc, run after the ring bound was cleared - leaked 14 samurai houses into the moat berm, 2026-07-20; Nagahara's SW arc leaked 1 the same way). The **governor's mansion is legitimately LARGE** - the historical provincial *yamen* / governor's compound was a whole city block (main hall, residence, retinue quarters, jail, granary, gardens - dozens of buildings), drawn here walls-only with the interior implied; so its size is correct (`city_governor_mansion_large` enforces that it is the grandest compound - a city block at least as large as any estate and >= 3x any ministry), but like the ministries and temples it must sit INSIDE the rampart, clear of the wall stroke and moat (`city_government_clear_of_wall_moat`). **The five other ministries cluster AROUND the governor's yamen** (the government district, threading into the samurai quarter - as in a Chinese provincial seat / Japanese castle town), not strewn about (`city_ministries_cluster_at_government`, within ~480px of the governor); only Rites sits apart, with the temples it oversees. In a *planned* city the offices are not scattered: the yamen sat where the main streets crossed and the bureaus LINED the avenues around it (the Chinese 'official street'; the jokamachi grid where compounds align tightly along the streets), so **every ministry must FRONT a city street** (`city_ministries_front_a_street`, within ~85px of one) - lay a short government avenue (an L wrapping the yamen works well) and seat the ministries along it. A street fronted by ministries counts as served (the `streets_have_buildings` helper now credits civic buildings - ministries, the governor's yamen, temples, flophouses - as frontage, not just houses and shops). **A government office stands in its OWN ground** (`city_government_offices_dont_abut`): a ministry or the governor's yamen is a large, important compound and must keep a clear gap (~14px) from every other building/compound - ordinary city houses may touch each other, but these may not abut a neighbor (this caught the Ministry of Works touching the Ministry of Justice, and the Ministry of Rites touching the Temple of Daikoku). When two offices are too close, separate them - and mind the knock-on (the Rites/Daikoku fix needed the SE caravan inn nudged east to make room, since Rites is wedged against the ring road). The **internal streets** must likewise stay inside the wall (`city_streets_clear_of_wall_moat`) - the elliptical wall is a *polygon* whose chords cut *inside* the smooth ellipse, so size streets/compounds against the actual wall points (or just inset them), not the ideal ellipse.
- **A servant dwelling inside the samurai ward is drawn as a RANGE ON ITS MASTER'S STREET EDGE, never as a freestanding house in a rank** (GM 2026-08-02, after the ward was cleared of barred kinds and the packs promptly refilled the same ground with servant houses: *"I swear I'm seeing way MORE commoner houses in the samurai neighborhood now!"*). The servant glyph is a laborer glyph with a 4 ft trim and one shade of tan, so caste-correct data reads as commoner fabric the moment it is drawn detached and ranked. **The COUNT is right and must not be cut**: budgets.md gives a provincial city 120 servant families of which **72 are attached to samurai households** (30 to wealthy at property 2, 42 indentured to non-wealthy at property 1) against 60 samurai families - about one servant household per junior house and two per senior - and they are property-holding, taxed families, so they must be housed and counted. It is the ARRANGEMENT that carries the rule: **bind each ward servant household to a specific samurai house** and draw it as a long, thin range (15 ft deep - the measured *nagayamon* depth - running its master's own frontage), or leave it undrawn for households with no room, whose servants sleep under the master's roof (itself attested below ~100-300 koku). **`s.servant_ranges()`** does this: it seats each ward household's range on a free FLANK flush with the house's front plane, falls back to the REAR service row (China's *houzhaofang*) where a terraced neighbor leaves no flank, shortens the range before giving up, and gives a senior house two and a junior one, per the budgets ratio. It is idempotent, so it may be re-run after a late household top-up. **ORDERING:** call it AFTER every ward samurai house is placed (a house seated later can come to rest across a range's doorway - `top_up` clears only its OWN door, not its neighbors') and AFTER `s.ward`, which is what defines the interior; but BEFORE the exact-population fill, which then tops the servant count up in the commoner quarters. A range is drawn at **>= 3x its depth** (45 ft at city grain) - below that it reads as a blob rather than a range, and a household with no seat that long simply goes without. Two legibility rules earned from settlement-review 2026-08-03, both now in the engine: **`s.building` scales its cosmetics off the THIN dimension** (corner radius, stroke, and a ridge that runs the length instead of a dash in the middle), because the fixed values tuned on a squarish house turn a 5 px-deep range into a pill - a rail or kerb, the sheet's vocabulary for small gray fixtures; and **both flanks are tried at full frontage before any shortened seat**, because a single short appendage stuck to one corner of a ROTATED house reads as an implement (the reviewer read four as a paintbrush and a meat cleaver before reading them as households). A seat is also refused if anything touches it more closely than its own host, or it reads as the neighbor's range. Gated by **`city_ward_servants_housed_as_ranges`**, which requires every servant inside the fence to name its host, abut it, and be a range rather than a cottage. Note the knock-on the GM should expect: with commoner fill barred, the ward closes the declared population with MORE SAMURAI HOUSEHOLDS (each arriving with its own range) rather than commoner terraces - which is the right answer for the city's emptiest quarter and keeps the samurai cohort inside its caste band. Both traditions reach this independently - a Japanese *buke-yashiki* housed its *genan/gejo/chugen* in the perimeter *nagaya* that IS the plot wall, the gate rooms, or *nando* off the kitchen; a Chinese elite compound housed them in the *daozuofang*, the south row whose windowless back is the street wall. Ranks of small uniform dwellings are a real castle-town texture but belong to *ashigaru kumi-yashiki* on the town FRINGE, not inside the fence. Full findings, measured examples and the disclosed liberty (our in-city samurai houses are unwalled, so the range alone has to carry a read that history gave to a wall): [`../../research/cities/government.md#servant-housing-in-the-samurai-ward---servants-are-drawn-as-walls-not-as-houses`](../../research/cities/government.md#servant-housing-in-the-samurai-ward---servants-are-drawn-as-walls-not-as-houses).
- **A provincial city is the first tier that TRAINS INDOORS: one state martial hall plus a rolled count of private dojos** (`city_has_martial_hall`, `city_martial_hall_has_archery_range`, `city_dojo_count_follows_samurai`, `city_dojos_among_samurai`; GM 2026-07-25, reasoning in "Historical grounding: martial training in a provincial city" below). A county town draws a **practice ground and no dojo at all** (buildings.md) - a dojo is a city institution. Every provincial city therefore keeps **exactly one `s.martial_hall(x, y)`**, the state training institution: its own walled compound (130 x 100 ft) holding a 120-tatami hall, the sensei's house, and a 100 ft archery lane with an azuchi butt, drawn in **state violet** like the ministries. It is NOT a wing of the governor's yamen - it is a separate compound, though standing near the government it answers to is right. Private **`s.dojos([seat, seat])`** are then rolled from the samurai cohort: **1 per full 200 samurai plus a remainder-fraction chance of one extra, floored at 1**, so a ~3,000 city (~10% samurai = ~300) keeps 1 + a 50% roll and a ~4,000 city keeps exactly 2. Each is a 76 x 44 ft walled lot with a ~59-tatami hall and a training yard, drawn in ordinary building **tan** (private, not state) - and with **no archery lane**, which is the state hall's to keep. Provide **two vetted seats** so either roll can land; the roll is recorded at `meta['dojo_roll']` and gated, so a stale hand count cannot ship. Both kinds must stand **in or against the samurai neighborhood** - a dojo serves samurai and nobody else. Place them BEFORE the quarter's packs (like the fire tower) so the rows flow around them, and find the seat with `s.open_seat(...)` at that point in the gen rather than guessing; mind that the caption is wide relative to the compound (~70px of text against a 43x33px footprint), so in a ward packed to its budget the CAPTION is usually what binds, not the compound - `s.martial_hall(..., label_xy=(x, y))` hands it to a nearby pocket, or centers it low on the compound itself when the ward has no pocket at all (Nagahara).
- **A small city GATES its samurai/government ward; it does not WALL every neighborhood** (`city_samurai_quarter_gated`, `city_samurai_ward_sealed`). The walled-ward (Chinese *fang*) system - a continuous internal wall around every quarter - was a great-capital, Tang-era feature (Chang'an's wards were 30-80 hectares each, one outsizing a whole provincial city) and was torn down by the Song. At provincial-city scale the samurai/government quarter is **sealed by a light earthwork/palisade fence** (`s.ward(name, boundary, gates)`) whose ends abut the city wall, pierced ONLY at **kido** wooden ward gates (`s.kido`) where a street crosses it - so the gates can't simply be walked around and the road network has no ungated samurai<->commoner path. The gen must run the quarter's access streets THROUGH the fence line (extend them to the road) so each crossing is a gate. The check wants a ward declared, >= 2 kido near the yamen, every street/road/**ring road**/alley crossing the fence gated within ~32px, and both fence ends within ~45px of the wall. (The ring road is a closed loop near the wall, so it crosses the fence at the two points where the fence ends approach the rampart - gate both.) Keep commoner packs/alleys OUTSIDE the fence. **A commoner lane that APPROACHES the ward fence must reach it and END AT A KIDO GATE** (`city_lanes_reach_ward_gates`) - laborers come in to work in the samurai quarter, so the lanes they use pull right up to a gate rather than stopping a sliver short. (`city_samurai_ward_sealed` only catches lanes that *cross* the fence ungated; this catches lanes that stop *short* of it.) A lane endpoint within ~60px of a ward fence, heading at it (within ~37 deg) with a clear path (no building or the main wall between), must sit on the fence (within ~6px) AND have a kido within ~34px, else it fires. Two important distinctions: (1) **the MAIN city wall is exempt** - a lane may stop short of the outer rampart (that is the city's own edge, not a neighborhood boundary); only internal ward fences pull a lane in. (2) **Interior ward lanes are exempt** - a government/samurai lane near the fence from the INSIDE is not an entry; the check uses an interior anchor (the governor's yamen, else the fence centroid) and skips any endpoint on the same side as it. When you extend an alley to a gate, the run-out *past its last building* is a legitimate **gate-access spur** and does not count against `alleys_serve_buildings` (that check now subtracts the spur for an alley ending at a kido). Add the gates via `s.ward(..., gates=[(x, y, horizontal), ...])` - `horizontal=False` for an N-S lane crossing an E-W fence leg. **The fence ends must MEET SOLID wall** (`city_ward_fence_meets_wall`): not merely near the wall *polygon* (a point can be 40px inside the rampart yet read as on it, since `poly_dist` is 0 for interior points - use `edge_dist`), and NOT landing in a **gate opening** (where the wall is cut, so the fence meets nothing and the ward can be walked around) - extend the fence to solid rampart, jogging clear of the gate if need be. **And the wall renders ON TOP of the fence** at the junction (`city_ward_fence_under_wall`): the fence runs UNDER the rampart, not painting over it - `s.ward` lays a short wall cap (recorded with the fence's z AND its polyline `pts` in `M["wards"][i]["wall_caps"]`) over each end so the rampart wins. **That cap must lie FLUSH along the wall** (`city_ward_cap_flush_to_wall`): it FOLLOWS the rampart - walking the wall +/-16px through any vertex in the span - rather than being a single straight tangent. A straight cap laid where the fence abuts AT a wall CORNER cannot follow the bend, so it juts past the corner and reads as **two wall sections overlapping instead of one bent wall** (the Nagahara SW corner, GM 2026-07). The check samples each cap vertex against the wall polyline and fires if any sits > 4px off it. (Full walled wards become plausible only at the future capital-city tier, ~12,000.) **And the sealed ward holds ONLY its own residents** (`city_samurai_ward_residents_only`; GM 2026-08-02, on Minami: 2 laborer houses in the middle of the samurai neighborhood and a merchant row hugging the inside of the west fence, leaked in by whole-interior top-up sweeps whose rectangles overlap the ward). Historical grounding: an Edo-era jokamachi zoned samurai and chonin ground apart as a matter of LAW (*bukechi* vs *choninchi*), and a Chinese provincial seat likewise kept commerce off the yamen quarter - a laborer terrace between two samurai houses inside the palisade is not variety, it contradicts the fence around it. So only samurai houses (`samurai`/`samurai_large`), their live-in domestics (`servant` - **but see the next bullet: a servant dwelling in the ward is service accommodation BOUND to a samurai household, never a freestanding house in a rank**), and government ground stand inside the fence; `laborer`, `merchant`, `shop`, `inn`, `burakumin` and the rest of `settlement.WARD_BARRED_KINDS` are refused. `monk_house` is deliberately allowed - a temple may legitimately stand inside the ward (Tango's Bishamon, the warrior fortune beside the garrison quarter) and its clergy row belongs with its temple, held there by the temple-neighborhood checks. Enforced at BOTH ends: `s.building` refuses a barred kind whose center lies inside a declared samurai ward (a whole-interior top-up rectangle overlapping the ward simply seats those households elsewhere on a later pass), and `s.ward` fails LOUDLY if a barred building already stands inside when the fence is declared - hoist `s.ward` ahead of every commoner pack. The ward INTERIOR is the fence closed with the wall arc between its wall-abutting ends, taken as the SMALLER of the two enclosed regions - a ward is a quarter carved off the city, never the larger half (all three pool cities: 21-25% of the walled area).
- **The kido squares to the LANE it bars, not to the fence it hangs in** (`kido_aligned_with_ward_fence`; GM 2026-07-26, refining the 2026-07-24 fence rule). A kido exists to shut a WAY - it is barred at night against traffic - so its roofed bar stands SQUARE ACROSS THE ROADBED running through it, and the ward fence meets the gate at whatever angle the fence happens to run. Only a gate with NO lane through it falls back to the LOCAL FENCE TANGENT, where the same anti-stamp doctrine still holds: a 30-degree fence run gets a 30-degree gate, never an axis-aligned glyph (as `city_wall_towers_aligned` does for rampart towers). **The two readings agree wherever a lane meets its fence squarely**, which is most crossings - and that is why the pure-fence rule looked right for two days. They diverge on an OBLIQUE crossing, and there the fence reading is plainly wrong: Tango's SW ring-road gate, drawn on its ~44-degree fence jog while the ring road it bars runs at ~172 degrees, sat 38 degrees off square to its own roadbed and read as a glyph dropped on the road; Nagahara's SW gate was 44 degrees off the same way. Both pre-fix manifests are frozen in `pool/regressions/`, alongside the older axis-aligned-on-a-slant pair. `settlement.lane_through_gate` / `kido_bar_deg` are the single definition of "a road runs through here" and are used by BOTH `s.ward` (which places) and the check (which grades), so the two cannot drift; a lane running ALONGSIDE the fence (within 25 degrees of it) is excluded, since the gate does not bar it. Gates in `s.ward(...)` are plain (x, y) seats (a legacy horizontal flag is accepted and ignored); `s.kido(rot=, guard_side=)` covers hand placement. The check pins the recorded `rot` (legacy manifests fall back to the horizontal flag) to within 7 degrees mod 180; a kido more than ~16px from every fence is exempt (nothing to align to).
- **The guard box stands ward-side, on the VERGE - never in the roadbed** (`kido_guard_box_clear_of_lanes`; GM 2026-07-26). The gate watch belongs to the ward it seals, so `s.ward` hangs the box on the WARD-INTERIOR flank (it rotates and sides with the gate; the old fixed-per-orientation flanks put Nagahara's E-fence boxes on the street side). But the box is a small BUILDING, the one solid thing in the kido group, and a patrol road with a shack in its bed is not passable - Tango shipped BOTH its ring-road gates that way. `s.kido` now walks the box outward from the bar's end, trying the near side of the opening then the far side at the same distance, until it clears every lane bed by a ~12 ft verge AND stands off the rampart, compound walls and any mural tower. Three things it had to learn the hard way: **(1)** the ring road CURVES past the gate, so a box set back along the road walks into a bed straight-line arithmetic said it had cleared; **(2)** where the fence meets the rampart the box can slide onto a mural bastion, and since the gate cannot move and a coverage-thin curtain needs its tower, the BOX is what yields - to the other flank of its own gateway, an equally plausible spot for a watch shack (widening the tower keep-out instead was tried and dropped a legitimate tower, leaving a coverage hole just outside the exempt band); **(3)** the far flank can put it on the city wall, so the rampart is an obstacle too - its OWN ward fence is not, since the fence runs through the gate by construction. The check reads the box's recorded footprint (`M["kido"][i]["guard"]`), because the group bbox cannot tell the bar from the shack; the whole kido group is overlap-EXEMPT (the bar must cross both lane and fence), so this is the only rule protecting the box.
- **Keep-clear rules read the kido's TRUE parts, not its bbox** (`kido_quads`). Each gate records `parts` - the rotated quads of the roofed bar, its two posts and the guard box. An axis-aligned bounding box was honest while every kido was axis-aligned; once they turn onto their lane it overstates badly (Nagahara's SW gate at ~115 degrees has a bbox ~60% larger than the glyph, and `kido_clear_of_wall_towers` read it as sitting on a tower the gate plainly clears). `bbox` survives only for the label-occlusion pass, where over-stating is the safe direction.
- **Reserve a ward gate's ground with `s.kido_reservation(x, y, fence)`, not a hand-written rect.** `s.ward` draws near the END of a city gen, long after the packs, so each gate's ground must be blocked up front - but the glyph is NOT symmetric (it reaches ~36px on the guard-box flank and ~10px on the other) and its angle now follows the lane it bars, so a hand-tuned rect goes stale silently and a square big enough to be safe at any angle costs real housing (a +/-45 square cost Tango its merchant band and a public well when it was tried). The helper asks the engine for the same seat `s.ward` will take and returns the drawn extent inflated by a large dwelling's half-diagonal (block_polys are CENTER-tested for urban packs). **ORDERING:** call it AFTER the lanes through the gates are drawn - in particular after `s.ring_road(...)` - or it cannot see the road the gate squares to.

### Historical grounding: martial training in a provincial city

Researched 2026-07-25, prompted by the GM asking how many dojos a provincial city should have. It extends the county-tier finding already recorded in [`buildings.md`](../../buildings.md) ("A dojo is a city institution; county training is courtyard keiko") up the settlement ladder, and it is the reasoning behind every number in the martial-training bullet above.

  - *(why: [`../../research/cities/government.md#martial-training-is-an-urban-institution`](../../research/cities/government.md#martial-training-is-an-urban-institution))*

**What the L7R numbers force.** Working from budgets.md rather than from the anchors:

- A **county town** holds ~20 resident samurai (~15 working). No student body, no living for a sensei. Zero dojos - it draws a practice ground instead, which is the finding already in buildings.md.
- A **provincial city** holds **~300 resident samurai** (~10% of ~3,000; ~225 working, ~80% of them bushi-track per the caste table). That is a small *han*'s retainer band, which is exactly the size that historically seated a domain school - so the tier that seats a governor seats a martial hall.
- A **domain capital** holds ~1,560 resident samurai; **Otosan Uchi** ~40,000.

**The decision.** Roughly **one martial establishment per ~100 resident samurai**, counting the state hall as one of them. That ladder reads right at every rung: a county town at 0.2 rounds to none; a provincial city lands at ~3; a domain capital at ~15, which is where l7r.md's "the city's many dojos" starts being literally true; Otosan Uchi at ~400, of which 3-4 would be famous - the same long-tail-under-a-few-greats shape late-Edo Edo had. Note the ratio is on the **samurai count, not the population**: a dojo serves samurai and nobody else, and the samurai-share schedule in budgets.md already carries the density variation between tiers.

The generator splits that total into the two kinds it actually draws. The state hall is fixed at exactly one (a state institution is a program item, not a roll). The private tail rolls on the GM's formula - 1 per full 200 samurai plus a remainder-fraction chance of one extra - which puts a provincial city at 1-2 private halls and a 2-3 total, the lower-middle of the ~1-per-100 band. Keeping the private tail thin is deliberate: the *machi-dojo* boom was a million-person-city event, and a seat of ~3,000 has not had it.

**The countryside is deliberately not counted** (GM 2026-07-25). A province holds ~550 samurai in all - ~300 in the city, ~120 across its 6 county towns, ~90 on country estates - but only the city cohort supports institutions; the rural samurai either ride in or make do with the magistracy's practice ground. Asking separately about the countryside would double-count, because a provincial city's size already scales with the countryside that feeds it.

**Sizes, in real feet.** Everything is sized to its program, with no legibility inflation:

- The **hall** is 60 x 36 ft = 2,160 sqft = **120 tatami** (a mat is 3 x 6 ft). At the ~90-135 sqft per drilling samurai that buildings.md established for the county practice ground, that floor holds ~20 pairs at once - the officer cohort plus a school class.
- The **archery lane** is 100 ft long, covering the kyudo standard 28 m (92 ft) shot, with an **azuchi** earthen butt at its head. It is the same ~90 ft clear lane the Mode A azuchi uses, and it sits **inside** the compound wall - an unwalled shooting lane in a city street is a hazard, and the hall's whole point is that the province's youth train in one enclosed place.
- The **compound** is therefore 130 x 100 ft (13,000 sqft, ~0.30 acre): lane plus butt plus wall margins across, hall plus lane plus circulation deep. That is well below a ministry office compound (224 x 148 ft), which is right - a ministry is a bureau of clerks and archives, this is one hall and a yard.
- A **private dojo** is a 76 x 44 ft lot holding a 44 x 24 ft hall = 1,056 sqft = ~59 tatami, the **low end** of the Edo town-dojo band on purpose, plus a training yard. No archery lane: there is no room for a 92 ft shot on a 76 ft lot.
- The **weapon rack** (~8 x 2 ft) and the **striking posts** (*tategi*, ~1 ft) are sub-glyph at 3 ft/px, so they draw as fixed-size location markers under the Mode A stroke convention. Marking the gear rather than scaling it is the honest form of the finding - what makes a training ground read as established is the durable equipment practice leaves behind.

**Two deliberate departures.** (1) The compound is drawn as walls plus its three interior features with the rest implied, like the governor's yamen - a real bugeijo also had a changing room, an armory, and a viewing platform. (2) State violet vs private tan is a **map convention, not a historical one**; nothing about a domain school's construction differed from a private hall's. The color carries the ownership distinction that the map otherwise could not show.

**One knock-on worth knowing.** Adding the compound to `citybudget.CIVIC_PROGRAM` grew the derived interior, which grew Nagahara's budget-sized ring by 1px, which flipped which wall edge `seg_dist` called nearest for one untouched mural tower - and a correct tower failed `city_wall_towers_aligned`. The check now scores the best of the **two** nearest edges, because a tower seated near a vertex of the wall N-gon is legitimately square to either run that meets there.
