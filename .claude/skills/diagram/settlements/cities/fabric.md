# Urban fabric: streets, frontage, housing, fire defense

*Part of the provincial-city docs - the tier definition, population and caste numbers, and the list of city-scale checks live in [`../cities.md`](../cities.md). The shared town/city feature vocabulary is in [`../urban-features.md`](../urban-features.md).*

**Load this file when:** laying the street grid and filling the blocks - shop frontage, row-packed commoner terraces, door orientation, merchant estates, the Imperial-road commercial ribbon, civic amenities, and the fire-watch towers that packed wooden terraces make necessary.

---

- **Walled merchant compounds: 1-3 per provincial city, ROLLED 30/40/30, because the gate is a GRANTED privilege (GM 2026-07-23).** A gated compound is not something wealth can buy - it is a right the daimyo explicitly grants to a merchant family, mirroring the Edo-period system of individually granted merchant privileges (an audience with the daimyo at the New Year's celebration, permission to carry a persistent surname across generations despite not being samurai, and the like). So of a provincial city's ~12 very-rich merchant families (budgets.md, 2% of ~3,000), most can AFFORD the wall but lack the legal standing to build it - only 1-3 hold the grant. `s.merchant_estates(seats)` rolls the count per city (`MERCHANT_ESTATE_WEIGHTS`, seeded on the map seed like the torii re-roll; `count=` pins it), places the first n of a hand-vetted seat list (provide >= 3 so any roll lands), records the target in `meta['merchant_estate_roll']`, and `merchant_estates_match_roll` gates drawn == rolled - the pre-roll state (both cities hand-coding exactly 1, a copied pattern with no recorded reasoning) can never silently return. Seat-vetting lessons from wiring Tango (its gen comments carry the details): scan for court-sized pockets against the PRE-estate fixed features only (streets/wall/moat/water, early fixed wells with the |dx|>=39-or-|dy|>=31 wellhead clearance, frontage shops, civic + label ground - pack housing places after the estates and flows around; late well passes yield to the court blocks), keep seats off samurai/burakumin pack ground (caste), and pin-vet every seat with `count=3` against the full gate even when the current seed rolls fewer - a reseed may seat any of them. The engine registers each court in `self.placed` (SAT-distance) as well as `block_polys` (center-test), so pack houses keep real clearance from the compound wall. Towns hand-place (no roll column yet - Hoshizora/Hirameki carry none today); the capital column gets added when capital maps exist.
- **The Imperial road runs straight N-S through the city, gate to gate** (`city_imperial_road_through` - the road must run off both the top and bottom edges). It quarters the interior together with the implied E-W axis. The **`"Imperial Road"` label must sit OUTSIDE a gate** (`city_road_label_outside_walls`): inside the walls the same roadway is a *city street* the city maintains, not an Imperial responsibility, so pass `s.road(..., label_xy=(x, y))` a point beyond a gate (the default label anchor is the polyline midpoint, which for a city is the center - inside the walls).
- **A city ON the Imperial road LINES that road with commerce** (`city_imperial_road_has_commerce`). The through-road is the city's prime frontage: caravans and travelers pass along it, so it carries a **commercial ribbon** of shops + traveler services (inns), not bare housing-backs or empty ground. This is a **general rule for ANY city with an Imperial road, walled OR not** (a city *without* a road skips it, its commerce staying in the market district). The check measures the road's portion running *through* the city - bounded by the **wall** if there is one, else by the **urban footprint** (the bounding box of the city's buildings) - and wants commercial buildings (`shop`/`merchant`/`inn`) fronting it at roughly **>= 1 per 130px** of that length - a floor that catches a bare spine. Build it as a true **two-sided market street**: `s.frontage(<road sub-segment>, ["shop", ...], skip=<the road poly>, both=False, rows=2)` for the west side and a second call (a north-running sub-segment) for the east, plus a `s.pack(...)` to deepen a road-market block and a `s.inn(...)` or two for travelers. Capture the road polyline in a variable so you can pass it as `skip=` (a frontage row sits *against* the corridor it fronts). The dead frontage to watch for is the stretch between two quarters that both turn their backs to the road (Tango's was the band between the burakumin quarter and the merchant avenue) - fill it. Place the ribbon LAST in the gen (after every quarter's pack) so it flows into the gaps and perturbs no RNG-seeded placement. **General guidance on commercial intensity:** a provincial city's merchant/artisan caste is ~25% of households (~150 of 600, the highest commercial share of any tier), so a healthy commercial face is roughly **1 storefront per ~25-30 residents** (Tango ~3,000 -> ~105 `shop`+`merchant`+`inn` frontages); a road-city sits at the denser end of that band, with the increment concentrated as the road ribbon. (Domain capitals, all of which have an Imperial road, always carry this ribbon.)
- **A walled provincial city must keep >= 1 burakumin neighborhood INSIDE the walls** (`walled_city_has_burakumin_inside`): a city under siege cannot be without its burakumin (corpse-handling, leather, sandals, executions), so however many live outside, a neighborhood always sits within. Not all burakumin need be inside.
- **City streets form a crossing GRID of blocks** (`city_streets_have_buildings`, the same "no street to nowhere" rule as towns). Per quarter, draw a few crossing `s.street(...)` lines (2 N-S x 2 E-W = nine blocks is a good unit), then fill the whole quarter with **one dense `s.pack(bbox, [...], face_streets="fill")`**: the pack faces near-street buildings toward the streets (so every street is lined) and packs the block interiors with tight rows, while respecting the street corridors (so nothing sits on a street). **Density is what lines the grid** - the failure mode is a *sparse* pack leaving a street stretch empty, so pack to the real per-caste counts. Two rules of thumb learned building Tango: (1) don't draw two streets on the same line (a doubled corridor pushes buildings out of fronting range); (2) the big footprints (merchant 54x36, samurai 56x40) need `step >= ~0.8 x footprint` or they collide and place *fewer* - a larger step paradoxically packs more. (`s.frontage(street, [...], rows=N)` - front row plus tight rows behind - also works and is good for a single avenue, but multi-row frontages collide at grid intersections, so prefer dense `pack` for a true crossing grid.) **A larger city street must be LINED with buildings close to it** (`city_larger_streets_lined`): roadside land on a commercial/residential through-street is prime real estate, so no long stretch (>~140px) may run with the nearest building more than ~58px off it - stricter than `city_streets_have_buildings` (which tolerates a building up to ~105px away and so misses a street with a wide bare margin). Narrow gravel ALLEYS are exempt (the "small streets" threading block interiors need no frontage), and so is the GOVERNMENT avenue (its frontage is the spaced ministry compounds, governed by `city_ministries_front_a_street`, not shops/houses - the check exempts a street fronted by >= 2 ministries / the yamen). **Frontage `skip`-object GOTCHA** (this caused the very bug the check now guards): `s.frontage(street, ...)` sits tight against the street by *skipping that street's own corridor*, but it matches the corridor by **object identity** (`poly is skip`). If you pass `s.street(...)` one list literal and `front(...)` a *fresh* literal of the same coordinates, the skip will NOT match the registered corridor, the frontage gets blocked off the street by its own corridor, and the storefronts silently fail to place - leaving a bare avenue. **Capture the street in ONE variable and pass that same object to both** `s.street(AVENUE, ...)` and the frontage call. The grid must also be **one connected network wired to the Imperial road** (`city_streets_connected`) - run at least one street per quarter so its paved bed actually OVERLAPS the road's or another street's. Connectivity is **width-aware**: two lanes count as joined only when their centerline gap is less than the sum of their half-widths (the beds physically touch, so you can walk between them) - a street that stops even a roadbed short of the next reads as a *separate* network and fires (this caught the laborer grid ending 40px shy of the road). Extend the road-facing street of each quarter until it MEETS the road (to its centerline). Kido ward gates do not break connectivity - the street centerline runs on under the gate. Streets must also never come ALMOST together without meeting (`city_streets_no_near_miss`): a pair that closes to within ~2-30px but does not cross reads as a mistake (the classic case is a grid corner where a cross-street stops a sliver short of the through-street) - make the streets actually CROSS (extend one past the other) or pull them clearly apart, never leave a sliver gap. The mirror-image fault is a **stub** (`city_streets_no_intersection_stub`): a street that crosses another and then STOPS a sliver (~3-50px) past it. Fine to cross and run on (to the next block or the edge), or to terminate AT the junction (a clean L/T corner), but not to overshoot it by a little - so at a grid's outer corner, end the two outermost streets at their shared corner point rather than extending each past the other. **A related, more general rule covers ALLEYS too** (`city_lanes_meet_when_aligned`): if two lanes - streets OR alleys - are **heading straight at each other and stop just short**, with a CLEAR path between (no building, ward fence, or wall in the gap), they should simply **connect**. Unlike `city_streets_no_near_miss` (which only compares street-vs-street segment *proximity*), this fires on an *aligned endpoint* - a lane end whose outward direction points within ~37 deg at another lane within ~80px - so it catches collinear lanes that resume a sliver further on (the classic case: a quarter's vertical lane and a continuation lane in the adjacent strip, ending 10-56px apart on the same line). The fix is to extend one lane to meet the other (mind any check that scales to lane length, like `alleys_serve_buildings` - if extending a lane tips its served/length ratio, close the gap from the *other* lane's side instead). A gap that a building/fence/wall genuinely blocks is exempt (the lane is correctly routing around it). The rule is generic to any city with lanes, walled or not. **Lane RENDER LAYERING is enforced** (`city_lanes_layered_by_width`): where two linear features cross, the WIDER renders on top - the Imperial road over the city streets it crosses, streets over the alleys. The engine handles this automatically (`s.street`/`s.alley`/`s.road` defer to one ground layer flushed in width order, so a narrow lane never paints over a wider one); the check guards it via each feature's recorded draw-z. **And lanes meet as clean CROSSROADS** (`intersections_are_crossroads`): the engine flushes the ground block in three sub-layers - all EDGE strokes (the dark curb-borders), then all BED strokes (paved surfaces), then all center-marks - so every edge sits below every bed and the beds merge into a continuous junction. Without this, one lane's edge-border paints a line across the other's mouth where they meet (a street looks like it dead-ends at the road instead of flowing into it). The check guards the invariant: `M["ground_edge_zmax"] < M["ground_bed_zmin"]`. **WALLS render OVER the ground lanes** (`city_lane_under_wall`, `city_lanes_under_ward_fences`): a road/street/alley that runs INTO a wall - the exterior city/town rampart OR an internal neighborhood (ward) fence - must pass **under** it, not paint over it. The engine draws ramparts in a dedicated **wall layer** (`s.add_wall`, recorded `wall_z`) above the ground block and below the TOP layer, so this holds by construction; the city wall (and town arc) are drawn with a **genuine gap** at each gate (`_gapped_ring`) rather than a land rect painted over the stroke - so the wall can sit on top yet the road still shows THROUGH the opening (a painted-over rect would erase the road once the wall is on top). A lane only breaches a wall at a gate, where there is no stroke to render over, so touches/crossings within ~50px of a gate are exempt. The checks fire if any lane that meets a wall has a draw-z >= the wall's (i.e. it would render over it) - so if you ever draw a rampart with plain `s.add` instead of `s.add_wall`, or a lane ends up in the wall layer, it is caught.
- **Shops front the streets; poor housing fills the block INTERIOR, reached by alleys** (`businesses_front_streets` - almost every `shop`/`merchant` within ~85px of a street; `poor_housing_mostly_interior` - most `laborer`/`burakumin` are NOT). The historical pattern: a street grid divides a quarter into blocks, businesses line the paved streets (`s.frontage(street, ["shop", ...])`), and the residential majority jams the deep interior (`s.pack(bbox, ["laborer", ...], face_streets="core")` - "core" leaves the street-facing band for the shop frontage and packs dwellings only DEEP, off the street). **The more mercantile a quarter, the more streets**, but **merchant HOUSING is varied and roomy - the deliberate contrast to the laborers** (uniform, small, jammed tight). Behind the storefronts the homes mix by wealth band (`city_merchant_housing_varied`; budgets.md provincial-city merchants, of ~600 households: very rich 8% -> **walled estates** via `s.merchant_estate(x, y)`, a fenced court with a large house inside, recorded in `M["merchant_estates"]` NOT `M["manors"]`; rich 12% -> **large houses** `kind="merchant_large"`; poor+other 80% -> **small houses** `kind="merchant_house"`). And they are **SPREAD OUT** - `city_merchant_housing_spread` wants the merchant homes' median neighbor gap >= 1.3x the laborers' (pack with a bigger `step`; a few denser blocks are fine, the median is robust). Walled estates need DEEP block cores, so do NOT over-street the merchant quarter into shallow strips: a couple of N-S streets off the avenue leave top-to-bottom blocks the estates and roomy homes fit into. **The estate COURT (the fenced rect, not just the house inside it) is checked for clearance** like any compound: it must sit clear of the wall and moat (`city_merchant_estates_clear_of_wall_moat`) and not overlap any other building - temple, compound, house, or another estate (`city_merchant_estates_clear_of_buildings`; the estate's own inner house, centered in the court, is exempt). So keep estates in the block CORES, well inside the rampart and clear of the temple neighborhood - not jammed against the west wall or sitting on a temple. It IS fine (and historically common) for an estate WALL to abut a neighboring building, but then its **GATE must open onto open ground, never into that building** (`city_merchant_estate_gate_clear`): pass `s.merchant_estate(x, y, gate_dir=...)` the side facing clear space (the estate abutting the Temple of Benten opens NORTH, not south into the temple). The gate point + direction are recorded in `M["merchant_estates"][i]`, and the check rejects a gate whose threshold fronts a compound (temple, ministry, the yamen, or another estate court). A laborer quarter, by contrast, has only a couple of through-streets and very deep blocks. Those deep blocks are subdivided by **unpaved gravel/plank ALLEYS** (`s.alley(pts)` - thinner than a street, a pale speckled lane, a narrow no-build corridor so the dense core leaves a gap), the lanes the interior housing is actually reached by. **An alley is checked like a street** - it must not run over a building (`no_structure_on_street` covers alleys too), nor cross the wall or moat (`city_streets_clear_of_wall`, `city_streets_clear_of_moat`); place alleys BEFORE the frontage so no building sits on a lane, and clip them inside the wall (the elliptical rampart curves in). **No GIANT cluster may be cut off from circulation** (`no_isolated_dwelling_cluster`): housing packs deep, but a big contiguous block of dwellings with no street OR alley within ~95px has no way in or out - lace every deep block (including any space-filling band, like Tango's strip between the laborer and government quarters) with alleys so every dwelling is reachable. Small deep cores are fine; the check flags only a large (>30) isolated cluster. And an alley must EARN its length by UNIQUELY serving dwellings (`alleys_serve_buildings`): each building is credited to its NEAREST lane only (the one it actually fronts), exactly as `streets_have_buildings` scores streets, so a lane counts only what no other lane already reaches. This catches BOTH a lane running off into a half-empty corner (a lane to nowhere) AND a **redundant** lane - one laid beside or across another that already serves the same block. The rule of thumb is **one lane per block**: a block's interior needs a single spine to reach it, so don't add a perpendicular cross-arm the spine already reaches (Tango's west laborer blocks share one continuous vertical spine at x1810 and carry no horizontal cross-alley; only the east block keeps its own L), and don't shadow a street with a parallel alley a few tens of px away (the street already fronts those dwellings). The threshold scales to the buildings (~1 uniquely-served dwelling per 30px of the alley's own length), so it bites at the city's dense small-footprint grain rather than relying on a fixed town pixel-gap. **Exception: a gate-access spur.** An alley that runs out to a **ward kido gate** past its last building is providing gate access, not block-service, so that terminal stretch (from the gate to the nearest building the alley fronts) is subtracted from its length before the ratio - extend a commoner alley to its ward gate freely without tripping this check. Why nearest-lane and not raw proximity: a redundant alley still sits *near* plenty of buildings (raw proximity is fooled into passing it), but those buildings are nearer the spine or street that truly serves them, so the redundant lane's UNIQUE tally collapses toward zero and it fails. Samurai (not poor) may front their streets; merchants front as businesses AND count as dwellings (dual-use). **Laborer HOUSING varies by wealth too** (`city_laborer_housing_varied`): like the samurai (senior/junior) and merchant (estate/large/small) tiers, the hinin are not uniform - budgets.md's provincial-city laborer cohort is ~12.5% **"master" (rich) laborers**, the rest standard. So a clear minority get **larger homes** (`kind="laborer_large"`, ~50x34) among the overwhelming majority of small standard `laborer` dwellings (~34x24). Place the large ones on the **prime back-street FRONTAGE** (the wealthier laborers take the roadside lots, with room around them) via `s.frontage(...)`, and keep the standard poor laborers jamming the deep interior cores (`face_streets="core"` packs). The check wants `laborer_large` at roughly **6-20%** of laborers (a generous band around the 12.5% target, since the big homes are room-limited to street frontage) and a strict standard-laborer majority; `laborer_large` is exempt from `poor_housing_mostly_interior` (it is the wealthy tier, so it *should* line streets).
- **`s.bound` makes placement fill the wall's SHAPE.** Set `s.bound = [list(p) for p in WALL]` after drawing the wall; every `pack`/`ring`/`frontage` then rejects positions outside it, so a dense pack over a generous bbox fills the elliptical ring cleanly (partial blocks at the rampart) instead of a rectangle with empty corners. Reset `s.bound = None` before placing anything OUTSIDE the wall (the estates, off-map fields).
- **Civic amenities port up from the town tier** (a city is a bigger version of the same market center): merchant houses keep fireproof *kura* (`s.merchant_storehouses(...)`, `city_has_merchant_storehouses` >= 5), there is market-day lodging (`s.flophouse(...)`, `city_has_flophouse`) - one INSIDE the walls (`city_flophouse_inside_walls`) AND one just OUTSIDE EVERY gate (`city_flophouse_outside_each_gate`, within ~520px): **convenience lodging at the busy market gate** for peasants, carters, and peddlers arriving from either direction (see the WHY below - this replaces the old "stranded by the dusk gate-curfew" rationale, which the research found is a *Tang* artifact that had relaxed by the Song/Ming rice-south period we model; the outside-gate placement itself is well-grounded in the extramural gate-suburb/*guanxiang* clustering pattern). **WHY the count (`flophouse-research.md`, deep-research pass 2026-07-22, GM decision):** the outside-gate/gate-suburb PLACEMENT is historically supported, but how MANY cheap inns a settlement held is genuinely unknowable - the ruling class did not count or record peasant lodging (gazetteers foreground official post-stations). The symmetric "1 in + 1 outside each gate" is therefore a deliberate, legible stylization on the generous side of an uncertain range, an intentional demographic choice for a Rokugan that is more peasant-attentive than its historical models, *extrapolated by combining historical precedent with Rokugani demographics*. (Considered and declined: concentrating on only the busiest gate; drawing a distinct animal-yard cart-inn compound.) The **in-wall flophouse is a humble doss-house and belongs in a humble quarter** (`city_flophouse_in_humble_quarter`): the laborer section, or - Tango's case - the agrarian sector, never within ~110px of a temple/merchant/samurai (the nicer neighborhoods) and never within ~150px of (in or beside) the burakumin quarter. **Each city gate gets a CARAVAN transit cluster just inside it** (`city_gate_caravan_facilities`): a town inn can hide its small stables, but a wagon-train arriving at or passing through a city brings **many dozens of draft animals (oxen, packhorses) plus guards, porters, and drivers**, so within ~340px of each gate there must be all THREE of a **flophouse**, a prominent **inn** (`s.inn(x, y)` - a warm building with a red hanging sign), and a **large stables** (`s.stables(x, y)` - stall-division lines), with **open ground around the stables** (the check fails if more than 4 dwellings crowd within ~75px - the animals need room to be tied up or penned; no need to draw the fencing). This caravan cluster is the one place an in-wall flophouse may sit beside an inn/stables rather than alone (the humble-quarter check exempts a flophouse that has both an inn AND a stables within ~170px). There is also a theater stage (`s.theater_stage(...)`, `city_has_theater_stage`), which - like a town's - must sit ADJACENT to a temple (`theater_stage_by_temple`); a **CITY theater stage is bigger than a town's** (`city_theater_stage_larger_than_town`, viewing-ground width >= 185, vs the town baseline ~120-150). Tango's sits in the Temple of Benten's precinct (the two merchant estates that crowded Benten were moved to the freed central ground). Place the theater stage/flophouse BEFORE the dense packs so the packs flow around them. (Not ported: the farmer-plurality - a city has zero in-city farmers (but it DOES have caste-count bands of its own, `city_caste_counts_in_band` - see Scale and density above); the monastery clan-patron system - a city has temples; `wall_sections_irregular` - a city wall is a regular defensive ring; the hill and gate-market rules.) **The full lodging program (DECIDED, GM 2026-07-24; research in `flophouse-research.md` "Inn counts, occupancy, and the by-employment household"):** a non-post-station highway seat of ~3,000 runs ~8-15 lodging houses - the 2-4 proper inns + 2-4 flophouses the map already draws as dedicated glyphs, plus **5-8 small family hatago that are DELIBERATELY NOT distinct buildings**: historically these were ordinary shophouses whose back rooms took lodgers (households living on the meal trade, farming, and porterage - lodging was the add-on, and on procession nights the whole town became the billet), so on the map several of the road-market and gate-market `shop` rows are understood to sleep travelers. No check counts the implied hatago; the inn/flophouse floors gate the dedicated glyphs.
- **Fire defense ports up too, at city scale.** A city's quarters each need a **fire-watch tower** (`s.fire_tower(...)`, `city_has_fire_towers` >= 2; `fire_tower_in_commoner_quarter` keeps each in a commoner quarter, not the samurai ward; `fire_towers_dispersed` keeps any two towers at least one watch radius apart, and `fire_tower_amid_its_district` makes each tower stand amid the rooftop district it is nearest-tower for - so the towers actually spread across the commoner quarters instead of bunching in one quadrant). Because the commoner warren packs wall-to-wall, a tower stands where it actually has SIGHTLINES: a cleared block or an open seam between building clusters. Because the towers annotate existing gaps, place them **LAST, after the packs and `farmsteads()`**. `meta(fire_tower=False)` to suppress. See the "Fire towers" grounding below.
- **City commoner housing goes down as CONTIGUOUS TERRACES - the row-packing doctrine** (`s.rowpack(bbox, kinds)`; gated by `city_row_housing_touches` >= 55% of row-class dwellings touching a neighbor, and `city_row_housing_gap` median nearest-neighbor edge gap <= 2px). Lay each quarter's laborer/servant/burakumin/merchant_house stock with `rowpack`, NOT `pack`: E-W rows of touching units (a 0.4px hairline seam keeps the SAT overlap gate honest), the real ~3-6 ft eave gap between rows, an idobata court every 2 rows, and the caller's roji/alleys (draw them FIRST) fronted tightly while real streets keep a frontage band for the shops. Wells go down BEFORE the rows so the terraces break into courts around them. The SAMURAI quarter is the exception - bushi kept detached compounds with yards, so it stays `pack` (its scatter is the deliberate contrast that makes the terraces read). See "Historical grounding" below for the why.
- **A merchant estate's wall stands on dry, private ground - off the streets** (GM doctrine 2026-07-19; gates `merchant_estate_wall_clear_of_water` + `merchant_estate_wall_clear_of_fire_towers` + `merchant_estate_wall_clear_of_streets`, town+city scope). The walled compound of a very-rich urban merchant must not run its perimeter through WATER - canal, dock basin, moat, river, pond - or through a FIRE TOWER (nor wall one INSIDE the court: the watch reaches its tower from public ground), or stand IN the cleared band of any STREET, alley, road, or the ring road (a compound wall may LINE a street - that is what a walled compound on a block looks like - but the public way stays open). *The why:* a wall footed in water is undermined, and the canal/dock edge is WORKING waterfront (boats, porters, the towpath that makes the merchant rich) - a private wall may approach the quay but never stand in it; the fire watch is municipal infrastructure needing its own footing, daylight round the braced frame, and watch access - it cannot be embedded in a private compound wall. The whole perimeter is walked, gate gap included (a courtyard gate opening straight onto water or into the tower frame is the same siting error). Placement enforces it too: `s.merchant_estate` slides through a small candidate fan to the nearest clear seat when the requested spot violates (draw water + towers BEFORE the estate), and raises rather than drawing a wall the gate will reject. Both checks landed red-first against the shipped Nagahara (its estate wall clipped the dock basin AND the labeled fire tower - pinned as `pool/regressions/merchant_estate_wall_fires_on_dock_and_tower_nagahara.json`).
- **Doors face OUTWARD; rows stack at most TWO deep** (GM doctrine 2026-07-18; gates `city_house_doors_unblocked` + `city_rows_max_two_deep`, town+city scope). A FARMHOUSE always faces SOUTH - its dooryard garden and threshing ground need the sunlight, so its orientation is canon and it is exempt everywhere. A CITY house has no sun constraint; what it must have is an **unblocked entrance**: the door (drawn on the building's local +h/2 side, so facing derives from `rot` in the manifest) opens onto walkable ground - street, roji, court - never into the back of another house an eave-gap (~3-6 ft) away. The pair rule follows: terrace rows come in **back-to-back pairs, both fronts outward** (`rowpack` places the first row of each pair at rot 180, the second at rot 0; between pairs a walkable roji of ~12 ft, widening to the idobata court), because the middle row of a 3-deep stack is walled on both faces - those households would be trapped. `frontage(rows=2)` flips its rear row 180 (the street row faces the street, the back row faces the back lane - the ura-dana pattern); scattered `pack` houses pick the open cardinal via `s.open_face_rot`. The check boundary is `DOOR_CLEAR_FT = 7` real ft - between the eave band (<= ~6 ft, drainage not entrance) and the walkable band (>= ~10 ft) at every map scale. *Historical grounding:* an ura-nagaya unit had exactly ONE entrance, opening onto the roji its row fronted (Edo tenement rows were laid out around lane access; machiya opened to the street they taxed frontage on); back-to-back pairs sharing a rear wall were the densest honest pattern - three-deep does not occur because the middle row has no lane, and a household with no door to a lane cannot exist.
- **Fire towers: the fire-defense of a dense, ENCLOSED wooden core** (drives `walled_town_has_fire_tower` / `city_has_fire_towers`, `fire_tower_in_commoner_quarter`). Premodern Japanese cities burned constantly - Edo was the "city of fires," and the Meireki fire of 1657 killed ~100,000. The institution we depict is the **fire-watch tower** (*hinomi-yagura*, 火の見櫓) - a tall braced-timber tower with an alarm bell (*hanshō*, 半鐘) the watchman struck in a cadence that told the city how *near* a fire was. **Why walled towns and cities but NOT open towns:** the tower belongs to the dense, contiguous, *enclosed* wooden core. Two reinforcing reasons. (1) **Density** - fire is catastrophic only where roofs touch (the packed intramural *machiya*/tenement warren); an open road-town strung along the highway, fields running off every edge, has natural breaks (the road, the field-gaps, the surrounding open ground), so a standing tower does not earn its cost. (2) **The wall traps the fire** - the rampart that keeps raiders out also keeps a blaze *in*, so the watch over the rooftops is most needed exactly where there is a wall. **The unwalled county seat is NOT an exception - it is the confirming case (GM question 2026-07-24, researched; this reverted the 2026-07 audit's brief widening of the town check to unwalled seats).** The real-world analog of an unwalled tax-administration seat is the *jin'ya*/*daikansho* town, and its fire posture was defensive, not observational: a *hanshō* bell on a ladder, rooftop platform, or simple frame at the office or watch post (the local temple bell doubling as alarm elsewhere), night patrols with clappers, stored water (Takayama Jin'ya, the surviving Hida daikansho, preserves its fire-prevention water system), and above all the plastered fireproof *kura* protecting the tax rice and ledgers - losing the tax rice was the career-ending disaster, so the storehouse was fireproofed rather than the horizon watched. The freestanding countryside fire tower everyone pictures is a *Meiji-and-later* institution (early Meiji rural fire watch was still a wooden ladder and a bell; the standalone towers spread end-of-Meiji through Showa with the volunteer *shōbōdan*), so an Edo-flavored unwalled town with a central tower is roughly a century anachronistic. China agrees at both ends: the Song built staffed *wanghuolou* fire towers in hyper-dense Kaifeng and Hangzhou, while ordinary market towns had nothing of the kind (and Chinese county seats were walled anyway). On our maps the unwalled seat's fire story is carried by the kura on the yamen, headman, and merchant houses; a small *hanshō* bell-frame at the magistrate's compound would be the one defensible extra glyph, and plain omission is equally accurate. **L7R/Sengoku adaptation:** the famous chartered townsman brigades (the *machi-bikeshi* "iroha" companies) are a peace-era Tokugawa institution; in a Sengoku-flavored Rokugan the fire-watch is the **magistrate's** (yoriki/doshin/ashigaru manning the tower), not self-governing townsman companies. The tower is drawn as an OPEN braced frame with a red bell (distinct from the solid guard towers ON the rampart - a fire tower is a *civilian interior* structure watching the rooftops, not the wall); it is an overlap-checked struct, so it must stand clear of the wall, roads, buildings, and open water (the pond - Tango's west tower once landed on the pond rim, which is what closed that gap with `no_structure_on_pond`), and `fire_tower_in_commoner_quarter` keeps it in the commoner warren, never the samurai ward. It also keeps **>= 5px of daylight** around its frame (`fire_tower_standoff`): the blanket overlap check only catches true intersection, so a tower butted flush against a house passed it while reading as a collision - the glyph's roof cap overhangs the recorded frame by ~2px a side, and an open braced tower's footing and ladder sit clear of the neighboring eaves (it stands on a seam, not in a party-wall row). The one neighbor neither pass can see is a **wellhead** (`fire_tower_clear_of_wells`): wells are overlap-EXEMPT (a wellhead's nominal footprint may kiss a dense-city building, because houses ring a tenement court closely), so a tower dropped onto a well trips no generic check - but the tower's footing would stand in the well court blocking the quarter's shared draw-point (the idobata), so the tower keeps the same >= 5px of daylight from every well's clearance disc (this caught Tango's west tower standing squarely on a well; the bad manifest is pinned in `pool/regressions/`). **When a settlement rates MORE than one tower, they DISPERSE ACROSS THE QUARTERS** (`fire_towers_dispersed` + `fire_tower_amid_its_district`): historically the fire-watch was parcelled out per neighborhood - each Edo machi block-group kept its own tower, and the shogunate's official watch stations were likewise distributed one to a district - because a tower's useful watch is its visual neighborhood of rooftops. Two rules enforce it. (1) Pairwise floor: two towers inside one watch radius of each other duplicate coverage, so every pair stands at least one watch radius (the same ~230px the commoner-quarter check uses for "near its rooftops") apart. (2) District centering: a comfortable pairwise gap is NOT enough - Tango's original pair stood 341px apart yet both NW of center, leaving the NE laborer warren (the city's biggest rooftop mass) unwatched. So every commoner dwelling (inside the walls, when walled - the extramural gate-market rows are not what the towers exist for) is assigned to its NEAREST tower; that partition is the de facto watch districting the drawn towers imply, and each tower must stand near its district's center of mass (offset <= max(0.9 x district RMS radius, one watch radius)) - AMID the blocks it watches, not at their far edge with a whole unwatched quadrant in its ledger. In practice: put one tower amid each major commoner quarter, sized to the tower count (Tango: NE laborers, west servant/burakumin, south-central merchants -> 3 towers). Place it LAST, after the packs and `farmsteads()`, on a cleared seam the finished map already has. *(Deliberate divergence from literal scale, per the legibility license: a single tower stands in for a quarter's whole fire-watch; real cities had many.)*
- **Why we DON'T depict firebreaks (a deliberate omission, so it is not re-added).** The other historical urban fire-defense was the **firebreak** (*hiyokechi*, 火除地 / *hirokoji*, 広小路) - a cleared strip of open ground a blaze could not jump, which, too valuable to leave idle, filled with removable stalls and theater stages and so doubled as the market/amusement ground (Edo's *hirokoji* became its entertainment districts). We tried two depictions and dropped both. A **drawn** plaza (a shaded clearing placed before the dwelling packs) forces the packer to flow houses around an *invented* clearing, which reads as a designed plaza rather than the *absence* of building a firebreak actually is. A **label-only** marking (a run-aligned annotation dropped onto a gap the finished map happens to leave) is just naming existing dead space - it adds a word, not a feature, and getting the gaps and label orientations to read well takes real effort for no real payoff. So a firebreak is **not a feature of these maps**: the towers carry the fire-defense story on their own. If a future map genuinely needs a firebreak (a wide ceremonial fire-plaza that is itself the subject), build it as a real, deliberately-placed feature with its own glyph - do not resurrect the marked-gap approach.

- **City commoner housing is CONTIGUOUS - the row-packing doctrine** (drives `s.rowpack(...)`, `city_row_housing_touches`, `city_row_housing_gap`; GM-confirmed 2026-07 in the 1px=3ft scale pass).
  - *What the history says.* Urban commoners in both reference cultures did not build detached-with-yard. Machiya street frontage was continuous - party walls or touching eaves, because street frontage was taxed and precious; back-lot *nagaya* were literally ONE ROOF over a row of family units; Chinese county-seat courtyard housing shared rammed-earth or brick party walls in continuous street walls. Detached-with-yard was a samurai (and rural) form.
  - *What we had.* The measured pre-doctrine Tango: 553 row-class dwellings with a median nearest-neighbor gap of 12px (~31 ft at the old grain) and ZERO touching pairs - a suburb, not a city quarter. Pinned as `pool/regressions/city_row_housing_fires_on_scattered_housing.json`.
  - *The drawn fabric.* Rows of touching units (hairline 0.4px seam - drawn as distinct outlines that merge into a terrace strip, which is also what it WAS: independent structures grown together); a ~3-6 ft eave/drainage gap between back-to-back rows (rain dripped between roofs; gutter and night-soil access); an idobata court every couple of rows (the communal well courtyard a tenement block's life turned around); access by roji drawn at the linework floor, not by ambient spacing. Obstacles break a terrace naturally - the occasional gap is historically honest (post-conflagration cities kept a few firebreak gaps), it just isn't the default texture.
  - *Knock-ons.* This packing is WHY the fire furniture exists (packed wooden terraces burned - the fire-watch towers and block wells stop being decorative); and the samurai quarter's detached-compound scatter (`pack`, not `rowpack`) is the deliberate visual contrast - the one district that historically DID build detached.
