# Sizing the wall: budget-first planning, capacity, quarter density

*Part of the provincial-city docs - the tier definition, population and caste numbers, and the list of city-scale checks live in [`../cities.md`](../cities.md). The shared town/city feature vocabulary is in [`../urban-features.md`](../urban-features.md).*

**Load this file when:** deciding how big the wall should be, or a sizing/density check has failed. Covers `citybudget.plan_city` (always size a walled city budget-first), `city_capacity`'s four verdicts, and per-quarter density. Read this BEFORE picking any wall dimension.

---

- **Size the wall to HOLD the city - no large empty swaths inside** (`city_no_large_empty_space`, ported from the town's `wall_hugs_the_town`). A wall encloses what it must defend; an oversized ring with a big empty perimeter would not have been built. The fix is the inverse of the town's: rather than pulling a wall in around a fixed built area, **size the ring to the ~600 households and fill it densely** (the city's 3 ft/px grain - `bscale = 1/3` - keeps the true-size footprints small enough to pack ~600 into a hugging wall). A city keeps SOME open ground, but every deliberate open is CLAIMED by a feature record (a right-of-way, a field, a working yard); ground claimed by NOTHING, sitting on wall-protected premium land, would not have been left bare. *Detection (REBUILT footprint-aware, GM 2026-07-23):* the GM caught a ~230x95px bare pocket (~600x330 real ft, ~1.6 ac) just inside Tango's north gate that the original detector passed - that pass sampled an 80px grid and called a cell "used" within 120px of any building CENTER, a radius so wide a single house sanitized a 240px disc, so only vast voids could ever fire. The rebuilt detector counts every claiming feature at its real FOOTPRINT (building/compound/grove rects, field and ground polys, well / stable-yard / torii discs, road / street / alley / ring-road / water rights-of-way, ward fences, the rampart + patrol strip, the pond), marks 32px-grid cells >= 20px clear of ALL of them as dead ground, and fails any contiguous dead cluster >= 4,000 px2 of core. *Calibration (pool-wide dry-run, 2026-07-23):* Tango's pocket measures 6,144 px2 of core; the largest legitimate opens anywhere else measure 2,048 (Tango) / 1,024 (Nagahara) - the threshold sits between with ~2x headroom both ways (pre-fix Tango is frozen in `pool/regressions/` so the check keeps teeth). *Remedies, in preference order:* fill the ground (extend a quarter / drop in a neighborhood), or claim it as deliberate working ground - the standing EASY option is **`s.animal_ground(cx, cy, r, label=...)`**, a standalone stable-yard scatter (beaten earth, hitching rails, ground litter; it auto-avoids roads, fields, water, the rampart, and every footprint, and records `M['stable_yards']`, which the detector counts as claimed). Extra animal space is the natural claim when the pocket adjoins a gate or the stables: first applied to Tango's north gate, where Phoenix-border caravan traffic wants marshalling room (the north gate serves trains coming down from, or departing toward, Phoenix lands).
- **Tango's quarters** (a specific layout, not a universal rule): the road + E-W axis split it into NW (agricultural district + in-wall burakumin + a pond), NE (laborer terraces + garden tracts), SW (merchant district + the temple neighborhood with the Rites ministry), SE (governor's mansion + the other five ministries + the samurai neighborhood, with the samurai estates just outside the SE wall). Other cities arrange their quarters differently; the gate checks the *contents*, not which quarter holds them.
### Sizing the wall to the population (`city_capacity`, `--capacity`, `--capacity-map`)

The hard question every city raises is *"do the walls enclose too much empty space or too little for the target population?"* Guessing a wall size and then grinding placements to fit is backwards. Instead **measure** with the space-budget analysis in `check_village.py`:

- **`city_capacity(M)`** grid-samples the walled interior and classes every cell as **dwelling / civic-overhead / water / trunk-road / residential-street / field / OPEN**. From that it reports: `target_dwellings` (population / 5), `placed` (dwellings actually drawn), **`inherent_capacity`** (dwellings the residential-capable ground CAN hold at the canonical well-packed density `RHO_CANONICAL = 0.00149` dwellings/px^2 - Tango's 561 placed over its ~378k px^2 of non-reserve residential ground; feature 009 recalibrated this from the original 0.00127, which had divided by ground that still contained the agri reserve's ~72k of non-field slack and so under-read what packed urban ground delivers), the area budget, `open_frac`, and a **`suggested_wall_scale`** (>1 enlarge, <1 shrink) that resizes the ring ONCE to the right size. Reserve ground of ANY kind (agri included) is deducted from the residential-capable ground - it is committed to non-housing and must never dilute the density norm.
- **The verdict** is one of four, and each says exactly what to do next:
  - **`too_small`** - even well-packed the wall cannot hold the target (`inherent_cap < 0.9*T`). ENLARGE the wall (multiply all coordinates by `suggested_wall_scale` about the wall center).
  - **`too_big`** - far more room than the target needs (`inherent_cap > 1.4*T`). SHRINK it.
  - **`underpacked`** - the WALL is right but the placement is too sparse (`placed < (1 - population_tol)*T`, i.e. below the *same* line the `population_consistent_with_housing` check fails at, default 7%). Do NOT resize - add dwellings / densify the quarters.
  - **`about_right`** - wall sized and packed to target. Done.
- **`city_wall_sized_to_population`** is the gate check that fails on `too_small` / `too_big`, so a mis-sized wall is a first-class, automated finding rather than something discovered by hand late.
- **`--capacity-map` prints an ASCII map of the interior classification** (each cell 40px), so the report shows WHERE the open ground is, not just how much. Read it to decide *where* to add a quarter (or that the open ground is all civic precinct / setback and the answer is denser packing, not a new quarter).

**The Nagahara worked example (the wall-sizing process in action, 2026-07):** first cut at 430x400 declared 3,000 -> `enlarge` (inherent 446 < 600), suggested scale x1.13. Scaled the whole generator x1.15 about the center -> `densify` (wall now right, placement sparse). The `--capacity-map` showed the open ground was the civic-committed west half (temple neighborhood NW, sealed government ward SW). The first pass densified the east; feature 006 later showed the honest fix was to fill the empty NW itself (the monzen pocket) so the quarter is not merely under-built - see the per-quarter doctrine below. End state: `sized_and_packed`.

**Why `RHO_CANONICAL` is honest but the aggregate model over-read:** the density is calibrated on Tango (a fully-packed city), so `inherent_capacity` is what a *well-packed* quarter delivers. The ORIGINAL `city_capacity` was a global aggregate (total residential-capable area x that density vs total placed), which is blind to DISTRIBUTION - a dense east plus an empty west averages to "fine." It passed a visibly-broken Nagahara (dense east, empty NW temple quarter, ~35 commoner dwellings spilled outside the walls). Feature 006 (below) replaced the aggregate with a per-quarter model.

### Quarters and per-quarter density (feature 006)

Density is a SPATIAL property, so it is judged PER QUARTER, not city-wide. The measured proof: Tango (good) and the broken Nagahara had the SAME in-wall dwelling count (521 vs 525) and the SAME median block density (4.6/10k px^2) - an aggregate or anonymous-grid check literally cannot tell them apart. The only difference was WHERE the empty ground sat. So the generator DECLARES its intent and the check reads it.

- **The generator declares quarters.** `s.quarter(poly, zone, kind=None)` records a region + `zone` in {`residential`, `civic`, `mixed`, `reserve`} into `M["quarters"]`; a `reserve` also carries a `kind` (`drill_ground` / `garden` / `agricultural_district`). A `drill_ground` (bare earth + dashed muster perimeter) and a `garden` (green sward) are DRAWN so their open ground reads as deliberate, not accidental; an **`agricultural_district` draws NOTHING of its own** (GM 2026-07-22) - its combs, in-wall farmhouses, and label already carry the visual, and the faint dashed boundary it used to add read as a stray dotted line cutting through the farmhouses and across the Imperial road above the burakumin neighborhood. The quarter is still recorded in `M["quarters"]` regardless, so per-quarter density judging is unaffected. A walled city tiles its interior with wedges split at the crossroads (road x=CX, axis y=CY); Tango and Nagahara both do this.
- **The checks (all walled-city-scoped):**
  - `city_quarters_declared` / `city_quarters_tile_interior` - a walled city MUST declare quarters that cover the interior (>=85%) without overlap (<=5%) or spill outside the wall (<=3%).
  - `city_residential_quarters_dense_enough` - every `residential`/`mixed` quarter's average density (over NON-civic ground: quarter area minus the civic compound footprints inside it, so a government ward's yamen does not read as under-built) must sit in `[QUARTER_DENSITY_FLOOR, QUARTER_DENSITY_CEIL]` = `[0.30, 2.30]/1000px^2`. A `residential` quarter additionally must hold no DEAD ZONE - `largest_empty_gap` (the biggest empty pocket radius, counting wells and civic compounds as served) must stay under `DEAD_ZONE_MAX` = 150px, so a half-dense/half-empty quarter fails even with an in-band average. The band spans ~7.7x because a commoner warren runs ~4-6x denser than a samurai/official ward (Edo land-vs-population inversion); the dead-zone guard applies only to PURE residential (a mixed quarter legitimately holds a civic forecourt).
  - `city_commoner_dwellings_inside_walls` - HARD ZERO commoner dwellings (laborer/servant/burakumin/merchant) outside the wall. Only samurai country estates, farmhouses, the wharf SHOPS, and gate-market shops are legitimately extramural (the guan-xiang/wharf/villa/farm categories); a lone commoner house outside defeats the wall and has no economic anchor. `population_consistent_with_housing` also counts IN-WALL dwellings only, so a generator cannot hit its target by spilling.
  - `city_civic_quarter_not_mostly_open` - a `civic` quarter may be up to `CIVIC_OPEN_TOL` = 70% open (a yamen/temple precinct is majority courtyard/garden) but must actually HOLD its compounds; open-and-structureless reads as merely empty.
  - `city_reserve_within_cap` - total `reserve` ground <= `RESERVE_CAP_FRAC` = 20% of the interior. Civic buildings alone are only ~3-6% of a Chinese county seat; the big open consumer is the drill ground plus under-built garden/farm, and ~20% (drill ground + gardens + an agricultural district) is historically conservative. Beyond it the wall encloses more open ground than a provincial seat justifies -> shrink.
- **The reworked verdict** (`city_capacity`, surfaced by `city_wall_sized_to_population` + `--capacity`): `enlarge` (can't hold target even packed), `shrink` (far more room than needed, OR only fillable via over-cap reserve - emptiness cannot be laundered as reserve), `densify` (wall right, placed below the population floor), `sized_and_packed` (done). `placed` counts in-wall only; `--capacity` prints the per-quarter density table so you see WHICH quarter is under-built. The `densify` boundary tracks `population_tol` so the capacity verdict and the population check never disagree.
- **Anti-trap discipline:** the pre-feature broken Nagahara is pinned as `pool/regressions/city_density_broken_nagahara.json` and MUST keep firing the new checks (a known-bad map that has to fail - the discipline the earlier aggregate model skipped). The band was calibrated against BOTH Tango (good, must pass) and that fixture (bad, must fail).

**Worked examples:** *Tango* declares 4 wedges (NE laborer residential; SE government mixed; SW merchant+temple mixed; NW split into an agricultural-district reserve above + a burakumin residential quarter below) and, per the GM's call, was enlarged a hair (uniform x1.015, RX/RY 480/450 -> 487/457) so its honest inside figure meets 3,000 rather than trimming the declared population - the earlier "hugged" ring had been faking ~12 dwellings past the wall. *Nagahara* declares 4 mixed/residential wedges; its formerly-empty NW temple quarter is filled by a MONZEN-MACHI commoner pocket (temple-town housing - historically a temple neighborhood was dense), which fixed both the emptiness and the population without enlarging the wall. Both cities: zero extramural commoners, every residential quarter in band, `sized_and_packed`.

  - *Known WIP (none).* Both worked cities (Tango, Nagahara) pass clean, per-quarter. Capitals are the next tier (a later task).

### Budget-first wall sizing (feature 009, `citybudget.py`)

Every settlement mode grows from a first principle - villages from water flow, manors from a declared square footage - and the city's is the **space budget**. The old order (guess a wall, place everything, iterate on the `city_capacity` verdict) converged on maps the checks accepted but the GM did not: the shipped Nagahara read fully green while ~17% of its interior (~116k px^2, five pockets >= 35k px^2) was unaccounted open ground, because every aggregate measure treats open ground as "fillable" rather than "unjustified". So the order is inverted: **compute the budget FIRST, derive the wall from it, and hold the drawn map to the promise.**

**The workflow** (see `specs/009-city-area-budget/` for the full feature):

1. `budget = plan_city(CityProgram(population=..., river=..., agricultural_district=..., aspect=..., nring=...), canvas=(W, H))` - BEFORE anything is drawn. Audit it with `python3 citybudget.py --plan --population 3000 --river`.
2. Take the wall from `budget.wall.rx/.ry` (never hand-pick RX/RY) and record the promise: `s.meta(budget=budget_to_manifest(budget))`.
3. Render order as ever (river -> walls -> roads -> ring -> ... -> packs), with the budget's `dwelling_target` feeding the same population/caste floors as always.
4. `city_wall_matches_budget` (walled-city gate) compares the wall's enclosed area against `budget.required_interior_px2`: over +8% = the empty-space defect (shrink, or declare+draw the ground as an itemized line); under -5% = the program cannot fit (enlarge, or trim the program). `city_capacity` must read `sized_and_packed` on the FIRST derivation - if it wants a resize, the budget model (not the wall) is what needs fixing.

**The model**: `required interior = (packed + spaced + civic + water + extras) / (1 - circulation% - agri%)`, then the wall is the ellipse **N-gon** enclosing exactly that (`0.5*N*sin(2pi/N)*rx*ry` - the drawn 20-22-gon's polygon area, NOT the smooth ellipse's `pi*rx*ry`, which would systematically undersize every wall).

**Historical grounding / the why behind each constant** (research: `specs/009-city-area-budget/research.md`; China first, jokamachi tiebreaker):

- **Inventory** from the budgets.md Provincial city caste table (600 families at pop 3,000: servants 120 / laborers 240 / merchants 150 / burakumin 30 / samurai 60; zero farmers unless an agricultural district overrides). ~2/3 of samurai families live in-wall (measured: Tango 33, Nagahara 41 of 60; the rest hold extramural estates per the estate doctrine).
- **Gross ground costs at DRAWN footprints** (legibility floors included - budgeting real-world square footage would undersize the wall): packed row dwelling ~690 px^2 gross (Tango healthy-quarter range 448-822), samurai house ~2,480 px^2 gross (measured samurai-ward ~2,915; the pair solves Tango's back-prediction to +0.2% while keeping the measured ~3.6x packed:spaced ratio).
- **Civic program is a FLOOR, not per-capita** - a pop-2,000 seat still carries the yamen, 6 ministries, temples, theater, gate furniture. Itemized at Tango's measured compounds (~64k px^2 total ~ 9% of interior; the historical civic share of a county seat is ~10%, range 5-15% - yamen 3-5% + temples/school 3-5% + granary/drill 2-4%, per Chang in Skinner's *The City in Late Imperial China*). The program also carries a small **adept-monk housing** line (5 x C_PACKED; GM 2026-07-24): 2-3 ordinary homes per temple precinct for the married adepts among its monks, real households OUTSIDE the lay caste table's 600 families (see "City temples" above).
- **Circulation 7% of interior at drawn widths** (measured 6.8-7.0% on both shipped cities). The historical envelope is 10-20% of ground (Chinese county seats ran deliberately sparse nets - Pingyao's "4 big streets, 8 small streets, 72 lanes"; Edo machi-wari grids compute to 13-18%) - but no source states the Chinese figure outright, so it is a triangulated envelope, and the MAP-calibrated constant wins because the budget must price what the maps actually draw (deep blocks + alley warrens sit at the sparse end).
- **Deliberate open ground must be DECLARED and DRAWN, never ambient.** The Chinese norm was 25-30% of the enclosure unbuilt (walls sized to administrative rank and growth expectation; intramural fields/ponds as siege insurance and flood refuge - Quanzhou still 1/4 vacant in 1945, Suzhou enclosed farmland into the Republican era). On a diagram that roominess reads as emptiness unless drawn as its kind, so the budget credits open ground only as itemized lines: the **agricultural-district toggle** (+15% of interior, Tango's measured declared reserve; inside the feature-006 20% reserve cap) or explicit `extras` lines (drill ground, gardens). A future city that wants the roomier historical character opts in legibly; it can never launder slack.
- **Tolerances (+8%/-5%)** are pinned by the two anchors: shipped Tango encloses ~0.25% under its budget (must pass); the pre-feature Nagahara encloses ~+21% (must fail - pinned at `pool/regressions/city_budget_fires_on_the_too_empty_nagahara.json`). Under is tighter than over because an undersized wall breaks packing immediately.

**Calibration provenance**: constants measured 2026-07 from `pool/provincial-cities/tango.json` (GM-accepted) and the pre-feature `pool/provincial-cities/nagahara.json` (GM-rejected); the back-prediction test in `test_citybudget.py` re-derives Tango's shipped 487x457 ring to +0.1% and is the regression guard on the whole constant set.
