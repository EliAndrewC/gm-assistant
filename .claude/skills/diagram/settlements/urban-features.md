# Urban features: state markers, trades, wells, gate furniture, caste geography

*Part of the Mode B settlement docs. The index, the `meta()` knobs, the workflow and the validator contract live in [`../settlements.md`](../settlements.md).*

**Load this file when:** drawing a town OR a city - this is the vocabulary both tiers share: notice board, justice works, trade works, tanning yards, stable yards, wells, drum tower, gate furniture, status zoning.

**Research:** the historical basis for these rules - what was found, the decision it drove, and every disclosed departure - is in [`../research/urban-features.md`](../research/urban-features.md). Load it when you are CHANGING a rule or questioning one, not to follow it.

---

- **Several merchant houses keep an attached storehouse (*kura*).** Because most Rokugani farmers are **tenants**, their (often absentee) landlords' rent-rice and bulk goods are stored in town - over and above the ordinary inventory storeroom a shop already has. So a noticeable **minority** of businesses run the classic narrow-front / deep-lot merchant compound with a fireproof *kura* behind the shopfront. Call `s.merchant_storehouses(count=6)` AFTER placing the businesses: it tucks a small kura behind several merchants (the **back**, opposite the street-facing awning), drawn as an annex like the farmhouse `shed`, so it needs no open ground in the maximally-packed quarter (there is none). `town_has_merchant_storehouses` requires **>= 3** - more than a token one or two, but still a minority.
- **Notice board (kosatsuba) - the state's standing law at the settlement's busiest point** (`town_has_kosatsuba` default-on for every town, walled or not; `kosatsuba_by_the_road` sites it within ~60 ft of a road/main street; `kosatsuba_faces_the_road` squares it to that way; `s.kosatsuba(x, y, rot)`; opt out with `meta(kosatsuba=False)` for a suppressed or backwater seat). (why: [`../research/urban-features.md#the-notice-board-kosatsuba-siting-is-a-traffic-decision`](../research/urban-features.md#the-notice-board-kosatsuba-siting-is-a-traffic-decision)) **The TWO boards are distinct institutions and coexist** (multiple boards per settlement were normal): the settlement kosatsuba posts the state's standing law where everyone passes, deliberately imposing (like the execution ground, it was designed to represent the strength of authority); the magistrate's manor-gate board (part of the Mode A compound program - buildings.md "Notice board") posts the bench's OUTPUT - verdicts, edicts of the court, bounties - for the people who come TO the court (litigants, petitioners, bounty hunters). Because the manor deliberately sits at the settlement EDGE (`manor_gate_faces_town`), defaulting the town board to the manor's gate would put it exactly where the fewest feet pass - the inversion the siting check exists to prevent. Placement: **every tier auto-sites it with `s.place_kosatsuba()`** - do not hand-pick a seat. The two towns that did were both wrong and both wrong the same way (GM 2026-07-27, "fix both of them"), so the defenses that used to stand here are struck rather than amended. Hoshizora's board sat on the SW arrival stretch by the theater/flophouse, defended on the grounds that the packed merchant frontage upstream had no clear verge and the arrival stretch was the honest second best: 3 structures within 250 ft against a peak of 30, a tenth of the traffic on offer. Hirameki's sat on the main street just inside the front gate, defended on the grounds that every arrival passes it: 10 against a peak of 29 - arrivals do pass it, but the town does not, and the board is for the people who live there rather than the ones coming through. What neither map lacked was frontage; what they lacked was a seat that could hold the board AND its caption, which is a different question, and the reason a hand-picked board drifts outward at all. Auto-sited, they now stand at 19 and at 29 - Hirameki's on the busiest ground its own map has. Drawn at TRUE size (~12x5 ft roofed frame over the 7x3 ft board, the Mode A size) wherever true size is legible - at hamlet/town grain (1 ft/px) that is a 12x5 px glyph, and the label carries the read. **Cities port the institution UP, not away** (`city_has_kosatsuba` + `city_kosatsuba_per_gate`, same siting check with the limit in REAL feet - 60 ft is 20 px at the 3 ft/px city grain; GM 2026-07-24): a real city posted MANY boards - Edo kept the principal high-board at Nihonbashi plus subsidiary boards at its gates and bridge approaches - so a city **draws the SET and labels ONE** (GM decisions 2026-07-24, superseding the initial one-board-stands-for-the-set depiction): the **principal board at the central market node** PLUS a board on every main gate's approach corridor - the presence floor is gates + 1 (`city_has_kosatsuba`), and `city_kosatsuba_per_gate` holds each gate's board within ~800 real ft of its gate (the corridor, not the furnished throat). Only ONE board carries the label - **whichever board has room for it**, no particular one (the same one-label convention as the fire towers and gate markets); an UNLABELED board also fits the tight gate verges a labeled one cannot - Tango's busier south gate could not hold the label box but takes the bare board fine. **At the coarse tiers the glyph is a LOCATION MARKER** (GM call 2026-07-24, taking the escape this paragraph had documented as available): the true frame draws 6x2.5 px at village grain and 4x1.7 px at city grain, and that DID prove too small - on Nagahara, two of the three boards were invisible until the GM went hunting for the missing one, and the third read only because it carried the label; rotated upright, a 1.7 px sliver beside a ward gate reads as gate hardware, not as a feature. So `s.kosatsuba` floors the DRAWN glyph at `KOSATSUBA_MARKER_MIN_PX` (11 px) on its long axis with the 12:5 aspect preserved, which is the wells' doctrine exactly (SKILL.md "to scale"): the marker denotes the board's to-scale LOCATION with legible pixels that are not themselves claimed to be to scale. The floor NEVER shrinks a board, so hamlet/town maps are unchanged; only village and city grain lift. Calibration: 11 px is what the true frame already draws at 1 ft/px, the tier where the board has always read - and it lands the city marker just above the ~8 px wellhead, the smallest city glyph that reliably reads. The manifest keeps TRUE `w`/`h` (a size audit still reads real feet) and adds `vw`/`vh`, the drawn box - which is what the placement reservation and `_struct_rect`'s overlap tests use, since collisions are about drawn pixels. **Villages and hamlets carry the board too** (GM 2026-07-24; `village_has_kosatsuba` / `hamlet_has_kosatsuba`, same opt-out and siting - the siting routes include the LANE network at these tiers):  At these tiers the board is AUTO-SITED by `s.place_kosatsuba()` (called inside `roll_village()` for seed-rolled maps - it draws no RNG, so the seed stream is untouched - and as a one-liner in the hand gens): a deterministic probe over the manifest's own route fields (the validator's same-source doctrine) that scores verge spots for the most dwellings within ~260 px and hugs the verge. The lane corridor's no-build clearance is deliberately BYPASSED (it is a HOUSE setback - homesteads must not crowd the tread - and at village grain it is wider than the whole 60-ft siting band): the board stands off only the tread itself, which is exactly the historical posture - the board hugs the roadside, that being the point.
  - **The CAPTION is part of the seat, or the board walks out of town** (GM 2026-07-27, from "the notice board in Ubame does not look well-placed; I'd expect it to be in a higher-traffic area"). The glyph is 11 px and fits almost anywhere; its caption does not - and the busiest frontage is exactly where there is least room for one. So a siter that hunts for ground big enough to hold BOTH walks away from the traffic **by construction**, and Ubame's board duly ended up across the bridge on the far bank from the whole town, ~120 ft past the east end of the shop rows: 8 structures within 250 ft against 23 on the high street. Nothing in the gate could see it - `kosatsuba_by_the_road` passes at every point on a road, including the stretch beyond the last house, so the automated check is silent on the only thing the institution is for. `place_kosatsuba` now scores the caption instead of hunting for it: a seat whose caption fits (below by default, `label_above` where the lower band is taken) outranks any seat whose caption does not, and among those the busiest wins; a board with nowhere to put a caption is still placed, so the label check still reports rather than the siter silently failing. `clear_label_seat` / `label_seat_clear` are the shared probe, factored out of the punishment ground, which had the same problem first. **Whether a feature is on the traffic is a JUDGMENT call and belongs to the `settlement-review` subagent** (its TRAFFIC-SITING SWEEP), not to a check - there is no mathematical rule for "lots of foot traffic", which is exactly why this shipped green for so long.
  - **The board FACES the road - `rot` is the road's bearing, never across it** (`kosatsuba_faces_the_road`, GM 2026-07-27). Siting has two halves and only the first was checked: a kosatsu is a **broadside signboard** - a 7x3 ft face under a little roof, read by someone walking past without leaving the road - and the glyph's LONG axis is that face. Stand it perpendicular and the traffic the siting check fought for meets the board's ~6 in of thickness edge-on, so the institution fails with `{scale}_has_kosatsuba` and `kosatsuba_by_the_road` both green. The historical boards stood square to the highway frontage (post-town *kosatsuba*, Edo's Nihonbashi high-board) for exactly this reason. The check demands the long axis run within **30 degrees** of some route segment inside the siting band - any segment in the band, not merely the nearest, because a board at a junction or on a bend legitimately faces one of the ways that meet there. Calibration: 30 degrees still presents ~87% of the face to traffic (`cos 30`) and clears the worst legitimate case in the pool by ~12 degrees (a board on a road bend takes the bend's bearing and sits 18 degrees off its nearest segment). `place_kosatsuba` derives `rot` from the segment it probed, so auto-sited boards are right by construction; the rule exists for HAND placements, which is where it broke - Nagahara's north-ward board hugged a cross street 11 px away and was drawn 90 degrees to it (frozen in `pool/regressions/`).
- **The justice works - a punishment ground INSIDE, an execution ground OUTSIDE** (feature 015; `s.place_punishment_spot()`, `s.execution_ground(cx, cy, rot)`, `s.boundary_marker(x, y)`; opt out with `meta(punishment_spot=False)` / `meta(execution_ground=False)`). These are **two institutions, not one feature drawn twice**, and they are sited by opposite logics - conflating them is the modeling error the whole design exists to avoid.
  - **The punishment ground** is the everyday face of the magistrate's authority: a cangue frame, a flogging post, and a kneeling stone on ~30x12 ft of tamped earth at the market or the magistracy frontage. Its governing variable is **foot traffic**, exactly like the notice board's, which is why it is placed by the same kind of probe (`place_punishment_spot` scores street verges by the dwellings within ~260px and hugs the verge) rather than by a hand-picked rect. **Both probes bypass the lane CORRIDOR and neither may stand in the BED** - the corridor is a house setback (homesteads must not crowd the tread) and a display that everyone passes is the whole institution, but bypassing the setback must never mean sitting in the road. Each siter used to build its own partial list of beds to stand off (the board's omitted town streets and alleys, the punishment ground's omitted alleys and the ring road), so a seat clipping an alley was *proposed* rather than refused and had to be hand-sited on the map (Tango, reported by another session 2026-07-27); the gate caught it after the fact, since the overlap matrix forbids SOLID x WAY. Both now read `way_beds(M)` - every drawn bed on the map, whether or not this tier samples candidate frontages from it. It draws **no board of its own** - the crime rides on the cangue, as the historical inscription did - and it stays inside the rampart where there is one. Checks: `{scale}_has_punishment_spot`, `punishment_spot_in_the_core`, `punishment_spot_by_the_traffic` (~60 real ft of a street), `punishment_spot_only_at_a_seat_of_justice`.
  - **The execution ground** is bare, unbunded waste ground on the way out of town, past the boundary stone. Furniture: crucifixion post SOCKETS (posts were raised for a sentence and taken down - the socket is what stands), an iron burning stake, a sand beheading bed, a head-display stand with its crime board facing the road, a well for washing the blade, and a disposal pit. Sized by tier: ~60x60 ft at a county seat (unfenced, weedy - it must read DISUSED), ~100x60 ft at a provincial city (screened on three sides, road side open), ~150-250 x 50-80 ft at a capital. `rot` lays the ground's **road side (local -y)** toward the road, the same convention the tanning yard uses for its water side. Checks: `{scale}_has_execution_ground`, `execution_ground_outside_the_settlement`, `execution_ground_by_the_road`, `execution_ground_past_the_boundary_marker`, `execution_ground_clear_of_the_dead`, `execution_ground_off_the_farmland`, `execution_ground_on_the_outcast_side`, `execution_ground_only_at_a_seat_of_justice`.
  - **The boundary stone** (dosojin) is a LOCATION MARKER in the wells' sense - a real stone is ~3 ft, sub-glyph at every tier, so the true footprint is in `w`/`h` and the drawn box in `vw`/`vh`. It must stand **outside** the settlement, on the road out, between the settlement and the ground. "Outside" is tier-dependent and deliberately NOT the execution ground's version of the same word: **walled, the rampart settles it** (a stone beyond the wall is past the line even with a roadside suburb against it - Hirameki's stands 104 ft from an extramural laborer row); **unwalled, it must be 60 ft clear of the nearest dwelling's WALL** (`BOUNDARY_STONE_CLEAR_FT`) - not the 120 ft the ground owes, because that is a POLLUTION separation and a dosojin pollutes nothing: it is a marker at the village edge, and all it must not do is stand among the houses. The ground keeps BOTH clauses, since kegare is a separation from people wherever they live, while the stone only has to say where clean ground ends. Getting this wrong is invisible without the unwalled clause: a wall-only test does not relax on an unwalled map, it passes **anything**, and Ubame's dosojin duly shipped among the west-end shops with a fully green gate (GM, 2026-07-26).
  - **And the stone must stand nearer the settlement's edge than the ground does** (`execution_ground_no_nearer_the_houses_than_its_stone`, GM 2026-07-27). The between-ness rule above compares two distances to the core CENTROID, which orders the pair radially about one point - and a settlement is not a disc, so a ground tucked in beside a quarter on a different bearing satisfied it while lying inside the stone's line for most of the town (Ubame: 86 of 118 dwellings nearer the killing ground than the stone). The settlement's edge is the **rampart** where there is one and the **nearest dwelling** where there is not; measuring a walled city to its nearest dwelling lets a lone hinterland farmstead stand for the town. Every distance here is wall-to-wall, never center-to-center - see the dev-loop doc, "Centers, footprints, and aggregates".
  - **Placement order:** the punishment ground goes in AFTER the urban packs and the notice board (it is probed onto a verge the engine will actually give it); the execution ground and its stone go in BESIDE THE FUNERARY CLUSTER, before the hinterland scrub and `village_grove`, so nothing is drawn over them. All three are `_CROP_HARD`/`_CROP_CITY` keys - they set the frame, because a ground clipped at the edge would read as "somewhere off that way", which is the one thing its siting is not.
  - **Siting one of these:** use [`site_justice.py`](../site_justice.py) rather than guessing coordinates - it proposes seats cheapest-on-the-frame first and adjudicates each against the real gate (see the dev-loop doc, "Siting a feature with interacting rules").
  - **Practical traps, all paid for once already.** The ground must sit inside the map's existing view: `crop_to_content` clamps the frame to the canvas, and on a city `crop_city` will happily drag the frame out to reach a far-flung ground and thereby detach the road from the edge it must run off. A verge-hugging feature's default below-label lands on the frontage it hugs, so `place_punishment_spot` probes the label outward too - and the label test uses each building's **axis-aligned** box, which for a rotated shopfront is much larger than its `w`/`h`.
- **The FARM BELT is within reach of wells too** (`farm_wells_within_reach`, town+city; `s.farm_wells()` after `s.farmsteads()`; fixtures: Tango + Hirameki pre-fix). The urban idobata checks scope to commoner dwellings in the core, so the farmsteads ringing the outside fields had NO well coverage at all - Nagahara's interior steadings sat a median ~970 real ft from the nearest well (GM's eye: "too sparse", 2026-07-21). The rule: **no farmhouse stands more than 500 REAL FEET from a well** (a ~2-minute bucket walk; historically a farmstead often dug its own dooryard well, and Rokugan's unusually well-run domains sink wells liberally - the same liberty as the literal urban idobata count). **Map-edge steadings (within 150 real ft of the view edge) are exempt** - their fields already run off-map and their well is presumed just off the edge with the rest of the steading. The placer seats each well in a steading's dooryard or the open margin between steadings (the belt's ground is mostly crop, where a wellhead may not stand); where the only open ground is field-ENVELOPE rim slack, that is a legitimate seat (unplanted margin), tested against the DRAWN plots instead of the smoothed outline.
- **NO WELL IN THE PADDY WATER** (`wells_clear_of_paddies`, GM 2026-07-27: *"wells on dry crops are okay, but not in rice paddies, surely"*). A paddy is a puddled, bunded basin held under standing water through the growing season, so a wellhead drawn in one is standing in the water it is meant to be an alternative to, and a shaft sunk there takes the field's own surface water rather than groundwater. **A DRY plot is expressly different and stays allowed** - a *hatake* plot is worked ground you can walk on; this rule is about standing water, not about crops. **What counts as the water is `paddy_wet_rings`** (settlement.py), read by BOTH halves - `_well_ground_clear` refuses the ground, the check returns the verdict - so siter and checker cannot disagree. It prefers a field's **DRAWN basins** (`plot_polys`), the ink a reader actually sees, and **falls back to the field outline only where a field records no basins**. Both halves of that earned their place: reading the smoothed OUTLINE as water condemns the fan's unplanted **rim slack**, which is legitimate margin ground and is exactly where `farm_wells` seats the well of a steading boxed in by crop (the two `test_farm_wells_*` fallback tests pin this, and are what caught the envelope reading); while a basins-only rule would silently never run on a single hamlet, village or town, since only the three provincial cities record `plot_polys` - and a check that never runs looks exactly like a check that passes. Strictness matches the dry-plot rule: the DRAWN head (`vr`) may not lap the water. **Why it survived so long:** `_well_ground_clear` already refused a stream, channel, ditch, canal, pond and dry plot, its docstring reading "you do not dig one in the middle of a crop plot" - the WET plots, where it matters most, were simply never added; and the overlap matrix could not cover for it either, since `fields` is classed `PADDY_RECONSTRUCTED` (permissive) because a plot polygon is not stored. This is one of the precise paddy checks that class defers to. **A consequence worth knowing:** enforcing it can still leave a steading with nowhere legal for a well - Tango's two east-fan farmhouses are the case on record, and deleting their hand-seated well re-fires `farm_wells_within_reach` - and the answer is a hand `s.well` seat adjudicated against the gate, not a relaxation (see that gen's comment; `_fits` measures with circumscribed circles and refuses seats every real-footprint check accepts).
- **Public WELLS dot the commoner warren** (`city_neighborhoods_have_wells`, `city_wells_in_block_interiors`). Ensuring every commoner could draw water was a defining civic concern of a premodern city; the communal neighborhood well (井戸, the *idobata* social hub) served a courtyard / cluster of ~10-20 households, so the warren is sprinkled with them - one within a short walk of any home. They are the ONLY visible part of the water system: the aqueducts, underground cisterns, and rain barrels that fed the shafts are too small or literally subterranean and stay OFF the map. A wellhead (`s.well(x, y)`) is a stone curb under an open-sided roof, SMALLER than a house, and sits in a block INTERIOR off the lanes. Scatter them with **`s.place_wells(bbox, spacing)`** (a grid that tries a few offsets per cell, dropping any candidate on a lane / compound / the bound via the same `_fits` test the houses use) BEFORE that quarter's `s.pack(...)` so the houses flow around them; for a cramped, lane-laced quarter the grid can't reach (Tango's shallow burakumin lane-strip, the gap-fill band between the alleys), hand-seed a candidate list through **`s.well_at(x, y)`** (places only if the spot is clear). Tango runs ~47 wells (roughly the literal count for ~600 households at ~1 per 10-20, in the same draw-everything spirit as the houses). Two checks enforce this from different angles. **Coverage** (`city_neighborhoods_have_wells`): fires if any commoner dwelling (laborer/burakumin/merchant kinds) INSIDE the walls is more than ~290px from a well - samurai have private wells in their compounds (excluded), servants are interleaved and share the commoners' wells (ride along), and a transient gate market OUTSIDE the wall is not housing (excluded). **Density** (`city_well_density_sufficient`): assigning each commoner dwelling (servants included) to its NEAREST well, fires if any well is the nearest for more than **26** households - because coverage alone can look fine on AVERAGE while the busiest wells in the dense laborer warren are swamped (the 290px reach is satisfied by one well per radius, but a dense warren packs far more than 20 households into that radius). The ceiling is a touch above the historical 20 because the nearest-well split over-counts where two wells are nearly equidistant. The dense laborer/gap-fill quarters therefore need tighter `place_wells` spacing than the merchant quarter. `city_wells_in_block_interiors` is the placement backstop: a wellhead must sit clear of the lanes and not overlap a building/compound. **The SAMURAI/GOVERNMENT quarter has NO public wells** (`city_samurai_quarter_has_no_public_wells`): samurai drew from PRIVATE wells inside their own walled compounds, and gathering at the communal *idobata* was a commoner-district institution (beneath samurai status) - so a public wellhead must not sit among the samurai dwellings. The check is RELATIVE (a well is "in the samurai quarter" if most of its nearest dwellings are samurai), so it correctly ignores a commoner well sitting a block from the quarter across the ward fence rather than relying on a brittle absolute distance. Don't call `place_wells`/`well_at` in the samurai quarter. The drawn wellhead is a **location MARKER under the stroke convention** (GM ruling 2026-07-21, see SKILL.md "to scale": a real curb is ~3-4 ft, sub-glyph at every tier, so the marker denotes the well's to-scale location without claiming its own pixels are to scale) and **scales with the map grain**: `s.well` draws its ~24.8 ft marker footprint through `px()` (city ~8px, town ~25px, village ~12px) and records the drawn radius as `vr` on each well. A fixed-pixel wellhead looked right in a dense city but shrank to a quarter of a house at village/town scale, where buildings are drawn at full size - the well must track the same grain. `wells_sized_to_buildings` enforces this at EVERY scale: mean well diameter over the median dwelling's larger dimension must land in **[0.35, 0.85]** (a well is naturally smaller than a house, but not a dot). The reserved footprint and block-poly also scale with `vr`, so at every scale the well still reserves only a TIGHT courtyard (houses ring it closely) - each well costs roughly its own footprint, not a whole house-plot, so keep that reservation small or a city's worth of wells visibly deflates the population. NOTE wells still displace SOME housing, so re-check the population/caste bands after adding them (to keep Tango's depicted population a little above its declared 3,000 - dwellings x5 - the laborer packs are stepped tight, and the busiest west edge of the warren is given a few extra `well_at` wells so no well exceeds the density ceiling).
- **The RURAL tiers need wells too** (`settlement_has_wells`, `settlement_dwellings_watered`, for `town` / `village` / `hamlet`). Every settlement keeps communal wells - one per ~20-25 households - so `s.place_wells(<dwelling bbox>, spacing=..., near=85)` AFTER the farmhouses. **Pass `near=` for the rural tiers** (the city omits it): the bare grid would scatter wells out into the open fields and countryside between the field-rings, but `near=N` only keeps a candidate that has a dwelling within `N` px, so wells sit AMONG the houses. (A well must serve households around it; `wells_among_dwellings`, below, enforces this at every scale.) A dispersed farm household may instead draw from the **irrigation network it sits beside**, so a dwelling counts as *watered* if a well **OR** a channel / pond / stream / moat is within reach (~380px at the rural grain): the well count makes wells PRESENT, the coverage check makes sure no household is left dry. Tune the spacing per map (the hamlet shares ~1-2, a 60-household village ~6-9, a town ~16) so the worst-served dwelling - usually a far field-ring farmhouse - is within reach; the city tier keeps its own finer suite (density, block-interior placement, the samurai-quarter exemption) instead.
- **A well never stands in the crop - because OUR well is the DOMESTIC one** (the overlap matrix's `GROUND` x `SOLID` rule; GM question 2026-07-26). A well in a wheat field is a real picture - north-China dryland farming lifted groundwater onto the crop with a **jiegao** shadoof or a **lulu** windlass, and in the Ming-Qing the lulu became the north's usual device, upgraded to animal power and wells tens of meters deep. Japanese *hatake* leaned on "rainfall or natural underground sources" for the same reason, having lost the canal fight to the paddies. But an irrigation well is a different OBJECT from ours: it is identified by the lifting gear standing over it, and our glyph is a stone curb under an open-sided roof that the gate already requires to have a dwelling within ~95 ft - it *is* the *idobata*, the neighborhood gathering point (the Japanese *kabata* well served drinking, cooking and washing and was "cherished as a place where people gathered for a chat"). A lone curb in a hatake plot is therefore wrong AS DRAWN, and a reader could not tell it from a domestic well anyway. **If we want field wells, that is a new feature** - curb plus windlass frame, sited in the plot it serves, scoped to a dryland district - not a relaxation of this rule. (why, incl. the kabu-ido finding that villages CAPPED their own well numbers, which cuts mildly against our 2-3x liberty: [`../research/urban-features.md#wells-in-crop-fields-two-different-objects-and-only-one-of-them-is-ours`](../research/urban-features.md#wells-in-crop-fields-two-different-objects-and-only-one-of-them-is-ours))
- **A well must sit AMONG the dwellings it serves** (`wells_among_dwellings`, every scale): a communal well is the draw-point for the households around it, so one out in open ground with no building within ~95px is unreal. A city's `s.pack(...)` runs after `place_wells` and fills in around the wells (so they end up surrounded); the rural tiers must use `place_wells(..., near=...)` (their grid runs after the houses and would otherwise drop wells into the fields). This is the bug it caught: Hirameki's wells scattered into the empty countryside.
- **A set-apart SHRINE keeps its OWN WELL close by** (`remote_shrine_has_own_well`, rural tiers). A shrine/temple set sufficiently apart from the village (its nearest dwelling is >150px away) is too far to use the village's shared wells, so it needs its OWN ablution well (*temizu*) right beside it - and specifically a WELL, not just a nearby ditch/pond (those are not ablution sources). A shrine among/near the houses shares theirs (exempt). Place it with **`s.shrine_well(cx, cy)`** AFTER the hall + houses + village wells: it tries widening rings until a well fits clear of the hall/torii/graveyard/lanes, so a large hall pushes its well onto an outer ring; the "close by" test is measured to the hall's EDGE (a big monastery's well sits further from its center), as is `wells_among_dwellings` (a shrine's own well stands beside the shrine, not among houses, so religious halls count as served). **A HILLTOP/mountain shrine is exempt** (it sits on `M['hill']`) - it draws from a spring/basin, and you cannot dig a well on a hill (no current pool map uses a hill-shrine; the exemption stands for any map that does). Both Hoshigaoka's water-mouth shrine and Kikuta's flat-ground Benten sit apart from the houses, so each keeps its own ablution well beside the hall.
- **Wells - research + deliberate liberty** (`wells_sized_to_population`, GM 2026-07-21). - **TANNING YARDS - the burakumin trade, and the feature that decides where their quarter sits** (drives `s.tanning_yard` and the checks `settlement_has_tanning_yard`, `tanning_yard_on_water`, `tanning_yard_outside_walls`, `tanning_yard_clear_of_dwellings` (120 real ft **wall to wall** - it read center-to-center until 2026-07-27, which let Tango's yard stand 76 ft off a farmhouse; the standoff is reserved with a block poly BEFORE the farm rings run, and that radius owes the rule its 40 px PLUS both half-extents, since a block poly keeps house CENTERS out), `tanning_yard_clear_of_water`, `tanning_yard_clear_of_fields`, `tanning_yard_discharges_to_nothing_drawn_from`, `tanning_yard_below_every_intake`, `tanning_yard_square_to_its_water`, `tanning_yard_on_the_outcast_side`; GM 2026-07-24, overlap + downstream rules 2026-07-25, the alignment rule 2026-07-26, the outcast-side rule 2026-07-27.   (why: [`../research/urban-features.md#tanning-yards-hides-come-from-fallen-draft-stock-and-water-is-the-gate`](../research/urban-features.md#tanning-yards-hides-come-from-fallen-draft-stock-and-water-is-the-gate))
  - *Placement rules.* The yard stands (a) ON the water - its ground must abut the bank within ~20 ft, because a yard set back on dry ground could not work; (b) on DRY ground even so - the footprint stops AT the bank and only the staking frames reach into the shallows; (c) strictly OUTSIDE the walls, with the kiln - the workers may live in-wall, the work may not; (d) DOWNSTREAM of everything the settlement draws from; (e) NEVER on cropland; (f) SQUARE TO ITS BANK - the yard's long axis runs with the watercourse, not with the map; and (g) ON THE QUARTER'S SIDE of the settlement - the yard and the burakumin quarter face the same way out. (d) WAS a judgment call for want of flow data; since the water-flow work it is two real checks. `tanning_yard_discharges_to_nothing_drawn_from`: the yard's own course must end off-map (or in a field drain that does), never in a pond, a field, an irrigation ditch or the moat - and the sink is read by FLOW, not by polyline order, since `frm`/`to` are anchored positionally. `tanning_yard_below_every_intake`: no sluice, weir or channel head on that same course may lie below the yard, compared by ARC POSITION along the course. Graph topology alone cannot see the second one - a channel tapping the river 200 ft below the yard and one tapping it 200 ft above are the same edge - which is why the check measures distance along the water rather than walking the anchor graph.
  - *(b) is enforced by its own check, `tanning_yard_clear_of_water` (GM 2026-07-25), not by the generic `no_structure_on_stream`* - which demonstrably cannot see it: the generic check tests a fixed ~6 px half-width tuned for village brooks (the same gap `lumber_yard_clear_of_water` was built for), and it let the real Tango yard sit ~10 ft over its 22 px stream and the real Hoshizora yard sit ON a drain ditch (both frozen in `pool/regressions/`). The dedicated check tests the yard rect - with its true rotation - against every watercourse's REAL half-width (streams, channels, canals, river, moat, field ditches, pond) via segment-to-rect distance, so a thin ditch THREADING UNDER the rect between its corners is caught too, which corner-sampling misses. The why: the soaking pits are dug earth (a pit dug below the waterline is just more stream), and the racks cure hides for 2-4 months - ground that floods rots the stock. The staking frames are the ONE deliberate in-water element, drawn BEYOND the ground rect out in the shallows - so a yard whose GROUND reads as "a platform over the water" is always this defect, never a design; there is no platform variant. Exact abutment of the bank line is legal, and the good maps hug it (Nagahara clears its bank by ~1 ft).
  - *(e) is `tanning_yard_clear_of_fields` (GM 2026-07-25): the yard never overlaps a field outline, dry plot, or flower field.* The why is the trade's whole siting logic: kawaramono - "riverbed people" - worked the unplowable floodway margins precisely because taxed, producing land was never theirs to take, so a yard drawn on a field asserts ground that is simultaneously cropped and ruined for cropping. Physically a paddy is a flooded basin that cannot carry a tamped work floor, and the pits' lime and bate liquor poison the soil. Abutting a field is fine - marginal bank ground borders cultivation everywhere - only OVERLAP fires.
  - *(f) is `tanning_yard_square_to_its_water` (GM 2026-07-26): a stream at 30 deg takes a yard at 30 deg, a moat or irrigated channel or drainage ditch likewise.* The why is what the ground IS. A tanning yard is a working FRONTAGE, not a building: `s.tanning_yard` ranks the soaking pits along the water side (local -y), stands the drying racks behind them, and puts the staking frames out in the shallows beyond, so every hide crosses a single edge of water. Leave the yard square to the map on a slanted bank and that edge stops being an edge - the near corner goes into the stream while the far end of the pit rank strands a yard-length inland, and half the rank loses the thing the whole ground exists to share. Riverside works follow their bank for the same reason a wharf does. **Which** course is "its water" is decided by REACH rather than by nearest, because a yard at a confluence legitimately fronts either course that meets there and the nearest-by-centerline answer is not even stable: Hoshizora's yard sits 3 px from a drain ditch bearing 43 deg and 5 px from the channel its intake cut actually taps at 83 deg, so by centerline the ditch wins and by intent the channel does. The reference set is therefore every course whose BANK (centerline distance minus that course's real half-width - a 40 px river's centerline stands 20 px off a yard that abuts it) falls inside the same ~20 ft reach `tanning_yard_on_water` uses, and the yard need only be square to ONE of them; a yard with no bank in reach is already failing that check, so this one abstains rather than reporting the same defect twice. **Tolerance is 15 deg**, the `city_wall_towers_aligned` figure rather than `city_gate_furniture_aligned`'s 6, because `rot` is set by hand against a hand-drawn meandering polyline and a correct yard sits a few degrees off whichever segment it happens to be measured against - Hoshizora's own fronting channel bends from 83 to 56 deg within 50 px. It still separates cleanly, because the failure mode is not a wobble but an AXIS-ALIGNED yard on a diagonal bank: the pool's three good yards sit at 2.1, 3.8 and 7.2 deg off, and the pre-fix Tango yard sat at 22.9. **Tango is the map this rule was written against** - its outfall reach runs (1995,2020) -> (2038,2122) at 67.1 deg and the yard stood at `rot=90`, one corner in the water and the rest of the ground walking away from it. It is now `s.tanning_yard(1992, 2066, rot=67.1, ...)`, turned to the bank's bearing and nudged ~4 px down the normal so the ground still clears the bank by ~5 ft and the staking frames reach the shallows.

  - *Who it may stand next to - and the exemption that is deliberate.* The stench separation is the crematory's existing **120 ft** (`town_has_cremation_ground`), reused rather than freshly invented, since that is already this project's figure for "a nuisance kept off the houses." **Burakumin dwellings are exempt from it.** That is not an oversight: kawaramono lived on the ground they worked, and that adjacency is precisely what the segregated quarter IS. Counting their houses as something to stay away from inverts the whole institution - and it did, in the first pass here, which returned zero viable sites at Hoshizora until the exemption was added.
  - *A long walk to work is not a defect* (GM ruling 2026-07-24). Tango's quarter is in-wall NW and its yard is outside the SE rim; Hoshizora's crematory is across town from its quarter. Do not "fix" these by moving quarters closer to their work: being made to walk is one of the indignities the caste is subject to even in a Rokugan that is kinder to its underclasses than the historical societies it draws on. There is deliberately NO proximity check pairing a yard with its quarter.
  - *(g) is `tanning_yard_on_the_outcast_side` (GM 2026-07-27): the yard lies within 90 deg of the burakumin quarter's bearing from the settlement core - the same dot-product form, and the same threshold, as `execution_ground_on_the_outcast_side`, whose rule this simply extends to the other burakumin-run works.* **It is deliberately NOT a distance rule, and the first draft of it - which measured feet - was wrong.** A walled city legitimately keeps its quarter INSIDE at the margin while the works go out to the water, because the caste's in-town duties (night soil for samurai and wealthy merchants, corpse removal, execution and prison service, and the leather CRAFT itself - sandals, drum heads, armor lacing, bellows, all clean quiet work done at home) are a different work site from the wet stinking phase (soak, unhair, dry) that has to be on a riverbank. Nagahara's yard stands ~1,390 ft from its quarter and is CORRECT; a metric rule would have condemned it. What the rule forbids is the yard facing the opposite way out of town, which sends the tanners' daily carcass haul back through the whole settlement - the traffic real castle towns routed around with designated carcass ways. **The cremation ground is excluded on purpose**: it is monk-run and follows the temple/funerary complex, which need not be the outcast side at all - Hoshizora's stands 130 ft from its Monastery of Bishamon and almost exactly opposite the quarter, and that is a correct map. The check abstains on a settlement with no non-burakumin dwellings, where the core lands on the quarter and no bearing exists. **Two maps WAIVE it, for two different reasons** - Tango because the Emperor lies southeast and that status claim takes the quarter opposite its own works, Ubame because its land falls southwest while its only tannable water is the valley stream in the east, so the fall and the water genuinely disagree in that hill valley. (`meta(tannery=False)` remains as the general opt-out for a settlement that genuinely has water but no site on it - it is just not Tango.) **Hoshizora** has no watercourse near its quarter at all (the valley stream runs across the far west), so the yard takes the drainage ditch under the NE comb, ~280 ft below the quarter's doors - defensible rather than merely convenient, because that comb is fed by its own brook off the high ground NE and discharges OFF-MAP EAST, hydrologically separate from the west stream the town drinks and irrigates from. Hoshizora is also the one map drawn with `water="ditch"`: an irrigation drain has no current to stake hides in, so that yard ponds its own soak behind a gated intake cut instead of showing staking frames.

  - *Furniture keeps off the ROADS and the WALL - and the yard draws at CROP time (GM 2026-07-24; `stable_yard_furniture_clear_of_roads_walls`).* Hitching rails and dung heaps must not overlap any way tread (road, ring road, street, alley) or the rampart's clearance: the road-side rail's whole purpose is keeping tethered stock OFF the through-road, so a rail whose tip lands on the tread defeats itself, and a heap on the tread or against the wall fouls the public way / the patrol strip. Two mechanisms deliver it. (1) *Full-extent probes:* the old placement tested only each glyph's CENTER, so an 18px rail could lay its tip (len/2 + 2.4px post reach) on a road - candidates now probe the rail's tips and the heap's ellipse edge, and `_corridor_buffers` gained the alleys + the ring road (a rail tip on an alley is the same defect). (2) *Flush-late:* the yard used to draw at stables() time and could not see streets drawn AFTER it - Nagahara's wharf-yard heap sat on a later-laid NE-quarter street (the pinned real fixture). stables()/animal_ground() now QUEUE their yards and `flush_stable_yards()` (auto-run by `crop_city`) draws them when the map is complete - the placement-and-check-read-the-same-data doctrine (see PLANK BRIDGES). Ripple-free: the scatter runs on its own seeded RNG, and a bonus of late drawing is that the watering point sees every real well, so the dig-your-own fallback fires only when the neighborhood genuinely lacks one (the dug wellhead also probes the well checks' own way margins). Rails and heaps are recorded per yard (`rails` / `dung_heaps` on `M['stable_yards']`) so the check tests the drawn geometry; legacy pinned manifests without those keys are skipped, never retro-failed. **And a heap keeps ~24px (72 ft) clear of every RAIL LINE on the map** (`dung_heaps_clear_of_hitch_rails`, GM 2026-07-25, two rounds): both flanks of a rail are working tie-up space, so a heap dumped against it sits where the animals stand and blocks one side. Round 1 floored at 14px (42 ft, "just beyond the ~9px animal row") and paired each heap only with its own yard's rails; the GM still read the result as directly against the posts - a heap edge ~8 ft off the animals' rumps IS the working row - and Nagahara carried a heap 22.5px from a neighboring yard's rail that nothing measured. Round 2 floors 24px against every rail map-wide, placement holds 25 (and rails symmetrically avoid earlier yards' heaps); the heap's edge ends ~38 ft past the animal row - out of the tie-up space, still reading as the yard's muck pile (fixtures: the real Tango 7.9px / Nagahara 12.6px round-1 captures + the round-2 Nagahara capture at 16.4px same-yard / 22.5px cross-yard, which passed round 1).
  - *Wells, troughs, and hitching posts never overlap ONE ANOTHER (GM 2026-07-25; `wells_troughs_rails_clear_of_each_other`).* Nagahara's flophouse yard drew a hitching rail straight ACROSS a wellhead and then stacked the trough cluster on both: three glyphs on one spot, which a reader cannot resolve into anything, and which implies a layout nobody would build - you do not draw water through a rail, and no yard ties its stock over its own draw-point. They collided because the three are placed at three different MOMENTS (the wells long before the yard exists, the rails when it draws, the cluster after), so no stage had ever measured the pair. The rule is deliberately GLYPH-level: it demands only that the DRAWN extents not intersect, and attaches no feet-of-clearance number, because these three are *supposed* to stand side by side - the troughs hug their well (the bucket-pour relay above) and the animals stand between rail and trough. Near is right; on top of is not. Wellhead-to-wellhead counts too: two heads sunk on one spot are one unreadable blob. *Mechanism.* `settlement.py` carries ONE definition of each glyph's drawn extent (`wellhead_quad` / `trough_quad` / `rail_quad`), used by BOTH `_stable_yard`'s `_glyph_free` placement test and the check - the placement-and-check-read-the-same-data doctrine again (see PLANK BRIDGES), so what is drawn and what is validated cannot drift apart. Placement keeps `YARD_GLYPH_SLACK` (2px, ~6 real ft) of margin over the check's strict no-overlap floor, EXCEPT against the one well a cluster deliberately hugs, whose ~1.5px bucket-pour gap must not be widened - the troughs exist to be poured into from that wellhead. Each stage tests what is already down: a rail avoids every wellhead on the map, the pinned cluster (it must hug its well) walks around the rails, and the dug-your-own head predicts its own size (`_well_vr`) to clear both. A refused rail seat now RETRIES around the candidate rings rather than costing the yard a rail - the tie-up room is the whole "in active use" signal. Every pair on the map is tested, ACROSS yards as well as within one; the cross-yard half is preventive (no natural yard spacing has produced one - the 90-150px offset grid was searched at three yard radii) but it is precisely the hole the dung-heap rule shipped without and had to be widened for twice. Fixture: the real pre-fix Nagahara capture, rail center 5.7px from the wellhead and lying over the cluster box.
  - *Placement - no move needed (GM asked whether it belongs against the wall).* The yard sits a little INSIDE the wall behind the defensive wall-base setback (Tango's intramural ring road), near the gate, fronting the road where carts pull in - NOT flush against the masonry (the wall base was kept clear for defense/circulation) and NOT on the rampart. City-scoped for now - a town's single caravan stables keeps plain open ground until the yard geometry is made scale-aware.

- **CHARCOAL YARDS - the fuel wholesaler's premises, and the two rules that shape it** (feature 016; `s.charcoal_yard(x, y, rot, sheds)`; checks `settlement_has_charcoal_yard`, `charcoal_yard_keeps_fire_gap`, `charcoal_yard_has_cooling_ground`. Opt in with `meta(charcoal_district=True)` - an ordinary county seat declares nothing and draws none, the same shape as `meta(granary=True)`. Fixtures in `pool/regressions/charcoal_yard_*.json`.)

  (why: [`../research/urban-features.md#charcoal-yards-a-tallied-depot-a-cooling-ground-and-a-weighing-floor`](../research/urban-features.md#charcoal-yards-a-tallied-depot-a-cooling-ground-and-a-weighing-floor))
  - *What it is.* A **sumi-don'ya**'s yard: roofed stacking sheds for conditioned stock, an **open cooling apron set apart from them**, a weighing floor, and cart standing. `rot` lays the yard's **road side against local -y** - the same square-to-your-frontage convention the tanning yard uses for its bank, because a charcoal yard is a CART frontage and every bale crosses one edge.
  - *The stock is ROOFED* because dryness is the whole value: white charcoal commands its premium for an odorless, smokeless burn, and a damp burn loses it.
  - *The stock also gets OPEN GROUND*, which is the non-obvious half. **Charcoal self-heats**: fresh charcoal absorbs oxygen fast enough to raise its own temperature to ignition, worst of all as tightly-packed fines. The documented handling rule is to stand new stock in the open, apart from cooled and conditioned stock, for at least 24 hours (8 days of air exposure clears it). So a yard that put arriving loads straight in with the old stock is the yard that burns down, and the **cooling apron is that rule made visible** rather than decoration. `charcoal_yard_has_cooling_ground` holds both halves - an apron AND at least one roofed shed.
  - *The FIRE GAP is 30 real ft* clear of every other structure, and the number is placed against this project's existing ladder rather than invented. The hazard here is an **unattended** ignition inside a large fuel mass, so it sits an order above the attended-forge figure and well below the smell-carried-on-air figures:

    | separation | figure | defending against |
    |---|---|---|
    | farrier from a stall range (`farrier_keeps_fire_gap`) | ~6 ft | sparks from an **attended** open forge onto hay |
    | **charcoal yard from any structure** | **30 ft** | a stack that self-heats **unattended** |
    | **refining forge from dwellings** | **60 ft** | a live worked fire under forced blast, plus noise and smoke |
    | crematory / tanning yard from dwellings | 120 ft | putrefaction and smoke carried on the air |

    30 ft is about one flame-height clear of a fully-involved 10-12 ft stack, the usual rule of thumb for radiant ignition of adjacent timber. **Do not borrow the 120 ft nuisance figure**: it defends against smell over distance, which is not this hazard, and using it pushes the yard off the cart route that is its entire reason for existing.
  - *The WEIGHING FLOOR is not furniture.* The charcoal *tawara* had **no standard weight** in the traditional system (unlike rice), so charcoal could not be traded by count - it had to be weighed at the point of sale. That is also independently why a magistracy's hold on this trade is documentary: a seal on the tally is worth something precisely because the quantity is not self-evident from the load.

## Circulation inside a dense quarter: worn footpaths, not streets

A `settlement-review` pass found the warrens reading as a scatter of identical boxes rather than as
fabric, and the manifests confirmed it precisely: the CITIES were fine (Tango 6 alleys + 8 streets,
Minami 9 + 7, Nagahara 3 + 10) and walled Hirameki had 2 streets, but the **unwalled towns had
nothing at all** - Hoshizora and Ubame each recorded zero lanes, zero alleys and zero streets, so
every warren hung straight off the trunk road.

**A dense commoner quarter is served by narrow TRODDEN FOOTPATHS between the house rows** - packed
earth, single-track, no paving, which was far beyond a quarter's means (the same reasoning behind
`lane(worn=True)` at hamlet and village scale). They are paths, not streets, and they should never
be drawn or labeled as streets.

`s.pack(..., footpaths=N)` lays one every `N * step` of the district's depth. Two implementation
points, both learned the hard way:

- **The path is laid BEFORE the grid scan**, so `_fits` refuses any spot on the tread and the
  clearance is true by construction. The first design reserved every Nth grid ROW instead, which was
  depth-dependent and therefore useless: under the exact-population rule these quarters are only two
  or three rows deep, so the reserved row was never reached and no path was drawn at all.
- **The path is TRIMMED to ground that is actually free.** A corridor only refuses spots taken after
  it exists, and a district bbox routinely overlaps frontage placed earlier in the gen, so an
  untrimmed tread was drawn straight through a shopfront. The longest clear run is kept, and the
  path is emitted only if enough survives to read as a path rather than a stub.
- **Clearance scales with `step`**, because `_near_corridor` tests a building's CENTER: at a flat
  margin a house sat 15px off the tread and put its footprint through it. (The centre-vs-footprint
  family again - see the skill's `CLAUDE.md`.)

Expect a footpath to shift the packing: it costs a little capacity, which moves caste counts and
every downstream jittered spot. The documented answer is the one used here - widen the district a
little, then **pick a seed that lands the counts back in band** (Ubame 914 -> 923).

## The refining forge should read as a yard, not an appliance

The anti-pareidolia work (no face) held, but the glyph still read as "a small machine / control
panel" at fit zoom. The cause was grammar rather than size, and the fixes cost no scale:

- **One charcoal bay with a single division, not three evenly-spaced tabs.** Three dark chips inside
  a light casing is the visual grammar of a control panel. A fuel store is one heap under one roof.
- **The quench trough is desaturated slate-green, not blue.** It was the only saturated blue on the
  sheet, so a 10 ft trough pulled the eye like an indicator lamp. Water reads from the trough's
  SHAPE and its place by the anvil.
- **The shed has a RIDGE line.** Without it the roof is a plain tan rectangle - a casing - and
  everything inside reads as components mounted on a panel. The ridge is the one mark that says
  "this is a building seen from above".
- **More air** between the working range and the stock on the floor.

Do NOT fix this kind of read by inflating the glyph: the to-scale rule stands, and the levers are
count, contrast and spacing.
