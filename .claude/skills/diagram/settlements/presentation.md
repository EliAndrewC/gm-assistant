# Presentation: labels, subtitles, and the frame

*Part of the Mode B settlement docs. The index, the `meta()` knobs, the workflow and the validator contract live in [`../settlements.md`](../settlements.md).*

**Load this file when:** writing ANY label, subtitle, or annotation, and before setting the crop. Short - read it in full.

---

- **No legend/key box, no descriptive subtitle, and do not label what is visually evident.** A settlement map should be self-evident: just the place name as a title (no "an average farming village..." subtitle/summary line), and inline labels only for the few non-obvious elements (fallow fields, the tax-free plots, named buildings). Do NOT state counts the reader can see (e.g. "2 torii" / "7 torii") - the torii are drawn. **No two body labels may overlap** (gated by `no_label_overlaps`). **No label may run off the edge of the image** (`labels_within_image`): plenty of things rightly run off the frame - farm fields, roads, samurai country estates, farmhouses, the countryside continuing beyond - but a label that spills past the edge is clipped and unreadable, so every label's bounding box must sit inside the frame. The frame is the cropped **view** (a village crops to its content, a city crops tight to the walls, so the `viewBox` bounds, not the full canvas) or, uncropped, the canvas; the title is placed directly (not in `M["labels"]`) and sits inside by construction. **The title is auto-placed over BLANK space** (`title_clear_of_features`): `s.title(name)` scans the framed window top-first for a text box that clears every feature (buildings, fields, water, groves, the pond) and drops the name there - so the place name never sits on the map content (it records its box in `M["title"]` and the check verifies it). Call it **AFTER `crop_to_content`** so the search runs over the final window. **Every settlement map shows a SCALE BAR** (GM 2026-07-20, matching the Mode A compound sheets): `s.title()` draws it directly under the place name - a 100 map-px bar with Mode A's end-tick/mid-tick furniture, the real distance beneath it, and a fine-print `(1 px = N ft)` line. 100 map-px is a round real distance at every rung of the GM's scale ladder (100 ft at hamlet/town 1 ft/px, 200 ft at village 2 ft/px, 300 ft at provincial city 3 ft/px), so the bar needs no per-scale tuning. The blank-space search and the recorded `M["title"]` bbox cover title + bar together (so `title_clear_of_features` gates the bar's placement too), and `scalebar_matches_declared_scale` gates that the bar exists and its distance agrees with `meta["ftpx"]` - a manifest with a title but no scalebar predates the bar and needs regeneration. **There is NO compass** - the maps are always north-up (drawn convention), so a non-diegetic N/S rose added clutter for no information; it was removed project-wide. The fix for an off-subject label is to move the `s.label(...)` to where the thing it names is actually visible - e.g. Tango's "farmland" label moved from a spot west of the crop onto the visible moat-irrigated south field that runs off the bottom edge. **Label text renders ON TOP of everything** (`labels_render_on_top`): no part of a label may be covered by anything. `s.label(...)` / `s.title(...)` draw into a dedicated **topmost label layer** (`s.add_label`, above even the TOP layer of gate furniture / kido / torii), so the architecture guarantees it; the check guards the invariant by flagging any label whose bounding box is overlapped by a structure drawn over it (a higher draw-z) - the structures it knows the footprint of are the gate guard-houses/towers (`M["gate_structs"]`), the kido ward gates (which now record a `z` + `bbox`), and the torii. (This caught the kido ward gates painting over the "Ministry of Retainers" / "Ministry of Revenue" labels.) **A label must also not sit on a feature it does NOT name** (`labels_clear_of_other_buildings`, **town + city scale**): a label may cover the feature it labels - its own building/compound, or (for a zone/neighborhood label) any building of that cluster - and may clip a street-fronting shop or an interleaved servant house (those line every quarter, so they are never treated as a victim), but where it spills onto a DIFFERENT-identity building it tells the reader that building is something it is not. Each labeled structure (buildings by kind, flophouses, temples, ministries, the governor's yamen, gate furniture, merchant estates, manors) carries a GROUP; the label text declares which group(s) it may overlap (`"flophouse"`->flophouses, `"guard house"`/`"inspection"`->gate furniture, `"Ministry of..."`->ministries, `"Temple of..."`/`"shrine"`->temples, `"samurai..."`->samurai houses + estates, `"graveyard"`/`"cremation"`/`"mausoleum"`/`"ossuary"`->the FUNERARY group (the funerary structures cluster, so a funerary label may cover any of them, but NOT a temple), a `"...street"`/`"...road"` label->the merchant frontage it runs along, etc.; a farmland/market/title label names no building, so it must clip none). Funerary structures (cemeteries, mausoleums, cremation grounds, ossuaries) are themselves victims now, so a temple/monastery label may not sit on a graveyard and vice-versa - the Hoshizora "Monastery of Bishamon" went `label_below=True` and its graveyard `label_above=True` so neither sits on the other (`s.shrine_hall(..., label_below=True)`). Anything else its bbox overlaps fires. The fix is to nudge the label onto its own subject and off the neighbor - move the `s.label(...)`, or pass `label_below=True` to `s.flophouse(...)` so a flophouse jammed against the gate labels south into its cluster instead of up over the guard house. (This caught a "flophouse" label over a merchant estate, a "gate guard house + inspection" label over a flophouse, and the "Temple of Benten" label over a merchant house.) **A finer companion catches the SAME-GROUP case** (`city_civic_label_on_its_own_building`): because `labels_clear_of_other_buildings` lumps every ministry into one `ministry` group, it permits a ministry label to sit on a SIBLING ministry - so a NAMED civic building's label (a ministry by name, the governor's yamen, a named temple) must additionally not overlap any OTHER named civic building. (This caught the "Ministry of Justice" label sitting on the "Ministry of Works" office.) Fix by separating the two offices (which the abut rule below already wants) or moving the label onto its own building. **One caption per named district** (GM 2026-07-21, caught on Nagahara): a neighborhood that already carries its label ("temple neighborhood") never gets a second caption for a sub-pocket of itself - the monzen-machi commerce strip is PART of the temple neighborhood, so a separate "monzen" caption was redundant clutter. Sub-pocket features that need naming are named by their own glyph labels (a specific temple, a graveyard), not by district-tier captions. Rule-of-thumb + review-pass check, not automated Python (same family as don't-label-the-obvious). **Naming convention (not gated, but apply it): reserve "quarter" for a genuine ~1/4-of-the-settlement division** - a CITY has quarters - and call a small TOWN sub-area a **"neighborhood"** (or "enclave"/"row"), never a "quarter": a town's outcaste cluster is a "burakumin neighborhood", its warrior cluster a "samurai neighborhood". (The city, Tango, already uses "neighborhood" throughout; the towns were the offenders.)
- **A building's subtitle must add information, never restate the label or the obvious.** Drop subtitles that repeat the label - a "Monastery of Bishamon" needs no "(town monastery)" note (`religious_subtitle_not_redundant` gates this for religious halls: the subtitle may not contain "shrine"/"monastery"/"temple") - and drop subtitles for the visually evident, like "atop the hill" under a manor that is plainly drawn on a hill. KEEP subtitles that genuinely add context: "(still tended)" on a shrine, "hunting lodge by the Shirin Forest" (what the compound actually is). Do NOT label a manor with the settlement tier ("county seat", "provincial capital") - that names the town, not the building, and is redundant on a town map.

- **The frame is a CONTENT crop (`s.crop_city(margin, west=..., ...)`, GM 2026-07-23 second pass), sized to show the fans without wasted space.** History: the pre-rollout crop (MARGIN ~96px, "a city map is about the city") cut every comb fan to a ~46px sliver - the fans were always there, just invisible, which is why the ring read as bare/dry through two features of filler work. An interim hand-widened frame (~250-260px past the moat) proved the fans; the settled form is the content crop: the frame hugs the moat ring + every KEPT satellite (gate markets, flophouses, funerary grounds, wharf stalls, placed labels) + `margin` - **and the AGGRESSIVE margin (35px, the `crop_city` default) is the standing default for ALL cities** (GM 2026-07-23: "the aggressive crop [is] the default for all cities unless I state otherwise"); a new city gen calls `s.crop_city()` bare plus one farm-band side override, while the fans, hems, farmsteads, and estates do NOT set the frame - they CLIP at the edge ("it's okay if farms get cut off... the point is to show they're there, not to show the entire field"). Per-side margin overrides keep a representative farm band on a flank with no satellite anchor. Consequences to mind when editing a city gen: features parked "at the frame edge" under a hand frame (funerary grounds, drain tails) become FRAME DRIVERS or in-frame danglers under the content crop - site funerary just past the fields, and run drains off well beyond any plausible frame; the title placard searches the framed window and treats placed LABELS **and the wall/moat/ring-road/through-road** as obstacles (title_clear_of_features gates the label half; with no blank corner left after an aggressive crop, the placard had landed straddling the rampart). And **a label never pins the frame** (GM 2026-07-23): if a feature label is the only thing holding a side open, RELOCATE the label inward (Tango's "samurai estates" label moved in among its manors) rather than padding the crop around it. Placement lesson for new fans: a comb's fall must run ALONG the visible band or off-frame from a near-rim sluice - plots start ~120px downhill of the tap, so an outward fall from a tight frame vanishes entirely; and `build_comb`'s chirality (canal B swings 58 deg CCW of the fall, canal A 42 deg CW) rules out some falls near a curved moat rim - both hands were tested and rejected for a south fall on Tango's east rim before settling on the east fall.
- **Crop the frame to the CONTENT so the image is only as big as what it shows** (`s.crop_to_content(margin=30)`; a nonstandard image size is fine and expected). This is the GENERAL village-layout step (the city's tight-to-the-walls crop below is the same mechanism, hand-sized). WHY: a fixed canvas leaves dead margins - a village's paddy runs off to one side, the settlement sits in a corner - so `crop_to_content` computes the bounding box of the **HARD** features + `margin` and `set_view`s to it, tight to `margin` on every side. The frame is set ONLY by hard content: `_CROP_HARD` + the fields' **visible** extent + the pond. The fields use a recorded `vis_bbox` (the drawn plots), NOT the house-blocking envelope polygon - that envelope can have an invisible tail past the last plot, and framing to it re-introduces dead space (a real bug: the south margin was once ~210px too low by the envelope's tail).
  - **Everything else clips at the frame; it never extends it.** The **commons** scrub AND the linear/off-map **runners** (streams, channels, lanes) are drawn and simply CUT at the crop edge, trailing off as "more wild ground / more map this way". We used to *extend* the frame to preserve ~2/3 of a trailing commons ("bleed"), but that repeatedly held the frame open on empty back-slope grazing - past the graveyard on the west, ~130px past the lone pond on the east - which is exactly the dead space the crop is meant to remove. So the commons now clips like the marsh: **the frame hugs the real settlement (houses, fields, pond, groves, graveyard), never the scrub around it.** (If a map ever *wants* a wilderness feature to widen the frame, add it to `_CROP_HARD`, don't reach for a bleed rule.)
  - **Water turns on a SWEPT BEND, never a mitred corner** (GM 2026-07-25; `settlement.fillet_polyline` for a polyline's own vertices, `waterfields.round_channel_joints` for the seam between two tapering records of one run). A hand-dug earthen ditch that changes direction does it on a curve: a sharp corner scours on the outside and silts on the inside until the water has rounded it anyway, and the crew that maintains the ditch re-digs the bend, not the corner. Sharp corners belong to MASONRY - a stone-lined aqueduct, a sluice box, a revetted canal - and nothing at these scales is lined, so nothing on these maps should show one (revisit only if we ever draw a literal aqueduct). **Radius = ~2.5 channel widths**, capped at 35% of either adjacent leg: alluvial channels bend at ~2-3 channel widths (Leopold & Wolman meander geometry) and a dug farm ditch is no tighter, so the bend scales with the ditch instead of being a fixed drawing quirk - a fat head-race sweeps wide, a thin lateral turns tight, both at the same real ratio, at every map scale. The cap keeps two neighboring bends from eating the leg between them. What is deliberately NOT rounded: an OFFTAKE where a branch leaves a main (a notch cut in the bank is a junction, not a bend - the main is not turning there), and turns under ~8 degrees (no visible elbow, and rounding them would only add points).
  - **The communal windbreak does NOT set the frame** (GM 2026-07-20): `crop_to_content` no longer counts `village_groves` - the belt CLIPS at the view edge, and a partially visible belt reads as "the wood continues". Prefer the SMALLER crop: a larger frame whose only extra content is more of the same grove is wasted image (this held Kikuta's north edge ~300px open). Two checks gate the rule: **`crop_hugs_content`** (each view edge within 56px of the outermost frame-setting content - crop margins run 30-44, so a conforming crop passes with a little slack while a grove-held edge fires) and `hard_features_within_frame`, which for `village_groves` now demands partial visibility instead of containment (a belt entirely OUTSIDE the view is still a lost feature and fires). Homestead `groves` still set the frame (each hugs its own farmhouse, so it never drags it). Fixing this exposed a latent crop bug: wells record `{x, y, r}` and the crop's poly/w-h reader silently ignored them - the grove band had been covering for the wellheads; the crop now reads the `r` form too.
  - **Call order matters for SET-APART hard features.** Anything the frame must CONTAIN has to be placed BEFORE `crop_to_content`: the large features (water, fields, houses, groves) and also any **outlying** hard feature that sits beyond the cluster - a back-slope **graveyard**, an outlying **shrine** - or the tight crop will clip it (this bit Ueda: its west-set graveyard, placed after the crop, got sliced once the frame stopped being padded by scrub). Only features that DROP INTO the framed space (wells among the houses, monk plots) and the **title** go AFTER the crop - the title is placed last so it lands inside the window (there is no compass - see the label note above). **`hard_features_within_frame` gates this** (village/hamlet): every hard feature - houses, groves, the graveyard, the shrine, wells, the torii - must lie INSIDE the cropped `meta["view"]`, so a deferred feature that lands off-frame and gets silently clipped is caught rather than only spotted by eye.
  - Linear / off-map **RUNNERS** (streams, channels, the connector lane) are ignored - they clip at the frame, and their off-edge checks already read the crop (`meta["view"]`) as the map edge, so a connector/feeder/brook that ran to the old canvas edge still "runs off" the new one.
  - **Crop advisory (SOFT default, `crop_relocatable_singletons` / the `ADVISORY crop_could_tighten` line):** unlike the hard invariants, this NEVER fails the gate - it prints a hint. It flags the case where a SINGLE, freely-relocatable feature is the ONLY thing holding a crop edge out, and there is EMPTY space to move it into - so moving that one feature crops the image significantly smaller without disturbing anything else. Fires only when ALL hold: (1) a village/hamlet that crops to content (a city/town frames bespoke); (2) removing the feature pulls an edge in by **>= 150px** (it is the unique extreme, big slack); (3) the feature is not **hydrologically or terrain anchored** - a **pond that sources a field** (a channel `frm=pond -> to=field`) is a valley-head reservoir that belongs uphill of the field, and a shrine whose center sits on `M["hill"]` can't move to flat ground; both are exempt automatically; (4) an equal-size EMPTY landing, clear of all SOLID occupancy (buildings, fields, groves, forest, marsh, hill - **but not the sparse commons scrub, which a pond can simply replace**), fits INSIDE the tighter frame. Because the field-sourcing-pond exemption covers every irrigation pond, the *singleton* form fires on **no** pool map - it stays a general detector for a genuinely free feature (a standalone/decorative pond, a mis-sited cemetery). A map that still WANTS to silence a fire on an intentional placement can set `meta(crop_advisory=False)`. Not a manifest-recorded check, so no regression fixture - covered by `test_checks` unit tests.
    - **A candidate can be a GROUP, not just a lone feature (`_shrine_group`, `members > 1`).** A village SHRINE and the churchyard GRAVEYARD it is responsible for (plus its ablution well and torii) form ONE sacred precinct - you relocate the whole precinct, not the altar alone. The single-feature detector is BLIND to this: remove the shrine alone and the graveyard still pins the same crop corner (and vice versa), so each reads as "not the unique extreme" and neither fires. So the advisory also builds **group candidates**: for each shrine, gather the cemetery within ~300px, the well within ~150px, the torii within ~140px, **and the shrine's own `shrines`/`religious` mirror record** (the same shrine is stored under both keys, and both are crop-drivers - miss the mirror and the copy left behind pins the edge). A group is only a candidate when a **real companion** (a graveyard/well/torii) attaches - a bare shrine plus its mirror is still just the singleton. The whole group's COMBINED bbox is what must find an empty, DRY (marsh is solid occupancy here), appropriate landing inside the tighter frame; it moves as one rigid unit. **Why it exists:** Hikari-no-Sato's Bishamon shrine + its graveyard sat together at the far SW, holding the S crop edge out ~198px with a lot of empty dry ground higher up - a tighter crop was available but no single-thing move could see it. The group form "realizes" the shrine and graveyard can both move together. Hikari is the driving test case (`test_crop_advisory_realizes_the_bishamon_group_on_the_real_hikari_map`). This is a HINT, not an auto-move: the generator still places the precinct by hand; the advisory just flags when relocating it as a unit would pay off.
    - **The valley-head pond, and the NUDGE (the right way to reclaim a pond's poke).** The check now *auto-exempts* a field-sourcing pond (above), because moving it into the frame would drop it downhill of the field's water-entry - backwards for a gravity feed. So you do **not** relocate it. **But you can still reclaim the cheap part of the slack with a small NUDGE.** A pond only needs to *contain its sluice* to feed the field, so draw it decoupled from the `build_comb` anchor (keep `POND`/`SLUICE`/`build_comb`/feed-anchor untouched - the field is unchanged - and draw at a separate `POND_DRAW`), sliding it a little so its far edge sits **flush with the field/dry-plots** instead of poking past. The sluice stays inside the drawn ellipse, so the `frm=pond` topology still anchors. Ueda's pond is nudged ~157px W (E edge flush with the dry-field strip, ~9% smaller image); its remaining N poke is the irreducible valley-head cost and is simply accepted. Re-orienting the whole field to dodge the poke is **not** worth it: a pure mirror (NE-high -> NW-high) just flips which side the pond pokes and erases the deliberate variance between sibling villages.
    - **Where a nudged pond's channels meet it, they must JOIN at the RIM, not overlap the water.** Nudging the pond so it *contains* the sluice means the head-race would otherwise be drawn starting *inside* the pond (over the water). Clip each channel's in-pond vertices to the pond boundary before drawing (Ueda's `_clip_pond`) so it emerges cleanly from the rim - the same "join, don't overlap" rule roads/streams already follow. Clip the DRAWN geometry only; the recorded channel keeps its sluice endpoint so `frm=pond` still anchors. (An inflow brook drawn *before* the pond is already covered by the water, so it needs no clip.)
- **Neighborhood/zone labels must sit WITH the cluster they name** (`city_labels_placed_with_subject`). A label like `"samurai neighborhood"`, `"merchant district"`, `"burakumin neighborhood"`, or `"samurai estates"` must be (1) **entirely on the same side of the wall** as what it labels (the laborer/burakumin/samurai/merchant/agricultural labels INSIDE the rampart, the extramural-estates label OUTSIDE), (2) **among its buildings** (the nearest of its subject within ~130px for a house cluster, more for sparse manors/temples/fields), and (3) **not floating over a foreign field or compound**. The subject is inferred from the label text (`estates`->the extramural manors, `agricultural`->the IN-WALL fields, `temple`->temples, else the matching building kind). The fix is always to move the `s.label(...)` onto its own cluster - not the quarter's edge over a neighbor, not out over the moat. The label TEXT is recorded in the manifest (`M["labels"][i][5]`) so the gate can match label to subject.
- **ONE FEATURE MUST NOT SILENTLY HOLD THE FRAME OPEN** (drives `crop_not_held_open_by_one_feature`, `meta(crop_outlier_ok=...)`, and the shared `settlement.crop_boxes`; GM 2026-07-25; fixture: `crop_not_held_open_by_one_feature_fires_on_tango_tanning_yard.json`).
  - *The question it asks.* `crop_hugs_content` asks whether the MARGIN is generous. This asks the opposite: whether a SINGLE element, standing far outside everything else, is by itself forcing a bigger image. Move that one thing inward and the whole map crops tighter - which is worth knowing before shipping a map 15% taller than it needs to be.
  - *The discriminator is the RATIO, not the gap* - and measuring the pool first is what found that. A pond or a forest that extends the frame IS the outlying content: big, and meant to be out there. Every legitimate case in the pool has a gap roughly equal to the feature's own size (ponds 1.03-1.35x, moritono's forest 1.14x), while Tango's tanning-yard caption stood 178 px past everything with a box 10 px tall - **17.8x**. So the rule is `gap > max(60px, 3 x the feature's own extent)`: "it drags the frame further than its own size". A flat gap threshold would have condemned every hamlet's source tameike.
  - *It reads `crop_boxes()` - the SAME contributor list the crop itself uses.* The two crops take different sets by design (a city frames on its moat ring, satellites and labels while its paddy fans and farmhouses clip at the edge), so the function takes a `city` flag and both `crop_to_content` and `crop_city` were refactored onto it. This is the recurring engine trap from the dev-loop doc: a check that re-derives what the generator computed will drift from it. Verified behavior-preserving by regenerating all 17 maps and confirming every viewBox byte-identical.
  - *A city's extramural trade works are framed by their CAPTION, not their footprint* - `_CROP_CITY` carries every label box but not `tanning_yards`. That is why Tango's frame is set by the words "tanning yard" rather than the yard. Worth knowing before chasing a footprint that is not in the set. **`kilns` LEFT this exclusion on 2026-07-27**: it belonged there while the kiln was a 28 x 18 ft mound whose caption was wider than it was, but the kiln WORKS is 140 x 120 ft with its own housing, wider than any caption of it, and a satellite whose whole point is where it stands must not clip at the frame.
  - *When the outlier is legitimate, DECLARE it* with `meta(crop_outlier_ok=True)` and the reason, rather than relaxing the rule. Tango is the worked example, and it is a genuine conflict between two rules: `tanning_yard_below_every_intake` forces the yard below fse1's irrigation tap, and the tap channel's corridor blocks everything above y~2052, so the shallowest legal seat saves 8 px of a 1402 px image and is STILL flagged. The declaration converts silent bloat into a reasoned cost.

- **Label doctrine: empty ground wins** (drives the ministry auto label side, the deferred road label, `s._label_hits`; GM, 2026-07).
  - *The rule.* If a label can sit somewhere that still clearly labels its subject but covers NOTHING else, it must; when every candidate covers something, take the least. The Ministry of War label sitting over samurai houses when the ground below the office was bare, and the "Imperial Road" label over a farmstead's trees when the road's other flank was empty, were the motivating defects.
  - *Generator, not checker, does the work* (keeps the render cheap - the GM's explicit constraint): `s._label_hits(x, y, text, size)` counts already-placed footprints (placed rects + homestead grove rects) under a candidate label box - a few thousand AABB compares. Ministries score above vs below and take the clearer (`label_below=None` = auto; pass True/False to force). The ROAD label is **deferred to `finish()`** - at road-draw time the map is still empty, so scoring would see nothing.
  - **...but empty ground is PLENTIFUL, so NEARNESS is the second term** (GM 2026-07-26). Overlap count alone made every clear candidate tie at zero, and the winner then fell out of generation order - so a caption could float 40-55px out in bare ground and score exactly as well as one tucked against its subject. Tango shipped both halves of that: "Imperial Road" 55px clear of the roadway, and "gate market" 42px east of its stall rows, reading as if it named the execution ground it had drifted next to. **`s.place_caption(text, box, size)` is now the way to caption a specific feature** - use it instead of a hand-picked `s.label(x, y, ...)` whenever the caption names a *thing* (a market row, a works, a road); a district/zone caption names an *area* and stays a plain `s.label`. `s.frontage(...)` records the row it just laid as `s.frontage_box`, so `s.place_caption("gate market", s.frontage_box, 10)` needs no coordinates at all.
  - *How the ladder picks a seat.* It steps outward from the subject (`LABEL_MIN_AIR` 5px, `LABEL_AIR_STEP` 6px, 9 rungs) and takes the NEAREST seat that covers nothing; only when the whole ladder is blocked does it fall back to least-covered. Within a rung, ties go to the seat LEAST CROWDED by other captions - two seats equally tight against the subject are not equally good if one is also up against a neighbor's caption, which is precisely how "gate market" came to read as a second line of "execution ground". Then the hint, then declaration order. A caption may sit anywhere along its subject, so a long subject gets slides along its long side by default (quarter and 40% steps each way); without that freedom the ladder has one seat per flank per rung and abandons a whole flank whenever a neighbor's caption holds that one latitude.
  - *Two constraints are honored during the search rather than left to the gate*: other placed labels (and the title placard) count as obstacles, and candidates outside the cropped view are discarded outright - a clipped caption is unreadable.
  - *Captions are DEFERRED to `finish()`*, like the road caption, and for the DRAW ORDER reason (this skill's `CLAUDE.md`: "must not be drawn ON something? run AFTER it"). Seating one at call time judges it against half a map - Tango places its gate markets before the execution ground exists, so the north market's caption took the flank that later filled with the execution ground and its label. The deferred captions seat first and the ROAD caption last: most-constrained-first, since a road segment with a wide slide set has far more room to move than a market row's short frontage. One consequence to know: a deferred caption does not anchor the crop, so it can no longer hold a side of the frame open (which the GM wanted anyway - "a label never pins the frame"); the ladder's frame constraint keeps it inside the window instead.
  - *The road caption is captioned against the roadway's CROSS-SECTION plus its tangent*, not the segment's bounding box - for Hoshizora's diagonal Imperial road that box is a 486x256 square whose edge is ~280px from the actual road, so a caption seated 5px off it sat nowhere near the road. An authored `label_xy` is now only a HINT (which flank, where along the road); it can no longer set the DISTANCE, which is what let a hand anchor 102px out survive as a 55px gap.
  - *Checks stay narrow.* No new label-vs-everything gate (expensive, brittle across nine maps); `labels_clear_of_other_buildings` / `no_label_overlaps` remain the backstop. The one addition is **`label_hugs_its_referent`**, which measures the finished gap from the subject box the ladder records (element [6] of the label record) against a cap of 3x the font size. It is a backstop too, not the mechanism: it catches a caption that ran the whole ladder and still landed nowhere near its subject. Labels with no recorded referent - every district caption - are exempt by construction.
