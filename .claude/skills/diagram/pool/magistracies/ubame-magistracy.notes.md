# Design notes: Ubame County Magistracy

**Subject**: seat of County Magistrate Bayushi no Daika Koharu - Ubame county, Moriguchi province, Daika domain (Scorpion Clan).  The easternmost of Moriguchi's five counties: a working charcoal district with its own stands and kilns in its hills, newer iron furnaces sited where the fuel already was, and the county the road from the Kitsune Mori arrives in - so it is where the Fox trade is conducted (`l7r.md`, "The Kurogi and the dynasty province of Moriguchi").  The county town sits 15 miles west of the forest edge but **directly on the Fox/Scorpion border**, and the compound is sited so that its east wall IS that border (GM ruling, 2026-07-25).

**Program type**: magistrate's manor (county magistracy) - see `buildings.md` "Compound programs".  Third instance of the program after Ochiba (the template) and Hayakawa (the validation instance); every knob is set differently from both.

**The magistrate** (from his Obsidian Portal record): male Yogo-trained bushi, Rank 5.5, Recognition 3.5, Honor 2.0, XP 105.  Deferential, gossipy, repeats your last word or phrase, estranged from his spouse, raising an acknowledged bastard son, waiting for a relative to die.  He holds Ubame by the weight of his lineage rather than by competence.  On the 7th day of Yayoi last year the hermit **Shoda** struck him from behind with a woodsman's axe while he was hunting boar inside the Kitsune Mori; humiliated, he posted a **100 koku** bounty on the old man - a punishing fraction of his ~1,000 koku/yr discretionary income.  His real currency is other men's slips of the tongue, traded upward in letters to the Daika capital while he waits for his uncle Bayushi no Daika Tesshin, the provincial Minister of Justice, to die so the seat can reshuffle to him.  (Governor Bokuden has quietly begun arranging for it to go elsewhere; Koharu does not know.)

## Knob settings

1. **Tier**: county magistracy (baseline), but carrying a **border customs establishment** the other two pool manors do not.  This is why the envelope is the largest of the three (see "Envelope" below).
2. **Posting wealth**: **rich posting, broke incumbent.**  Ubame is the richest of Moriguchi's five counties and the fabric shows it - a deep office hall, a long residence, a mature grove.  The 100 koku bounty ate this year's discretionary income, so nothing new is being built and nothing has been repainted.  Deliberately NOT drawn as dereliction: Ubame is not Hayakawa's poor-posting end of the knob, it is a rich house having a thin year.  **The bounty board IS the drawn expression of the "broke" half** - the sheet shows what the money went on rather than what it stopped paying for, which is the honest way round for a knob whose effect is an absence.
3. **Granary weight**: **TERMINAL store** (the opposite end from Hayakawa's staging node) - an upland forest county with no water carriage, so what is stored sits until the overland carts run.  But the GRANARY is deliberately modest (46.7 x 26 ft, a single kura at the top of the single-kura band), because **this county's tax is largely not grain**: it arrives as charcoal, bar iron, and forest goods.  The terminal-store character therefore shows in the **sealed charcoal store**, not in a grain row.  Labeled `granary` (never `rice granary`), per the granary-contents grounding.  *(Corrected 2026-07-25: an earlier draft of these notes claimed a "two-bay row" the drawing never cashed - both reviewers caught the mismatch.  The knob is right; the row was the wrong way to express it.)*
4. **Garrison emphasis**: light.  No watchtower, no armory, no azuchi.  The county is quiet; its security story is the border, not bandits.  The duty watch doubles as the jailers, and the cell sits by the service gate so prisoner transfers skip the formal gate.
5. **Staff housing**: **option (b)** - barracks plus attached family rowhouses inside the walls (Ochiba is (a), Hayakawa is (c)).  Justified by the dynasty province: the whole provincial government of Moriguchi is Kurogi, they are long settled, and their sergeants marry in the county rather than rotating through it.  Drawn as a consolidated garrison range - barracks backing the divider with the rowhouse abutting beneath it.
   **Sizing note**: option (b) is option (a) PLUS married rowhouses, so the practice ground follows the **(a) band, 1,200-2,000 sqft** - there is no separate "(b) band", and an earlier draft invented one to justify a 996 sqft patch that only supported ~7-11 drilling.  Now 57.3 x 25.3 ft = **1,452 sqft**, which carries the resident platoon at the doc's 90-135 sqft per drilling samurai.
6. **Tenure character**: **lineage-held, but COUSIN-TO-COUSIN.**  A dynasty province reshuffles its posts within the lineage on a death rather than filling them by appointment, so Ubame's past magistrates are the current one's kin but not his direct forebears.  This is a genuine third case for the buildings.md alcove rule (Ochiba: office-continuity, no alcove; Hayakawa: lineage-held father-to-son, ancestral alcove):
   - the alcove here holds the **KUROGI lineage's** tablets, not one household's - hence the on-sheet label `inner rooms & lineage alcove` rather than "ancestral alcove";
   - and the visible consequence is that successive households have moved in and out without ever taking their fittings, so the residence reads as layered and half-suited rather than as one family's accreted house.
7. **Resident particulars** (the "Ochiba slot"):
   - **The parley room in the border wall** - the county's defining feature; see "The border" below.
   - **The bounty board** - a second, roofed board outside the main gate carrying one bill: `100 koku - the hermit Shoda`.  This is the campaign's front door: Wasp bounty hunters walk up to this wall.
   - **The writing pavilion** - a small detached study standing apart in the inner garden.  The office hall is where the county's business is done; this is where Koharu's own is - the nightly gossip-letters to the Daika capital.  A *hanare* / detached garden study is a real idiom, and setting it apart from BOTH the hall and the house is the point.
   - **The shuttered wing** - the west block's westernmost bay, drawn with closed shutters and a duller fill.  His wife's rooms, closed since the estrangement, and deliberately sited at the far end from the reception: the dead wing sits in the dead corner.
   - **The kennel** - boar hounds beside the stables.  The hunt that put him under Shoda's axe; on-sheet it is just a kennel, and the fact that the dogs have not been out since is a GM detail, not a caption.
   - **The wood-kami altar** - a small unregistered altar in the shrine grove, beside the proper Inari shrine.  This is the Kurogi habit the rest of the domain sneers at ("more comfortable among the Fox than among the Scorpion; absorbed the forest's habits of deference to trees and spirits").  Kept small, kept tidy, never mentioned in a report.  The on-sheet note is `(personally maintained)` - **GM ruling, 2026-07-25**, and the wording matters:
     - The altar is a **public** shrine that a **private individual** keeps up.  Anyone may pray at it; there is simply no one from the Ministry of Rites behind it.  ("Private" was rejected for implying restricted access, which is the wrong fact.)
     - The Ministry "neither approves nor disapproves, because it has never been asked."  That neutrality is the whole point, and it is why every NEGATIVE label failed: `unregistered`, `unsanctioned` and `unofficial` all define the altar by something the ministry has not done, which drags the approval/disapproval axis in by the grammar and invites the reader to ask whether it is in trouble.  `unsanctioned` is the worst of the three - it has settled almost entirely on the approval sense and implies permission was required and not obtained.  Stating the positive fact sidesteps the axis entirely.
     - It also dodges a canon inference the drafts were leaning on: `l7r.md` documents the registration of PEOPLE with temples (births, marriages, deaths, peasant movement) and the ministry's CONTROL of shrines through a staffing hierarchy (country monk / preceptor / provincial abbot, `l7r.md` 832), but never a register of shrine buildings.  `(personally maintained)` asserts none of that.
     - The sting survives intact because the contrast forty feet away does the work: the Inari shrine has a preceptor, a place on the calendar and an allotment; this one has a man with a broom.  And "personally" leaves open WHOSE person - a habit passed between Kurogi households is more damning than one man's quirk, and fits the cousin-to-cousin tenure knob.
   - **Inari** is the shrine's dedication - the DEFAULT for an administrative compound, and deliberately not a divergence.  It only looks pointed here because Inari is also the Fortune of foxes, which nobody in this county treats as a coincidence.  A modest standalone hall in a grove, NOT Ochiba's two-altar hall.
   - No relics, no workshop, no dojo, no guest house (the guest room is a bay of the residence's east block).
8. **Justice-front furniture**: two boards outside the gate (the bench's ordinary notice board, plus the roofed bounty board), and nothing else.  Koharu's bench runs on flattery and gossip, not on ceremony - no petition window, no grievance drum.

## The border

The GM's ruling (2026-07-25) is that the door which receives the Kitsune **is** the border, so crossing its threshold enters or leaves Fox lands.  What that drives on the sheet:

- **The east wall is the boundary line.**  Its outside face is Fox soil.  The compound therefore has TWO dignified faces - the ceremonial south face to the county town and a frontier east face - which inverts the usual "most impressive on the public approach, plainer at the rear."
- **The parley room is built INTO the wall**, with a door on each face and the border running across its floor; the wall is drawn **broken around it**, the idiom buildings.md reserves for a structure that genuinely IS part of the wall (a nagayamon, a postern).  Two pairs of kneeling mats face each other across the line: the Kitsune kneel on Fox ground, the Kurogi on Scorpion ground, and the charcoal tallies are agreed without either party stepping off its own soil.  **Historical grounding:** frontier negotiation *on* a marked boundary - meeting on the line precisely so that neither delegation concedes ground by attending - is the standard practice wherever two jurisdictions must transact regularly, and it is the honest architectural answer to "the county where the Fox trade is conducted."  It also explains, in one drawn feature, why the Kurogi are the only Scorpion the Fox will deal with: the arrangement costs the Fox nothing in face.
- **Boundary stones** are set ON the line north and south of the room (a marker pair reading the line, not a warding pair like Hayakawa's salt) - stone-gray, deliberately not Ochiba's cinnabar idiom.  Drawn 3 x 3.7 ft: an Edo domain-boundary pillar is a ~1-1.5 ft shaft (up to ~3 ft with a plinth), so this is the wells' ~2x marker convention, not a claim of size.  The physically large period border markers were earthen *mounds*, which is a different thing and not what is drawn.
- **The parley room's inner door feeds the border court**, a swept receiving garden - never the hearing court, where criminals kneel (the guest-doors-feed-courts rule).  The **cart gate** further south is the goods crossing: sealed loads in and out, opening onto the cart yard.  A real customs crossing has a person-door and a cart-gate; the parley room is the one that matters.
- **The Fox road** arrives from the east and forks - the wide branch to the cart gate, a narrow path to the parley room's outer door.
- **A note box** (bottom-left) carries the one fact the drawing cannot state: that the wall IS the line and that the room's floor is cut by it.  This is the single permitted instance note-box, the counterpart of Hayakawa's salt-wards box.

**Geography, resolved.**  An earlier draft flagged an apparent conflict between a border at the town and canon's Kurogi kilns "on the Scorpion side of the wood."  The GM's `l7r.md` revision settles it and the diagram is consistent with the settled version: Ubame's own stands and kilns are **in its own hills**, west of the line; the Fox burn their own charcoal in the Mori and sell it burned, because a kiln reduces roughly six parts wood to one of charcoal, so hauling logs 15 miles would mean carting five or six times the tonnage for the same fuel.  What crosses this border is therefore **finished charcoal**, which is exactly what the tally shed handles - and the compound correctly has no timber yard.

## The charcoal and iron front

Ubame's wealth is charcoal and the iron smelted beside it, not rice, so the outer court's working heart is not just a granary:

- **Tally shed** + abutting **weighing floor** (open covered work space, colonnade-hatched) form one operation under one range.  The magistracy owns no kilns, no furnaces and no carts; as with Hayakawa's barges, its whole hold on the trade is **documentary** - the seal on the tally.  The on-sheet note says exactly that (`charcoal and bar iron / sealed here, never owned`), which is the non-obvious fact; an earlier draft captioned it "charcoal weighed, tallied and sealed here", which three adjacent labels already said.
- **Sealed charcoal store**, a plaster kura rather than a shed.  Shrine-grade white charcoal is the county's most valuable movable good, it commands its premium for an odorless smokeless burn (`l7r.md`), and it must stay bone dry - an earthen kura is the dry building.  **Historical grounding for the fire gap:** charcoal fines self-heat, which is why a charcoal store is set apart from its working yard rather than abutted into the range; the cart yard between the tally range and the store IS that gap, doing double duty as the loading apron (a quantified ~16 ft).
- **Odor is a design constraint, not just hygiene**: white charcoal is bought for an *odorless* burn and charcoal is the most odor-absorbent thing in the compound, so no privy sits near the store's doors.  The first draft put one seven feet in front of the east door; it now sits by the gatehouse, across the yard.

## Envelope and sizing

- **290 x 200 ft = 58,000 sqft (~5,388 m2)**, the largest of the three pool manors (Ochiba 267x200, Hayakawa 247x200) but comfortably inside the audited Joge (~1,900 m2) <-> Takayama (~9,900 m2) band.  The extra 23 ft of width is **not** posting-wealth inflation: it is the border establishment (parley room, border court, tally shed, weighing floor, charcoal store, cart yard), a whole functional range the other two counties do not have.  The size-audit re-derived this independently and upheld it: the extra width is 23 x 200 = 4,600 sqft and the border range occupies 5,659 sqft, and coverage holds at **39%** (a padded envelope shows up as coverage falling *below* the 37-42% band).  **Do not shrink the walls.**
- Perimeter-hugging 51%; zero central vacancies (every open region is a named feature).
- Staffing per the buildings.md anchors: ~15 working samurai, a standing ashigaru contingent, ~4 heimen clerks commuting from town, ~10 servants on-grounds.

## Deliberate choices and tolerated stretches

- **The residence's public half is the EAST block.**  Reception (zashiki) - Koharu's quarters - guest room face the inner garden, with the compound's one genkan on the reception bay and the divider gate on the same axis; the private family half (shuttered wing - family quarters - inner rooms & lineage alcove) sits west beside the kitchen and the service alley.  The shoin-to-garden axis is the organizing fact of an elite residence's sunny south face (buildings.md grounding: the prized formal garden faced the reception rooms).  *The first draft had these reversed* - the zashiki looked at the kitchen's back wall while the family got the garden, and the ceremonial genkan opened into the 4.7 ft handcart alley shared with the kitchen postern.  Both reviewers caught it; the fix was to swap the two blocks' room programs rather than move any wall.
- **The clerks' room is a BAY of the office hall, not a separate building** (as Ochiba; Hayakawa has the standalone version), deliberately wide because the charcoal and iron tallies are most of this county's paperwork.  The width is the instance fact and it is visible; the `4 heimen clerks` caption that used to sit under it was a program fact (every county runs 3-4 commuting heimen clerks) and has been removed.
- **The shrine grove runs the full depth of the inner court's east side**, and is drawn as a real wood - nine crowns at 18-19 ft canopy.  It started as a modest precinct with 6-8 ft crowns, which made a 48 x 93 ft "grove" render as bare stipple: a void cleared by a name, which is exactly the laundering the packing sweep exists to stop.  Drawing the canopy honestly (a mature evergreen oak runs 20-40 ft) both fixes the size error and makes the ground genuinely occupied.  Thematically load-bearing rather than a patch: a forest county's magistracy keeping a stand of the wood inside its own walls is the Kurogi accusation made visible.
- **Single approach torii** at the shrine, not a seven-arch avenue: per [[feedback-torii-count-numerology]] a compound shrine takes 1-2 arches.
- **Residence as two offset blocks** (gankō / flying-geese massing) with an engawa along each south face, family only - the karo has his own house in the residence court.  Each labeled area is a multi-room ZONE, not one room (the settled legibility simplification).
- **Occupant room-labels** kept as the GM's schematic convention (`Koharu's quarters`, `family quarters`) rather than okami/nakanoma/nando.  Historically an anachronism (real rooms were named by position/function and used flexibly), permitted by buildings.md, and carried on all three pool manors - flagged for GM reaffirmation, not a per-sheet defect.
- **Five privies**, not four: one attached at each end of the residence (a well-appointed house kept a guest privy by the reception suite and a family privy toward the back), plus garrison, stable-yard and gate/cart-yard.  The first draft hung the house's only privy off the *shuttered* wing - 153 ft and five rooms from anyone who lived there.
- **Cell placement by the service gate**: free variable per the grounding; here it pairs with the transfer route rather than with the gatehouse (Hayakawa's choice).
- **No interrogation room**: global rule (grounding) - do not flag.
- **Gate widths, corrected for the stroke's square line caps.**  `stroke-linecap="square"` extends each wall segment half a stroke-width (1.5 ft) past its endpoint, so every opening drew 3 ft narrower than its stated width - which had left the goods cart gate (12 ft) *wider than the ceremonial main gate* (10.3 ft of actual ink).  Endpoints are now pulled back by the cap so ink equals intent: main gate 13.3 ft > cart gate 12 ft > service gate 10.7 > divider nakamon 8 > kitchen postern 6.7.  **This is a template-level bug: Ochiba and Hayakawa both use `stroke-linecap="square"` on their wall groups and both are presumably drawing narrower openings than their comments claim.**  Not fixed here (out of scope for this map) - flagged for the GM.
- **Point glyphs at TRUE size** per the retired-doctrine ruling: kneeling mats 3x3 ft (hearing court and parley room), notice board 7x3 ft, bounty board 9.3x4 ft, hearth ~4.7 ft, fire tubs 2.5 ft, weapon rack 8x2 ft.  **Wells and boundary stones are location MARKERS** under the stroke convention (~2x), as are the striking posts (r2).
- **19 fire-water tubs**, gutter-fed and seated against their buildings, 2 at the kitchen.  Proportionate to ~19 wooden buildings (the docs' ~8-12 is calibrated to a dozen).  The two plaster kura - tax archive and sealed charcoal store - carry NONE: halls burn, kura endure.
- **Named open ground**: inner garden, shrine grove, kitchen garden, hearing court, forecourt (~1,440 sqft marshalling apron), border court (~1,100 sqft receiving garden), practice ground (1,452 sqft), cart yard (a 16 ft loading apron plus a 27 x 16 ft cart-turning and staging pocket by the gatehouse).  No unnamed central void.
- **Kanji dropped from the subtitle.**  Ubame is 姥目 in `l7r.md` and the triangle holds (姥 uba "old woman" + 目 me "eye" = *ubamegashi*, the ubame oak that names the county), but the render fonts carry no CJK face and resvg dropped the glyphs.  Recorded here instead of drawn.
