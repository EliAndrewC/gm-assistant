**Money research: how far goods traveled, and what distance did to their price**

Questions from the gm-assistant money skill (feature 220; first answers in `.claude/skills/money/questions.md`, "Fuel";
estimates E0167-E0168). Rewritten 2026-10-22 at the GM's request: generic shipping questions, not charcoal alone.
China first for routes and transport (the project's usual order), Japan beside it; each figure beside the same place
and period's day wages. Low priority.

### 1. Freight by land and by water

- **Question**: what did it cost to move goods overland (porter, pack horse or mule, ox or horse cart) per unit of
  weight per distance, and by water (river boat, canal, coastal ship) for the same? How many times cheaper was water?
- **Figure needed**: freight per weight per li or ri, by mode, beside a laborer's day; the land-to-water ratio.
- **Canon beside it**: Fox lumber is "shipped downriver" and charcoal is "significantly easier to transport"
  (`l7r.md`, "The Fox Clan's economy"). Ledger: a pack horse ~4-7 sen a ri at the post stations (0260), a porter
  ~1 sen a ri; guessed long-haul ~0.5-1 sen a bale of charcoal a ri.

### 2. How far did goods go before carriage priced them out?

- **Question**: how far did ordinary goods usually travel overland and by water (firewood and charcoal, rice, salt,
  cloth, tea, silk)? Where was carriage as much as the good itself? Is there a known rule (value per weight against
  distance, market radii) in the economic-history literature for Japan or China?
- **Figure needed**: typical and maximum hauls by good and mode; the distance at which carriage doubled the price.
- **Canon beside it**: Ryoko Owari is about 500 miles overland from Fox lands; Fox white charcoal sells "at a premium
  throughout the Empire". Ledger guess: a bale of Fox white charcoal 35-60 sen near the kilns, 60-110 in a
  neighboring clan, 250-600 at Ryoko Owari overland (E0167-E0168).

### 3. Tea caravans

- **Question**: how far did tea travel overland and by water, how was it packed (bricks, jars, chests) and carried,
  and what did it cost at the end of the road against at the source? The Tea Horse Road to Tibet, the Kyakhta tea
  trade, and tea reaching Edo (Uji, Shizuoka) are the obvious cases.
- **Figure needed**: route lengths, load per animal, and the price at origin against destination.
- **Canon beside it**: the setting has tea caravans going many hundreds of miles (e.g. "a tea caravan heading for
  Shiro Etsuko").

### 4. A famous good far from home

- **Question**: what did a premium good with a name (Kumano or Tosa white charcoal, a noted tea, a noted sake) cost
  near where it was made against in a distant city, the same year if possible? 0279 found Kumano white charcoal at
  2.2-2.5x Tama's white in Edo in 1716.
- **Figure needed**: the two prices, the distance and the mode of carriage.
- **Canon beside it**: as question 2.
