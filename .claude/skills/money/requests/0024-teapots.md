### 1. Teapots, plain to fine

- **Question**: in Edo-period Japan, what did a teapot (*dobin* or *kyusu*) cost, at three grades: a plain earthenware
  pot from a local kiln, bought at or near the kiln; a moderately good stoneware pot from a known kiln district (Seto,
  Banko, Tokoname, Shigaraki or similar) bought in a town shop; and a fine pot from a noted workshop, the kind a samurai
  household uses to receive guests. How many lidded pieces a potter threw in a day, and what a kiln district's wholesale
  price was against the shop price. Rice bowls and other small domestic ware are useful neighbors if teapots are
  silent. In China, Yixing teapots at the same grades.
- **Figure needed**: sen (or mon / laborer-days) per teapot at each grade; pieces thrown per potter-day; the
  wholesale-to-retail ratio.
- **Canon beside it**: none on pottery; the ledger's unskilled day is 12.5 sen and the skilled day 17-34 sen.
- **Region order**: Japan first, then China.
