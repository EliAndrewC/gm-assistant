what a trader kept. diagram 0280-0283 (issue #12) already gave land and river freight and how far goods traveled; this
asks the next step: the price at both ends, and the sea leg.

### 1. Producer-to-market price ratios for traded goods

- **Question**: for goods that were made in one region and sold far away, what was the price where the good was made
  (at the farm gate, the producer's market or the regional wholesaler) against the price in the city where it was
  consumed, at the same date? Japan-first: tea (Uji or Suruga against Edo or Osaka), sugar (Satsuma, Ryukyu or the
  Sanuki/Awa white sugar against Osaka or Edo), raw silk (the Joshu, Shinshu or Fukushima silk districts against Kyoto's
  Nishijin weavers), and iron (Izumo or the Chugoku tatara iron against Osaka). China: tea (Fujian or Anhui against
  Beijing or Canton), raw silk (Jiangnan against the north), sugar (Guangdong or Fujian against the Yangzi or Beijing).
  Spices, incense woods and medicinal roots if a source turns up. Say how far apart the two places were and how the good
  traveled (sea, river, land).
- **Figure needed**: the ratio of consuming-city price to producing-region price per good (plain number), with the
  distance; and each end's price per unit weight in silver (grams per kg) so the ledger can carry both ends.
- **Canon beside it**: `setting/l7r.md`, Ministry of Revenue: *"the typical effective tariff is about 15% of declared
  sales value"*, charged at the gate of the city where goods are sold, never in transit. The ledger's charcoal rows
  model the far-market price as the kiln price plus freight, *"a 15% tariff per border and a merchant's margin"*
  (`questions.md`, charcoal). And `setting/l7r.md`, Sparrow: *"tea, which can always be sold to raise moneys needed to
  buy the food which the Sparrow cannot grow themselves"*.
- **Region order**: Japan-first (Tokugawa wholesale records), then China.

### 2. Coastal sea freight

- **Question**: what did it cost to carry goods by sea along a coast, per weight and distance, and how long did the
  voyage take? Japan: the Higaki and Taru kaisen on the Osaka-Edo run, the Kitamaebune on the Sea of Japan route, the
  rice-transport (kaimai) rates. China: the coastal junk trades (the shachuan north of the Yangzi, the Fujian junks).
  How did sea freight compare with the river and pack-horse rates diagram 0280-0283 found?
- **Figure needed**: sea freight in silver per 100 kg per 100 km (or per koku-burthen per voyage, with the distance),
  and voyage days for a named run.
- **Canon beside it**: the ledger's river row from diagram 0280: *"freight by secondary river, Qing (Cheng's estimate,
  late 1600s), 100 kg carried 100 km: 4 sen to 5 sen"*; `setting/l7r.md`: the Yasuki Estates *"are at the edge of
  Earthquake Bay, and are therefore the main harbor city for southern Rokugan"*.
- **Region order**: Japan-first, then China.

### 3. What a trader kept: wholesalers' markups and shipowner-traders' returns

- **Question**: what share of the selling price did a merchant keep after costs, (a) for a wholesaler (ton'ya) between
  producer and city, and (b) for a shipowner who bought cargo at one port and sold it at another on his own account
  (the Kitamaebune "buy-and-sell" model, against carrying freight for hire)? What did one round voyage return on the
  cargo's value, and how many voyages a year? China: the coastal and Yangzi merchant houses, where records exist.
- **Figure needed**: net margin as a percent of selling price, per kind of trader; return per voyage as a percent of
  cargo value; voyages a year (count).
- **Canon beside it**: `setting/l7r.md`, interest: 15-20% a year in cities, about 25% in the countryside including fees
  (the cost of the money tied up in a cargo); the 15% gate tariff above.
- **Region order**: Japan-first, then China.

### 4. Coastal ships: size, crew, cost

- **Question**: how large were ordinary coastal trading ships (in koku of burthen), how many crew did each size carry
  and what were they paid, what did a ship cost to build and how long did it last, and what did chartering one cost?
  Japan: the bezaisen classes (the 1,000-koku sengoku-bune and the smaller coasters). China: the coastal junks.
- **Figure needed**: burthen in koku (count), crew (count), crew pay (silver per month), build cost (silver), working
  life (years), charter (silver per voyage or month).
- **Canon beside it**: the Imperial koku, *"1 koku = 48 gallons (180 liters) of rice"* (`setting/l7r.md`, Money), the
  unit a ship's burthen is measured in.
- **Region order**: Japan-first, then China.
