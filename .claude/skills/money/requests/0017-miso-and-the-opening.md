**Money research: miso and soy sauce in the basket, and which goods the opening changed in Edo 1860-67 and Beijing 1900-19**

Questions from gm-assistant (the goods-dollar koku, features 223 and 224), asked by the GM on 2026-10-25 after diagram#17's answers:

> yes go with your recommendations for the Meiji, and yes let's switch the basket to the recorded prices

**Where things stand.**
- **Meiji.** Tokyo 1882's lamp oil (kerosene) and cloth (linen) are now excluded from Rokugan's cross-era mean, and its food counts at full weight. The same rule ("keep the food, exclude any good foreign trade visibly changed") is to apply to Edo 1860-67 and Beijing 1900-19, on evidence rather than our guess.
- **Miso and soy sauce.** The standard basket holds 52 kg of soybeans per adult per year (Bassino & Ma's basket A). Diagram 0249 says these stand for miso, soy sauce, tofu and natto, priced as whole beans from a wholesale Osaka table. The GM rules that the basket switch to the prices the Mitsui Kyoto books actually record in 1745-54:
  - miso, 15.43 monme for 10 kan (about 1.28 g of silver a kg);
  - soy sauce, 91.06 monme a koku (about 1.57 g a liter).

  To do that we need quantities, which we will not guess.

### 1. How much miso and soy sauce a year
- **Question:** how much miso and how much soy sauce did an ordinary adult (a laborer's household) in Kyoto or Osaka use in a year in the mid-1700s, or the nearest time and place with a figure? If no consumption figure exists, how much soybean goes into a kg of miso and a liter of soy sauce in Edo-period practice, and what share of a household's soybeans went to miso, soy sauce, tofu and natto?
- **Leads:** Morisada (守貞謾稿) on tofu, miso and sardine sellers, listed in 0249's absence notes; domain or village consumption records; the Bassino & Ma basket's own source for its 52 kg.
- **Figure needed:** kg of miso and liters of soy sauce per adult-year, each with its source and verdict, plus whatever share of the 52 kg is left for tofu and natto.
- **Use:** the basket's soybean line becomes miso and soy sauce at these quantities.

### 2. Miso and soy sauce in the other eras
- **Question:** do the Mitsui books (Edo and Osaka from 1818, Kyoto from 1791) or any other series price miso or soy sauce in any era we hold? Do the Chinese eras price soy sauce or bean paste (jiang 醬)?
- **Figure needed:** grams of silver per kg or liter, per era, in the format of 0286 and 0302.
- **Use:** otherwise 18% of the basket's weight rests on Kyoto 1745-54 alone, with no averaging across eras.

### 3. Modern US prices
- **Question:** what are the cheapest plain household packs at a national US retailer of (a) miso (white or red, by the kg) and (b) soy sauce (naturally brewed, by the liter), in the snapshot rule's sense?
- **Figure needed:** a price a kg or a liter, with a link and the date seen.

### 4. Which goods the opening changed in Edo 1860-67 and Beijing 1900-19
- **Question:**
  - **Edo 1860-67:** of rice, salt, lamp oil, firewood, soybeans, barley and wheat, and cooking oil, which goods' prices were visibly changed by foreign trade after 1859, for example by exports pulling a good's price up or imports pulling it down? Raw silk and tea are the textbook exports.
  - **Beijing 1900-19:** the same question for rice and millet.
  - **Tokyo 1882:** its soybeans are 0.65 times rice, below every Tokugawa era's 0.89-1.43. Is that the opening (imports from Manchuria?), a grade difference, or something else?
- **Figure needed:** per era and good, changed (with the evidence) or not changed, or SILENT.
- **Use:** a changed good is marked `excluded` in its era file and leaves Rokugan's cross-era mean.
