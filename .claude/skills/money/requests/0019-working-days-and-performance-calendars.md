**Money research: how many days a year each kind of worker worked, and how often players performed**

Questions from the gm-assistant money skill, asked by the GM (2026-10-26), verbatim (dictated):

> w.r.t. the "over a 250-day year" - Do we have any indication of how many days per year different types of laborers
> worked? I assume that different professions had different number of days worked per year. That is actually something
> that I would be interested to know. Like, did live-in servants ever get time off? Were porters pretty much kept busy
> all the time because there was always portage to do? Did armed guards get hired out when needed and otherwise have to
> find other work between caravan trips? Would literate clerks actually be hired out for a day or two of jobs, or were
> they pretty much only ever hired full-time? Did playhouses put on performances literally every night? one performance
> per night. Two, it occurs to me I don't even know what Shakespeare did in the Globe Theater. With regards to the
> frequency of performances, much less what was standard at different levels, like for a theater troupe that works in a
> town and does rounds around to the different villages versus an acting troupe that puts on performances in a
> provincial city in front of a local temple versus a successful acting troupe with their own indoor theater, etc.

Why it matters: the toolkit turns a day's wage into a year's with the 250-day convention that 0250 found NOT ATTESTED
(a laborer's year = 1.89 kg of rice a day x 250 days / 151.5 kg = 3.12 koku). Wage dollars, laborer-days on every price
sheet, and any yearly income built from a day rate (a guard's, a porter's, a troupe's) rest on it.

What the record already holds (so the session does not redo it): 0250 (no count of a laborer's working days; 250 is
borrowed from European comparisons); 0115 (servants changed households on two fixed days a year, degawari; nothing on
time off); 0307 (live-in merchant clerks on yearly wages; no day pay for a hired scribe found; no guard's pay found;
Beijing escort agencies paid yearly); 0260 (porter and horse fares per stage, nothing on how steadily they worked); 0308
(city kabuki stars on yearly contracts from the 11th month; Shima villages hired puppet companies for three-day festival
runs, twice a year); 0214 (Kotohira raised a temporary playhouse about three times a year before 1835); 0187 (plays on
shrine and temple ground at festivals).

### 1. Working days by kind of worker
- **Question**: for Tokugawa Japan (Ming/Qing China beside it), how many days a year did each of these actually work or
  get paid for, and what were the regular days off: an unskilled day laborer (hiyatoi); a live-in servant (hokonin), and
  whether servants had fixed holidays (the yabuiri home-leave days, festival days); a post-station porter or packhorse
  driver (and whether stations kept them busy or idle between travelers, by season); a construction worker or carpenter
  (rain days, winter); a farm laborer by season?
- **Figure needed**: days worked or paid per year, or holidays per year, per kind of worker, with place and date.
- **Canon beside it**: `l7r.md` has a weekly county market day; no working calendar.
- **Region order**: Japan first, China beside it.

### 2. Engaged by the job or by the year
- **Question**: were these hired by the job or kept on all year, and what did they do between jobs: an armed escort of
  goods or cash (and China's escort agencies' guards: salaried staff or hired per trip?); a literate scribe or clerk
  (could one be hired for a day or two of writing, e.g. letter-writers, copyists, a temple's or village's hired hand?).
- **Figure needed**: the terms of engagement (day, trip, season, year), and trips or jobs a year where recorded.
- **Region order**: Japan first, China beside it.

### 3. How often players performed, by kind of troupe
- **Question**: how many performances a day, days a week and months a year did each give, and what were the seasons
  and closures: (a) an Edo, Kyoto or Osaka kabuki or puppet theater with its own roofed playhouse (daytime or night,
  how long a program, the theatrical year and its seasons); (b) a troupe playing on shrine or temple ground in a
  provincial city (miyaji shibai), licensed for how many days; (c) a country troupe touring villages and a county
  town (how many engagements a year, how many days each); (d) for comparison, Shakespeare's company at the Globe (time
  of day, days a week, how often the bill changed, closures for Lent and plague)?
- **Figure needed**: performances per day and per year, run lengths, and the months a troupe was idle, per kind.
- **Canon beside it**: `l7r.md` "Entertainers": *"Each county generally has an acting troupe, which usually consists of
  around 5-10 entertainers ... Acting troupes usually make most of their money on market day, though they will
  frequently travel through the village districts of the county during the rest of the week to perform."* Provincial
  cities have several competing troupes; a domain capital has numerous entertainers, some tied to one establishment.
- **Region order**: Japan first, China beside it, England (the Globe) as the GM's comparison.
