### 1. Empty charcoal bales

- **Question**: in Edo-period Japan, what did an empty charcoal bale (*sumidawara*: the woven straw, reed or *kaya*
  grass bag with its end caps and ropes) cost when bought on its own, rather than as part of a bale of charcoal? Who
  made them (the charcoal burners themselves, or village straw-workers in winter as by-employment), how long one took
  to weave, and whether they were traded as a separate good (by the hundred, to charcoal makers or dealers). Rice
  bales (*komedawara*) and other straw goods (*warazaiku*: straw mats, sandals, rope) are useful neighbors if
  charcoal bales are silent. In China, the equivalent: the basket or matting charcoal was packed in, and its price.
- **Figure needed**: sen (or mon / laborer-days) per empty bale; laborer-hours to make one; and what share of a full
  bale's price the bale was.
- **Canon beside it**: none on bales; the ledger's black charcoal bale is 15-21 sen (~4 kan, ~15 kg), 1.1-1.6
  laborer-days (diagram 0279), and an ox-cart load is ~33 bales.
- **Region order**: Japan first, then China.
