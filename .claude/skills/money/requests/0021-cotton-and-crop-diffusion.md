### 1. Cotton in Japan and China: when did it become ordinary clothing?

- **Question**: for Japan and for China, separately, when did each of these happen: first reliable record of the cotton
  plant or cloth; cloth arriving mainly as an import; first cultivation on any scale; cultivation as a regular commercial
  crop; and cotton becoming the normal clothing of ordinary people? What did cotton replace (hemp, ramie, kudzu and other
  bast fibers, silk), and how fast? How did this differ by region (in Japan, for example, the cold north against the
  Kinai) and by class, in particular low-ranking samurai against commoners? Answer the GM's direct question: in what
  century would a cotton kimono, or the Chinese equivalent, have become widespread?
- **Figure needed**: a dated stage for each country (year or century, with place), and the century in which cotton
  clothing was widespread, with who was wearing it. Any share of households or of clothing a source gives is welcome.
- **Canon beside it**: both quotations above.
- **Region order**: both; Japan and China equally.

### 2. Cotton in India and Korea, and whether it belongs on the Columbian list at all

- **Question**: the GM's framing treats cotton as a post-Columbian crop. Check that premise: which cotton species were
  grown in South and East Asia, were any of them present before 1492, and when did American species (upland, for
  example) reach India, China, Korea and Japan, and did they matter there? For India and Korea, give the same dated
  stages as question 1, including Korea's role, if any, in cotton reaching Japan. Then the GM's specific point: did the
  roughly 100 years between Columbus and the Tokugawa restrictions have any bearing on Japanese cotton?
- **Figure needed**: dated stages per country; for each species, its date of arrival and its importance.
- **Canon beside it**: none directly.
- **Region order**: India and Korea, with the species question across all four countries.

### 3. Tomatoes, potatoes and tobacco

- **Question**: for each of tomatoes, potatoes and tobacco, in China and Japan first, then India and Korea: when was it
  first recorded, when was it first cultivated, and when, if ever, did it become a common crop or a common part of diet
  or habit? How many years passed between arrival and common use? "Potato" needs care: sources in Chinese and Japanese
  may mean the sweet potato, the white potato or a native yam. Keep the plants separate and say which one each date
  concerns.
- **Figure needed**: per crop and country, dated stages and the lag in years from arrival to common use, or a statement
  that it never became common before a stated date.
- **Canon beside it**: the tobacco quotation above.
- **Region order**: China and Japan first, then India and Korea.

### 4. What decided how fast a crop spread

- **Question**: the GM's working position is that tomatoes were not a big part of Japanese cuisine even when available,
  and that China did not cultivate them on a large scale for centuries; check both. More generally, what do historians
  say made one new crop spread quickly and another slowly in these countries (cuisine, climate, the crop's tax
  treatment, commercial demand, competition with an existing crop, trade routes)? Did the Tokugawa restrictions on
  foreign trade actually stop new crops from reaching Japan, or did they keep arriving by way of China, Korea, the Ryukyus
  or Dutch trade?
- **Figure needed**: the dated facts on tomatoes, and the factors historians name, with author and the crops they apply
  to. Mark as COMPARATIVE anything drawn from outside these four countries.
- **Canon beside it**: the GM's statement on tomatoes above.
- **Region order**: China and Japan.
