**Money research: campaign costs II - arrows, notices and paper, rents, entertainment (Edo first)**

Second half of the GM's campaign questions (gm-assistant money skill, feature 219, Amendment 1; first answers in
`.claude/skills/money/questions.md` on gm-assistant `main`). Japan-first, China as comparison, each price beside the
same place and period's unskilled day wage.

### 1. Arrows

- **Question**: what did war arrows and arrowheads cost in Japan (Sengoku or Edo) and Ming/Qing China, how many did a
  fletcher make a day, and were arrows or heads routinely recovered after battle?
- **Figure needed**: price per arrow (ordinary and fine), fletcher output per day.
- **Beside it**: ledger guesses: ordinary war arrow 4-8 sen, fine samurai arrow 10-30 sen; skilled day 20-40 sen.

### 2. Notice boards and paper

- **Question**: what was posted on village and town notice boards (official *kosatsu* plaques written on wood, pasted
  paper, or both), who could post, and what did ordinary and large paper (and an ink stick) cost?
- **Figure needed**: price per sheet by size and grade; what a notice was made of.
- **Beside it**: canon/research: every farming settlement has a notice board (diagram's record); ledger guesses: a
  large sheet 1-3 sen, a wanted poster 2-4 sen.

### 3. Rents

- **Question**: what did Edo (and castle-town) rents run for a row-house room (*nagaya*), a small house, a merchant
  townhouse and a modest samurai residence, and for a storehouse (*kura*) or warehouse by the week or month? Ratio of
  rent to the building's value.
- **Figure needed**: rent per month by type, and the rent-to-value ratio.
- **Beside it**: ledger guesses: a room 15-40 sen a month, a small house 50-150 sen, a modest estate 5-20 koku a year,
  a remote warehouse 20-60 sen a week; GM notes: 2 bu per tenant for really poor housing.

### 4. Plays and entertainers

- **Question**: what did kabuki and noh seats cost by class (standing single-act tickets, pit, boxes), what did rural
  and temple-fair performances charge or collect, and how were troupes made up (actors only, or with musicians,
  jugglers, acrobats)? As a comparative footnote only: did English traveling players pass the hat before a play's end?
- **Figure needed**: price per seat class and per donation; troupe composition.
- **Beside it**: ledger guesses: standing 3-8 sen, pit 2-5 zeni, box 1-3 bu a head, street donation 0-1 sen, a
  village festival run 1-3 koku.
